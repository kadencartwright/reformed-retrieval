“And thus,” he says, “if the consequence is to be inferred correctly from this antecedent, it must
have this sense: therefore Antichrist, so far as depends on himself and in relation to his proper
measure, is not fit to terminate God’s intuitive knowledge.” He answers by conceding it: “And this
is true, because the basis of the stated termination—by which things not existing in time terminate
God’s intuitive knowledge—is not derived from the fitness of the things themselves considered in
themselves, but from their being drawn by a higher power to a certain more excellent mode of being.”

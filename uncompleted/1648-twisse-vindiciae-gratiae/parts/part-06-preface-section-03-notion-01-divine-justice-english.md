SECTION THREE NOTION I

PERKINS

God is always just, although human beings do not understand how he is just.

ARMINIUS

The first notion is entirely true, but a caution should be applied: nothing repugnant to human judgment should be attributed to God and defended as just under this pretext—that it is judged unjust by corrupt human judgment—unless it is established by a conclusive argument that this can fittingly be attributed to God. For in order to attribute any action or work to God, it is not sufficient to say that God has done it justly, although from this antecedent, “God has done it,” this consequent, “Therefore, justly,” necessarily follows.

RESPONSE

We willingly admit the caution applied to the first notion. Unless we have proved that God does something, we shall not ask that it be asserted as justly done; but if we have shown that it proceeds from God, it must be reckoned altogether just and rightfully done by God, although it appears repugnant to the judgment of human reason. For example, when Solomon teaches that God has made all things for himself, even the wicked for the day of evil, although human reason may perhaps murmur against it, we must nevertheless maintain that God is in no way unjust in this. Likewise, if the Apostle teaches that God, like a potter, makes from the same mass one vessel for honor and another for dishonor, whenever our sense protests, it must be restrained, while God must be acquitted of injustice.

Again, if God commanded Pharaoh to let his people go and meanwhile hardened Pharaoh’s heart so that he would not let them go—if only, I say, this is established from the testimony of the Holy Spirit—it must be venerated by faith as a just and holy work of God. And if the judgment of our mind is at any time offended by it, that judgment must be reproved and accustomed to think soberly. Finally, whenever Scripture testifies either that the Lord commanded Shimei to curse David, or that he openly caused Absalom to violate his father’s concubines, or that the ten tribes of Israel defected from the royal tribe, or that his own Son,

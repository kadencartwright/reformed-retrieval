VERSE 2. Better, he says, is a palmful of rest than both fists full of trouble
and affliction of spirit.

So that he may not appear to be mad without reason, the idle man states the
reason why he scorns labor and sits down in idleness and sloth: namely, a
quiet and tranquil life, even if it is meager and slender. Here observe his
deception, for he cloaks the shamefulness of idleness with the glorious name
of peace and tranquility. He next makes the pretense

[Google Books pthrWjdL0BgC | reader image PA181 | printed pp. 181–182 | begins with complete se after catchword on p. 180; ends on p. 182 immediately before Vers. 3]

that he is content with a little, although Solomon in Proverbs 13:4 affirms
that he is exceedingly desirous and insatiable in his appetites. For just as
dark, dry bread is pleasing to the laborer, so the man who lives in idleness
pursues delicacies because of the defect of his stomach. Thus we see vice
aping virtue and Satan transforming himself into an angel of light. Hence,
pleased with himself in these petty arguments, the idle man also has very
great confidence in his own wisdom. Related to this is the fact that, while
guarding himself from danger, he wishes to seem to foresee the evils that
threaten him from labor. Proverbs 26:13.

This hypocrisy is seen in other sins. The spendthrift says that he does not
wish to regard his money as God or to be a slave to his moneybags. The miser
pretends that he does not wish to give himself to pleasures like a beast and
cast aside the care of his household.

On the other hand, the idle man hardens himself in his idleness because of the
grief and unrest attending riches acquired by great labor. It is just as if he
were to say: “I see that those who weary themselves with constant and restless
labor, with long watches and night studies, heap up a great mass of wealth;
but I also see that these same men are subject to great troubles from this
abundance of wealth. Therefore I settle in my mind that it is better to
indulge in idleness than, by laboring constantly, to kindle men's envy against
me, which will tear away the peace and tranquility of my life.” For because it
was taught in verse 4 that the flame of envy is kindled by labor and an
incorrupt life, the idle man concludes that labor must be avoided.

And this is the wisdom of the flesh: because of the troubles which accompany
virtue and piety, to maintain a disposition which recoils from virtue and
piety. Thus everyone abandoned Christ when he was being led to the cross. See
Mark 15:37–38. Thus no one stood beside Paul when he pleaded his case before
the Roman tyrant. 2 Timothy 4:16. Thus in this age men permit the ministry of
the Church, exposed as it is to many difficulties, to be deserted. Scarcely
anyone is found who will allow his son to be instructed in sacred letters, by
whose aid he might be rendered fit and suitable for the ministry of the
Church. By this disposition they sufficiently declare how little concern they
have for preserving the worship of God and propagating it to posterity. Yet
without it they will lose either their commonwealth and their household
estate; or if any such thing remains, it will be more destructive to them than
if they had none at all. See the contrary example in Christ, John 11:7–9, and
in Paul, 1 Thessalonians 2:2.

*Translator's notes:*

1. A `vola` is the hollow or palm of the hand. The contrast is between one
   palmful of rest and both fists full of vexation.
2. The PA179 catchword `se` supplies the first word of the prose on PA181. The
   identical source markers disclose the join, and the paired texts do not
   repeat the catchword.
3. `Cupediæ` are delicacies or dainty foods. Cartwright's point is that the
   inactive man's professed contentment masks an appetite made fastidious by
   idleness.
4. The Latin preserves the printed references, including `Marc. 15. 37. 38`,
   and the difficult alternative beginning `sine quo tamen aut Rempublicam`.
   The English conveys the contextual construction without repairing either.
5. Running heads, printed page numbers, catchwords, and the repeated verses
   3–4 lemmata are outside this unit.

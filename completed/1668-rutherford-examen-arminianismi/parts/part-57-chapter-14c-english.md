It is asked whether a greater certainty of perseverance is required of us
than the nature of the free act itself allows, and than the certainty we have
from the indifferent acts of free choice. In *Scriptum Synodale*, article 5,
p. 198, the Remonstrants teach that the whole certainty of perseverance must
ultimately be resolved into the constancy of free choice, in whose power it
lies to persevere or not to persevere. We, on the contrary, maintain that
every such certainty is founded upon God's truthfulness, immutability, and
constancy, upon the Mediator's intercession, and upon the sealing of the Holy
Spirit.

1. Given Arminian certainty, all confidence and hope of persevering, and thus
tranquillity of conscience, is founded upon wretched human liberty, which is
more changeable than any hinge.

2. A person is only as blessed, and only as certain of the blessed state of
adoption, as he can confidently promise himself that through free choice he
will cooperate with God's grace.

3. This position entrusts and commits all God's promises, the eternity and
stability of the covenant of grace, Christ's intercession, and its fruit with
God the Father to human liberty, that most wretched guardian, after forsaking
the fountain of living waters.

4. They teach this in order to establish from it that absolute certainty

[1668 KBNL/UvA witness | logical printed p. 641 | PDF p. 690 | split word joined: absolutam]

of perseverance overturns all religion, the nature of free obedience,
commands, promises, and threats, and that it is better and more useful to lack
that certainty. Yet they themselves believe it highly probable that an
indefectible gift of perseverance is given, though in a miraculous and
extraordinary manner, to certain people confirmed in grace. It follows that
their men of stricter mortification are released from every bond of religion
and obedience. For, as they would have it, these things are *asystata*—it is
incompatible that someone should be obliged to obey God and yet be endowed
with the indefectible gift of perseverance. But they do not see that holy
fear can coexist perfectly well with certainty of perseverance, and they
always rely upon this hypothesis: that fear of God and faith are mutually
incompatible. Certainly, fear of hell and the spirit of slavery, which the
regenerate do not receive, Romans 8:15, conflicts with true faith. 1. Perfect
love casts out this fear, 1 John 4:18. 2. This fear is forbidden, Luke 12:32.
3. The regenerate worship God “without fear,” Luke 1:74; and to say that
Zechariah speaks of the fear of human beings is contrary to the sequence of
the text, for verse 75 adds, “That we may walk in holiness and righteousness
before him all the days of our life.” Nor does such fear harm a person under
the New Testament. 4. The fear commanded us has spiritual joy joined to it,
Psalm 2:11, and its object is God's goodness, Hosea 3:5. This is certainly an
argument that it is not fear properly so called concerning final ruin and

[1668 KBNL/UvA witness | logical printed p. 642 | PDF p. 691 | split word joined: esse]

apostasy, but rather a devout caution and holy concern to employ all those
means by which we may persevere in the state of grace.

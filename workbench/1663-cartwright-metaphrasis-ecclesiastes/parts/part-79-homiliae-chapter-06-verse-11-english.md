VERSE 11. When goods are many, they multiply vanity; what more does the person
gain?

Another argument is taken from circumstances accompanying riches: as they
increase, vanity grows and sprouts together with them. For as riches
increase, care to preserve them increases; fear of losing them increases; the
danger increases that they may be snatched away either by secret plots or
open force, or perish by fire and conflagration; the expense of contending at
law with adversaries, restoring collapsed buildings, and rescuing land from
sterility and barrenness increases; and anxiety to transmit these things to
posterity increases. From this Solomon concludes that little or no profit
returns to the person.

Let us therefore turn our mind to the pursuit of piety, which, as the Apostle
says, is useful for all things and has promises for the present and future
life. 1 Timothy 4. Thus far Solomon has treated the first part, namely, the
weakness of riches. The weakness of the person in using them follows.

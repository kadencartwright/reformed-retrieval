# Calvin's Fragment of an Apologetic Preface for the *Institutes* (c. 1544)

This directory presents the complete surviving Latin fragment conventionally
called *Fragmentum praefationis novae alicui Institutionis editioni
destinatae*, together with a new English translation. It is an apologetic
passage meant for a preface to an edition of Calvin's *Institutes*, not a
wholly independent monograph and not a complete recovered preface.

> **Editorial status:** This is an AI-assisted draft transcription and
> translation checked line by line against high-resolution facsimile images,
> not a peer-reviewed critical edition. A qualified Neo-Latinist should
> independently collate it before formal publication or reliance on a
> disputed reading.

## Work, date, and witness identity

| Field | Value |
|---|---|
| Author | John Calvin (Ioannes Calvinus) |
| CO editorial title | *Fragmentum praefationis novae alicui Institutionis editioni destinatae* |
| English identifier | *Fragment of an Apologetic Preface for the Institutes* |
| Date | About 1544, as inferred by the CO editors; the body has no date |
| Genre | Unfinished or unincorporated prefatory apology; edition-state paratext |
| Subject | Calvin's Eucharistic teaching, the German church concord, Luther, and the 1543 *Institutes* |
| CO extent | CO IX, printed pp. 841–846 |
| Base witness reported by CO | A scribal manuscript among the papers of the pastors of Neuchâtel, corrected in a few places by Calvin |

The CO editors report finding the fragment among papers preserved in the
library of the pastors of Neuchâtel. An amanuensis wrote the body. Calvin
added above it in his own hand: *Sequitur exemplar excusationis quae
praefationi inseretur* (“What follows is a copy of the apology that is to be
inserted in the preface”). He also made a few corrections. CO says these were
so minor that its editors silently corrected scribal errors and did not think
it worthwhile to record the traces of correction in the margin. Consequently,
this directory transcribes the **CO edition state**; CO's report does not
permit reconstruction here of every pre-correction manuscript reading.

The same sheet first contained a shorter set of proposals apparently intended
for French princes on behalf of people persecuted for religion. CO reports a
heading shared by both texts, *Pro afflictis in Gallia et praefationi addenda
institutionis 1539* (“For the afflicted in France and things to be added to
the preface of the 1539 *Institutes*”), but does not identify that heading as
Calvin's autograph. The shorter French-affairs text is a separate work and is
not included here.

The shared heading's “1539” is not the date CO assigns to this apology. The
body says the offense arose from the “latest” edition of the *Institutes*, and
the editors observe that the passages Calvin quotes verbatim first appeared
in the edition published in 1543. They therefore date the fragment to about
1544 (CO IX, prolegomena, p. LXVII). It is best understood as a draft defense
of the recent 1543 revision, prepared for possible insertion into a new
preface. It was neither conceived nor transmitted as a freestanding
systematic treatise.

## Authorial text and CO editorial apparatus

The large title at CO IX 841 is the nineteenth-century editors' descriptive
title. It is recorded above as a work identifier but bracketed as editorial
in `latin.txt`; it is not represented as Calvin's heading. The running heads,
printed page numbers, and three CO footnotes are also excluded from Calvin's
Latin. Calvin's autograph superscription is reported here from the
prolegomena, not inserted into the body as though it were the fragment's first
sentence.

The three excluded CO notes provide a useful edition-state crosswalk:

| CO IX location | Passage keyed by the note | 1543 *Institutes* parallel identified by CO |
|---|---|---|
| p. 841, note after *Fateor enim* | From *quod et semper in ecclesia receptum fuit* through *virtutem quoque sentiamus* (p. 842) | Chapter XVIII §19; CO I 1003; “almost word for word” |
| p. 843, note at *Qua stupida superstitione* | From that phrase through *quae naturae veritati non obscure repugnant* | Same chapter, §§21–22; CO I 1004; “almost the same” |
| p. 844, note at *De ratione* | From *De ratione* through *verae humanitatis* | Same chapter, §30; CO I 1010 |

The first and third notes print both the opening and closing words of their
ranges. The second supplies only its opening cue; direct comparison with
§§21–22 shows that the sustained parallel ends at *quae naturae veritati non
obscure repugnant* before Calvin resumes the apology in his own retrospective
voice.

This apparatus must not be read as saying that a c.1544 fragment was later
inserted into a book printed in 1543. CO's prolegomenon says the reverse:
Calvin excerpted wording from his recently reprinted work and then explained
and defended it in the proposed preface. In the third footnote, *postea*
means that the corresponding passage occurs **farther on** in chapter XVIII,
at §30, after the parallels in §§19 and 21–22. The mixture of verbatim
quotation, retrospective explanation, and a prospective prefatory setting is
precisely why this is an edition-state/paratext unit rather than an
independent monograph.

## Transcription and collation

The PDF text layer was used only as a provisional aid. Every printed line of
Calvin's fragment was then manually compared with 4167-by-5250-pixel renders
of PDF pages 500–502. The normalized Latin follows
[`TRANSLATION_POLICY.md`](../../TRANSLATION_POLICY.md):

- line wrapping is removed and words divided only at line endings are joined;
- CO sets the fragment as one continuous prose block; blank lines around page
  markers in `latin.txt`, and the additional rhetorical paragraphing in the
  English, are navigation aids rather than inferred authorial paragraph
  breaks;
- CO spelling and punctuation are retained, including *quum*, *nunciatum*,
  *loquutio*, *prosequutus*, *attrectari*, *Pronunciat*, and *reiicio*;
- the Greek *εὐλογία* is encoded in Unicode;
- three words divided at printed page turns are joined, with the new locator
  placed before the whole word on its new page: *san-guinem* at 842/843,
  *sen-tire* at 844/845, and *il-luxit* at 845/846;
- CO's three notes and their superscript cues are omitted from the body but
  documented above; and
- no letters remain conjecturally supplied or illegible.

The main textual cruxes are not image uncertainty but the transmitted syntax.
At p. 843, *quam ... communionem* lacks an expressed antecedent; at the same
page, *destinato* functions compactly in *ac si destinato in eos dicta
forent*; and at p. 844, plural *absurda* is followed by *quae ... esse
apparet*. All three readings are clear in the facsimile and have therefore
been preserved rather than silently regularized. The English notes explain
the minimal syntactic recasting needed for intelligibility.

## Fragment boundaries and completeness audit

The text preserved by CO begins with *Certe si laborem hunc meum* at p. 841
and ends with *quodammodo discerpamus* at p. 846. `latin.txt` and
`english.md` mark both surviving boundaries explicitly. CO prints no ellipsis
at either edge, and the final sentence is grammatically complete; “fragment”
therefore describes the surviving manuscript and its unrealized prefatory
function, not a license to invent words at either boundary.

The edition accounts for all authorial text on the six printed pages. The
counts below tokenize each uninterrupted sequence of Unicode letters as one
word and assign each word joined across a page turn to the page on which the
whole normalized word is placed.

| Printed page | Boundary in the witness | Latin word tokens |
|---:|---|---:|
| 841 | Opening through *Fateor enim* | 258 |
| 842 | Eucharistic analysis through the first half of *sanguinem* | 243 |
| 843 | Continuation through *in* before the page turn | 375 |
| 844 | Continuation through the first half of *sentire* | 363 |
| 845 | Continuation through the first half of *illuxit* | 70 |
| 846 | Continuation through the surviving close | 76 |
| **Total** | *Certe* through *discerpamus* | **1,385** |

Nothing authorial in CO IX 841–846 is silently omitted. The exclusions are
limited to the editorial title (retained as bracketed metadata), running
heads, page furniture, and the three apparatus notes fully inventoried above.
The separate French-affairs draft described in the prolegomena is outside
this work's boundaries.

The English was translated afresh from the normalized Latin and checked
against the same page images. No modern English translation was consulted or
used as a source for its wording.

# Preface to Bucer's Proceedings of the Second Regensburg Colloquy (1547)

This directory presents the complete Latin text and a new English translation
of John Calvin's unrealized preface for Martin Bucer's account of the second
Regensburg colloquy. Calvin dated the preface 1 October 1547. The nineteenth-
century editors printed it under the heading *Praefatio in Buceri Acta
Ratisbonensia altera anno 1546 instituta*.

## Work and witness identity

This text requires an unusually important distinction between the work Calvin
wrote, the book for which it was planned, and the witness printed by CO.

The CO prolegomena report that Bucer, through Oswald Myconius, asked Calvin to
write a preface and assume public responsibility for an edition that would
appear to have been undertaken without Bucer's knowledge. Calvin's text
therefore speaks as though Bucer had privately entrusted the work to him and
as though Calvin were publishing it on his own initiative. In a letter to
Myconius dated 26 October 1547, however, Calvin said that he had deliberately
kept the preface brief because he had not inspected the book. Bucer abandoned
the proposed arrangement before Calvin's preface reached him and published the
book under his own name in 1548. The preface was consequently not part of that
printed edition.

The CO editors state that they were the first to print the preface. Their base
text was not an authorial printed edition or Calvin's autograph, but a
contemporary copy in Geneva manuscript Cod. 106, fol. 198. They also knew
copies in Geneva Cod. 111, fol. 176, the Bern archives, *Epistolae* VII,
p. 843, and Simler, vol. 65. In all these witnesses the address
*Ornatissimo viro Osvaldo Myconio* precedes *Ioannes Calvinus pio lectori S.*
The CO editors identify the former as the address of the covering letter to
Myconius and the latter as the opening of the preface itself. Accordingly,
`latin.txt` includes the salutation to the pious reader but excludes the
address to Myconius.

The large title at CO IX 851, the running head at 853, page numbers, and the
separator following the text are nineteenth-century editorial or typographic
matter. The conventional Latin title and its English rendering are retained
only as identifiers; neither is represented as Calvin's own heading.

## Files

- [`latin.txt`](latin.txt) is a complete diplomatic-normalized transcription
  of the preface. It preserves edition spelling and punctuation, joins printer
  line-end divisions, and marks all four printed CO pages.
- [`english.md`](english.md) is a fresh English translation made from that
  transcription and checked against the page images. Its notes identify
  consequential lexical choices and the preface's counterfactual publication
  setting.

## Verification and use

The Latin was initially extracted from the scan and then collated manually,
word by word, against 300-DPI images of both relevant scan pages. Each image
contains two printed CO pages. Three printed-page turns divide Latin words:
*autorem* at 851/852, *expensis* at 852/853, and *obruitur* at 853/854. The
normalized text joins each word and places the new page marker immediately
before the whole word.

No complete public-domain English translation was located in the project's
bibliographic survey completed 11 August 2026. This translation was produced
afresh from the Latin; no modern English translation supplied its wording.

The English in this directory is an **AI-assisted scholarly draft**. It has
received a sentence-by-sentence source check but not an independent review by
a human Latinist. Until that review occurs, quote the Latin and printed-page
locator for exact-wording claims and identify the English as this project's
draft translation.

Suggested citation:

> instituta*, Geneva, 1 October 1547, in *Calvini Opera* IX (1870), 851–854,
> normalized Latin and draft English translation, *Reformed Retrieval*.

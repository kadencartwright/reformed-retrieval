VERSE 3. If a man should beget a hundred children and live many years, however
numerous the days of his years may be, if his soul is not satisfied with that
good, and moreover he has no burial, I say that a stillborn child is better
than he.

The vanity of the man is described who sets no end or limit to his labor for
acquiring riches and who, although he has neither son nor brother to succeed
him in his goods, nevertheless torments himself and cheats his own natural
appetite. Here, however, Solomon affirms that this miser's condition will be
no better even if he begets a hundred children; rather, it will be worse. He
will leave them naked and destitute of the conveniences of this life, and on
their account he is usually more restrictive toward himself. Solomon then
aggravates his misery by another contrary circumstance: however many years he
may live, no good comes to him from this length of life; instead, the evil is
increased. For by living sparingly and harshly throughout the duration of his
life, the torment and grief of both soul and body grow. Having many children
is a great gift of God.

Long life is also rightly reckoned among God's gifts. Yet avarice alone takes
away the sweetness of good gifts and turns God's honey-sweet blessings into
bitter curses. From all these things it is evident how highly piety should be
valued. Its possession cannot be taken away from us; it will benefit us not
only while we live but will also profit our descendants after our deaths, for
God blesses them on account of godly ancestors. Finally, piety turns all our
curses—poverty and the loss of goods—into a blessing. Romans 8.

Another attendant circumstance of this miser is that he is so utterly
stripped and plundered of all goods that there are no means left to arrange a
funeral and pay him the rites that are due. An example of this appears in King
Jeconiah, Jeremiah 22:19, who was buried with the burial of a donkey after he
had plundered the people and diverted the sacred treasure of the temple to
private use. See the similar example in Isaiah 14:19–20 concerning the
Babylonian monarch who had laid waste to almost countless nations. If we open
our eyes, we shall find that examples from our own times are not lacking.
Hence it is evident that obtaining an honorable burial is among one's
blessings, in contrast to those who pay no heed to burial, even though concern
for it has always existed among barbarous and savage nations.

Solomon next aggravates this evil by a comparison with lesser things. For the
condition of a stillborn child is preferred to the condition of this miser.
Above he equated the miser with a newly born child brought into the light,
because he departs naked from this life just as that child does. Now he
compares him with a stillborn child, whose condition is worse than that of one
who has seen the light.

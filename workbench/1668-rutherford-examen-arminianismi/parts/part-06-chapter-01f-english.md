Allied to this question is another: whether anyone may be saved in any
religion whatever.

The Remonstrants must necessarily teach this as a consequence, against
Vedelius, book 1, chapter 2.

1. They teach that a church which holds the things necessary to know and
believe for salvation—which for them are very few—is a true church even if it
errs in everything they call controversial. But those who are in a true
church can be saved.

2. If Arians, Socinians, Papists, Antitrinitarians, Sabellians, and Tritheists
believe the necessary things and hold their heresies merely through a simple
error of mind, they can, according to the Remonstrants, be saved. For
in their judgment heresy is innocence and piety. But most of those people
believe all that is necessary for salvation. Therefore salvation will be
found in any one of those sects. Indeed, who would deny that among the Jews,
who acknowledge the Old Testament as the true Word of God, many through a
simple error of mind drawn from a corrupt education believe that Mary's son
was an impostor and false prophet? Yet they believe the whole Word of God,
although more obscurely.

It is therefore reasonably asked whether the Arminian liberty of prophesying
is not libertinism and atheism. The Remonstrants deny it against Vedelius,
book 1, chapter 1. We prove it.

1. It is libertinism and atheism to teach that a person is bound to believe a
meaning contrary to Scripture because his own conscience pronounces it true.
For in this way the libertine would be obli—

[1668 KBNL/UvA witness | logical printed p. 95 | PDF p. 146]

—ged to believe from 1 John 3:9 that the regenerate cannot sin, are subject to
no law, and may fornicate, commit adultery, and kill the innocent, because
Christ is our sanctification. Yet the former doctrine is taught by the
Remonstrants.

2. An atheist and libertine is someone who believes his erroneous conscience,
and not God's Word, to be his guide.

3. An atheist and libertine is someone who thinks himself not bound in
conscience to follow the true meaning of God's Word because his conscience
pronounces another meaning truer to him.

4. An atheist and libertine is someone who believes in his conscience that he
is subject to no censure, whether civil or ecclesiastical, no determination,
no confession, and no formula of concord of either church or synod, even when
it has been made according to God's Word; and he thinks that believing this is
innocence and openly defends it as piety. But this is what the Remonstrants
believe.

5. An atheist and libertine is someone who believes that he may be an Arian,
Antitrinitarian, or Socinian as his conscience dictates to him, and may wander
through all impure sects because his conscience pronounces that they hold the
fundamentals.

6. An atheist and libertine is someone who believes that he may conceal the
truth he believes to be divine and may tolerate false and erroneous opinions
about God in others—even people who conceive God to be simi—

[1668 KBNL/UvA witness | logical printed p. 96 | PDF p. 147]

—lar to wood, stone, and four-footed beasts—provided they believe this through
—lar to wood, stone, and four-footed beasts—provided they believe this through
—lar to wood, stone, and four-footed beasts—provided they believe this through
a simple error of mind and are otherwise good and upright people. But this
is what the Remonstrants believe.

7. An atheist and libertine is someone who thinks that liberty of prophesying
can extend not only to things that are disputed, but also to things that can
be disputed. But the Remonstrants reply in book 2 against Vedelius, chapter
10: “Necessary things are not disputed by their nature; rather, those things
are disputed which have not been clearly and plainly defined in God's Word.”
Yet many raise questions about things that are clearly defined in God's Word.
For that Christ is the God-man (Θεάνθρωπος), that there are three persons of
the Godhead and one God, and that people are justified by faith alone without
works are clearly and plainly defined for us in the Word. But for Arians,
Socinians, Arminians, Antitrinitarians, and Papists these things are not
clearly defined in the Word.

Second, weighty controversies concern things expressly (ῥητῶς) stated in the
Scriptures, such as “This is my body,” and “the heavens must contain Christ
until the day of the restoration of all things.”

8. Atheists and libertines are those who teach that no mortal person, where
eternal salvation is concerned, voluntarily and deliberately holds heresies
or false opinions about God, and therefore that such people commit no crime
worthy of punishment; see book 2 against Vedelius, chapter 8. On this account,
those who sacrifice their children to Molech and blaspheme God do not sin;
neither do Jews who main—

[1668 KBNL/UvA witness | logical printed p. 97 | PDF p. 148 | stop before 2 TIM. 3.16, 17]

—tain that Mary's son was an impostor, because, as the same place says, “a
crime is not committed except by someone who knows that what he commits is a
crime and worthy of punishment.” Yet not a few Jews are ignorant that it is a
crime to sacrifice children to Molech and call Mary's son an impostor. Paul
also, while sinning in this way through ignorance when he was in Pharisaism,
was no sinner.

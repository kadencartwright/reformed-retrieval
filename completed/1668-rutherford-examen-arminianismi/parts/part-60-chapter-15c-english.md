It is asked whether the Remonstrants rightly teach in *Apology*, chapter 22,
folio 257, that it is possible for there to be no church of Christ on earth,
but for all churches to fail totally and finally. The Remonstrants teach in
the same place that, if the discussion concerns a possibility that can pass
into actuality, it is false that all can fail. But if it signifies a mere and
real possibility—not that general possibility by which, for example, it is
said to be possible for all people at one and the same

[1668 KBNL/UvA witness | logical printed p. 666 | PDF p. 715 | split word joined: momento]

moment to marry, for everyone to think one and the same thing, or for letters
thrown into the air to coalesce into a poem, and so forth—then it is true.

We teach the contrary. 1. If all can fail, Christ's kingdom will not be
eternal but will come to an end, contrary to Isaiah 9:6; Luke 1:32–33;
1 Corinthians 15:25; Micah 4:7; and Daniel 7:14. Their claim that Christ is
not deprived of his kingdom but remains invested with full right to the
kingdom, and thus remains king by right even if all his subjects individually
were to fail, is futile. They say this because his royal power in no way
depends upon his subjects and because Christ's royal power is causally
dependent upon no mortal. But if all his subjects individually were to fail
totally and finally, so that as spiritual King he did not actually flow into
his subjects by the gracious influence of his Spirit and did not actually
confer peace, righteousness, and joy in the Holy Spirit—the fruits of his
kingdom, Romans 14:17—his eternal kingdom would cease and in this way come to
an end. But the perpetuity of his kingdom in this world consists in the act,
I say, of reigning and in actual government: that he reigns over his own on
Mount Zion, that is, in the true and living church, Micah 4:7. Indeed, eternal
peace of conscience will exist in his kingdom, and this peace would
necessarily fail if all his subjects can fail. Isaiah 9:7: “Of the increase of
his dominion and of peace there will be no end, upon the throne of David and
in his kingdom, to establish it and uphold it with

[1668 KBNL/UvA witness | logical printed p. 667 | PDF p. 716 | split word joined: jure]

right and justice, from this time and forever.” Luke 1:33: “And he will reign
over the house of Jacob forever.” The house of Jacob is the holy seed, the
church, the body of Christ. But if that house should fail and become heretical
and apostate, he will not actually reign over them but will cease to reign in
the house of Jacob.

Indeed, bare royal power is not promised to Christ. Rather, a willing people
is promised to him, Psalm 110:3, and a seed that will serve him, which will be
counted to him in every age; those who come will announce his righteousness to
a people yet to be born, Psalm 22:31–32. Indeed, more than bare and titular
power belongs to him, and this promised glory of his kingdom is that they will
render him most devoted obedience. Psalm 72:5: “They will fear you as long as
the sun exists and while the moon endures, throughout an eternal generation.”
Verse 6: “As when rain descends upon mown grass, as showers water the earth.”
Verse 7: “In his days the righteous will flourish, and abundance of peace,
until the moon is no more.” And what, I ask, does Isaiah 11 say of Christ the
King, that shoot from the felled trunk of Jesse and branch flowering from his
roots? Verse 4: “He will vindicate the poor justly and reprove the meek of the
earth with equity.” Verse 6: “Therefore the wolf will dwell with the lamb, and
the leopard will lie down with the young goat,” and so forth. Verse 9: “They
will not do evil or destroy on all my holy mountain, for the earth will be as
full of this knowledge of Jehovah as waters covering the channel of the sea.”

2. By a faithful promise God gave to his Son,

[1668 KBNL/UvA witness | logical printed p. 668 | PDF p. 717]

as King and precisely as King whom he anointed as King upon his holy mountain,
the nations and the ends of the earth as a possession and inheritance,
Psalm 2:6, 8; a seed that will serve him, Psalm 22:31; a willing people,
Psalm 110:3; and dominion from one sea to the other and from the river wherever
the ends of the earth extend, Psalm 72:8, 10–12. But if it were possible for
each and every mortal to lapse into apostasy so that there were no church, all
these promises would be false and void.

3. Given all God's decrees concerning the salvation and glorification of the
elect; given God's oath made to Abraham's seed, Hebrews 6:16–18, and to his
Son, Christ, concerning his eternal priesthood and therefore his eternal
intercession for his church, Psalm 110:4; Hebrews 10:11–12; Hebrews 8:1–2;
chapter 9:24–27; and given Christ incarnate and dead, having bought the church
by his death and blood and obtained it by right of redemption, raised and
glorified, having entered heaven, made intercessor at the Father's right hand,
appointed King and Judge of the dead and the living, and made head of the
church—it is blasphemous, I say, to assert that it is possible for there to be
no church which he should bless with a blessing pursuant to God's oath, for
which he should intercede at the right hand of God the Father, and of which he
has been made King and Head.

4. It is just as contingent on God's side that all should finally fail and
perish eternally

[1668 KBNL/UvA witness | logical printed p. 669 | PDF p. 718]

as that letters thrown into the air should coalesce into a poem and all people
should think the same thing. For God no more unalterably determines people to
persevere than he determines letters thrown in this way to coalesce into a
poem. According to the Arminians, both are contradictory: that a cause should
operate contingently and that it should be unalterably determined by God.
Moreover, the dominion of providence has no greater place in one than in the
other for procuring indefectible perseverance and preserving Christ's church
on earth, because, as the Arminians would have it, both are contradictory.

6. It cannot be denied that believers honor Christ as King with royal honor.
Psalm 45:12: “And the king will delight in your beauty; and since he is your
lord, bow down to him.” 1 Timothy 1:17: “Now to the eternal King be honor.”
Revelation 2:10: “And they cast their crowns before the throne, saying, ‘You
are worthy, O Lord, to receive glory and honor and power,’” and so forth. But
if it were possible for each and every one to fail so that there were no
church on earth or in heaven, this royal honor of Christ would not exist.
Thus, the honor of Christ the King would depend upon human free choice so that
it either exists or does not exist absolutely. But if the discussion concerns
royal power as distinguished from actual honor, the Remonstrants make it
dependent upon creatures in many ways. First, parents are free to beget people
who will believe, or

[1668 KBNL/UvA witness | logical printed p. 670 | PDF p. 719]

not; it was free for Christ to be crucified or not; and it was free for
sinners to crucify him or not. Consequently, whether through the resurrection
Christ would be established as “a King invested with no royal power” and as
head of the church was entirely contingent upon and dependent upon the
creature's freedom.

7. The gates of hell would prevail most powerfully against the church founded
upon the rock if it were possible for there to be no church and for it to be
cast down from the rock into hell, contrary to Matthew 16:18; Isaiah 49:15–16;
Jeremiah 31:35–37; and Jeremiah 32:40.

JOHN 10:28.

“My sheep hear my voice.”

It is asked whether the doctrine of the marks of the church is useless and
harmful. The Remonstrants and Episcopius, disputation 28, thesis 1, say that it
is. We deny it.

1. We are expressly commanded to join ourselves to the true church in which
the voice of Christ the Shepherd sounds. Song of Songs 1:8: “Go out by the
footsteps of that flock,” and so forth.

2. The saints desire to join themselves to the church. Song of Songs 5:16:
“Where has your beloved turned, that we may seek him with you?”

3. It is necessary for salvation that a person associate himself with the
true church. Hence the apostles will that those who repent and believe receive
the church's sacraments as a sign of their union with the church. Acts 10:48;
Acts 2:38–39.

4. Communion with the saints in the external worship of God is necessary for
all who

[1668 KBNL/UvA witness | logical printed p. 671 | PDF p. 720 | split word joined: profitentur]

profess Christ, and it is included under the communion of saints which
Christians believe.

It is asked whether there are no marks of the church other than the profession
of saving faith and the external observance of Christ's commandments. The
Remonstrants say so in *Confession*, chapter 22, thesis 78. We deny it.

1. A profession of saving faith belongs to every impure assembly. Therefore,
unless a profession is sincere both with regard to the act of professing and
with regard to the object they profess—namely, the pure Word of God—it does not
distinguish the church from every other assembly.

2. Among the Remonstrants, the profession of saving faith is separated from
the preaching of the Word, which is truly a mark of the church.

3. The profession of saving faith and observance of Christ's commandments is
the mark of a single individual believer rather than of the church. The marks
being sought are the marks of the church not simply insofar as it is a
homogeneous body consisting only of believers, but insofar as it is a
heterogeneous body, the house of Jacob, the family of God, the flock, and the
city of the living God, consisting of pastors who call, persons who are
called, and the means of calling—the heard Word and the sacraments. These,
therefore, are rightly established as its signs: the sincere preaching of the
divine Word and the administration of the sacraments. They are clearly
expressed in Acts 2:41: “Therefore those who gladly received his word were
baptized, and on that day about three thousand souls were added to the
church.”

[1668 KBNL/UvA witness | logical printed p. 672 | PDF p. 721]

Verse 42: “And they continued in the apostles' doctrine and fellowship, the
breaking of bread, and prayers.” Here are the pure preaching of apostolic
doctrine and the right administration of the sacraments. And Acts 4:32: “The
multitude of those who believed was of one heart and one soul.” Verse 33: “And
with great power the apostles gave testimony to the resurrection of the Lord,
and great grace was upon them.” There saving doctrine and agreement in the
same doctrine together with mutual charity are proposed as marks of the
church.

It is asked whether our theologians wrongly establish the preaching of true
doctrine as a mark of the church. The Remonstrants say so in *Confession*,
chapter 22, thesis 8.

The preaching of the Word has various relations.

1. Insofar as it is the instrument for gathering the church, it belongs to the
church in becoming, but in this respect it is not a mark of the church.

2. Insofar as it accompanies the church already gathered, it can be referred
either to believers or to unbelievers. If it is referred to believers, they
know through the preaching of the Word that the church is the church. Just as
rationality is the specific form of a human being, so the act of using reason
is a formal act flowing from that form and a mark flowing from the inner
essence. In this way pure doctrine is as it were the essence of the church,
and the preaching of the Word is as it were an outward formal act flowing from
the church's essence and easily known by a believer. Although the preaching of
the Word is a means of the church in becoming, it is nevertheless a mark of
the same church in its established being,

[1668 KBNL/UvA witness | logical printed p. 673 | PDF p. 722]

a mark which the believer readily recognizes and by which he recognizes the
true church. But when this mark is related to unbelievers, the preaching of
the Word does not in this way make the church known to them. For since an
unbeliever does not have ears to hear, the preaching of the Word is as unknown
to the unbeliever as the church is. Yet in the first act and by its own nature
it is not equally unknown, but is better known by nature and also to us; and
in the second act, when it convicts, it also makes the church known.
1 Corinthians 14:24: “But if all prophesy, and some unbeliever or uninstructed
person enters, he is convicted by all and judged by all.” Verse 25: “And thus
the secrets of his heart are made manifest; and so, falling upon his face, he
will say that God is truly among you.” But as long as the unbeliever remains
an unbeliever, there is no mark which in the second act makes the church known
to him.

Nor is it enough for them to say that the preaching of the Word is not a mark
of the church because it is the pastor's action rather than the church's. 1.
By the same reasoning, the profession of saving doctrine is the action of one
believing person rather than of the church—for properly speaking, actions
belong to supposits—yet for the Remonstrants it is a mark. But preaching of the
Word is twofold. One kind is private preaching, or preaching performed in just
any fashion; this is not a mark of the church. The other is public and
relational preaching ordered toward a visible assembly called by the voice of
a public herald. Nor do we say that the visible church immediately fails when
formal preaching in sermons ceases and no assembly is gathered for hearing

[1668 KBNL/UvA witness | logical printed p. 674 | PDF p. 723]

the Word and administering sacred things. Thus, when we make preaching a mark
of the church, we mean the promulgation of the Word through reading, through
preaching, or—if formal sermons are absent—through communicating it according
to the measure of the Spirit imparted to each believer, though the integrity
of the church, which is a heterogeneous body, is then impaired. The rationale
of a homogeneous whole is certainly not the same as that of a heterogeneous
whole. For just as charity is twofold, so are obedience to the Word and
hearing the Word: one is partial or private, the other total and public.
Charity and hearing the Word in the former sense are marks of disciples rather
than of the church. “By this everyone will know that you are my disciples, if
you love one another,” John 13:35. But there is another charity and obedience
which are public and relational and are referred to the publicly preached
Word; this is a mark of the church.

Nor do the *Apologists*, chapter 22, folio 241, rightly formulate the state of
the question about the marks of the church as concerning “the sign through
which a person who does not know what true doctrine is may arrive at knowledge
of the true church.” For a sign which by its own nature is apt and fitted to make
the church known differs greatly from a sign through which someone actually
arrives at knowledge of the church. In the former sense, we say that the
preached Word is not a mark that actually makes the church known with respect
to an unbeliever, yet is still an intrinsically indicative mark. No one can
recognize a college of grammarians unless he first knows

[1668 KBNL/UvA witness | logical printed p. 675 | PDF p. 724]

what grammar is. If you wish to point out an Englishman to someone by the fact
that he is a man dressed in gilded purple, but that person does not know what
gilded purple is, the purple will not be an identifying mark to the person who
does not know purple.

But in order to set forth our doctrine of the marks of the church more
precisely against our Adversaries—the Roman Catholics, Arminians, Socinians,
and Separatists—the following distinctions must especially be noted.

1. The church insofar as it is true and the church insofar as it is visible
have different marks. The mark of the church insofar as it is true is that
Christ's sheep hear his voice, John 10:27–28. The mark of the church insofar
as it is visible is that, as God's household, it publicly professes and
preaches Christ's name and faith.

2. One thing is the mark of the mystical church, and another the mark of the
ministerial church. The mark of the mystical church is not the bare profession
of the Word but also holiness of life, for the Savior says, “By this everyone
will know that you are my disciples, if you love one another.” The mark of the
ministerial church is the pure preaching of the Word, just as a standard is
the κριτήριον, “criterion,” of a particular army and distinguishes it from all
other armies.

3. One mark of the church is indicative of the church in the first act and by
itself and its own nature. Another is a mark in the second act and actually
makes the church known. Or to put it this way, one thing is a mark and another
a sign. The pure preaching of the Word, by itself and its own nature, is for
everyone an essential mark intrinsically indicative of the church, because
the

[1668 KBNL/UvA witness | logical printed p. 676 | PDF p. 725 | split word joined: prædicatum]

preached Word is that which explains to everyone what the church is and how it
is distinguished from every other assembly. For what a thing is cannot be
explained through something more unknown or equally unknown. But the pure
preaching of the Word is not a mark of the church in the second act that
actually makes it known to each and every person, because to the very many who
labor under blindness the preaching of the Word is as unknown as the church
itself. To a blind person, dawn is not a mark that makes the coming sunrise
known. For if you were to show a blind person that the sun would soon rise
because dawn had now risen, you would demonstrate something unknown to him by
something equally unknown. Yet dawn does not thereby cease to be, by its own
nature and in the first act, a mark primarily indicative of sunrise. In
exactly the same way, to an unbeliever whose eyes the prince of this world has
covered and darkened, the preaching of the Word is not a mark that in the
second act makes the church known. For the preached Word is as unknown to him
as the church itself. What then? The preaching of the Word is nevertheless by
itself an intrinsically indicative mark of the church.

4. Considered in different ways, the preached Word assumes different
relations. 1. The preaching of the Word, insofar as it is the instrument for
gathering the church, is a means and not a mark of the visible church,
Romans 10:14; 1 Corinthians 1:23. For the preached Word is truly prior by
nature both to faith, through which the invisible church is constituted, and
to the profession of faith, through which the visible church is

[1668 KBNL/UvA witness | logical printed p. 677 | PDF p. 726 | split word joined: constituitur]

constituted. Therefore, under this consideration it cannot be a mark of the
church. Yet the Separatists, Arminians, and Socinians wrongly infer from this
that the preaching of the Word is not a mark of the church once gathered. For
the same royal standard is both the means of gathering an army and a mark for
distinguishing the king's already gathered army from all others. Next, the
Word preached in just any fashion and in passing before the church is
gathered—that is, an itinerant, occasional, and unfixed preaching of the
Word—is not a mark of the visible church. Thus Paul preached the Word to the
Athenians and to many Gentiles who held it in ridicule and contempt, yet that
assembly was not a visible church. But fixed and established preaching of the
Word is a mark of the church, for where the pastors' tents are fixed, there
are the flock and the fold, Song of Songs 1:8. The Word preached in a fixed
rather than an itinerant state is considered, first, simply as it is preached
and solemnly heard. In this way it is a mark of the ministerial church,
because visibly pastoral actions indicate a visible flock. It is also a mark
of the mystical church, either already existing there or soon to exist,
because God does not light the lamp of the preached Word in vain and without
purpose. Or, 2. the preached Word is considered as having the efficacy of the
Holy Spirit joined to it. In this way, against the Socinians, we say that it
is a mark of both the invisible and the visible church: of the invisible,
because the

[1668 KBNL/UvA witness | logical printed p. 678 | PDF p. 727 | split word joined: Verbum]

Word preached with fruit gives it being and thus essentially constitutes the
invisible church; but as that same preached Word enters the senses and shows
that the church exists where the Word is preached, it is a mark of the visible
church. Therefore, whatever the Racovians may teach to the contrary, nothing
prevents the preached Word both from essentially constituting the invisible
church and from being a mark of the visible church.

5. Some marks of the church are improper, accidental, and temporary; others
are proper, essential, and perpetual. The glory of miracles and the courage
and constancy of martyrs once belonged to the former kind, but these are
neither essential nor perpetual marks.

6. Insofar as the church is the effect of the Word and of the immortal seed by
which it was begotten, it is better known to us and posterior to the preaching
of the Word, because an effect is better known to us than its cause. We are
speaking here, however, of the mystical church of the saints. Nothing,
therefore, prevents the preaching of the Word from being both better known and
less known than the church in different respects.

7. In this controversy about the marks of the church, the discussion may
concern either a precise, essential knowledge of the church or a confused
knowledge of it. If it concerns the former knowledge, the preaching of the
Word leads only true believers to knowledge of the church, because they alone
understand what the

[1668 KBNL/UvA witness | logical printed p. 679 | PDF p. 728 | split word joined: Verbum]

preached Word is and, from it, what the church is, as shown and demonstrated
to them through such a preached Word. Therefore, to them it is a mark that
makes the church known. But if the discussion concerns confused knowledge of
the church, then to unbelievers and those not yet regenerate the preaching of
the Word is a mark better known than the visible church and leads them, as
such persons, by a knowledge that is always cruder and more confused, into
knowledge of the visible church. Thus the Word is always the means of every
knowledge we can have of the church. In the same way a teacher is better known
to a pupil than Aristotle's *Logic*, because the teacher shows a novice pupil
the recondite doctrine shining forth in Aristotle's *Logic*.

By these distinctions, whatever the Separatists, Roman Catholics, Arminians,
or Socinians assert against this mark can easily be refuted. Nor do we
maintain that preaching of the Word in just any form is a mark of the visible
church, but only fixed preaching.

1. Hearing and doing whatever God commands made the Jewish church conspicuous
and visibly wise in the sight of the nations, Deuteronomy 4:6.

2. The preaching of the Word and administration of the sacraments are so
proper to the visible church that they distinguish it from every other
assembly. Psalm 147:19–20: “He shows his Word to Jacob, and his statutes and
testimonies to Israel. He has not done so for every nation.” Deuteronomy
12:29–32; Isaiah 2:2–3; Isaiah 59, final verse.

[1668 KBNL/UvA witness | logical printed p. 680 | PDF p. 729]

3. A definition of the visible church is drawn from Acts 2:42: it is an
assembly which professes the faith and continues in the apostles' doctrine,
in fellowship, and in the breaking of bread. In this way that assembly is
distinguished from every other assembly.

4. The planting and gathering of visible churches is always explained in the
Word by the preaching of the Word and the administration of the sacraments,
Matthew 28:19–20.

5. Christ's sheep hear his voice in pastors who have been legitimately sent,
John 10:27–28.

6. Finally, the church is an assembly built upon the doctrine of the prophets
and apostles, Ephesians 2:20. Nor for any other reason is it called “the pillar
and foundation of the truth,” 1 Timothy 3:15, than because it teaches,
professes, and shows God's Word to others.

Thus Augustine, *On the Unity of the Church*, chapter 12; Tertullian, *Against
Heretics*; Jerome on Psalm 133; and Chrysostom, homily 49 on Matthew, maintain
that we must seek the true church through the preached Word of God, not
through human traditions.

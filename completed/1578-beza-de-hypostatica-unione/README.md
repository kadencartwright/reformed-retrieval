# Theodore Beza, *De hypostatica duarum in Christo naturarum unione*

> **Working edition:** This directory contains a complete AI-assisted
> transcription and translation of the delimited work. It is not yet an
> independently reviewed or publication-ready edition.

## Work identity

- **Author:** Theodore Beza
- **Full collected title:** *De hypostatica duarum in Christo naturarum
  unione, adversus Jacobi Andreae assertionem*
- **Date:** 1578
- **Subject:** The hypostatic union and Reformed–Lutheran Christological
  controversy

## Base witness and boundaries

The controlling text is Beza's author-revised 1582 *Tractationes theologicae*,
volume I, printed pp. 625–645, logical scans 649–669:

- [volume record](https://www.e-rara.ch/zuz/content/titleinfo/7831557)
- [isolated chapter PDF](https://www.e-rara.ch/download/pdf/8014001.pdf)
- [IIIF manifest](https://www.e-rara.ch/i3f/v20/7831557/manifest)

Beza's reply ends near the top of printed p. 645. The appended extract headed
*Ingenua D. Lutheri confessio de duabus Christi naturis* begins on that same
page and is documentary material, not a continuation of Beza's reply. It must
be kept separate if included. The superficially promising [1579 EEBO-derived
witness](https://archive.org/details/bim_early-english-books-1641-1700_theodor-beza_1579_0)
is a much longer dialogue with Johann Pappus, not this short reply to Jakob
Andreae, and its OCR must not be substituted for the target text. This tract
therefore requires fresh transcription from the Public Domain 1582 images.

## Current files

The `parts/` directory now contains a complete working transcription and
translation of Beza's reply:

- `parts/complete-latin.txt`
- `parts/complete-english.md`

Both files carry the full sequence of printed-page markers 625–645 and end
with Beza's final sentence near the top of p. 645. The distinct Luther extract
beginning later on that page is explicitly excluded. The 1582 page images,
rather than the unrelated 1579 Pappus dialogue, controlled the transcription.
An apparent repeated *abstracto* in the 1582 printing on p. 641 is preserved
and explained in the English note. Independent Latin and historical review is
still pending.

# Calvin, *A Victory Song Sung to Christ* (1541)

This directory presents the complete authorial Latin and a new English
translation of John Calvin's *Epinicion Christo cantatum*. It includes
Calvin's prose preface to the reader and all sixty-one elegiac couplets.

> **Editorial status:** This is an AI-assisted draft transcription and
> translation checked line by line against the facsimile, not a peer-reviewed
> critical edition. A qualified Neo-Latinist should collate it independently
> before it is used for formal publication or as the sole basis for a disputed
> interpretation.

## Work and witness identity

- **Author:** John Calvin (Ioannes Calvinus)
- **Latin title:** *Epinicion Christo cantatum*
- **English title used here:** *A Victory Song Sung to Christ*
- **Date assigned by the title:** 1 January 1541 (*Calendis Ianuarii anno
  M.D.XLI*)
- **Place of composition:** Worms
- **First publication:** Geneva, Jean Girard, 1544
- **Extent in the edition:** CO V, printed pp. 417–428
- **Form:** a prose preface followed by 61 elegiac distichs (122 verse lines)

The work date and edition date must not be conflated. Calvin dates the song to
New Year's Day 1541 and says in his preface that he composed it at Worms for
amusement and circulated it only among a few friends. He printed it in 1544
after learning that an inquisitor's edict, confirmed by the court of Toulouse,
had already condemned it and thereby generated public curiosity.

The *Calvini Opera* editors report that only two copies of the 1544 first
edition were then known, at Bern and Basel, and that they collated the Basel
copy. They also describe a 1555 bilingual printing containing Conrad Badius's
French verse translation. Those bibliographical reports occur in the CO
editors' prolegomena, pp. XLVII–XLIX; neither the editorial prose nor Badius's
French is part of Calvin's Latin text presented here.

## Authorial and editorial matter

CO V 417–428 contains several materially different layers:

- The reproduced 1544 title page at pp. 417–418 is publisher/bibliographical
  paratext. Its title, author, work date, place, printer, and publication date
  are recorded here, but its display typography is not treated as a second
  occurrence of Calvin's poem.
- Pp. 419–420 are blank.
- Calvin's authorial text begins at pp. 421–422: the repeated title, his prose
  address *Io. Calvinus lectori*, and then the complete poem at pp. 423–428.
- Running heads, page furniture, the CO editors' four biographical footnotes
  on Eck, Cochlaeus, Nausea, and Pelargus, and the editors' prolegomena are
  excluded from `latin.txt`.
- The translator's short identifications of those four polemicists are new
  explanatory notes; they are not translations of the CO apparatus.

This separation matters because treating the whole twelve-page CO extent as
Calvin's prose would silently mix nineteenth-century editorial scholarship
with the sixteenth-century work.

## Transcription and collation

The PDF text layer was used only to produce a provisional reading. Every line
of the preface and poem was then inspected against the rendered images of PDF
pages 273–276. The normalized Latin follows the repository policy:

- prose is reflowed and words divided only at printer line endings are joined;
- the 122 verse lines, their order, and the detached final distich are
  preserved;
- edition spelling and punctuation are retained, including *Quum*, *Vormatiae*,
  *Iaccho*, *caetera*, and the period before the final *Ut* clause;
- readings were verified from the image and OCR substitutions corrected where
  necessary, notably in *munia*, *perpete*, *cruentas*, *ruet*, *Vibrat*,
  *variis*, *Edomiti*, *numine*, and *inflatus*;
- the edition's apparent metathesis *flatigiosa* is retained rather
  than silently emended to intended *flagitiosa*; the English renders the
  unambiguous sense, “shameful”;
- no supplied letters remain conjectural or illegible; and
- the two consecutive page markers for each image are placed together because
  this portion of CO is set across the full text measure under paired printed
  locators, leaving no visible internal transition at which to separate its
  odd and even numbers.

The especially consequential reading is *Edomiti* at CO V 426. The facsimile
prints one word, not *et domiti*. It is not an ethnonym but the masculine
plural perfect participle of intensive *edomo*: “those thoroughly subdued.”
The following relative clause identifies these captives as those who wage war
against Christ. The image likewise clearly has Christ's sacred *numine*
(“divine majesty/power”), not *nomine* (“name”), as some later quotations do.

## Meter and translation

CO calls the work *carmen elegiacum*. Its sixty-one distichs follow the
Neo-Latin elegiac pattern: a dactylic hexameter followed by a pentameter. The
last distich is separated typographically from the first sixty and functions
as a modest authorial coda. The translation preserves every couplet and uses
one English line for each Latin line. It is unrhymed accentual prose-verse; it
does not claim to reproduce quantitative hexameters and pentameters.

The English was translated afresh from `latin.txt`, with the page images
rechecked where grammar, names, or word division affected the sense. No modern
English translation was consulted or used as a base. Badius's 1555 French was
not used as an English intermediary; the short closing excerpt quoted in the
CO prolegomena was considered only afterward as an early reception check, and
the Latin remains controlling. Ambiguities that cannot responsibly be hidden
in smooth English—especially *cardine laetitiae*, the fifth *lustrum*, the
compressed Satan-and-pope syntax, *fracta ... summa*, *Edomiti*, *frons*, and
*lupata*—are explained after the translation.

## Completeness audit

The authorial content is complete relative to CO V 421–428:

- 1 repeated author/title/date heading;
- 1 complete prose address to the reader;
- 22 distichs at pp. 423–424;
- 28 distichs at pp. 425–426;
- 10 continuous distichs plus 1 detached closing distich at pp. 427–428;
- **61 distichs / 122 Latin verse lines total**, matched by 122 English verse
  lines.

The six printed locator pairs 417–428 have all been accounted for. Nothing
authorial has been silently omitted; the only exclusions are the blank leaf,
duplicated title-page display matter, and material explicitly identified above
as nineteenth-century editorial apparatus.

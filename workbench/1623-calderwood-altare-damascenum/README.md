# David Calderwood, *Altare Damascenum* (1623)

> **Working edition in progress:** This directory contains an AI-assisted,
> diplomatic-normalized Latin transcription and an independent close English
> translation. Neither has received human review, and neither is
> publication-ready.

## Work identity

- **Author:** David Calderwood (1575–1650), writing in the first edition under
  the anagrammatic pseudonym “Edwardus Didoclavius”
- **Title:** *Altare Damascenum, seu Politia Ecclesiae Anglicanae obtrusa
  Ecclesiae Scoticanae*
- **Edition used:** 1623, without stated place or printer
- **Structure:** Calderwood's large examination of English ecclesiastical
  polity, with Daniel Tilenus's *Paraenesis* quoted and refuted at relevant
  points, followed by separately authored or anonymous Scottish-polity
  documents

## Controlling witness and rights

The controlling witness is the 1623 first Latin edition in the Princeton
Theological Seminary Library, digitized by the Internet Archive as
[`altared00cald`](https://archive.org/details/altared00cald). The volume and its
scan are public-domain source material. Internet Archive page images, not its
OCR, control the transcription.

The cleaner Leiden reset of 1708,
[`altarasce00cald`](https://archive.org/details/altarasce00cald), is a comparison
witness only. It explicitly identifies David Calderwood and calls itself an
edition “more elegant and corrected than the former,” but the digitized copy
is textually defective near the beginning: it jumps from printed p. 3 to
printed p. 6. The p. 3 catchword `Capituli` does not join p. 6 (`Hoc caput
alterum`), so printed pp. 4–5 are absent. Nothing is silently supplied from
that reset.

The 1621 English
[*Altar of Damascus*](https://quod.lib.umich.edu/e/eebo/A17571.0001.001?view=toc)
is a much shorter antecedent, not a translation of this expanded Latin work,
and it has not been used as an English wording source. The selected modern
translation of propositions identified in the
[research inventory](../../research/untranslated-latin-treatises.md) is both
partial and CC BY-ND; it likewise has not been used as a wording source.

## Composite map and attribution warning

The controlling volume has 1,088 access-PDF pages (1,090 IA frames). Its
principal boundaries are:

- PDF pp. 1–4: cover, binding, and ownership matter, excluded.
- PDF p. 5: 1623 title page; PDF p. 6 is its verso.
- PDF pp. 7–12: Calderwood's unnumbered address `Lectoribus benevolis`.
- PDF pp. 13–967: the main *Altare Damascenum*, printed pp. 1–955.
- PDF pp. 968–973: index to the main work.
- PDF pp. 974–976: Andrew Melville's separately attributed
  `Anti-Tami-Cami-Categoria`.
- PDF pp. 977–992: the separately attributed letter `De regimine Ecclesiae
  Scoticanae`, by “Hieronymus Philadelphus.”
- PDF pp. 993–1084: the anonymous *Vindiciae* of that letter against John
  Spottiswood.
- PDF pp. 1085–1088: blank or binding matter, excluded.

The main work is itself composite in presentation. Calderwood incorporates and
refutes Daniel Tilenus's *Paraenesis ad Scotos*, and the final chapter directly
addresses Tilenus's taunts and accusations. Quoted adversarial matter must not
be attributed to Calderwood merely because it appears within his book. The
letter on Scottish polity, Melville poem, and anonymous *Vindiciae* are also
distinct documents, not silently part of Calderwood's continuous authorial
prose. Part 01 contains none of the appended documents and does not reach the
embedded Tilenus refutation.

## Editorial method

Latin long *s*, ligatures, line-end divisions, and typographic `u/v` are
normalized. Edition spelling and substantive wording are retained. Running
heads, signatures, catchwords, ornaments, and bare printed page numerals are
omitted. Marginal references and quoted documentary material are retained;
source markers record printed page, IA leaf, and access-PDF page.

The English is a fresh, full translation of the paired Latin, not a summary.
It has not been copied from either the short 1621 English antecedent or a
modern selection.

## Current unit

**Part 01 is complete as an AI-assisted, unreviewed working draft.** It begins
with Chapter I on 1623 printed p. 1 / IA leaf 13 / PDF p. 13. It includes the
chapter's opening account of royal supremacy, the enumerated customary royal
rights, appeals, the making of ecclesiastical canons, and the calling and
authority of synods. It ends on printed p. 17 / IA leaf 29 / PDF p. 29 with
Bilson's sentence denying synods independent authority to hear and settle
controversies when the prince refuses permission. The new numbered subsection
`2 Concernunt Canones sive leges Ecclesiasticas administrandas & exequendas`
on the same page is excluded.

- Latin: `parts/part-01-chapter-01-royal-supremacy-latin.txt`
- English: `parts/part-01-chapter-01-royal-supremacy-english.md`
- Both files contain seventeen synchronized source markers, printed pp. 1–17
  / IA leaves–PDF pp. 13–29.

The files remain **AI-assisted and unreviewed**. They require complete
comparison by a qualified Latin editor before quotation or publication.

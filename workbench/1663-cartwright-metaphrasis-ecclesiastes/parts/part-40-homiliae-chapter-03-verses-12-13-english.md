VERSE 12. I know that there is no good among them except that each should be
glad and do good in his life.

VERSE 13. Indeed, whenever any man eats and drinks and enjoys good from all
his labor, this is a gift of God.

The second objection is that the godly are dealt with wretchedly amid this
vicissitude of all things and their change into the contrary, since they
cannot escape it. I reply that this good remains for them: they can be glad
and do good for as long as they are alive. The movement and change of human
affairs does not take away from the godly the benefit of living joyfully. For,
resting by faith in the Lord, the godly make use of present things; resting
content in God, they live one day at a time and are not anxious about
tomorrow. Next, they know that nothing they enjoy can be taken away without
the will of their heavenly Father. What can rightly sustain them and render
their mind tranquil is that God will take away the benefits of this life only
for their good.

Solomon teaches further that this gladness of mind is shared with those who
devote themselves to holiness and good works, and that true and solid joy is
found nowhere except among those who pursue holiness. If he had said only
that there is no good in this life except that a person be glad and exult,
everyone would have seized eagerly upon this saying. But they reject the
addition about living well and piously, as though joy purchased at that price
were too costly, even though holiness of life is the source of true and solid
joy. For the joy of the ungodly, like thorns which fire consumes in a moment,
quickly passes away. Those who profess the truth but cast aside the pursuit of
piety and doing good ought to rejoice least of all. Thus the prophet Hosea,
chapter 9, §10: “Do not rejoice, Israel, as the peoples exult, because you
have prostituted yourself away from your God; you have loved a prostitute's
wages on every threshing floor of grain.”

VERSE 13. Indeed, whenever any man eats and drinks and enjoys good from all
his labor, this is a gift of God.

Yet it must not be thought that this gladness which a godly person enjoys is
owed to his own merit or can be obtained by any skill of his own, especially
amid such great variety and change of affairs. It is a free gift of God, which
he bestows upon his own despite everything that opposes it unwillingly and
resists it. Since more was said about this in the preceding chapter, I refrain
from speaking of it here.

*Translator's notes:*

1. Verses 12 and 13 form one objection and answer. The second occurrence of
   the verse-13 lemma introduces the final clarification that gladness is
   God's free gift, not a reward earned by merit or skill.
2. The edition prints the Hosea reference as `cap. 9. §. 10.` and the
   quotation as `Ne læteris Israël prout exultant populi`; the paired texts
   retain those readings without replacing them with a modern rendering or
   silently regularizing the reference.
3. The parenthesis `invitis & refragantibus omnibus quæ obsistunt` describes
   every opposing thing as unwilling and resistant while God nevertheless
   bestows his gift. Running heads, page numbers, the signature `L 3`, and the
   catchword for the next verse are omitted under the stated editorial method.

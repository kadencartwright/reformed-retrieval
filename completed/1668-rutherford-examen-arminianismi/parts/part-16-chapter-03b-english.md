EPHESIANS 1:4

“Just as he elected us before the foundation of the world”—that is, from
eternity.

It is asked whether election to glory was made from eternity. The Remonstrants
say in *Apology*, chapter 8, folios 190–191, that it is not an eternal action
immanent in God, but an external, transient action begun in time, contrary to
what has hitherto been commonly believed. We maintain that it was made from
eternity.

1. For in Ephesians 1:4, “God elected us before the foundations of the world
were laid.” In 2 Timothy 1:9, “Grace was given to us before the ages began.” In
Psalm 90:1, “You have been our dwelling place in every generation”; verse 2,
“When the mountains had not yet been brought forth, or you had formed the earth
and the habitable world,” and so forth. In Romans 9:11, “The children not yet
having been born,” and verse 13, “I loved Jacob, but hated Esau.” In Isaiah
54:8, “With everlasting kindness I have mercy upon you.”

2. For Christ was elected before this world existed, 1 Peter 1:20.

[1668 KBNL/UvA witness | logical printed p. 258 | PDF p. 309]

3. For if election began in time in this way, it would be a temporal promise,
and God would say, “Believe, and you will be elect to glory.”

4. For predestination to glory would be calling. But Paul says otherwise in
Romans 8:30: “Those whom he predestined, he called.”

5. For election is immutable, Romans 9:11; Hebrews 6:17. But a transient action
begins and ends.

6. For God would newly will and decree something which he did not decree from
eternity; he would do in time something which he did not decree from eternity
that he would do. This would be rash.

7. All the Holy Spirit's arguments in Scripture from God's eternal decree would
be null and empty, Jeremiah 1:5; Isaiah 14:24; 2 Timothy 1:9.

It is asked wherein that absolute and free good pleasure of God of which Paul
speaks in Romans 9 consists in the article of election. The Arminians maintain
that it consists in this: it belongs to God's absolute choice to establish any
conditions whatever of justification and glorification—whether faith, good
works, or even, as others teach, eating the fruit of a tree, or any good work
whatever. God's freedom shines in this, according to Romans 9: that he decreed
to have mercy upon believers and to save believers, when he could have saved
workers or had mercy upon workers. We, on the contrary, although we grant God
absolute freedom in electing either these or those means, nevertheless hold
that God's most free will and good pleasure in the article of election consists
in

[1668 KBNL/UvA witness | logical printed p. 259 | PDF p. 310]

this: that he elects this person rather than that one to eternal glory.

1. For in this way the Arminians, with their Corvinus on Romans 9, are compelled
to understand the elder brother as the law, and the younger as the gospel or
covenant of grace. Thus God hated the law and workers before they had done
anything good or evil, and loved the gospel and believers before they had done
anything good or evil.

2. If God's absolute freedom consists in the election of these conditions—the
gospel and faith—rather than the law and works, then the law and gospel are
subjects of such a kind that they can be said to do good or evil.

3. The law and gospel are born from a woman's womb.

4. God has mercy upon the gospel or faith, and hardens the law.

5. God hates the law before the law is born from a woman. These are empty
dreams.

6. But the entire text cries out that Paul is treating persons, not the
conditions of two covenants. For he introduces Isaac and Ishmael, Jacob and
Esau, and Pharaoh. Among these he loves certain persons and has mercy upon
them; others he hates, hardens, and does not have mercy upon.

It is asked whether, because God elected us in Christ in Ephesians 1 and God
blesses us in Christ with every spiritual blessing, Christ will therefore be
the

[1668 KBNL/UvA witness | logical printed p. 260 | PDF p. 311]

meritorious cause of our election and predestination to glory. Response: By no
means. The scholastics teach that no cause of predestination is given with
respect to the act of willing or predestining, but that a cause is given with
respect to the things willed—namely, that Christ is the meritorious cause with
respect to grace and glory.

3. Predestination is not so much grace as the eternal preparation and
ordination of a person to grace.

2. By “blessings” are understood the promised gifts of grace. But
predestination to glory, or the volition of good in God, is not a promised gift
in Christ. For all the promises are Yes and Amen in Christ, 2 Corinthians 1:20.

It is asked whether each and every mortal—whether the insane, the deaf,
infants, Indians, and those outside the visible church—is either elected to
life or reprobated. The Arminians deny that there is an election of such
persons, but only of those who are in the visible church and of adults who
possess the use of reason. We, however, maintain that there is election or
reprobation of each and every mortal without exception.

1. The deaf, the mentally incapacitated, Indians, and infants have immortal
souls, and after death they are either tormented in hell or enjoy eternal
blessedness with God. Therefore, by God's eternal providence, and thus by
predestination, they are destined from eternity to the possession of one or
the other of these two lots, and in time they are sent to it. Otherwise,

[1668 KBNL/UvA witness | logical printed p. 261 | PDF p. 312]

God is a rash agent and blind fortune reigns.

2. For those outside the visible church either sin or do not sin. If they sin,
they are capable of punishments and rewards, and God will render to each
according to his works on that day of Christ's coming; he will judge them and
the whole world. Therefore, from eternity he designated some for rewards and
others for punishments. If they do not sin and the law of nature does not bind
them, then they are perjurers, liars, fornicators, and murderers with impunity;
and those who are chaste, just, temperate, and despisers of vainglory are
without hope of reward.

3. “Those who sin without the law will perish without the law,” Romans 2:12.
Therefore, they are destined from eternity for punishments.

4. In Romans 9:11 Paul says that before the children were born, God elected one
and reprobated the other.

5. God leaves some who were born of good parents to their natural hardness, as
Esau and Absalom, while he efficaciously calls others born of the most wicked
parents. This proves that God has freedom to elect or reprobate infants.

6. Many infants are preserved alive, and God favors them with the gospel and a
gracious conversion. Others he removes from their midst by the sword, burning
alive, and slaughter, whether in the womb or hanging at the breast, as is
evident in the slaughter of the Sodomites, Amalekites, and others. Therefore,
by the same reasoning God has his freedom here concerning the eternal states
of infants.

[1668 KBNL/UvA witness | logical printed p. 262 | PDF p. 313]

7. The Remonstrants concede that prayers benefit infants, article 1, page 46.
But to what end, if neither election nor reprobation has a place among them?

8. According to the Remonstrants' doctrine, how was grace in Christ given to
all mortals within or outside the church, and how did the Savior die for all,
even Indians and Americans? How does God will each and every person to be saved
and the gospel to be preached to all, if neither election nor reprobation
pertains to such persons?

It is asked whether the Remonstrants rightly teach in *Synodical Writings*,
article 1, page 7, that glory is considered (1) under the aspect of an end and
(2) under the aspect of a reward. When it is considered under the aspect of an
end, they teach that God elects all to glory, John 3:16; 2 Corinthians 5:19;
1 Timothy 2:4, because he wills all to be saved. From this election he is
efficaciously moved to confer the necessary means. This election is not
peremptory, and neither faith nor salvation necessarily follows from it,
because, with this election posited, it is possible that all remain
unbelievers, page 9. But for them election is peremptory when God, considering
glory under the aspect of a reward, has peremptorily decreed to give it as the
recompense of obedience rendered. The object of this election is a dying human
being, and the gift of the Savior and faith precede this election. So they
teach. We judge this doctrine blasphemous.

1. For there is no election of each and

[1668 KBNL/UvA witness | logical printed p. 263 | PDF p. 314]

every person according to Scripture or even according to sound reason. Indeed,
it is a contradiction in terms and non-election.

2. By equal reasoning, eternal destruction ought to be considered as a reward
or punishment and as an end. Thus God reprobated each and every mortal from
eternity and destined them for destruction as an end, which is absurd. For God
equally wills all to be damned if they do not believe, from an affection of
justice, as he wills all to be saved if they believe, from an affection of
mercy. For if there are certain natural affections in God antecedent to
rewarding mercy, by equal reasoning there are in him such affections antecedent
to punishing justice.

3. For it is absurd that God should elect some to eternal glory as an end whom
he in no way elects to glory as a reward. Indeed, in this manner he ordains
human beings to glory and does not ordain human beings to glory.

4. The regenerate are truly called elect in this life. In Matthew 24:25 they
are those whom false Christs attempt to seduce. In Ephesians 1:4 they are those
who in this life are favored with every spiritual blessing in Christ. In
1 Peter 1:4 they are those who are sprinkled with Christ's blood and believe.
In Acts 13:48 they are those who believe because they were ordained to eternal
life. But according to this doctrine, no one is elect unless he is dead and
expires in faith—unless the discussion concerns an election by which Pharaoh,
Judas, and all the reprobate are elect, which is absurd.

[1668 KBNL/UvA witness | logical printed p. 264 | PDF p. 315]

5. For “those whom God predestined, he glorified,” Romans 8:30. This text cannot
be understood of peremptory election, whose object is a person dead in faith,
unless the sense is: “Those whom God predestined as dead in faith, he
efficaciously called and justified.” What could be more absurd? And if the
discussion concerns non-peremptory election, it is false, because not all such
persons are glorified, as they themselves teach.

6. For no wise person elects human beings to an end through means which he
knows will be ineffectual, and to an end which is nonexistent.

It is asked whether Christ is the meritorious cause of election; that is,
whether God loves no one unto eternal life and can elect no one to eternal life
unless Christ's merit is the cause of that election. The Arminians affirm it.
We deny it.

1. In 1 Thessalonians 5:9, “God appointed us to obtain salvation through Jesus
Christ.” Therefore, our appointment to salvation as an end precedes the merits
of Christ through which we obtain salvation. For if a physician appoints to
heal a sick person by cutting a vein, then the cutting of the vein did not
precede that appointment. In Ephesians 1:5, “He predestined us to adoption as
sons through Jesus Christ.” Therefore, Christ is the means of adoption, and
consequently cannot be the cause of predestination to adoption.

2. If Christ is the meritorious cause of election, he is either the cause
simply and insofar as he died, or insofar as he is

[1668 KBNL/UvA witness | logical printed p. 265 | PDF p. 316]

apprehended by faith. If the former, then because Christ died for each and
every person, as the Arminians, Jesuits, and Dominicans teach, all are elect to
glory. For when the cause is posited here, it appears that the effect must
necessarily be posited, unless they say that, notwithstanding the meritorious
cause, God elects some and does not elect others. And in that way, certainly,
he elects freely and without being moved by the meritorious cause. If Christ is
the meritorious cause of election insofar as he is apprehended by faith, as the
Arminians teach, then faith will be the cause moving God's will to elect. But
faith is the effect of election, for we are predestined to obtain salvation
through faith, 2 Thessalonians 2:13, and through the sprinkling of blood,
1 Peter 1:2.

3. For God elects us before we do anything good or evil. Therefore, it is not
because divine justice was satisfied in Christ the surety—for God elects us
rather than others without being moved by any external cause—but because he
has mercy upon whom he wills. Therefore, Christ's merits do not determine
God's will.

4. For predestination, with respect to the act of the one predestining, is the
eternal will of God. But all the scholastics teach that no cause of the divine
will is given outside God himself, excepting Alvarez, Vásquez, and some more
recent authors, who obscure the matter with crude distinctions.

5. For God decreed from eternity that Christ's merits should be the causes of
our salvation. But it is

[1668 KBNL/UvA witness | logical printed p. 266 | PDF p. 317]

contradictory for Christ's merits to be the causes of our ordination and
election to salvation. For in this way Christ's merits would both be causes
antecedent to election and effects consequent upon election. This itself is
our election: namely, God's decree by which he appointed us to glory as an end
and to participation in Christ's merits as a means conducive to this end.

It is asked whether God loves and regards no one with love unless that person
is in Christ, at least with respect to eternal life. The Arminians affirm it.
We deny it.

1. For God loves the elect before he gives or sends Christ to them, because if
he had not loved the elect unto life, he would not have given his Son to death
for them. John 3:16: “God so loved the world that he delivered his only-
begotten Son,” and so forth.

2. To be in Christ through faith is a temporal act. Therefore, it is not the
cause of God's eternal love.

3. For there is a twofold love in God. One is εὐδοκίας, or the love of
benevolence. No more can a cause of this love be given than a cause of God
himself, for this love is God himself loving human beings unto life from
eternity. With this love he loves us while we are still enemies, dead in sins,
and perishing in our blood, Romans 5:10; Ephesians 2:2–3; Ezekiel 16:7–9. Our
temporal acts cannot

[1668 KBNL/UvA witness | logical printed p. 267 | PDF p. 318]

transform God from one who hates us into a God who loves us and bears a new
affection and love toward us. The other is the love of complacency. This is an
act in God which is, as it
were, temporal and transient, and it arises from our good acts; just as its
opposite—namely, the act of displeasure and abomination—arises from our evil
acts. John 14:21: “But the one who loves me will be loved by my Father.” Verse
13: “If anyone loves me, he will keep my word, and my Father will love him.” But
if the discussion concerns love properly so called, no one can love God unless
he has first been loved by God, as in 1 John 4:19—namely, by the free love of
benevolence and beneficence. After the benefits of grace have been conferred
in Christ, the love of complacency immediately follows this love, once the
requisite conditions are posited; it is necessary in God as Judge of the world,
judging human beings according to his Word.

4. Neither Christ's merits nor our good or evil acts can transform God from one
who loves into a God who hates, or from one who hates into one who loves: “with
whom there is no shadow of change,” James 1:17. Christ's merits do indeed
reconcile us to God and bring it about that God, consistently with his
truthfulness and justice, can bestow the remission of sins, eternal life, and
Christ's righteousness—things which, from the most free love of his eternal
election, he freely decreed to give.

In favor of election from foreseen faith, the adversaries object in vain that
“without faith it is impossible to please God.” For by equal reasoning they can
say that no one can so

[1668 KBNL/UvA witness | logical printed p. 268 | PDF p. 319]

please God that he is elected to glory unless he is actually born, hears the
Word, and is converted to Christ. For in Hebrews 11:6 the apostle speaks of
such complacency. But by another complacency of good pleasure, God expressly
embraces many who are neither converted nor believing. Galatians 2:20: “who
loved me and gave himself for me.” Galatians 1:15: ὅτε δὲ εὐδόκησεν ὁ Θεός,
“when it pleased God—to reveal his Son to me.” Thus in Titus 3:4 God's
χρηστότης καὶ φιλανθρωπία, “kindness and love for humanity,” appears to human
beings before they believe and while they serve various desires.

It is asked whether, because God has absolutely destined someone to eternal
glory, Christ therefore cannot merit grace, favor, and eternal glory. For it is
not necessary that glory and a right to glory be obtained by Christ's merits
for the person whom God, apart from all Christ's merits, absolutely destined to
eternal glory. So the Arminians, article 1, pages 217–218. We, however, teach
that Christ's merits are entirely consistent with absolute election to glory.

1. For God so destines someone to glory that he also destines him to
participation in Christ's merits through faith.

2. For it is not contradictory that someone be absolutely elected to an
inheritance and absolutely elected to the grace by which he is rendered fit
for actual possession of the inheritance.

3. For in 2 Thessalonians 5:9, “God appointed them to

[1668 KBNL/UvA witness | logical printed p. 269 | PDF p. 320]

obtain salvation through our Lord Jesus Christ.”

4. For God so destines human beings to glory apart from merits that no one
will actually possess glory unless Christ's merits are applied to him.

5. For they argue in vain: “The one through whom, as Mediator, the Father
elects no one is excluded from peremptory election to salvation. But according
to us,” they say, “the Father elects no one to salvation through Christ as
Mediator. Therefore,” and so forth. We respond that the major is false, as is
the minor: the one through whom, as the Author of salvation, though not as the
meritorious cause of predestination to salvation, the Father elects no one is
excluded from election to salvation. For a king destines a ragged beggar to
share the inheritance of his kingdom and to be co-adopted as a son; it does not
follow that he is a ragged beggar when he is actually crowned. A commander
assigns an unarmed man to battle; it does not follow that the man is unarmed
when he goes into battle. Because Christ is not the meritorious cause of the
decree of election to glory, it does not follow that the decree of election
excludes him as the means of the decree's execution and as the Author of
salvation.

It is asked whether a conditional promise of some good can be subordinated to
God's immutable decree by which that good has already been absolutely destined
for the one to whom the promise is made. The Remonstrants deny it. We affirm it.

1. For God absolutely destined the throne of the kingdom in Christ

[1668 KBNL/UvA witness | logical printed p. 270 | PDF p. 321]

to David, and indeed by an oath, Psalm 88:35–37. Yet a conditional promise was
made to David's posterity if they walked in God's commandments, 1 Kings 3:14;
Psalm 132:11–12. God absolutely decreed to save his Son from Herod, and yet his
safety was conditional: if the parents fled into Egypt together with the
infant.

2. Means and end do not conflict. Peter's being absolutely elected to glory
and God's absolutely and irresistibly working faith in Peter do not conflict.
Therefore, neither do a conditional promise and an absolute decree.

3. For the Arminians' conditional promise and an absolute decree do conflict.
Their conditions are drawn from the books of jurists, not from Holy Scripture:
namely, conditions which remain indifferent and absolutely disposed to being
or not being when every decree of God has been posited; conditions released
from every dominion of God's providence and dependent upon that absolute
goddess, Contingency. We do not acknowledge conditional promises of this kind,
but only such promises as God unfailingly works in his elect through his grace.
These conflict no more with God's absolute decree than the volition of an end
and the volition of the means to the end conflict in God.

It is asked whether faith can be at once a condition required by way of duty
and free obedience on the part of the elect, and at the same time a thing
promised by God and

[1668 KBNL/UvA witness | logical printed p. 271 | PDF p. 322]

unfailingly effected in us by God. The Remonstrants deny it. We affirm it.

Indeed, each side concedes that it is a condition and a free duty. But that it
is also a promised thing and is unfailingly effected by God—which the
Remonstrants deny—is proved as follows.

1. For a circumcised heart is promised, Deuteronomy 30:6; a new heart, Ezekiel
36:26; that all will be taught by God, Jeremiah 31:33–34; John 6:44–45; that
all flesh will see the salvation of the Lord, Isaiah 40:5; and that the Spirit
of grace and supplication will be poured out upon David's household, Zechariah
12:10–11. Therefore, faith also is promised.

2. For the faithful pray for faith. Prayer, however, rests upon the word of
promise.

3. For saving knowledge of God in Christ is promised, Isaiah 11:9; Jeremiah
31:34, and the knowledge of Christ by which human beings are justified, Isaiah
53:11. This is nothing other than faith.

4. For the Spirit is promised, through whom they will see the Lord whom they
pierced, Zechariah 12:10–11. Therefore, faith is promised.

5. For faith is the gift of God which excludes all human boasting, Ephesians
2:8, 4; Philippians 1:29.

6. For the saints give thanks to God for their own faith and that of others,
Ephesians 1:15–16.

It is asked whether we therefore

[1668 KBNL/UvA witness | logical printed p. 272 | PDF p. 323]

invert the natural order because we maintain that the object of predestination
is a human being who is
to be created and is not yet created, since God thus ordained a nonexistent
thing to glory. The Remonstrants affirm it in *Apology*, chapter 5, folio 67.
We deny it.

1. For God made all things for himself. Therefore, he decreed to give being to
a human being so that he himself might be glorified.

2. For it is asked: to what end did God decree from eternity to create the
world? For the world from eternity was nonexistent. Certainly, so that he
himself might be glorified. Therefore, God decreed nonentities to his glory.

It is asked whether concern, fear, and caution in the use of the means conflict
with absolute election to glory. The Remonstrants affirm it, article 1, page
265; Corvinus, *Against Molina*, chapter 24, section 30. We deny it.

1. For those who are absolutely destined to glory are absolutely destined to
blameless holiness, Ephesians 1:4. But a chief part of holiness is godly
concern and caution lest we offend God, Proverbs 28:14; Philippians 2:12.

2. For godly concern is a thing absolutely promised by God in the new covenant,
Jeremiah 32:40, and consequently absolutely decreed.

3. For this opinion supposes that human beings know they are absolutely elect
to glory before they obey God with godly fear and religious concern and avoid
sins. This is false. For

[1668 KBNL/UvA witness | logical printed p. 273 | PDF p. 324]

such knowledge would be sheer presumption. But no one who does not walk in fear
and godly concern knows that he is absolutely elect to glory. For how can
anyone who is not concerned know that he is absolutely elect to concern?

4. Christ knew that he was absolutely destined not to die except at such an
hour, and yet he piously fled and avoided his enemies, John 11:53–54; Mark
1:54; John 10:39; Luke 4:29–30. And this is the Holy Spirit's inference:
“Christ's hour had not yet come; therefore, no one seized him.” Unless the hour
at which he had to die had been absolutely appointed, this consequence would
not be valid. For if the hour at which he had to die was conditionally
appointed, upon what condition was it appointed? Namely, if the Jews seized
him at that hour. No other condition can be devised. Thus the Holy Spirit would
argue: “Christ died at that hour; therefore, he died at that hour,” because on
their account the condition and the conditioned thing are the same here.

5. Godly fear conflicts as much with peremptory election as with absolute
election, because it is just as contradictory for one peremptorily elected not
to be glorified as for one absolutely elected to glory not to be glorified.

6. And with middle knowledge posited, there is no reason for the elect to ask
God with trembling for the grace to obey, because their finally obeying or not
finally obeying exists before and apart from every free will of God.

[1668 KBNL/UvA witness | logical printed p. 274 | PDF p. 325]

It is asked whether the number of the elect is determined by God's choice and
power, so that there are this many, neither more nor fewer. By necessary
consequence the Arminians deny it. We believe that both the numbered number and
the numbering number are certain.

1. For the one who yesterday was reprobate cannot be substituted in the place
of the one who yesterday was elect. John 10:14: “I am that good shepherd, and I
know my sheep and am known by them.” 2 Timothy 2:19: “The firm foundation of
God stands; God knows who are his.” Romans 9: “So that God's purpose κατ’
ἐκλογὴν, ‘according to election,’ might remain.” John 6:3, 39: “Whatever the
Father gives me comes to me, and the one who comes to me I will not cast out,
but I will raise him up on the last day.” Romans 11:28–29: The Jews are beloved
according to election; “ἀμεταμέλητα τὰ χαρίσματα, καὶ ἡ κλῆσις τοῦ θεοῦ,” “the
gifts and calling of God are irrevocable.” Prosper, *To the Chapters of the
Gauls*: “From these stones nothing is cast out, nothing diminished, nothing
snatched away.”

2. For those whom God appointed from eternity to be saved, he gives faith and
perseverance so that they are infallibly saved. For “all those whom he
predestined, being called and justified, he glorified,” Romans 8:30.

3. For Jehovah does not change; therefore, the sons of Jacob do not perish,
Malachi 3:6.

4. For ὅσοι, “as many as”—neither more nor fewer than those who were ordained
to eternal life—believed, Acts 13:48.

5. For it is impossible for the elect to be seduced, Matthew 24:24.

[1668 KBNL/UvA witness | logical printed p. 275 | PDF p. 326]

6. For the persons who are efficaciously called are designated, and those
inscribed in the book of life are certain and determinate human beings in
God's decree, Isaiah 49:12; 60:8–9; Hosea 1:10; Zechariah 8:22; 12:10, 12.

But according to the Arminians, the numbered number is not within God's choice.

1. For since God does not unfailingly produce the free acts of conversion, he
cannot convert and save more persons than he converts and saves. For he would
overturn freedom unless he left human beings to absolute indifference in
acting.

2. For the final conversion of all to God and final resistance to God calling
precede God's foreknowledge and free decree. Therefore, according to them, we
pray in vain that he would save more than are in fact saved.

3. For notwithstanding the action of the most special grace and the dominion
of most invincible providence, a reprobate can convert himself and thus pass
into the number of the elect, and an elect person can finally fall away and
thus pass into the number of the reprobate. Therefore, the number is not
determined within God's choice.

4. For the Arminians deny that God knows future contingents. Therefore, today
he does not know how many are to be saved and how many damned, because the
conversion or non-conversion of human beings not yet born and not yet called
through the preaching of the gospel is a future contingent. But that future

[1668 KBNL/UvA witness | logical printed p. 276 | PDF p. 327 | stop before CAPUT QUARTUM]

contingents are not knowable even to God is expressly taught by the
Remonstrants in *Apology*, chapter 2, folio 43.

MATTHEW 13:20.

But the one who receives the seed in stony places is the one who hears the
Word and immediately receives it with joy. Verse 21: Yet he has no root in
himself, but is temporary, etc.

Hence it is asked whether *pistis proskairos*, temporary faith, is essentially
saving faith, differing from the saving faith of those who receive the Word
in

[1668 KBNL/UvA witness | logical printed p. 564 | PDF p. 615 | split word joined: bonâ]

good soil not in species and nature but only in the accident of duration,
just as seed growing in stony soil and in good soil is produce of the same
species. The Remonstrants teach this in the *Synodical Writings*, pages
129–130, because Luke 8 says of them: 1. They believe for a time. 2. They
receive the discourse with joy, which is the mark of a teachable and upright
mind. We deny that this is truly saving faith; indeed, we contend that it
differs from saving faith in nature and species.

1. For the specific difference of faith is taken from faith's formal object.
But the object of temporary faith is not Christ as Christ and the promises of
the Gospel as such, because that object endures during persecution, whereas
the object here fails when persecution arises. Rather, it is something of the
Gospel and something of Christ: namely, outward prosperity, or the Gospel
insofar as it is clothed with outward prosperity. Therefore, when the Gospel's
prosperous success ceases, the faith fails, just as the effect is removed when
the cause is removed.

Objection. But outward prosperity has no aspect of truth. It is not,
therefore, the formal object, because it does not pertain to the intellect's
object.

Response. Outward prosperity is the motive, not the formal aspect, and they do
not believe the Gospel as a supernatural good. Rather, the object of temporary
faith is the truth of the Gospel promises insofar as they are pleasant, or as
goods in the genus of the pleasant and useful good, having promises of the
present and future life—not as an

[1668 KBNL/UvA witness | logical printed p. 565 | PDF p. 616 | split word joined: honesti]

honorable good or in the genus of honorable
goods. Thus the object of temporary faith is like a physical good that
naturally moves and thoroughly moves the appetite, while the object of saving
faith is a moral and morally honorable good.

2. For in the definition of an accident or adjunct through an addition, the
subject—or rather the *schesis*, relation and disposition between the subject
and the accident—is included in the definition: as a human being in the
definition of risibility, a nose in the definition of snubness, and a horse in
the definition of neighing. The relation of a human being to risibility
essentially distinguishes risibility from all other qualities of that genus.
Since, therefore, the subject of saving faith is an honest and good heart,
*kardia kalē kai agathē*, Luke 8:15, while the subject of temporary faith is a
stony, hard, rocky, and petrified heart, which is proper to an unregenerate
person, Ezekiel 36:26 and 11:19, and to a person *rhizan ouk echontos*, having
no root—that is, not rooted and established in Christ through true faith,
Colossians 2:7, *en Christō ouk errizōmenoi*, not engrafted or planted in
Christ as in moist soil—this temporary faith differs in species from the
faith in those who are rooted in Christ and have an honest and good heart.
And just as an honest and good person rooted in Christ differs in theological
species and essence, according to moral being, so to speak, from a dishonest
and wicked person who is not rooted in Christ and lacks the vital sap of

[1668 KBNL/UvA witness | logical printed p. 566 | PDF p. 617 | split word joined: salvificæ]

saving grace, just as stony and rocky ground lacks all sap, so the faith of
the one differs in species from the faith of the other, and not only in
duration or in a firmer or less firm rootedness in the subject, as less white
and more white differ.

3. Temporary believers fall away for this reason alone, Matthew 13:6: *Dia to
mē echein rhizan, exēranthē*—because they have no root, they therefore wither.
And Mark 4:7: *Dia to mē echein ikmada*—because they had no moisture; that is,
because they lacked the Spirit of sanctifying grace, who alone makes people
who are dry by nature fruitful. This is a sign that dry and stony people with
rocky hearts cannot fail to wither and fall away when the sun of persecution
rises. But it is neither true that people possessing true faith necessarily
fall away in time of persecution, nor do the Arminians dare to assert it.

4. Saving faith necessarily produces good fruits from a vital and
supernatural principle, as do all true believers, Psalm 1:5; Matthew 7:7;
Jeremiah 2:17, 20; John 15:5: “The one who abides in me, and in whom I abide,
bears much fruit.” Temporary faith, therefore, which produces no fruit—as is
the case with every temporary believer, whose faith is seed that never grows
to an ear—is dead faith. It is no more truly and essentially faith than the
faith of demons, James 2:19, or than a lifeless corpse is living. Nor can it be
answered that a temporary believer is alive, for this begs the question.

5. The purpose of this parable, because

[1668 KBNL/UvA witness | logical printed p. 567 | PDF p. 618 | split word joined: multi]

many often flocked to hear Christ
himself but afterward recoiled and fell away from him, is to show who are good
and honest hearers and who are temporary and by no means sincere; and who
possess only a faith glorious before the world, not true and saving faith.

6. If temporary faith is true faith because it is called faith in the text,
then those to whom Christ was unwilling to entrust himself have true faith,
John 2:24. Or if it is true and saving faith because temporary believers grow
for a time, then, by the same reasoning, those who serve riches and pleasures
and therefore cannot serve Christ, the other Lord—those in whom the seed of
the Word is choked by the cares of riches and pleasures—would possess true and
saving faith. For Luke 8:7 says, *Kai symphyeisai hai akanthai apepnixan auto*:
“And the thorns, springing up together with the seed, choked the seed.” Yet
those enslaved to Mammon cannot believe savingly, as even our opponents admit.
But if they are said to believe savingly because: 1. They assent to the Gospel;
2. They hear the Word with joy; and 3. They profess faith—then Herod, who heard
John “gladly and with pleasure,” Mark 6:20, and Simon Magus, who “believed,”
Acts 8:13, would possess saving faith. For assent, joy, and profession were not
lacking in them. Yet the essential requirement was lacking: namely, a good and
honest heart and vital sap. Finally, what our opponents say is trifling:

[1668 KBNL/UvA witness | logical printed p. 568 | PDF p. 619]

that
it is not denied that they possess a root, but only that they do not possess a
root in themselves. For a root must be in the very soil that possesses the
root; otherwise temporary believers possess from themselves the principle
from which their empty and deceptive faith flows, namely, a stony heart.

Nor does “grow” in this parable mean anything other than to spring up before
the world like seed with a deceptive hope of harvest and to promise an empty
hope of harvest where there is no true root. Yet when the word “grow” is
ascribed to seed sown in good soil, it means to flourish from a true root. It
is not absurd for the same word to have different senses when applied to
different subjects.

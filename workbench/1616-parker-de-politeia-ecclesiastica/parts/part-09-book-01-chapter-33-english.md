discipline is no more guilty than doctrine itself.

Second, the abolishing element in this conspiracy does not touch us at all.

5. The fourth stain with which the adversaries bespatter reformed discipline

custom called such men acephalous, that is, without a head.’”

First, the very insertion of the name “clergy” teaches that this ancient
custom, about which the Council of Paris speaks, reaches no further back than

Let us be permitted to be *akephaloi* in this way, for nothing is more

recognize in the reformers Esau’s voice under Jacob’s clothing.” Finally, John
Whitgift says: “The arguments of the Admonishers and Cartwright are Papistic.”

Is what is said about Papistic principles a calumny? Is Satan’s kingdom in the
Papacy so divided that it would furnish principles to us, who attempt nothing
other than its demolition? The fact that it sometimes supplies arguments
against itself reveals the inherent strength of the truth which we defend,
rather than some affinity with the Papists. Why should the ark not be
reclaimed from the Philistines? Why should Paul not agree with the Pharisees in
the article concerning the resurrection? Why, finally, should a sheep not, as
Augustine says, demand back its own skin from the wolf?

from Medina against Bellarmine. He gives the reason for his action in this
way: “As Hannibal in Ennius, cited by Cicero, exhorts his men and declares:
‘Whoever strikes down the enemy will be a Carthaginian to me, whoever he may
be’; so whoever defends truth and refutes error will be to me one of the
Protestants.”

“Trojan or Tyrian will be treated by me with no distinction.”

This is altogether right. As far as truth is concerned, there is no distinction
between Papists and Protestants. There has never been a heresy which did not
retain certain true principles; yet those principles are not of the heresy but
of the truth. Thus when we agree with the Papists in certain true principles,
we nevertheless do not borrow the principles from the Papists, because they
are not Papistic.

### QUESTION XVI

#### Do Daniel’s seventy weeks amply overcome the obstinacy of the Jews and demonstrate with the utmost clarity that the Messiah came into the world long ago?

As long as the veil of error is wrapped over the Jews’ eyes by God’s just
right hand, no one will have persuaded them of anything, even if he does
persuade them. Yet, besides other passages of Scripture, such a clear light
shines from Daniel 9 that it is capable of convicting those who most
obstinately deny the Messiah’s advent long since past and of rendering them
αὐτοκατακρίτους, “self-condemned.” We establish this with the following
arguments.

First. The one whom God’s Spirit foretold would come into the world and be
killed within seventy weeks of years after the decree to restore Jerusalem
came into the world long ago and was killed. But God’s Spirit foretold that
the Messiah would come into the world and be killed within seventy weeks of
years after the decree to restore Jerusalem. Therefore, the Messiah came into
the world long ago and was killed. The major premise rests on a maxim that is
most certain, most evident, and scarcely disputed between us and the Jews: the
truth of the divine Spirit and word, and the necessary and infallible
certainty of all the predictions of the Old Testament. The minor premise is
equally plain from Daniel’s explicit words in 9:25 and 26.

> Marginalia, printed p. 182: *Argument 1 for the Messiah’s advent long since
> past*.

This argument torments the Jews most wretchedly. To find some semblance of a
reply to offer the people, they twist themselves into various shapes, but
entirely in vain. The more they struggle, the more tightly this knot’s bond
constrains them. Their principal effort used to be directed toward evading the
minor premise, but today their most common reply is directed toward the major:
they try to avoid it with the following distinction. They say that some
predictions are absolute and others conditional. Daniel’s prediction of the
Messiah’s advent was conditional, provided the people did not delay his advent
by their sins; it was the people’s sins, they say, that prevented the Messiah
from coming at the time foretold by Daniel.

> Marginalia, printed p. 182: *Many evasions of the Jews are refuted*.

I reply: this distinction has no application here. The Messiah’s advent at the
foretold time was promised in the most absolute terms, and neither in Daniel
nor elsewhere is there the slightest indication of any condition whatsoever
in this promise.

Moreover, the Messiah’s advent flowed from pure divine grace and mercy, and
its principal purpose was the abolition of the people’s sins. Thus nothing on
the human side could delay an advent that depended absolutely on God’s sole
good pleasure and sheer grace—least of all sin, since that advent of the
Messiah had been ordained by God for the express purpose of removing and
destroying sin.

> Marginalia, printed p. 182: *The promise of the Messiah’s advent was
> absolute, not conditional*.

Finally, what sin of the people could have prevented the Messiah’s advent at
the appointed time? It must certainly have been far greater than all the sins
they committed before Daniel’s day. For despite the sins that brought about
the destruction of the first temple and the city, the temple and the city
were restored and the Messiah was promised. Yet if one excepts the guilt of
the Messiah’s crucifixion, which they deny was a sin, we read of no other
particular offense committed by them that surpassed their former crimes.
Jeremiah and the other Prophets bear witness that idolatry, adultery, incest,
murder, and every kind of most grievous iniquity were extremely common among
that people under the first temple. Although these did not prevent the
promise of the Messiah’s advent from being made, after the time appointed for
his advent—and especially after

[Printed p. 183 | IA leaf 213 | access PDF 213 | OCR object 212]

Jerusalem’s final desolation—the gravity of that people’s sins has visibly
diminished. For many centuries, even before the later temple was destroyed,
they were entirely free from idolatry, formerly their principal provocation.
Captivity has long restrained them from slaughter more than it has any other
nation. Apart from their obstinate rebellion against the gospel, which they
do not acknowledge, no one charges them in their other crimes with anything
beyond the crimes of their ancestors in ancient centuries or of the other
nations today. Their assertion that the Messiah’s advent was delayed by the
people’s sins is therefore utterly vain. The divine goodness sometimes
accelerates its benefits to sinners; it never delays them when a definite
time has been prescribed for a particular benefit.

Driven from this position, they resort to another and final ἀποφύγιον,
“refuge.” Since their hope concerning the Messiah has disappointed them so
often, they have now forbidden anyone, almost by a public edict, to inquire
further into the time of the Messiah’s advent. They now say that nothing about
it can be known and profess themselves entirely ignorant of when he will come
or why he has delayed his advent so long.

> Marginalia, printed p. 183: *Ignorance of revealed things is not modesty but
> obstinacy and stupefaction*.

Reply. Ignorance of divine mysteries not yet revealed is sometimes
commendable and always excusable. But in matters already revealed, especially
when we are pressed by a clear argument and urged by Scripture’s own words, a
profession of silence and ignorance is either an open surrender or manifest
obstinacy clinging to error against an unanswerable argument.

Three replies are made to the minor premise. First, that Daniel was not a
Prophet and consequently his predictions were neither divine nor true. Thus,
according to Galatinus, Porphyrion the Sadducee snarled. I reply: it is an old
cacoëthes, “wicked habit,” of pestilent men irrefutably convicted by the
Scriptures to vomit their blasphemies upon the Scriptures themselves. The
Roman Catholics indeed stitch the apocryphal additions about Susanna, Bel,
and the Dragon onto Daniel; in this respect they put a stumbling block before
unbelievers, leading them to reject all Daniel’s writings. Nevertheless, Jews
no less than Christians have hitherto defended the αὐθεντία, “authenticity,”
of Daniel’s true writings. God himself certainly testifies to Daniel’s divine
wisdom and truth in Ezekiel 14:20, “If these three men, Noah, Daniel, and Job,
were in its midst,” and 28:3, “Behold, you are wiser than Daniel.” Since God
attributes righteousness and wisdom to him among the most righteous and
wisest mortals, what shamelessness it is to try to accuse him of falsehood and
deceit.

> Marginalia, printed p. 183: *They wrongly deny Daniel canonical authority*.

The second and third replies come from Rabbi Solomon Jarchi in his commentary
on Daniel 9. He says there are several Messiahs but only one son of David
κατ᾽ ἐξοχήν, “preeminently”; that Daniel does not speak about him, but
in verse 25 about Cyrus, in verse 26 about Herod Agrippa, and in verse 24
about the ark and other instruments of the Jerusalem temple.

> Marginalia, printed p. 183: *The errors of Solomon Jarchi*.

Answer. See briefly how foul these claims are. First, when Scripture speaks
three times in one and the same passage about the Messiah without any
indication of a distinction, what boldness it is to forge three Messiahs in
place of one and actually exclude from all three references to the Messiah the
true Messiah, concerning whom alone that Scripture intends to speak for the
consolation of Daniel and the afflicted people. The abolition of sin, the
introduction of everlasting righteousness, the annulment of the ceremonial
law, and the other things ascribed there to the Messiah certainly fit no one
other than the son of David, the Redeemer.

> Marginalia, printed p. 183: *It speaks about the principal Messiah*.

As for the three other Messiahs substituted by Jarchi, Daniel says nothing
here about any of them. Cyrus is indeed called God’s shepherd and God’s
Messiah in Isaiah 44:28 and 45:1 because of the singular benefit to God’s
people for whose accomplishment he had been appointed by God’s extraordinary
providence. But no one, not even Jarchi himself, called Cyrus the principal
Messiah and Israel’s redeemer from sin and the devil; yet Daniel speaks here
about that one alone. It is clear from the following that Daniel does not mean
Cyrus. The Prophet speaks of the Messiah who would come after the seven and
the sixty-two weeks, whereas Cyrus died long before the first of the weeks.
Let the Jews begin the weeks from the city’s first ruin by Nebuchadnezzar, or
even from Jeremiah’s report made in Nebuchadnezzar’s first year—both
calculations are false, as we saw above. Nevertheless, even from this
beginning the seven and sixty-two weeks are badly calculated to Cyrus’s reign.
Indeed, even the seven weeks alone, which make only forty-nine years, are
badly calculated, for the interval from Jerusalem’s first destruction to
Cyrus’s reign is much too confined. This is why Rabbi Abraham Aben Ezra and
most of the other Rabbis reject Rabbi Solomon Jarchi’s interpretation
concerning Cyrus.

> Marginalia, printed p. 183: *Cyrus was not the spiritual Christ*.

As for his second Messiah, Agrippa, whom he claims the Romans killed before
the city fell, nothing in this tale is sound. First, why would Daniel produce
another Messiah for us in the same passage, one who died more than five
hundred years after the first? Second, who besides Jarchi ever called Agrippa
the Messiah? Herod the Great, and also his grandson Herod Agrippa, acquired
the title “Messiah” from certain Herodian flatterers because of the Jewish
kingdom each possessed and the great splendor of their power. But

> Marginalia, printed p. 183: *Nor Agrippa*.

[Printed p. 184 | IA leaf 214 | access PDF 214 | OCR object 213]

this last Agrippa, the son of Herod Agrippa, although he reigned in
Trachonitis, never held any power either in Jerusalem or in Judaea. So far was
he from being regarded as any sort of Messiah by any Jew. Third, their claim
that the Romans killed him before the city was captured is a rabbinic and
shameless invention. He lived on the friendliest terms with the Romans until
the end of his life and assisted the Vespasians to the best of his ability,
both the father Flavius and the son Titus, in subjugating the Jews. After the
triumph of the emperors Flavius and Titus over subdued Judaea, Agrippa
certainly received an enlargement of his kingdom from Flavius, and his sister
Berenice lived in the Roman palace on the most intimate terms with Titus,
almost in the place of a lawful wife.

Jarchi’s third Messiah, the ark and the altar, are far removed from Daniel’s
Messiah mentioned in verse 24. For the Messiah anointed there is a person,
whereas the altar and ark were inanimate vessels of the sanctuary. Second,
the thing anointed there is the Holy of Holies. Those vessels were indeed
sacred and holy, but the superlative title “Holy of Holies” does not suit them.
Third, the ark was sprinkled annually with the blood of propitiation, but I do
not recall any passage where either the ark or any other vessel within the
veil was anointed with holy oil, much less where the whole sanctuary itself
was ever drenched with oil. The sanctuaries of both the tabernacle and the
first temple were filled with a cloud but drenched with no anointing. We never
read that the sanctuary of the second temple was adorned either by the smoke
of a cloud or by any other extraordinary sign of God’s presence, apart from
Christ’s and the Apostles’ interaction with the people in the outer courts.
Fourth, three of those five things the Rabbis say were absent from the second
temple were the ark, holy oil, and the Holy Spirit. The remaining two, Urim
and Thummim and the sacred fire, do not concern us here. We observe only that
the anointing of the Holy of Holies cannot mean the ark being drenched either
in oil or in the Spirit’s presence, since on the Rabbis’ own testimony these
did not exist in that age. Thus the anointing mentioned by Daniel in verse 24
is left to Jesus of Nazareth alone, since he was not merely the holiest of all
creatures but the very Holiness of Israel, a man filled with the Holy Spirit
beyond all measure.

> Marginalia, printed p. 184: *Neither the ark nor the altar*.

Now that the futility of these three replies by Solomon Jarchi to our minor
premise has been sufficiently exposed, three further replies current among
the Jews remain, no less futile than the preceding ones. (1) They wish
Daniel’s weeks to be understood not as years but as jubilees, so that the
seventy weeks contain not merely 490 years but seventy times fifty years,
that is, 3,500. (2) There are two Messiahs: one humble and subjected to many
evils, the other glorious and suffering nothing. (3) A distinction must be
made between the time of the Messiah’s birth and the time of his appearance.
According to Daniel’s prophecy he was indeed born on the very day the second
temple was burned, but he still lies hidden in paradise at the gate of Rome
until, moved by the people’s prayers, he appears to save Israel. Let us also
hew down these mournful ditties. Reply to the first:

1. All the ancient Jews and Talmudists understood Daniel’s weeks as years.
The jubilees are an invention of more recent authors. After they saw every
date for the Messiah’s advent set by any ancient authority expire, they
devised a date so distant that it could not be convicted of falsehood for many
centuries, lest the wretched people, deceived so often, abandon all further
expectation of the Messiah.

Second, no weeks of jubilees are mentioned anywhere in the whole of Scripture
or by any approved author. Every week is either one of days or of years, and
no one would say that Daniel’s weeks were weeks of days, for then they would
not have completed the span of even two years. It remains, therefore, that
they must be understood as years.

Third, there is a common Talmudist tradition said to derive from the house of
Elijah and his prophetic disciples: the world will last six thousand years
and then be burned. They allot two thousand of these to the void, two thousand
to the law, and two thousand to the days of the Messiah. Yet contrary to the
common Jewish belief, the opinion before us assigns more than five thousand
years to the law alone.

Fourth, this reckoning is entirely contrary to the purpose of the Holy Spirit
in Daniel. Through Gabriel’s ministry God wishes to comfort Daniel and the
others, who were overcome by great grief, with the near advent of the Messiah
and Redeemer after seventy weeks. Nothing could be more alien to this than
comfort that would arrive only after 3,500 years.

Finally, these trifles expressly contradict the Prophet’s words. Daniel places
the Messiah’s advent before the ruin of the city and the second temple. Indeed,
he places that calamity after the Messiah’s death as its consequence and as
the deserved punishment for that incomparably great crime. But our storytellers
postpone the Messiah’s advent until three thousand years after the city’s
destruction.

> Marginalia, printed p. 184: *Daniel does not speak about years of jubilees*.

As for the second reply, concerning a twofold Messiah—one humble and one
glorious, one suffering and one

[Printed p. 185 | IA leaf 215 | access PDF 215 | OCR object 214]

triumphant—it contradicts all of Scripture. Scripture mentions only one, and
one of such a kind that he would first suffer humbly and afterward triumph
gloriously, ascending by the ladder of the cross into the triumphal chariot of
glory. Second, Daniel speaks about a most glorious Messiah who by his death
would introduce everlasting righteousness, abolish sin, seal up vision,
remove the ceremonies, and destroy rebellious Jerusalem by Roman arms. In the
same way the Prophet Isaiah, in chapter 53, had long before described in
gospel terms the entire suffering of the most glorious Messiah who justifies
the church and appeases God’s wrath against the sins of the elect; and long
before Isaiah, David had done so in Psalm 22.

> Marginalia, printed p. 185: *They wrongly distinguish the glorious Messiah
> from the humble one*.

The third reply requires no refutation other than a bare and simple account.
This will abundantly expose both its most foul falsehood and how readily
unbelievers open their hearts to firm belief in the most foolishly invented
little tales, though they have closed their eyes lest they be compelled to see
the clearest rays of divine wisdom in the gospel of Jesus Christ. Petrus
Galatinus explains this opinion as follows from the books *Sanhedrin* and
*Bereshit Rabbah*, the commentaries of Solomon Jarchi, and other works.

On the day the Romans—or rather the Jews—burned the temple, Rabbi Elias heard
a voice from heaven cry out that the sanctuary was falling. While the Rabbi
expected the world to end immediately and hastened into the streets to
proclaim this to the people, a voice from heaven again told him that Israel’s
Savior had been born that day. When Elias asked where, the voice replied,
“Bethlehem of Judah.” When Rabbi Elias went there, he found a woman outside
her door with a newborn infant lying neglected at her feet. The Rabbi asked
why the infant was so neglected. The mother replied that she considered the
child unhappy and ill-omened because he had been born on the day the temple
burned. Elias then told the woman, “Take heart and care for the infant, for he
is the Savior of Israel.” When the Rabbi returned to Bethlehem after five
years, he asked the mother how the child was doing. She replied, “Most
wretchedly. Although I have tended him as best I could, to this day he lies
mute, deaf, blind, and deprived of the use of his feet and all his limbs.” As
they spoke, a mighty whirlwind arose, seized the boy, and carried him into the
great sea. Elias immediately dissolved in tears at the removal of Israel’s
hope, but a voice from heaven comforted him when he fell silent. It predicted
that this abducted boy, the truest Messiah, would remain four hundred years in
the great sea, eighty years in the smoke of the flame of Korah’s sons, and
another eighty at the gate of Rome among the lepers, himself a grievous leper.
After those years, 565 years from the burning of the second temple, the
Messiah, cleansed of leprosy, would approach the Roman Pontiff as Moses
approached Pharaoh and order him to release the captive Jews.

> Marginalia, printed p. 185: *The tale of the leprous Messiah*.

Jewish lips fully deserve lettuce of this kind. They refuse to place any faith
in the manifest testimony of the Prophets and Apostles; we do not dignify
these claims with, nor think they require, any refutation. The argument from
the weeks therefore stands firm and entirely unshaken by every Jewish
objection. Certainly Daniel’s passage and other Scriptures produced the
greatest expectation of the Messiah among all the Jews around the time of the
birth of our Lord Jesus Christ. Although most rejected the true Messiah, at
that time they embraced many false Christs, resulting in the terrible
slaughter of many myriads. How many hundreds of thousands of unbelieving Jews
poured out their lives even after Jerusalem’s overthrow for that supreme
scoundrel Bar Kokhba!

Indeed, even the Gentiles at that time expected a great ruler arising from
Judaea for the whole world. Cornelius Tacitus, Suetonius, and others twisted
those oracles toward the Vespasians, but absurdly. Their rule was so unfruitful
for the world and of such brief duration that no oracles were needed to
foretell it. Flavius, the father, ruled for only nine years; Titus, the elder
of his sons, for only two; and Domitian, the younger son, for fifteen. After
that human monster, no gentler than Nero, the whole house of the Vespasians
was uprooted and withered. Assuredly, that great ruler born from the Jews in
that age, about whom not only the divine oracles but even the diabolical
oracles of the Sibyls predicted so many marvels, was none other than our Lord
Jesus Christ.

The ancient Talmudists bore ample witness to him in many passages. Indeed,
some in every age, convicted by the argument before us, embraced the Christian
faith. In his commentary on Daniel 9, Dionysius the Carthusian adduces the
confession of one such man six hundred years ago; and Nicolaus Abraham that
of another most learned Rabbi very recently converted in Paris—unless perhaps
that dialogue of Abraham the Jesuit is merely symbolic, not historical. In any
event, the many strong arguments Abraham asserts there are highly suited to
overcoming the most hardened obstinacy of the most perverse Jews.

> Marginalia, printed p. 185: *The testimony of Jews and Gentiles about the
> Messiah’s time*.

I derive the second argument for the principal conclusion, that the Messiah’s
advent is long since past, from verse 24 in the following form. Whenever
transgression has been completed, sin has come to an end, and

[Printed p. 186 | IA leaf 216 | access PDF 216 | OCR object 215]

iniquity has been propitiated, everlasting righteousness introduced, vision
and prophecy sealed, and the Holy of Holies anointed, then the prince Messiah
was not merely born but appeared publicly in the execution of his office. For
the principal works of the Mediator’s office are to propitiate God and
reconcile him when offended by sins; to introduce divine and everlasting
righteousness in satisfaction; by his blood to destroy all the elect’s sins,
not merely with respect to guilt but also to their deadly power; and by the
power of his Spirit to produce spiritual and constant obedience in the elect.
Once the prophecies were fulfilled by the coming of the body that fulfilled
all the shadows, it was also to close and seal further visions as unnecessary.
No one but the supreme Messiah, the omnipotent Son of the eternal God, was
equal to the performance of these thoroughly divine works. And even he,
although the Holy of Holies, was not equipped for them until he had been
anointed by the Father, that is, furnished with every mandate necessary to
execute his office.

I now supply the minor premise. All the things foretold were accomplished
within Daniel’s seventy weeks. For verse 24 explicitly says that the seventy
weeks were determined by God for this very end, that all the foretold works
should be accomplished within that span. Therefore, within the period of
those weeks the principal Messiah was not merely born but publicly executed
his office. Thus he came long ago and appeared in the world, performing his
principal office.

The minor premise is not disputed. To overturn the major, the Jews apply their
stratagems. They attribute all the foretold works not to the Messiah but to
themselves. They say that after the calamities of the second temple the people
will be subdued to such a degree that sin will cease among them and their
righteousness become perfect and everlasting. On its account God will bring
them back into their land, restore Jerusalem, rebuild the temple, and anoint
the Holy of Holies by the hands of the Messiah.

> Marginalia, printed p. 186: *The Jews’ replies are refuted*.

Reply. This answer teems with absurdities. (1) The foretold works had to be
accomplished while the second temple was standing. Yet they themselves show
that none of them was accomplished for many centuries after the second temple
burned, or indeed even to this day. (2) They make no attempt at all to explain
how they themselves will anoint the Holy of Holies or seal vision and
prophecy. Will they themselves anoint the Messiah several thousand years
after the seventy weeks ended? They certainly know by sad experience that
vision was sealed long ago. Although the Talmudists once invented frequent
and ridiculous visions and voices from heaven, more recent Rabbis acknowledge
that no further revelations are given to them. Certainly, shortly after the
year 1500, that universal and supremely solemn fast was celebrated. During it
all the Jews throughout the world—men, women, old people, and children—used
the greatest abstinence possible for an entire year and prayed most fervently,
requesting with great hope some sign concerning the Messiah’s advent. Yet to
this day they have received no answer whatsoever from God.

Third, what they assert about their righteousness in the Roman captivity is
entirely false. Apart from idolatry, from which they were no less free under
the second temple than they are today, they seem to have reformed from no
other sin. They are most avaricious, notorious for injustice in their usury,
very fraudulent, and most profane in their public worship, at least as they
appeared to me in the synagogue at Amsterdam. Most important of all, they are
most obstinate in their rebellion against the gospel, the sole way of
salvation. Indeed, so far are they from repenting at all for the crucified
Christ that they consider their principal sin—the one for which God destroyed
Jerusalem and the temple and overwhelmed them with every calamity they then
and afterward suffered—to have been their forbearance toward the Apostles and
the other Christians: they did not kill them all when they were in their power
or uproot Christianity when its root was still small. This is the Jews’
splendid righteousness under their present captivity, by which they think
they will earn God’s favor so that he forgives all their old sins and leads
them into the final happiness of which they dream.

Fourth, suppose—although it is utterly false—that their past calamities have
brought the entire Jewish nation to the pinnacle of reformation reached by
their ancestors or by any other most devout nation. Suppose they stand on the
summit of righteousness and piety reached by any mortal on earth. Will they
therefore infer that no sin remains in the whole nation? What insane
blasphemy! But even granting this unspeakable absurdity, can the nation’s
supreme innocence so satisfy God that it renders him propitious despite all
the nation’s most dreadful crimes over many centuries? Even today the Jews
rave their ancient ravings. They reject divine righteousness in
Christ’s satisfaction in order to establish their own, which does not exist.
They still harp on the same string, so that everyone everywhere may observe
that they have been rendered senseless by divine judgment.

We offer a third argument from the text, as follows. Before Jerusalem’s
destruction the Messiah was killed and confirmed the covenant with many,
verse 26.

Therefore, before Jerusalem’s destruction he came into the world and publicly
performed his office. The Jews commonly give two replies to the antecedent.
The first is that the Messiah who was killed was not the principal Messiah,
the son of David, but King Agrippa, the son of Agrippa. The second is that the
one Daniel affirms confirmed the covenant with many was not the Messiah but
Titus Vespasian. Shortly before the city’s siege he supposedly made a covenant
with many Jews, but did not long keep it and became a covenant-breaker.

Reply. These answers, just like the preceding ones, are filled with foul
falsehoods. First, the Messiah about whom Daniel spoke was the principal
Messiah and the son of David, who according to all the Prophets had to be
killed and by his suffering accomplish the works of redemption Daniel
mentions. Second, there is no suitable sense in which Agrippa could ever be
called any sort of Messiah, and this man was never killed, as we saw above.
Indeed, none of the four Herods mentioned by Scripture was killed; they all
died at home without any human violence. The first, called the Ascalonite and
the Great, the killer of infants, was tortured for many days by a most severe
disease from the divine hand and died because of his countless crimes. The
second, Herod the Tetrarch, the killer of John the Baptist, languished in
wretched exile in Gaul with his wife Herodias. The third, Herod Agrippa, king
of Jerusalem and all Judaea and the killer of the Apostle James, accepted
divine honors and died of phthiriasis, σκωληκόβρωτος, “eaten by worms.”
The fourth, the preceding man’s son, remained a very close friend of the Romans
to the end. After Jerusalem had been destroyed and the Jewish War completed,
he remained in the favor of the Vespasian father and sons above all the Jews,
and died a natural death. Josephus and others testify to all these things,
being far earlier than all the more recent lying Rabbis.

> Marginalia, printed p. 187: *The Jews’ replies are refuted*.

The same evidence makes clear that their addition concerning Titus deserves
no credence. Quite apart from the fact that Christ and his Apostles confirmed
that gospel covenant—the one confirmed with many in the last of the seventy
weeks—before Titus was born, it is also false that Titus ever violated any
covenant or ever made one with the Jews. Immediately before the city’s siege
he came from Alexandria to Jerusalem, having subdued Galilee and Judaea with
his father in the preceding years. This most merciful ruler indeed gladly
wished to give terms of peace, not of a covenant, to the Jews, who had already
been conquered and made subjects. But the faction of rebels oppressed the
besieged people under such harsh tyranny until the final day of the siege; and
the people were driven to such a pitch of madness by the very furies of divine
vengeance that they obstinately rejected every peace, much less entered into
any covenant with Titus.

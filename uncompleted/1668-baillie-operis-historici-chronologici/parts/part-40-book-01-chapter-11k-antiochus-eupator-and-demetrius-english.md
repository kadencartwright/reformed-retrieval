While Philip

[Printed p. 227 | IA leaf 257 | access PDF 257 | OCR object 256]

was carrying the wretched corpse to Antioch, Lysias made Antiochus Eupator,
the dead man’s son whom he had with him in Syria, king and led him against the
Jews with an enormous army of one hundred thousand infantry and twenty
thousand cavalry. With only a few men Judas Maccabaeus held the army off for
some time. His brother Eleazar rushed with all his strength at the largest of
the elephants, distinguished by its royal
trappings, thinking that it carried the king. When he reached the elephant,
after inflicting a great slaughter upon those in his way, he placed himself
beneath its belly and stabbed the beast from below. It fell and crushed
Eleazar, and Judas withdrew from the battle with his men.

> Marginalia, printed p. 227: *Eleazar, the brother of Judas, is crushed by an
> elephant*.

Victorious in this battle, Eupator besieged the Jerusalem temple. While the
besieged, oppressed by starvation, were preparing to surrender, Eupator heard
that Philip had occupied Antioch. In order to confront the greater evil, he
made a treaty with Judas Maccabaeus. He adorned the temple with gifts, but
contrary to his pledged word demolished its fortifications.

> Marginalia, printed p. 227: *The Jews’ peace with Eupator*.

When Antiochus Eupator came into Syria, he took Antioch and, in it, Philip,
whom he immediately killed. Lysias was all-powerful at court. Menelaus, the
false high priest of the Jews, appeared to him to be the source of the Jewish
revolts and was handed over to be killed. In the same way his impious
predecessor Jason, when he had fled from the Arabs into Egypt and from there
to Lacedaemon, died there in exile and deprived of everything. He was held in
such contempt by all that no one took care even to bury him in the earth; he
was left without a grave as prey for beasts and birds.

> Marginalia, printed p. 227: *Menelaus and Jason die badly*.

After Menelaus, Antiochus gave the high priesthood to Alcimus, a descendant
of Aaron and an exceedingly wicked man. Onias, the son of Onias III, despaired
of his paternal office when Alcimus was elevated to the high priesthood. He
went to Ptolemy Philometor in Egypt and obtained from him permission to build
in the Heliopolitan district a temple like the one at Jerusalem. In it the
Hellenistic Jews worshiped God wrongly and schismatically for more than a
hundred years, although according to the Mosaic rite.

> Marginalia, printed p. 227: *Onias builds a temple in Egypt*.

While these events were taking place in Asia, Demetrius, the son of Seleucus,
who lived at Rome as a hostage in place of his uncle Antiochus Epiphanes, asked
the Senate for the freedom to return home and wrest his ancestral kingdom from
the hands of Eupator, its unjust possessor. The Senate, considering the boy
Eupator a more advantageous king of Asia for their interests than the young
man Demetrius, refused him permission to return home. Nevertheless, chiefly
on the advice of the historian Polybius, he secretly fled from Rome even
without the Senate’s leave. When he came to Asia, he falsely claimed that he
had been sent by the Senate to his ancestral kingdom. This lie opened the
gates of many cities to him and gained him large forces. As he approached
Antioch, Eupator and Lysias were brought to him in the second year of
Eupator’s reign, and Demetrius immediately had both put to death. Thus the
entire line of Epiphanes was eradicated.

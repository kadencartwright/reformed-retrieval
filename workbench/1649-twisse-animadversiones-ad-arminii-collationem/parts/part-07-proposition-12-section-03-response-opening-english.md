RESPONSE.

Junius now admits that sin is the cause of that negative act attributed to God, yet now again
denies that its cause is in the creature. It is by no means easy to give a consistent account of
this difference. Unless I am mistaken, by the negative act Junius means the denial of blessedness
itself, not the decree to deny it. But when he says that the cause of this act is not in the
creature—unless he is speaking of the principal cause, the kind of cause which he judges must be
assigned to the divine will—I cannot grasp the sense in which this can be said without contradicting
what went before. When he immediately adds that the cause why you are not an Apostle or do not live
in Adam’s age lies in God’s will alone, he changes the state of the question. The question is not
whether there are some negative acts whose cause must be sought in God’s will alone. It concerns a
particular negative act: whether the denial of blessedness has no cause other than God’s mere good
pleasure.

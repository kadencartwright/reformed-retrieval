# Calvin to Francis Lismanino, Letter 2373 bis

Late December 1555 (editorial placement)

This is a fresh translation of the complete Latin letter at CO XV 913–914.
Calvin gives Francis Lismanino instructions and greetings for his return
journey to Poland and explains why a promised circular letter is not ready.
The sole reported witness is textually damaged; bracketed wording and notes
make the consequential uncertainty visible.

## Translation

### [CO XV, p. 913]

John Calvin sends greetings to the excellent man and esteemed brother Francis
Lismanino.

For the sake of remembrance, I thought it good to embrace briefly in this
writing certain matters that I recently discussed with you in person. If by
chance you turn aside on your journey to Master Peter Martyr and Master John
à Lasco, you will greet them both in my name. In their kindness, they will
receive you just as if you were carrying letters from me.

### [CO XV, p. 914]

Indeed, you have no need of a commendation, for if you go to them you will
carry one sufficiently ample with you. If I had expected you to make the
journey to Wittenberg, I would have written to Master Philip. But since at
this unfavorable season of the year the detour will be inconvenient for you,
I did not wish to burden you with letters to no purpose. Yet if a better
opportunity to see him is given than I expect, he will understand more fully
from your conversation the state of our affairs and the plan of your mission.

When you have entered Poland—may the Lord soon bring you there safely—do not
suppose that you have been entrusted with a message for only a few people,
but for all who aspire to a purer worship of God: report that from my heart I
wish them every good and send abundant greetings. Yet I desire first place to
be given to the noble and high-born men whose virtues and holy endeavors you
especially praised to me.

As for a general letter, the fact that I [changed] my plan after your departure
was caused not only by lack of time, but also by this reason: I remained
perplexed in choosing the kind of subject until the condition of the church
should be better established. I shall easily attain that through you when you
arrive on the scene.

As for our brothers who discharge the office of teaching, since I do not in
the least doubt that they run ahead of others with the fitting fervor, I did
not think they should be exhorted at present. If they recognize that my labor
could be of any use to them, you will tell them that my services are ready
for them. Yet, conscious of my limited capacity, I do not dare offer my
counsel too freely. Farewell.

## Translator's notes

1. Peter Martyr Vermigli and John à Lasco were natural stops or contacts on
   Lismanino's journey. “Master Philip” is Philip Melanchthon at Wittenberg.
2. At the first damaged phrase on p. 914, CO prints *adversa anni tempore*
   but proposes *adverso* in its apparatus. The transmitted adjective does
   not agree with *tempore*. “At this unfavorable season of the year” gives
   the evident sense without altering `latin.txt`.
3. The most consequential crux is *ut consilium a discessu tuo maturem*.
   CO proposes *mutarem*, “that I changed the plan after your departure.” The
   English adopts that conjectural sense in brackets because the printed
   *maturem* does not yield a satisfactory construction. Calvin is explaining
   why the circular or common letter mentioned in CO's synopsis was not
   completed as promised.
4. CO prints *donec melius comparata esset ecclesiae conditio*, “until the
   condition of the church should be better established,” but suggests
   *comperta*, “better ascertained.” The next sentence—Calvin expects to gain
   what he needs through Lismanino after the latter reaches Poland—strongly
   favors the apparatus conjecture. The translation nevertheless preserves
   the main text's “established” and records the alternative here.
5. *Literis communibus* denotes a letter for a wider community or a circular
   letter, not the private notes Lismanino was already carrying. Calvin
   postpones it until he has a clearer account of the Polish churches.
6. Neither place nor date survives in the body. CO places the letter at the
   end of December because it appears among a group written for Lismanino's
   journey, alongside letters dated from mid-December 1555 through February
   1556. The displayed date is editorial.

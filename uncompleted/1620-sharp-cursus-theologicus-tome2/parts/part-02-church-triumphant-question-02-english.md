### QUESTION II

#### On the canonization of the saints

Bellarmine, book 1, *On the Blessedness of the Saints*, chapter 7, says that
the saints are rightly

[1620 BSB Tome II | canvas 13 | printed col. 25 | bsb10175568]

canonized by the Church. He defines canonization as the Church's public
testimony concerning the true
holiness and glory of a person now deceased, and as its judgment by which it
decrees the honors owed to those who reign happily with God. These honors are
seven: 1. Enrollment in a catalogue by which they are commanded to be
publicly regarded and called saints. 2. Public invocation in the Church's
prayers. 3. The dedication of an altar and a church in their memory. 4. The
public offering of sacrifices in their honor. 5. The celebration of feast
days in their memory. 6. The painting of images. 7. The preservation of
relics in reliquaries and the honor paid to them. In chapter 8 he says that it
belongs to the pope alone to canonize someone who is to be regarded as a
saint by the whole Church. In chapter 9 he says that it must be believed that
the pope cannot err in canonization, and in chapter 10 that saints who have
not been canonized may be venerated privately but not publicly.

We, however, say that those who have lived holy lives are to be imitated and
that their memory ought to be dear to us. That canonization which the Papists
assert and dream up ought not to exist, as is proved by these arguments.

Argument 1. There is no command or example of this canonization in the
Scriptures, and so forth; Bellarmine concedes in chapter 9 that the pope can
err in some matters. Therefore.

Argument 2. There are many hypocrites who outwardly live holy lives in the
Church, and no mortal can be certain of their true piety. Therefore. Augustine
rightly says: *The bodies of many are honored on earth whose souls are
tormented in hell.*

Argument 3. God alone knows, by a judgment according to truth, who are his.
Therefore no

[1620 BSB Tome II | canvas 13 | printed col. 26 | bsb10175568]

person can determine this concerning an individual and thus cannot truly
enroll such people among the saints.

Argument 4. The Church's public judgment ought to be that those who truly
profess Christ are saints even in this life, Romans 1:7. They therefore
absurdly invent a canonization only of the dead.

Argument 5. Bellarmine, book 1, *On the Blessedness of the Saints*, chapter 8,
acknowledges that there was no public canonization in the Church before the
time of Pope Leo III, around the year of Christ 800. But by then Antichrist,
having been revealed, was reigning. Therefore canonization is an invention of
Antichrist.

Argument 6. What they say about the honors to be rendered to canonized saints,
as well as to those not canonized, is ungodly, as will be said afterward.

Argument 7. The pope has canonized parricides on account of their parricide,
such as the Jesuit Guignard, who was guilty of the death of King Henry III, as
is evident from the catalogue of martyrs printed at Rome; and others as well.
Therefore.

Argument 9. Bellarmine himself concedes in chapter 3 that some of the damned
have been honored in worship as saints and blessed persons. Therefore.

## OBJECTIONS

Objection 1. God willed that the sacred writers record individually the
glorious death and life of those who flourished in the Old and New Testaments.
Therefore.

Response 1. The inference is denied, because an account of the life and death
of the saints in Scripture differs greatly from Papist canonization, as is
clear from its description. Furthermore, in neither the Old nor the New
Testament was there an individual enrollment and appointment of feast days,

[1620 BSB Tome II | canvas 14 | printed col. 27 | bsb10175568]

as takes place in the papacy. Therefore. Finally, the Old and New Testaments
mention the life and death of many ungodly people, yet their canonization in
the Church is not thereby given. Therefore canonization does not follow from
an account of someone.

Objection 2. We are bound to honor the saints because their virtue merits it
and because this is useful to us for obtaining very many benefits by their
intercession. But unless we know who are holy persons, we cannot venerate
them. Therefore.

Response. There are too many terms in the argument, because honor is one thing
and worship as the Pontificians understand it is another, as will be said
afterward. Furthermore, we concede that they are to be honored by imitation,
holy remembrance, and so forth, but not in the way the Papists wish. Further,
it does not follow: “They are to be honored; therefore they are to be
canonized in the Papist manner.”

Objection 3. The saints are to be imitated, Hebrews 13:7. But we cannot be
stirred to imitation unless we know who they were and what they did, which is
accomplished through canonization. Therefore.

Response. The assumption is denied, because particular knowledge of those who
have done well is not required for acting well; it is enough if we know the
precepts and the practice. Furthermore, Hebrews 13:7 says nothing about Papist
canonization but deals with the pious remembrance of pastors, the practice of
their doctrine, and the imitation of their faith and patience. Finally, it
does not speak of the dead but of living pastors, as is evident from the
passage.

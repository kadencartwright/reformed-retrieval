VERSE 6. Even if he should live two thousand years and not enjoy good, do not
all these go to the same place?

This is an anticipation whose objection is: “Yet the miser has lived many
years.” The reply is this: grant that he prolongs his life to two thousand
years. Since he nevertheless perceived nothing good in life—no more than the
stillborn child did—this length of life establishes no inequality between
them, especially since by his length of life he has not attained a better lot
after death than the stillborn child. From all these things it becomes clear
how vile and abject the condition of the miser is. He believed that he would
carry himself to the stars by his riches, but the Holy Spirit presses and
casts him down beneath the lowest of all mortals.

Indeed, thus far in this entire discourse Solomon has not touched the
principal misery of the miser, confining himself only to describing the
misery that is bounded by the narrow limits of this life. For nothing is said
here about the second death, the most just wages of sin. In this respect, it
would have been better if the miser had never been conceived in his mother's
womb; and by the same reasoning the condition of a donkey or a pig is more
tolerable than the miser's.

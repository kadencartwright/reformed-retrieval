It is asked whether temporary faith is hypocritical, and temporary believers
therefore cannot be said to fall away when they renounce temporary faith,
because no one falls away from Christ without abandoning hypocrisy. The
Remonstrants affirm it. We deny it.

1. For hypocritical faith is understood in two ways. First, it is a faith that
holds itself out as true and saving faith but is not true and saving faith,
just as something holds itself out entirely as metal but is colored wood. In
this way temporary faith is rightly called hypocritical, because it is saving
faith only apparently, not truly—*kata doxan*, in appearance, but not in
reality.

2. Hypocritical faith is also used for that which is opposed to a false and
entirely fictitious faith and which has nothing

[1668 KBNL/UvA witness | logical printed p. 569 | PDF p. 620 | split word joined: omninò]

at all of faith. In this sense
temporary faith is not hypocritical but is true in its own degree, although
not saving. Thus bronze resembling gold in its tawny color is not hypocritical
metal, for it is true metal, but it is hypocritical gold. Similarly, a painted
human being is not a purely fictitious entity, nor something shaped that is
invented and fabricated. It is truly an entity and truly something shaped,
but it is not truly a human being.

For common essential attributes in the same category can be predicated of
many things while more specific ones are denied. Thus this is a substance; it
is essentially a body, but it is not living or animate. In the same way,
temporary faith, in the genus of saving faith, is hypocritical and only
apparently faith; yet it is not fictitious and hypocritical with respect to
every degree. Temporary faith is rightly called a *gradus ad rem*, a degree
toward the thing, or a material beginning; not a *gradus in re*, a degree in
the thing or species, or a formal beginning of justifying faith. Thus
yellowness is a degree toward whiteness, possessing something of the essence
of color, but it is not essentially whiteness and is distinct from it in
species. It is therefore only a degree toward the species. But white at degree
two is a degree toward whiteness at degree four or five, and indeed a degree
in the thing and a formal beginning; it differs from whiteness at degree four
in degree, not species and nature. Similarly, an embryo is a degree toward the
thing with respect to a living and animate human being, yet it is distinguished
in species from a human being. But an infant in the womb that is animate and

[1668 KBNL/UvA witness | logical printed p. 570 | PDF p. 621 | split word joined: actuatus]

actualized by a rational soul is a degree toward an adult of perfect stature,
and indeed a degree in the thing, distinguished from an adult in degree, not
in species and nature.

Therefore, just as an embryo, in order to become an animate human being, must
cast off the embryo's essential form—but not its entire essence, since it
retains the seminal mass—so temporary faith, in order to become saving and
justifying, must put off the specific form of temporary faith. Yet it need not
put off everything that belongs to the essence of faith, because the assent
given to every word of God must remain. The temporary believer must therefore
cast off what is fictitious and false in temporary faith if he wishes to
abandon hypocrisy. One who does this does not to that extent fall away from
Christ, provided he retains what is material in temporary faith, namely,
assenting to God's Word as true. But one who renounces these things falls away
from Christ to that extent.

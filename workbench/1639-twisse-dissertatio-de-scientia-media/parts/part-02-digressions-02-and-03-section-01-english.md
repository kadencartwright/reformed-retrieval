SECOND DIGRESSION. Whether ALVARES rightly shows that God knows future contingents with certainty,
because of the presence of things in eternity before they occur: and whether such existence leads to
certainty and infallibility of Divine knowledge.

It should first be noted that the presence of things in eternity can be considered in two ways;
first, from the side of eternity itself; second, from the side of contingent things, insofar as they
exist in eternity. For since presence implies a certain relation, things are said to be present to
eternity, and eternity is present to all things, and has them coexisting and present with it;
because when the measure is infinite and indivisible, it simultaneously encompasses all time and any
lesser measure, and contains all things insofar as they will have existence in it, as stated above.
This distinction, as I judge, is corrupted by its own subtlety. For since the presence of something
cannot consist with respect to anything else except by being mutual, the consideration of terms does
not admit an order of prior and posterior, but only simultaneity. Nor do I ever think I have heard
such a distinction applied to local existence, as if the existence of such a thing could indeed be
considered in two ways, either in relation to place, or in relation to the thing placed: Nothing at
all can be said to be present to me, unless I am also present to it. Thus, one must also
philosophize about the presence of things in eternity. God (says Durandus) is not present to a thing
unless the thing is present to him. We do not deny that eternity is present to all things, provided
they exist, and that it has them coexisting with itself, provided they exist at all. But things that
are not yet, that do not yet exist, neither coexist with eternity, nor does eternity coexist with
them, however infinite and indivisible it may be: just as neither does the immensity of God,
although infinite and indivisible, coexist with those small bodies that do not yet exist; nor do
those bodies that do not yet exist. It can be said to exist in the immensity of God, as regards
extensive containment: And in the same way, neither can things which do not yet exist be said to
exist in the eternity of God as regards the duration of containment: For what use is any containment
to them, in which they are to be contained, when they do not yet have any actual nature, capable of
being contained. Eternity, however, is to be described in no other way as embracing time, except
that it precedes time, and after time has come, it coexists with it, and where time has expired, it
will survive it: not because it coexists simultaneously, not only with the present, but also with
the future, and even with the past; which last point must also be stated in opposition, to show that
eternity not only contains all things which will exist in time, but also those which have existed in
it. The second distinction concerning the demonstration of God's infallible knowledge of future
contingents, whether a priori as from a cause or a posteriori as from an effect, follows
legitimately. Also, the last distinction concerning the consideration of God's knowledge, whether as
infallible or as intuitive knowledge, is rightly established. Therefore, the first conclusion is
that the actual present of things from the divine side of eternity is the proper reason why God
knows all future things certainly. This conclusion is in the cited places of St. Thomas, where he
proves that the knowledge of future contingents is infallible in God, because God's intuition is
carried by the eternal over all things as they exist in their presentality, since eternity, by which
divine knowledge is measured, contains within itself all future things by eminence. and the infinite
perfection of his eternity. Regarding the places of Aquinas, to which Alvarez points, we have
already confirmed in my judgment, they prove that Aquinas desires nothing else, since the whole span
of time is before him, clearly known in effect, or what is earlier, in flying effect, therefore
subsequent matters succeed the others as seen by God or are carried by his insight.

reasoning of futurity would be understood by the measurement of the thing with the Angelic age,
consequently not only the thing itself would be understood as Indeed, if one considers the part of
the Angelic age to which such a thing pertains, it would be understood as future. To everyone, as
far as I know, it is professed that the duration of the Angelic age is to exclude all parts and to
be now standing, not now flowing. In time, things that occur indeed have a part corresponding to
their part of time, because time is now: neither eternity nor age is so; both are rather now
standing; because no change is given in that nature whose measure is age or eternity. And we see how
priceless are all things promoted; indeed, they are far more suited to obscure the truth at this
peak than to illuminate it; yet they radiate their rays so effectively that they dispel all the
gloom poured over by others and expose themselves to the sight of all with their splendor.

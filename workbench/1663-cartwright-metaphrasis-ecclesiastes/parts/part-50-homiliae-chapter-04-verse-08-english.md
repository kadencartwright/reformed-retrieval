VERSE 8. If one man is stronger than another, the two of them will withstand
him; in short, a threefold cord is not quickly broken.

The third argument is taken from the aid which his companion furnishes to a
man fighting someone more powerful. On this matter, see the excellent example
in 2 Samuel 10:9–12. This must not be neglected in matters pertaining to eternal life
either. Conscious of our own weakness and ignorance, we should flee to others
who are better instructed in order to implore their aid. Hence no kind of men
has been found more wicked than monks and nuns.

From these three parts compared with one another it follows that this union
of mutual services by day, by night, in peace, and in war is for men

exceedingly well suited, so that there is no part of life from which the fruit
of this society is excluded.

He has recounted some parts of this union, from which it will not be difficult
to judge other things of the same kind. It would be a long journey to
enumerate all the benefits received from human society, such as relaxation
and refreshment of the mind and also the forming of counsels. For, as the
saying goes, more eyes see more than one eye; more hands make a burden lighter;
and there are hundreds of other things of this kind.

From comparison with the lesser case he concludes that the fellowship of many
and their mutual consolation are exceedingly useful and suitable for men. If
mutual assistance is useful when only two are bound to each other by union and
society, how much more when three are joined, and so on with the rest, since
they are better equipped and armed to overcome any difficulty. He illustrates
this with the comparison of a threefold cord, which is broken with difficulty,
and also adorns it with a proverb.

All these benefits and advantages of society and union must be set forth with
this qualification: the society and union must be lawful, have God as its
author—Hebrews 13—and always be joined with holiness. Otherwise what Solomon
declared in Proverbs 11:21 will come to pass: wickedness will not go
unpunished, though hand be joined to hand to defend and uphold it. See Isaiah
8:9–12.

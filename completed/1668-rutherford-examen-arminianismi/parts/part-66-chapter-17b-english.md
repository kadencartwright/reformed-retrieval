But now, omitting the other adversaries, let us engage the Remonstrants more
closely and at close quarters.

It is asked whether synods are neither useful nor suitable means for deciding
controversies. The Remonstrants say they are not and judge that it would have
been much wiser for all Christian teachers to abstain from composing creeds,
confessions, and rules. So Episcopius, disputation 26, thesis 10; so the
Remonstrants' preface to their *Confession*, page 10; so the Socinians, and
Smalcius

[1668 KBNL/UvA witness | logical printed p. 711 | PDF p. 760]

in his refutation of the book *On the Error of the Arians*, book 1, chapter 1,
page 6.

We teach that the declarations of synods must be upheld insofar as they agree
with God's Word.

1. If an erring brother is to be brought to the church, which decides against
the heretic or pronounces judgment upon the brother who offends his brother,
and if the one who does not hear the church is to be excommunicated, then
synods have decisive power. They must therefore be convened not only to
persuade, discuss, consult, and examine, as the Remonstrants teach. But the
former is true, Matthew 18:16.

2. If Christ promised that he would be present with his people when assembled
in Christ's name to pronounce judgment judicially, then their judgment must be
upheld. But the former is stated in Matthew 16:20.

3. In Acts 15:29 the apostles, presbyters, and brethren, assembled in synod,
deliver doctrines that must be obeyed in the Lord and that are called in Acts
16:4 “the decrees determined by the apostles and presbyters.” Yet any
individual apostle could have determined them infallibly by himself without a
synod, unless their intention had been to present a model for synods.

They do indeed say: “But they assembled the synod to convict the minds of
wavering people and false brethren, not because synods have

[1668 KBNL/UvA witness | logical printed p. 712 | PDF p. 761 | split word joined: aliquid]

any authority or right over consciences.”

But this is to concede the whole matter, because all synods are assembled for
the sake of people. Scripture in itself condemns all heresies, and a private
teacher can refute heresies from Scripture. But the Lord willed synods to be
assembled to convince people, and their decisions bind consciences only in a
certain respect and insofar as they agree with God's Word. Add that this
reasoning would militate against a parochial synod no less than a national
one. Indeed, in the Corinthian church it would not have been necessary for the
people to be assembled to excommunicate the incestuous man; Paul could have
done this by himself. Yet God willed excommunication to be performed
synodically in order to convince obstinate people.

4. The authority of synods must be upheld if acquiescence is owed to a
synodical sentence of excommunication commanded by the Holy Spirit, 1
Corinthians 5:4.

5. If synodical doctrine and decision have no authority, neither will the
creed called the Apostles' Creed have any authority. It gained such force by
the approval of synods, and the Remonstrants themselves acknowledge its
authority. Faith resting upon the creed's articles and heads of faith will
always be doubtful, fluctuating, wavering, and momentary rather than fixed.

6. The church at Thyatira and the church at Pergamum would be wrongly

[1668 KBNL/UvA witness | logical printed p. 713 | PDF p. 762 | split word joined: reprehenderetur]

rebuked for not removing false teachers if churches assembled in synod had no
divinely granted power to pronounce and declare authoritative judgments from
God's Word against erroneous and heretical doctrines.

7. Presbyters and churches would have no power to choose their ministers,
contrary to Acts 1:24–25; Acts 6:5; Acts 13:2; Acts 14:25; and 1 Timothy 4:14,
if there were no power and no authoritative judgment of synods that had to be
upheld. Hence Episcopius speaks badly in his disputation *On Synods*: “It is
far better and necessary that individual churches endeavor to remedy their
own evils by tolerating, persuading, and counseling.” Again, thesis 4: “We
determine that synods are useful only in the case that synodical assemblies
are convened to deliberate, discuss, examine, and persuade by reasons and
arguments.” But then no church can forgive sins, retain sins, or condemn a
heretic. Instead, it must tolerate all heresies and blasphemies and seek to
remove and extirpate errors from the church by persuasion alone. Christ would
have left no means of deciding disputes in the church and no authority if the
matter must be accomplished by persuasion alone, because heretics always have
something to oppose. Ministers of the Word assembled together would have no
authority judicially to silence those who contradict by means of God's Word.

8. For this reason they deny that God's Word in the mouth of a synod finally
binds

[1668 KBNL/UvA witness | logical printed p. 714 | PDF p. 763 | split word joined: conscientias]

human consciences: because heretics do not see that to be God's Word which
God's Word nevertheless truly declares to be God's Word. But if this argument
were valid, not only a canon of a synod but not even the canon and words of the
prophets, Christ, and the apostles would bind consciences. For the Sadducees do
not see as God's Word Christ's statement that the dead are to be raised, and
Hymenaeus and Philetus do not see as God's Word Paul's statement that the
resurrection has not already passed but is still future.

It is asked whether those who have the right to vote in a synod ought to be
wholly indifferent and bound to neither party. The Remonstrants say they
should in their *Confession*, chapter 25, section 2, where they explain it in
this way: those delegated to synods should be truly free and, while the case
is being examined, should be bound simply to no person, church, confession,
and so forth, but to God and Christ alone and to his holy Word. Their reason is
in *Apologia*, chapter 25, folio 287: because a judge in an adversarial
proceeding ought not to be one of the parties to the case.

Response. A delegated voter ought to be free; that is, while the case is in
progress, he should be sworn to no person, church, or confession, but to God's
Word alone. He should be sworn to no confession insofar as it is human,
whether during or after the examination of the case. But if they mean that a
voter should be bound to absolutely no party, even one that agrees with God's
Word, they speak nonsense.

1. Because

[1668 KBNL/UvA witness | logical printed p. 715 | PDF p. 764]

Peter, Paul, and James, before the Jerusalem synod was assembled, had already
condemned in their own minds the necessity of circumcision and the other
ceremonies, Acts 15. So Elijah, before the synod with the prophets of Baal was
assembled, had already condemned the worship of Baal, 1 Kings 18. And
Nicodemus and Joseph had resolved in their own minds to stand on Christ's side
before they went to the council. Nor would anyone call them illegitimate
members of the council.

2. Because they would have judges be, with respect to the truth, like prime
matter indifferent in itself, a blank tablet, or clean paper on which nothing
is written, their minds standing at the crossroads. But they ought to have
senses more highly trained, to be rooted in the truth, and to be established
in Christ through faith. For those who are wholly indifferent concerning any
divine truth are people who can be carried about by every wind of doctrine,
whose light is offered for sale in the marketplace.

2. Because in this way they imply that synods are useful for nothing. For if
the subject matter of the canons is a fundamental article, then the more
unbelieving a voter is, the better he is—which is most absurd. For if by divine
faith he believes the fundamental article that is to be formed into a canon,
then he is not free but is bound by faith to one party. If he does not believe
it but is an unbeliever on this point, then the more unbelieving he is, the
more lawful and proper a

[1668 KBNL/UvA witness | logical printed p. 716 | PDF p. 765 | split word joined: Suffragator]

voter he is. But in fact they deny that any canons can be framed concerning
fundamental articles, because fundamentals are not truly disputed. But if the subject
matter of the canons is something truly disputed, as distinguished from
fundamentals, there is no need for canons. For if it is truly disputed, then
even after the examination of the case has ended everyone can be free and
bound to neither party. What, then, is the synod for? To make sport for the
people.

4. Nor did the Remonstrants come to the Synod of Dort free and unbound to the
opinion of Arminius, which they had taught in writings, academies, and pulpits
before the synod was assembled.

5. “Right and truth are not within our power,” say the Apologists in the
passage cited. Therefore, no one—or necessarily only a rare person—comes to a
synod bound to no party.

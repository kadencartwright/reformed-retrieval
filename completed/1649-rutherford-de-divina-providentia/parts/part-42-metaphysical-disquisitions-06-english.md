QUESTION VI. Are possible things anything real?

I answer: They are not, because they signify only a purely extrinsic
denomination in relation to omnipotence.

I.

Because an essence that is only in potency is not a real thing, but is only in
potency and not in act, it is impossible for Peter simultaneously to be merely
in potency toward walking and actually walking.

[Marginalia: A possible is not anything real.]

2. GOD founded all things out of nothing—not out of a nothingness of existence,
but of essence. First, because God created the whole being and everything
belonging to being. Second, because the essences of things are not real before
the beginning of time. For then there would have been infinitely many real
entities coeternal with GOD, contrary to Genesis 1:1; and yet they would not
have been from God through his free creative efficiency. Those entities would
have existed from eternity just as they are now: true, good, beautiful,
pleasant, honorable, useful, one, ordered, numbered, and willed by God. Yet
they would not have come from the first efficient cause through free creation,
but through a coeternal and natural emanation.

Third, these absurdities would also follow. (1) The essences of things would
have existed from eternity and preceded the things themselves by infinitely
many imaginary ages. (2) The essences of things would be eternal, while the
things constituted by essence would also have arisen in time, which is
contradictory before GOD. (3) An infinite number and Platonic treasury of
souls, forms, and essences would have been with GOD from eternity, while I know
not what chimerical existences of things would have been created in time.

Nor do we pause over the dream of some that the essences of things were neither
created from eternity nor uncreated, but only creatable. First, the created and
the uncreated divide and exhaust the whole breadth of real being. Second, in
this way something real other than God himself would have existed with GOD
from eternity. Let that real entity answer God in Job’s place, chapter 28:
“Where were

“you, O real entity, when I laid the cornerstone of this whole structure of all
things?” Third, that creatable thing would be something uncreated, as would
everything capable of being made but not made. And if it was real, was it
something real coeval and coeternal with GOD, uncreated and independent, and
also something unreal, temporal, created, and dependent? But when did that
which arose before the beginning of time begin? At what moment? At what point
of reason?

Nor would I dare reject that Aristotelian statement: “With things incapable of
being otherwise, it does not happen that they are at one time true and at
another false; rather, the same things are always true or always false.”

[Critical note: The Greek type is partly confused in both copies. The

[Marginalia: Aristotle, *Metaphysics*, 9, chapter 12.]

Yet neither would I dare join Aristotle and the Jesuits in asserting that these
propositions—*A human being is an animal*; *A rose is a flower*; *Eating from
the forbidden tree of knowledge is a sin*—are of eternal truth. For the
connected terms of the propositions and their copula, *is*, were created in
time.

[Marginalia: No propositions are of eternal truth.]

But that the entities of things should be eternal while the truths of things
arose in time seems incomprehensible to me. Let the Jesuits, who see
everything, look to it. I do, however, grant that these propositions are
necessarily true, that their truths abstract from time, and that they are
always true in this sense: because of the identification of attribute with
subject, the concept of the attribute is included in the subject’s quidditative
concept. For one who conceives a human being conceives an animal. A human being
in potency includes an animal objectively in potency, and a human being in act
includes an animal in act, as the adversary, the Jesuit Hurtado, acknowledged.

But when a human being is conceived, a rational animal is conceived; and if
you posit a human being from eternity, you must posit a rational animal from
eternity. Essence and the thing possessed of essence cannot fail always to be
identified. Yet the thing possessed of essence was created in time, just like
its essence, and the identity between the essence and the thing possessed of
essence was created together with them when the thing constituted by essence
was created.

But you will say: *A rose is a flower* and *A human being is an animal* are true
in a different way because they are never false, unlike *A human being is
white* and *Peter disputes*, which are often false, though the former are not
therefore of everlasting truth.

I answer: these and those propositions are true in different ways. For in
*Peter disputes* the attribute is not included in the concept of the subject;
but in necessary propositions, because of such an identity of attribute with
subject, if you posit the subject you posit the attribute. Thus, if a human
being had existed from eternity, a rational animal would also have existed from
eternity. But al-*

*—though Peter had existed from eternity, neither whiteness nor disputing would
have existed from eternity.

Objection 2. *This proposition never began to be true: “Antichrist will come”
and “The last judgment is future.” Therefore there are at least some
propositions of eternal truth.*

I answer, first: this argument would establish that all propositions about
contingent things which are true at present had been necessary and of eternal
truth—for example, *A human being is white*—although there is certainly no
necessary connection in them between subject and attribute. Second, the truth
of *Antichrist will come* was in GOD’s eternal decree, but it did not exist
externally from eternity; nor does God form propositions from eternity. The
inference does not hold simply: *The world is willed by God from eternity;
therefore the world exists.* It does not follow.

What futurition is. Futurition is nothing other than a thing’s relation to its
determinate existence when its own time at last arrives.

[Marginalia: What futurition is.]

Suárez says: *The possible differs from a fictitious entity—for example, a
stag-goat—because it is not repugnant that the possible should come to be,
whereas it is repugnant that a stag-goat should exist.*

[Marginalia: Suárez, *Metaphysical Disputations*, disputation 31, section 11,
number 2.]

I admit that the possible differs from the impossible, but not by real
differences. It differs by an extrinsic relation to omnipotence. Although a
complex impossible presupposes something possible, and thus in its own way
presupposes the possible, as was said above.

But Suárez, with others, objects as follows: *A nonbeing cannot be produced;
but a possible can be produced; therefore a possible is not a nonbeing.*

[Marginalia: Suárez, *Metaphysical Disputations*, disputation 30, section 17.]

[Marginalia: Fonseca, *Metaphysics*, book 9, chapter 1, question 3, section 3.]

I answer: it is true that what is a nonbeing simply, and is a being neither in
potency nor in act, but is simply impossible and involves a contradiction in
coming to be, cannot be produced. A possible, however, is not a nonbeing in
that way; rather, the omnipotent one can draw being out of nonbeing.

will does GOD comprehend possible things?” is excluded.]

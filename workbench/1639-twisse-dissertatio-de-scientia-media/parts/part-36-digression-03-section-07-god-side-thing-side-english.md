[Book I, printed p. 70; BSB canvas 00096]

But when Navarrete says that God intuits things according as, on the thing’s side, they are future
in time, he appears to depart too far from the construction of his own opinion. For his doctrine is
that God intuits things not so much as they are future in time as present in eternity. Without doubt
he no more intuits things as future than as present or past, for by the knowledge of vision God
comprehends present and past things as well as future things. Perhaps the force of his opinion lies
in the qualification “on the side of the thing.” Perhaps this is the other member of the distinction
he employed above when he said that the production of things on God’s side is measured by eternity,
implying as the distinction’s other member that on the side of the things themselves their
production must be denied to be measured by eternity. His completed opinion therefore appears to be
this: a thing’s production on God’s side is measured by eternity, but not on the thing’s side. He
would distinguish intuitive knowledge of things before God in the same manner, saying that by
intuitive knowledge a thing is seen by God, on God’s side, as present in eternity, but on the thing’s
side as future in time.

VERSE 14. Or that wealth perishes in an evil undertaking, so that the son he
has fathered has nothing in his possession.

The first member of the division concerns the sudden and unexpected loss of
money that has been acquired. Scripture says universally of riches that they
are uncertain, 1 Timothy 6. Solomon likewise confirms that they have wings and
fly away from us with as much speed as an eagle flies toward heaven. Proverbs
23:5. Although this is true of every kind of wealth, it can be confirmed
especially of money, which in a single night is carried off by thieves who dig
under or break through a house, or in a single day by robbers who use violence
against a traveler on the road.

This loss is amplified from its opposite circumstance: however much care and
anxiety someone employs, however much effort and expense he applies, and
however often he passes a sleepless night in order to preserve his money safe
from force or fraud, keeping it shut in an inner room, in a chest, and under
lock, it is nevertheless lost. He further amplifies and intensifies the loss
from the accompanying quantity: the man has lost all his money, so that
nothing remains to him. Although this often happens with riches of every kind,
it occurs especially with money. Those who leave most other goods and
possessions behind carry all the money away, because they cannot remove the
other things without danger of being caught. This happens especially in civil
war, in which it has often been seen that a man of the most ample revenues and
possessions today stretches out his hand tomorrow to receive alms.

Finally, Solomon aggravates this plundering from another contrary
circumstance. Trusting in the shelter of his wealth, the man may have married a
wife and fathered children by her, yet nothing remains to sustain either
himself or his children and enable them to endure this wretched life. It is
easy to understand from all these things how much grief such a loss brings
with it. Since these things commonly happen to misers, they ought to turn us
away from the pursuit of avarice. Gluttons gorge themselves with a great
quantity of food and then vomit and disgorge all the food they have devoured.
In the same way, those who greedily pant after the acquisition of riches are
compelled to scatter what they gathered too hastily. By contrast, those who
eat and drink moderately and temperately retain, assimilate, and digest their
food, and nourishment results. In the same way, those who preserve measure and
moderation in acquiring riches obtain a very lasting possession, one that is
passed on to their descendants.

Hence it is evident with how much grief and anguish of mind misers are
tormented when the Lord exposes them to plunder. It distresses them that what
they were accumulating over a long

and sweat is enjoyed by others at leisure and without labor. To this is added
the fact that their goods come into the hands of people who devour and

lives by hanging.

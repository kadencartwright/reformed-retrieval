PRAISE BE TO GOD.

[1651 unpaginated chapter table p. 1 | IA leaf 558 | PDF p. 558]

EXERCISE I.

On the divine decrees and the harmonious association of the divine will with
human freedom.

Chapter 1. *On the nature of human freedom.* Page 1.

Chapter 2. *On the eternity and immutability of God’s decrees, and how
justification, which is an immanent act in God, is temporal, so that we are
justified by faith.* Page 18.

Chapter 3. *Whether contingents are subject to God’s absolute decree, and how
God’s conditional, disjunctive, and congruous decrees are overthrown.* Page
68.

Chapter 4. *Against Suárez, Vázquez, Molina, Lessius, Fonseca, and especially
Jacob Arminius, the harmony of contingency and the absolute decree is
explained.* Page 103.

Chapter 5. *Against the same Jesuits and Arminians, the new fable concerning
God’s middle, or conditional, knowledge is overthrown.* Page 187.

EXERCISE II.

On the notions of the divine will.

Chapter 1. *On God’s will of sign and good pleasure.* Page 213.

[1651 unpaginated chapter table p. 2 | IA leaf 559 | PDF p. 559]

Chapter 2. *The proposition that what each person is bound to believe is true,
and so forth, is resolved. Likewise, on the faith demanded from the reprobate
by the evangelical promises. Likewise, it is maintained against the Arminians
that God cannot fall short of his end.* Page 238.

Chapter 3. *Whether God’s good pleasure is the first rule of his justice,
goodness, and mercy toward creatures, against the Englishman Thomas Jackson
and Jacob Arminius.* Page 287.

Chapter 4. *On God’s antecedent and consequent will.* Page 323.

EXERCISE III.

On God’s temporal acts upon human freedom.

Chapter 1. *Whether free choice is determined by God, whether morally or in
the manner of physical influence, in acts both of nature and of grace.* Page
363.

Chapter 2. *On the efficacy of grace, against the Jesuits and Arminians.* Page
395.

Chapter 3. *The arguments of the Jesuits and Arminians against the
predetermining efficacy of grace are answered.* Page 477.

Chapter 4. *The monstrous fable of congruous calling is rejected against
Suárez, P. Fonseca, and Bellarmine.* Page 502.

THE END.

# On Various Articles

This directory presents the complete Latin-form witness and a new English
translation of the consilium headed *Ad diversos articulos* in the *Calvini
Opera*.

## Work and witness identity

| Field | Value |
|---|---|
| Historical responsibility | A French work attributed to John Calvin in CO; the body speaks throughout in a collective first person and has no subscription |
| CO consilium | VII.2, *Ad diversos articulos* |
| Addressees | Unnamed “dearest brothers” in several churches |
| Date and place | Not supplied in the body or CO heading |
| Language history | French original → Theodore Beza's Latin translation; the French wording has not been located here |
| Transcribed witness | Beza's Latin presentation printed by CO |
| Genre | Common pastoral and ecclesiastical letter addressing several disputed practices |
| Topics | Admission to the Lord's Supper; discipline and due process; papal benefices, baptism, funerals, and prayers for the dead; extraordinary preaching; mixed religious teaching; devotional objects; dice and cards; support of pastors' families; endurance under persecution |
| CO locator | X/1, pp. 258–262 |

CO attributes the work to Calvin and says that Beza translated it from French.
That establishes neither Calvin-authored Latin nor the wording of the absent
French source. The letter calls itself *literas communes*, uses “we” and “our”
throughout, and addresses multiple churches. Because no signature or named
co-speakers survive in this witness, this edition does not infer either a sole
speaker behind every plural or the identities of any possible colleagues.

## Source and transcription

The transcription follows Wilhelm Baum, Eduard Cunitz, and Eduard Reuss, eds.,
*Ioannis Calvini opera quae supersunt omnia*, vol. X, part 1 (*Corpus
Reformatorum* XXXVIII; Brunswick: C. A. Schwetschke, 1871), printed pp.
258–262. The volume is available through the University of Geneva's [complete
Calvin collection](https://archive-ouverte.unige.ch/unige:650) and as a [direct
volume X/1 PDF](https://access.archive-ouverte.unige.ch/access/metadata/f526a338-c95c-4f87-b600-04df723f502b/download).

The relevant openings were checked against 300-DPI renders of the UNIGE
facsimile. Their mapping is:

| UNIGE PDF page | Printed CO pages | Text represented |
|---:|---:|---|
| 150 | 257–258 | Opening of VII.2 on p. 258 |
| 151 | 259–260 | Continuation of VII.2 |
| 152 | 261–262 | Conclusion of VII.2, followed by VII.3 |

The downloaded PDF is 33,386,580 bytes and has SHA-256
`025973d6e4533769d0c2b870dd324497943bc14050eb2bcbcc4c964d4f2a1777`.
The transcription begins with *Dilectissimi fratres* and ends with *atque omni
bono cumulatos reddat*. Every word in that interval was collated against the
page images. It excludes the CO heading, bibliographical footnote, running
heads, page numbers, gathering mark, terminal furniture, and neighboring
works. Those are collection paratext or editorial apparatus, not part of the
historical letter.

CO's source note reads: “*Hoc quoque ex gallico versum Beza Epistolis
inseruit*,” and then identifies the Geneva p. 340, Lausanne p. 648, Hanover p.
729, Chouet p. 468, and Amsterdam p. 213 printings. CO supplies no variant
apparatus within the body and identifies no manuscript or surviving French
witness in this note.

The printed-page transitions are after *Domini* (258/259), *mortuis*
(259/260), *neque* (260/261), and *prorsus* (261/262). No word is divided by
any of these page turns. The Latin and English therefore retain all five exact
CO markers at their ordinary boundaries. Printer line-end divisions elsewhere
are silently joined in accordance with the repository policy.

## Textual and interpretive cautions

- CO prints the Greek word *ἀταξίᾳ* in the p. 260 Latin sentence. It is clear
  in the facsimile and has been preserved rather than transliterated.
- *Christianismus* on p. 258 concerns elementary Christian profession or
  instruction tested before admission to the Supper; the English retains the
  compact expression “his Christianity” and explains its scope in a note.
- The allowance concerning an extraordinary preacher is expressly not a grant
  of the ordinary pastoral office. It is conditional on sound doctrine, no
  superstitious prayer or mixture, and continued adherence to the regular
  congregation.
- *Preculae*, “little prayers/devotions,” is instantiated by the Hours and
  Paternosters. These printed terms, and the following distinction between a
  theoretically lawful use of games and the abuses alleged of dice and cards,
  have not been flattened into broader modern categories.
- No illegible word, supplied lacuna, or conjectural emendation was needed.

## Translation status and use

Mary D. Beaty and Benjamin Wirt Farley's copyrighted *Calvin's Ecclesiastical
Advice* (1991) translates the consilia collection. The project's bibliography
audit located no complete reusable public-domain English version of this
letter. That modern translation was not consulted, and no wording from it was
used. [`english.md`](english.md) is a fresh translation from the normalized
Latin witness; because that witness is derivative, it is not represented as a
direct translation of Calvin's unlocated French.

This is an AI-assisted working edition checked against the complete
facsimile, not a peer-reviewed critical edition. Readers should preserve the
French-to-Latin witness direction, the collective voice, and the absent date,
place, names, and subscription when citing it.

It is asked: Is sufficient grace ordinarily given to

[1668 KBNL/UvA witness | logical printed p. 478 | PDF p. 529]

all hearers of the Gospel? The Remonstrants say yes. We set forth our position
in the following assertions.

Assertion 1. A literal acquaintance with and understanding of the Word is
given to unbelievers and reprobates without the operation of the Holy Spirit.
Matthew 11:25–26; Matthew 23:23.

Assertion 2. Through the Word the Holy Spirit convicts reprobates of sin. John
5:45 and 7:28.

Assertion 3. The Word is an active instrument subordinate to the Spirit and
is powerful in its own operation. 1. The Word is living and powerful, 2
Corinthians 10:4; Hebrews 4:12. 2. Cicero's speech is efficacious and powerful.
Therefore, much more efficacious is the Gospel—the sword, arm, and power of
God.

Assertion 4. That efficacious and living quality which accompanies the Word
is not a habitual quality inhering in the Word, because many people grow blind
and become stupefied at the Word.

Assertion 5. The power which accompanies the Word is twofold. One power is
implanted in the Word and does not operate without the Spirit. Another adheres
to the Word and is the operation of the Holy Spirit, which either convicts or
converts. Therefore, the Word is powerful, but through God, 2 Corinthians
10:4. It is God's sword, but in Christ's mouth, Revelation 19:15; a bow from
which he shoots arrows, Revelation 6:2.

Assertion 7. The power of the Holy Spirit terminates in the mind, will, and
affections, animating those powers of the soul.

[1668 KBNL/UvA witness | logical printed p. 479 | PDF p. 530]

But the Word itself does not animate, nor does any habitual grace imbue or
inform it. Therefore, the Holy Spirit elevates the Word to an action nobler
than a bare act of persuading. Accordingly, the Word is a preparatory
instrument of conversion with respect to the act of persuading, but a
consummating instrument with respect to the physical and supernatural act in
whose eliciting the Holy Spirit concurs. For the Word has a merely objective
and moral influence and impresses the grammatical and literal sense upon the
intellect of the person called. (The Arminians profess that they acknowledge
no other influence, either of the Word or of the Holy Spirit: *Apology*,
chapter 1, folio 11.) But the Word is an instrument elevated by the Holy
Spirit with respect to inward and supernatural revelation, because the Spirit
conveys the grammatical sense to the Intellect with an entirely heavenly,
divine, and supernatural evidence. Hence Our Theologians make a twofold
revelation of the Word: one literal, objective, moral, and outward, which
reprobates do not lack; another spiritual, supernatural, real, and inward. In
each revelation the Word is an instrument subordinate to the Holy Spirit,
preparatory in the former and consummating in the latter. Hence:

Assertion 7. We say that sufficient grace does not ordinarily accompany the
preached Word.

1. Outward calling is often without inward calling, as has been proved. 2.

[1668 KBNL/UvA witness | logical printed p. 480 | PDF p. 531]

The Adversaries admit this. Arminius, *Against Perkins*, page 665, and
Corvinus, *Against Molina*, chapter 36, section 4, say that when the Word is
preached to conspicuously obdurate people, the operation of the Spirit then
rests and the Word is thus a bare letter. Hence they admit: 1. God has two
contradictory wills concerning those obdurate people, because through the
preached Word he invites them to repentance, while in the meantime he does
not will them to repent because he denies them sufficient grace. 2. The
commands set before the obdurate are illusory, no differently than if God were
to order stones to speak and dust to reason. They often object this to us, but
in vain.

3. Saving grace is a precious gem granted only to the few elect, Matthew
13:11; Matthew 16:17; Psalm 25:9–10, and not to all who hear the Gospel.

4. The promise of a new heart is not made to all, but only to the children of
the promise.

It is asked: Does no grace at all, not even common grace, accompany the Word
of the Gospel? Reply: In first act, grace that convicts the impenitent
accompanies the Word of the Gospel.

1. Just as the law is a letter that kills, so the Gospel is the ministry of
the Spirit, 2 Corinthians 3:6.

2. The Gospel is the saving Word, Titus 2:11, the word of reconciliation, 2
Corinthians 5:19.

3. If no grace accompanied the Gospel, the hearers of the Gospel would not be
more inexcusable than

[1668 KBNL/UvA witness | logical printed p. 481 | PDF p. 532]

the hearers of the Law. But they are more inexcusable, John 15:22; John 3:17;
Matthew 11:21–22; Matthew 10:15; Hebrews 2:3, 6:4–5, and 10:29.

But in second act, through abuse of the Gospel, the Gospel is, for the
obdurate, just as much as the Law a dead letter and a sealed book, Isaiah
29:11. Christ is a stone of offense and a stumbling block. The Gospel is a
fragrance of death unto death, 2 Corinthians 2:16.

It is asked: Is the essence of Conversion situated in the free act alone of
assenting to and believing the Word, or rather in the infusion of a new heart?
The Arminians place its essence in the free act of believing alone. We place
conversion essentially and in first act not in the free act of believing,
although active Conversion and Conversion to God in second act—which is
commanded to us as a duty—is essentially in such an act, but in the infusion
of new life.

1. Conversion with respect to first act is the work of God alone, who gives a
new heart, regenerates, makes alive, and infuses the sanctifying Spirit. Here
we are entirely passive, like clay in the potter's hand.

2. New creation is essentially the infusion of God's new life. But a free act
is not such an infusion.

3. According to the Arminians, a new heart can be infused into a human
being—that is, the mind can be illuminated and the affections imbued with a
new power—while the will remains not yet converted and renewed because there
is lacking

[1668 KBNL/UvA witness | logical printed p. 482 | PDF p. 533]

one free act of assenting to God as he calls. Hence the person will possess a
new heart and a new Spirit infused and yet will not be a new creature, which
is absurd.

4. One who is reborn would differ from one not reborn, and one begotten again
from one dead in sins, by a single act of free Choice. Nor would a sleeping
person truly be reborn after one free act of assenting to God as he calls,
since that free act then ceases in him.

5. This opinion makes regeneration a habit acquired through many acts of free
Choice, like the habits of Logic, Grammar, the Arts, and the Sciences. The
grace of Regeneration will thus be a bare name.

6. Once this one act of assenting to and believing God has been lost, we do
not see how the person does not fall entirely from the grace of regeneration.

It is asked: In conversion is some supernatural habit supernaturally infused
into a human being by God, or is every gracious habit in a regenerate person
acquired through repeated acts of faith? The Arminians deny the infusion of a
habit. We affirm it.

1. Scripture asserts it in this way. Ezekiel 36:26: “I will give you a new
heart and put a new spirit within you.” Isaiah 44:3: “I will pour water upon
the thirsty and streams upon the dry ground; I will pour my spirit upon your
offspring.” Zechariah 12:10: “I will pour

[1668 KBNL/UvA witness | logical printed p. 483 | PDF p. 534]

upon the house of David the spirit of grace and supplication.” Jeremiah
31:33: “I will put my law in their mind and inscribe it upon their heart.” It
is clear from Ezekiel 36:32 that such a Spirit is given neither by merit nor
by repeated acts of free choice: “It is not for your sake that I will do this,
etc. Be ashamed and be covered with shame because of your ways, etc.”

2. One who is reborn differs in this from one who is unregenerate: this
supernatural habit is present in the regenerate, but by no means in others.
Galatians 2:20: “I live, yet no longer I, but Christ lives in me; and the life
which I now live, I live through the Son of God, who loved me.” Ephesians
4:23–24: “Be renewed in the spirit of your mind, and put on the new human
being, who is created according to God in righteousness and true holiness.”
An act of faith, which can be taken off with one's tunic, is not what is put on
here. 1 John 3:9: “The one who has been born of God does not sin, for God's
seed remains in him.” This birth is the principle of new life, John 1:13.

3. The saints are God's temple and the Spirit dwells in them, 1 Corinthians
3:16. Christ gives in them a fountain of water springing up into eternal life,
John 4:14. But they are not God's temple through a bare power of believing,
because the bare power of believing does not denominate a sinner habitually
faithful, even according to the Adversaries. Nor is a bare act, which passes
away and which a sleeping person puts off, a fountain of springing water.
Therefore, it is a habit. But it is not a habit acquired by our own labor and
study, because

[1668 KBNL/UvA witness | logical printed p. 484 | PDF p. 535 | split word joined: vocamur]

we are called freely, not from works, 2 Timothy 1:9.

3. Although the active infusion of the life of God is not obedience which God
requires from us, as the Arminians say in the *Synodical Writings*, article 4,
pages 154–155, nevertheless passive infusion is commanded to us, and we are
obliged to possess new life both by the sum of the Law, Matthew 22:37, and by
God's express commands, Ezekiel 18:31; Ephesians 4:24; Colossians 3:10.

It is asked: Do the Remonstrants rightly teach in the *Synodical Writings*,
article 4, pages 154–155, that because a habit is not required simply for
acting but only for acting better, an infused habit is therefore not required,
and sufficient and accompanying grace alone suffices without such a habit? We
deny it.

1. To require some gracious habit or supernatural power only for believing
more easily and not for believing simply is the grace of the Pelagians. For
they said that grace is not simply necessary but is only for believing more
easily, just as we sail with oars and more easily with wind. Thus, on this
account, pure nature without grace will suffice for supernatural acts.

2. If their habit acquired by repeated acts is the formal principle of
supernatural acts, why should an infused habit not with better right be such a
principle? And if an acquired habit is such a principle, all the praise both
of conversion and of salvation will be owed to human industry rather than to
heaven-given grace. But if such an

[1668 KBNL/UvA witness | logical printed p. 485 | PDF p. 536 | split word joined: acquisitus]

acquired habit is not the formal principle, then that habit contributes not
entity but facility to the substance of the act.

3. If the habit of God's life is not infused into us, from where do the second
acts of faith remain? Then faith is not God's gift. 1. The habit of faith is
not infused, nor is the power of believing which denominates a person faithful
infused. For according to them the power of believing is compatible with
final unbelief, and they deny that the believing itself is infused, because
free Choice would thus be overthrown.

It is asked: If the power of believing alone came from God and the free act
from the human being, would the principal work of conversion nevertheless
come from God because the bestowal of the power of eating and drinking is a
nobler act than eating and drinking? The Remonstrants say yes, *Hague
Conference*, page 335. We deny it.

1. According to the Adversaries, bestowing the power of believing is not the
bestowal of God's new life in the way that bestowing the power of eating and
drinking is the bestowal of natural life. For to them actually believing is
Conversion, and the converted person differs from the unconverted through a
bare act.

2. The bestowal of the power of believing is not conversion or any formal act
of conversion. For such a power is bestowed upon many who never believe. But
according to them believing is Conversion. Therefore, the principal work of
conversion is given to free Choice and taken away from God.

[1668 KBNL/UvA witness | logical printed p. 486 | PDF p. 537]

It is asked: Because God converts us by irresistible power, is the will
therefore coerced, since the following are present here (as the Remonstrants
say, articles 3–4, page 20): 1. An external agent armed with irresistible
power. 2. A willing agent struggling and resisting. 3. A lesser resistance
overcome by a greater? We deny it.

1. Through the removal of the stony heart and the infusion of a new heart,
the Will is made willing by the external agent from being unwilling and
reluctant, Ezekiel 36:26.

2. A Will that wills reluctantly and acts against its inclination is a
coerced will. But there is no such Will here.

It is asked: Because God determines the Will indeflectibly to one thing, does
he therefore overthrow Liberty? The Remonstrants say yes. We deny it.

1. God, who is infinitely wise and omnipotent, does not overthrow the nature
of secondary causes except in the case of a miraculous transmutation of
substances, as when he converted water into wine and Moses' staff into a
serpent. But conversion is not such a miraculous transmutation of substances,
but one of the qualities of the same substance which remains in its own
nature.

2. God so moves the free will that it most freely moves itself to that very
thing to which it is premoved by God.

3. If certain created, infallibilizing motives infallibly bend the will while
Liberty remains unharmed, much more can the

[1668 KBNL/UvA witness | logical printed p. 487 | PDF p. 538 | split word joined: voluntatum]

Creator of wills accomplish this.

It is asked: Are only the elect inwardly called? The Remonstrants teach in
articles 3–4, page 32, that all in the visible Church, both elect and
reprobate, are inwardly called. We teach that only the elect are inwardly
called.

1. Romans 8:30: “Those whom he foreknew, he predestined; those whom he
predestined, he called; those whom he called, he justified; those whom he
justified, he glorified.” Therefore, only the elect and those who are
justified and glorified are inwardly called. For reprobates, who are not
justified and glorified, are also outwardly called.

2. In 1 Corinthians 1:24 the called are contrasted with those to whom Christ
and the Gospel are a stumbling block and foolishness and who therefore perish.

3. God gives efficacious means to glory only to those for whom he
efficaciously intends glory. Among these means is inward calling.

4. Those alone, even as many as were ordained to eternal life, were called,
believe, and obey the divine calling, Acts 13:48.

5. Inward calling flows from this sole source: God elected to eternal glory
those who were called in this manner, Ephesians 1:4–6.

It is asked: Does inward calling add nothing to outward calling except God's
mere willing and desire by which he wills all people to be converted and
saved? The Remonstrants say yes, articles 2 and 3, page 10. We deny it.

1. It adds inward grace which, from God's eternal election, is infallibly
efficacious.

[1668 KBNL/UvA witness | logical printed p. 488 | PDF p. 539]

Romans 8:28: they are also said to be called according to his purpose.

2. The infallible effect of inward calling is the creation of a new heart and
a new spirit, by which it comes about that we walk in God's precepts, Ezekiel
36:26–27. Outward calling has no such effect.

3. Otherwise obdurate and contumacious reprobates would be called in exactly
the same way as the elect who will certainly be converted. For according to
the Scriptures outward calling shines upon both, and God wills both to be
saved, if the Adversaries are to be believed.

4. Outward calling is mere persuasion, and according to the adversaries the
will to save those called puts nothing real in those called. Hence it follows
that their inward grace of conversion is mere Pelagianism.

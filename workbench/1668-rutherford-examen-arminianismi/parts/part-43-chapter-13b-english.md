The first argument is from God's immutability. If, in Romans 8:30, those whom
God foreknew he

[1668 KBNL/UvA witness | logical printed p. 554 | PDF p. 605 | split word joined: prædestinavit]

predestined; those whom he predestined he called; those whom
he calls he justifies; and those whom he justifies he glorifies; if, in Romans
11:29, God's *charismata*—his gifts and calling—are *ametamelēta*, not to be
repented of; if, in Romans 11:7, “Israel does not obtain it, yet the elect do
obtain it”; if, in John 13:1, those whom Christ loves he loves to the end; if
God's purpose according to election stands firm, Romans 9:11; if God's
foundation stands firm, having this seal, “The Lord knows those who are his,”
2 Timothy 2:19; and if it is impossible for the elect to be led astray,
Matthew 24:24—then true believers who are justified cannot fall from grace
totally and finally. But the former is true. Therefore, so is the latter.

Argument 2 is from God's truthfulness. What God, who cannot lie, has promised
in his Word, he most certainly and unfailingly performs. But God, who cannot
lie, has promised total and final perseverance in the Word to believers
engrafted into Christ. Therefore, God will bring it about that they persevere
most certainly and unfailingly. The major premise is not denied by our
opponents, except that they take refuge in a conditional promise of
perseverance: “Provided,” they say, “that the believers themselves do not fail
themselves; provided that they pray; provided that they make solicitous use,
in the fear of the Lord, of the grace that God supplies to them.” But the grace
by which they do not fail themselves and do not wish to fail, and the grace by
which they pray and fear God, is itself also part of the perseverance promised
by God, Jeremiah 31:40; Zechariah 12:10. It is not, therefore, a condition of
perseverance. For in that case

[1668 KBNL/UvA witness | logical printed p. 555 | PDF p. 606 | split word joined: Deus]

God would promise that the saints will persevere unfailingly, provided that
they are going to persevere. The minor premise is evident from Jeremiah 32:38, 40; Isaiah 54:10; and
Matthew 16:18. So Augustine, *On the Gift of Perseverance*, chapter 2.

Argument 3. From the perpetuity and stability of the covenant of grace. For
the continuance of believers in the state of grace is more stable than God's
natural covenant with the revolutions of the sun and moon, and than the most
settled law governing day and night, Jeremiah 31:35–37; than the mountains and
hills that cannot be moved from their place, Isaiah 54:10; and than the
covenant with Noah that the world would not be overwhelmed by waters again,
which God swore would never happen, in the same passage. It is as established
and certain as that which God bound himself to do not only by promise but by
the sacrament of an intervening oath, Hebrews 6:17–18; Psalm 89:30–35, etc.

Argument 4. From God's keeping and protection. Those who are kept by God's
power and protection for eternal life cannot fall from grace finally and
totally. But all the truly regenerate are kept in this way, 1 Peter 1:5; 1
Corinthians 1:8, 10; Philippians 1:6; 2 Thessalonians 3:3.

Argument 5. From God's omnipotence. If the one who is to snatch Christ's sheep
from the hand of God the Father and Christ must be greater and stronger than
the Father, John 10:28–29; if such a one must be greater and stronger than any
creature, Romans 8:35, 37–39; if God is able to save his own, Jude 24; and if
God employs his

[1668 KBNL/UvA witness | logical printed p. 556 | PDF p. 607 | split word joined: omnipotentiam]

omnipotence to present faithful people holy and blameless in the presence of
his glory—then the regenerate cannot fall from the state of grace. But the
former is true, as has been proved from the cited passages.

Argument 6. From Christ's intercession. If Christ lives for his own and acts
as their Advocate when they sin, 1 John 2:1; if he intercedes for them,
Hebrews 9:24–26; if he prays for all who will believe in him until the end of
the world, not only for the apostles, John 17:20–24, that they may persevere,
be preserved to the end, and obtain eternal life, and that their faith may not
fail, Luke 22:32—then the truly regenerate cannot fail to persevere to the
end. For the Father always hears the Son when he prays, John 11:42; John
6:37–38.

Argument 7. From the nature of divine grace, not simply habitual but equally
habitual and actual. Those who are born of incorruptible seed; who cannot give
themselves to sin or sin unto death with every effort and power of the will,
because God's seed remains in them; in whom there is a spring of the water of
life welling up to eternal life; and who are planted beside streams of water
so that they cannot wither—these cannot fall from grace finally and totally.
But all the regenerate are such people, 1 Peter 1:23; 1 John 3:9; Psalm 1:3;
John 4:14 and 7:38; Matthew 7:24; Luke 8:15.

Argument 8. From the covenant between God the Father and Christ. For the ends
of the earth are promised to Christ by God the Father, Psalm 2:7–8, as are
offspring and children

[1668 KBNL/UvA witness | logical printed p. 557 | PDF p. 608 | split word joined: dati]

given by God, provided he lays down his life for his own, Isaiah 53:10; a willing people,
Psalm 110:3; and dominion from sea to sea, Psalm 72:8. But if the saints can
fall away, these promises will be void, and God the
Father will not keep this covenant with his Son, which is blasphemous.

Argument 9. From the sealing of the Spirit. Those who have been sealed for the
day of redemption, 2 Corinthians 1:21; Ephesians 1:23 and 4:30; who have
received the firstfruits of the Spirit and the pledge; who have already been
saved in hope and are already seated with Christ at the Father's right hand—
these cannot fall away completely. But believers are such people. The reason
for the major premise is that hope does not put us to shame and does not
deceive, Romans 5:5. Nor is the seal of the Holy Spirit deceptive, nor does it
seal an uncertain thing. For what consolation, what peace, what joy would
arise from it if hope were deceptive and put those who hope to shame? Romans
14:17. The minor is evident from Romans 8:23–37. Nor is a seal in the heart
needed to confirm general truths in the New Testament, such as “the one who
believes will be saved.” For these are sufficiently confirmed to all by God's
testimony and Word. Even devils and many hypocrites do not deny them, and
believers more rarely and less seriously doubt them. And this seal no more
seals to Peter that his salvation is established, provided that he freely
perseveres, than it does to Judas the traitor.

Argument 10. From the saints' dominion and victory. Those who have so died to
sin that meanwhile it does not rule over them and they cannot give themselves
to sin;

[1668 KBNL/UvA witness | logical printed p. 558 | PDF p. 609]

and who obtain such victory through faith that they cannot be
conquered, cannot fall away. But sin no longer rules over the regenerate,
Romans 6:8–9. They cannot give themselves to sin, 1 John 3:9 and 5:4. Because
they are born of God, they overcome the world. They keep themselves so that
the evil one does not touch them, 1 John 5:18; Revelation 20:6; Romans
8:35–37.

Argument 11. From the distinction between temporary and true believers. If
some people depart from the faith that they professed for a time because they
were never true believers, then true believers cannot fall away. But the
former is true. Therefore, so is the latter. The reason for the major premise
is that possessing true and saving faith is the adequate cause why some
persevere, and someone falls away because he lacks this faith. Thus, if life
is the cause of motion and sensation, and a person neither moves nor senses
because he is not alive, then those possessing life necessarily can move and
sense. Proof of the minor: 1 John 2:19, “They went out from us, but they were
not of us; for if they had been of us, they would certainly have remained
with us; but they went out so that it might become manifest that they were not
of us.” So also Luke 8:18; John 2:23–24; Matthew 13:21; John 17:9; Isaiah
40:24, 30, 31.

Argument 12. From the relation between believers and Christ. For if believers
can finally and totally depart from the faith, Christ would not certainly be
the head of the Church, since he could lack members; not a Bridegroom, since
he could have no bride; not a King, since he could have no subjects; not a
Savior and Redeemer, since he could have none

[1668 KBNL/UvA witness | logical printed p. 559 | PDF p. 610 | split word joined: Salvati]

saved and redeemed; not the God
of the eternal covenant, since he could have no covenant people; nor made by
God our Wisdom, Righteousness, Sanctification, and Redemption, since there
might be no one capable of his righteousness, wisdom, sanctification, and
redemption when all had become apostates. Faith concerning the catholic
Church would fail; the praises and victory of the Lamb could fail; and the
devil would be victor and Christ vanquished.

For the Arminians' reply is empty: that Christ is King in this respect, that
he has right and dominion over all mortals, even though there were in fact no
one who acknowledged him as King, because he rules in the midst of his
enemies, Psalm 110:2. For Christ is King in the actual preservation of his
subjects for eternal life; Head in an actual influence upon his members; and
Bridegroom in that eternal marriage with the Bride, contracted in this life
and to be celebrated eternally in heaven. This is expressly taught in
innumerable passages of Scripture, but especially in Psalms 45 and 110,
Colossians 1, Jeremiah 31, and throughout the Song of Solomon.

Argument 13. From the unconquered dominion of divine grace. For if, after all
the operations of grace whatsoever have been posited, all could finally fall
away because of free choice indifferent to persevering or not persevering,
then Christ would be a weak King unable to preserve his own. His kingdom would
be destined to end and would not be eternal, contrary to Daniel 2:44; Luke
1:33; Psalm

[1668 KBNL/UvA witness | logical printed p. 560 | PDF p. 611]

145:13; Isaiah 9:7; Micah 4:7; Revelation 11:15.

Argument 14. From the prayers of the saints. We could not pray in faith and
certain confidence for the saints' perseverance, contrary to John 14:13–14.
Nor could we pray that God's name be hallowed, that God's kingdom come, that
God's will be done, and that we not be led into temptation, because it would
not lie with God to satisfy these prayers if it lies with free choice to fall
totally and finally from God.

15. From the confidence of the saints and from the thanks that those who
persevere owe to God. For I could not believe and hope that I will be preserved
by God's grace, nor would it be permissible to thank God for perseverance, if
it lies with human choice finally to fall away from God.

16. From the hope, joy, and consolation of the Holy Spirit. These would
collapse if the perseverance of the saints were not infallible and necessary,
because the hope, consolation, and joy under the hope of glory that the
Remonstrants supply to elect believers, according to the doctrine of the
apostasy of the saints, belong no more to them than to reprobates and
hypocrites. Their consolation amounts to this: “Because I am a person
outwardly called, I have from this the firm consolation that I shall be
glorified, if through free choice I do not fail God's grace and protection.”
But before he falls away, the bitterest apostate can seize this joy and
consolation by a right equal to that of believers; and afterward, if he
repents, he can seize it again although he will fall away again.

[1668 KBNL/UvA witness | logical printed p. 561 | PDF p. 612]

14. From the end of creation. For God would have created all the sons of men
in vain, contrary to Psalm 87.47. Nor would God have formed his elect people
for himself and for his glory, contrary to Isaiah 43:21: “My people, whom I
formed for myself, shall declare my praise.” The doctrine of God's gracious
and supernatural providence for his own would also collapse, contrary to
Isaiah 46:3: “Hear, house of Jacob, who have been carried by me from the womb;
I am with you even to old age, and even to gray hairs I shall carry you. I made
you, I shall bear you; I shall also carry and deliver you.”

18. From the doctrine of faith. All the articles of faith would be utterly
overthrown if the truly elect and regenerate could perish eternally. The
doctrine of Christ's conception and incarnation to save his own would be null
and void. The article concerning Christ's death and its efficacy, his
resurrection, and Christ's triumph over death, the devil, and hell would be
empty if these enemies could triumph eternally over all true believers and
the elect. To what purpose would be the doctrine of Christ's descent into hell
and his suffering of infernal torments, which it would have been fitting for
us to suffer according to the order of justice? To what purpose the doctrine
of Christ's ascension into heaven to make a new way for us through the veil of
his flesh; his advocacy and intercession at the right hand of God the Father;
and his preparation of heavenly dwellings, if it could happen that

[1668 KBNL/UvA witness | logical printed p. 562 | PDF p. 613 | split word joined: nemo]

no mortal ever ascended into heaven? The doctrine concerning the sending of the Holy Spirit, who would lead us
into all truth, and concerning Christ's remaining and dwelling with us until
the consummation of the age would also be empty if the Holy Spirit could
abandon his own. The doctrine concerning Christ's catholic Church would also
be null and void if it could become a synagogue of Satan. All its privileges
would be void: namely, that it is one by the unity of faith and the Spirit;
that it is holy; that it is catholic; and that it is the Sister and Bride of
the glorious Brother and Husband, Christ—if no such assembly could exist in
the world. What would the communion of saints be? What mutual love among the
members of Christ? What gratuitous justification and forgiveness of sins?
What glorious resurrection of the righteous, if all the truly regenerate
could fall completely from grace and perish eternally? Finally, what hope of
eternal life and of the glorious marriage between the Lamb and his Bride to
be celebrated eternally, if all mortals could become inhabitants of Gehenna
and black apostates? For all these benefits contained in the Apostles' Creed
would only have been offered, either never bestowed or completely taken away.

19. This doctrine is diametrically opposed to the Law and the Gospel. For on
this doctrine the regenerate, after performing good works throughout life,
can fall from the crown in the final hour of death. It is possible that there
be no eternal salvation through the Gospel and faith in Christ any more than
through the Law and legal righteousness, since the regenerate and true
believers

[1668 KBNL/UvA witness | logical printed p. 563 | PDF p. 614 | split word joined: possunt]

can fall from saving faith and the grace of Christ.

20. Finally, God's glory to be declared by the glorified, the glory of
redemption, our salvation, and everything by which the saints are
distinguished from hypocrites would depend upon mere contingency or fortune,
not upon the determining dominion of gracious providence. Nor would God know
the fixed number of the elect and reprobate, because this number, depending
entirely upon the free will of those who persevere or fall away, would be
altogether uncertain. For if future contingencies cannot be known by God, God
does not know who will persevere and who will not. Thus the perseverance or
apostasy of the saints escapes and passes beyond all God's knowledge and all
acts of providence.

# Paper of the Inquisitor Horrys

This is a fresh English translation of the complete French dossier item at CO
X/1 197–199. The paper states Horrys's position and was transmitted to Calvin
for an answer. It is contextual input, not prose by Calvin. No wording from
Beaty and Farley's modern translation supplied the English.

## Translation

### [CO X/1, p. 197]

A memorandum to My Lord, asking him to demonstrate that the commandments of
the old law are not the commandments given to Christians.

First, where there is another law, there are other commandments. In the New
Testament there is another law, called evangelical, which did not exist in the
Old Testament, in which the Mosaic law existed. Thus the commandments given
to Christians are different from the commandments given to the Israelites.

Second, Christians are not bound to observe any commandments unless they are
moral—that is, from the natural or evangelical law, which is to say the law
of faith. These are two rules for living, namely a human and a divine rule.
The first is fitting for a human being insofar as he is human, and the second
is fitting for him insofar as he is a Christian. Thus every commandment that
does not belong to the law of nature—that is, to natural reason—or to faith
does not bind us, even though it may be written in the Old Testament.

Third, the precept given to ancient Israel not to make a carved image and not
to worship it does not come from natural reason. Consequently, it is not a
moral precept and does not belong to the law, for it is not written in the
Gospel and New Testament.

### [CO X/1, p. 198]

Therefore it is a precept given specifically to the people of Israel to
preserve them from idolatry, to which they were strongly inclined and drawn by
the example of the Gentiles among whom they dwelt.

In the first place, it is quite clear that the said precept is not moral. It
is not contrary to reason to speak through images and signs, as God often
spoke to the prophets and saints of the Old and New Testaments. Through
various likenesses he wished to represent things that are remote from us.
Therefore, just as it is not contrary to reason to teach one's neighbor by
speech or writing, neither is it contrary to reason to teach him by
representative images and likenesses. God acted in this way. Accordingly, it
is lawful for us to follow him by doing so.

Indeed, he commanded that two bodily images of the cherubim be made within the
temple, and he represented himself and caused himself to be worshiped by
Abraham. And in Revelation, in his image and likeness as the Son of Man.
Therefore, just as we bow the head when the name of God and a mystery of grace
represented to us by words are read, and just as we kiss the Gospels because
they signify divine truth, so also we bow the head and venerate images by
kissing them insofar as they represent those same holy things signified by the
divine words and Holy Scriptures.

For all three—words, writings, and images—are only holy signs. That is, there
is no holiness in them according to their real being, as idolaters thought
about their idols. There is holiness only in their signifying character,
namely insofar as they signify holy things.

This precept also does not belong to faith and was not commanded by Jesus
Christ and his Spirit in the New Testament. Therefore, those who wish to
observe such commandments Judaize and renounce the faith, testifying by their
conduct that Jesus Christ did not abolish the old law by his death. This is
contrary to the New Testament Scriptures. Those who think that Exodus chapter
20 contains the commandments of our law Judaize even more plainly. Those who
print them in this way, or have them written on their columns and put them
forward as commandments given to Christians, are greatly mistaken.

For Scripture teaches us that those are the old precepts and those of the
Gospel are the new. The motives for fulfilling the old commandments are
temporal goods and fear of punishments, such as having been brought out of
Egypt, and being beaten and punished to the third and fourth generation when
they do not observe them. The motive for those of the New Testament is the
love of God and of heavenly goods. Therefore we have not received again the
spirit of slavery in fear, but the filial spirit, in order to perform and
fulfill the law of grace through love. Those of the Old Testament are
fulfilled according to reason and the law of Moses; these according to faith
and evangelical law. And therefore there is

### [CO X/1, p. 199]

as much difference between the two kinds of commandments as there is between
Adam and Jesus, between the human man and the Christian man. For this reason
Jesus Christ told us that he gives us a new commandment containing new
ordinances.

Moreover, those who cling to the letter written in the said chapter and in
other Scriptures of the old law necessarily confuse and mingle what is
ceremonial with what is moral. In this way they Judaize and keep the Decalogue
of the Old Testament while thinking that they live as Christians and keep the
Decalogue of the New Testament. For in that text it is said that the Sabbath
must be observed and that no stone altar is to be made, but only one of earth
and without steps, along with other such things written in the letter of the
Decalogue of Moses. All of this is cut away from the evangelical Decalogue
given by Jesus Christ and expressly set forth in the Gospels and writings of
the New Testament.

## Translator's notes

1. CO's heading identifies this as a copy of a paper by the inquisitor Horrys,
   given to prisoners for the Word in Lyon to be forwarded to Calvin. The
   first-person plural belongs to that paper's argument; it must not be
   reassigned to Calvin.
2. The opening *le prient* is awkward in the printed witness. “Asking him”
   conveys its apparent function without silently changing the French source
   to a conjectural form.
3. The sequence about Abraham and Revelation is syntactically incomplete,
   especially *Et en lapocalypse en son image et semblance du filz de lhomme*.
   The English leaves the final clause fragmentary rather than supplying an
   absent verb or harmonizing it with Calvin's answer.
4. “Real being” and “signifying character” render the paper's contrast between
   *estre real* and *estre signifiant*: it locates holiness in what a sign
   signifies, not as an inherent quality of its material existence.
5. The body gives no date, personal address, place, signature, or closing. Its
   connection with Lyon, Horrys, the prisoners, and Calvin comes from the CO
   dossier heading and source note, not from the argument itself.

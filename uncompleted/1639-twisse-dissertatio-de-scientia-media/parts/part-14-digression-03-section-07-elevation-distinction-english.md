[Book I, printed p. 67; BSB canvas 00093]

For he attributes this more excellent mode of being—namely, being in eternity—to things before they
exist simply, and does so by elevation before they possess any proper entity. This is just as if
someone were elevated to some political dignity, say kingship, or ecclesiastical dignity, say the
papacy, before he had been born or even conceived in the womb, which appears more than marvelous.
He therefore says that it does not belong to the thing through passive elevation; yet he does not
wish you to be free to suppose that it belongs to it through active elevation either. For it is not
credible that Antichrist elevates himself to existence in eternity before he has been conceived.
Although Lucifer once said in his heart, “I shall ascend into heaven; I shall exalt my throne above
the stars of God,” we nowhere read that he said this before he was born.

Again, Navarrete denies that this occurs through extrinsic elevation, yet does not wish you to
suspect that it occurs through intrinsic elevation. He says instead that it occurs through a
certain containment by which the thing is contained by eternity as eternity extends itself to all
future things in time. Such was the subtlety of the distinction, which could have been dispatched
briefly in this form: elevation to a more excellent mode of being is understood in two ways—either
through passive elevation received intrinsically or through a certain containment.

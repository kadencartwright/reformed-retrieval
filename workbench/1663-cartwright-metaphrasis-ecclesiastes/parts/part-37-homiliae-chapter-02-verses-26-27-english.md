VERSE 26. (For who would be going to enjoy it, and who would enjoy it at once
except me?)

He brings another argument from a comparison with the greater case. For since
Solomon, the most powerful and wisest king, could not attain these things, will
anyone ever be found who can, at his own pleasure, eat, drink, be nourished,
and be affected with joy from food and drink? If the man who abounded in so
great a variety of foods and seasonings and in an exquisite method—relying on
which, when his stomach disdained one kind, he could turn to another—did not
attain this, though he was imbued with so great a wisdom, by which he could
drive unnecessary sorrows away from himself, and furnished with so great a
knowledge of natural things, by which he could either preserve health or
recover it when lost, what will happen to others who in all these things were
and will be many parasangs inferior to him?

From this we are taught to be unwilling to attempt those things or promise
ourselves those things which men stronger than we in wisdom and powers could
not attain. In this class of conduct, those men sin who are led by the hope of
attaining the heavenly kingdom while applying no care or diligence and
straining no sinews by which either to avoid offenses and impediments or to
pursue the aids by which one travels to eternal life. This is especially so
since they can know that no one has ever been found among those dearest to God
who attained life without the greatest exertion, or rather struggle, of the
soul. Luke 13:24.

VERSE 27. For to the man who shall appear good in his sight, God gives wisdom
and knowledge with gladness; but to the sinner he gives the occupation of
gathering and heaping up, so that he may hand it over to the one who shall
appear good in his sight. Even that is vanity and affliction of spirit.

He proves that this is from the hand of God by the recipient, namely, the man
who is approved by God's gracious will. For if these things were placed within
the power and choice of men, everyone indiscriminately would be a participant
in this benefit. Here by the word “wisdom” he denotes the diligence which God
confers upon the godly, by which they acquire riches and the things which
pertain to the maintenance of life; by the word “knowledge,” skill in using
them suitably; and by the word “gladness,” the glad and pleasant enjoyment of
the things acquired. These proceed not only from God and from his power but
also from his gracious will, so that we should not boast about things acquired
by labor, but by crediting them to God should show ourselves grateful to him.

He further confirms this from contraries, because God indeed grants sinners
the wisdom by which they may accumulate and heap up riches, but he does not
grant their use, much less their glad and cheerful enjoyment. He confirms it
from the end for which they turn those things together and heap them up,
namely, that they may fall to and be left to those whom God graciously
approves. The sinner indeed sets this end before himself: that he may enjoy
them as long as he is alive and, after death, transfer them to others. But
God's plan is far different: namely, that sinners should be, as it were, the
stewards and treasurers of the godly, since godliness has the promises of this
life and the future life. Thus happiness in this life depends upon godliness,
which is the subject of this book.

Indeed, what is greater, the godly man not only transmits things acquired by
himself to his own people, but will himself possess by inheritance things
acquired by the wicked. Proverbs 28:8; likewise Job 27:16–19. These points are
illustrated by the parallel of an ass loaded with great treasures, which it
carries not for itself but for others. And we sometimes see it happen that

the wicked and sinners stuff themselves with the delights of this world and
receive the fruit of their labors abundantly in this life, while, by contrast,
the godly are pressed by poverty and grief. But one must consider what is said
in the Psalms, Psalm 76:16, and Proverbs 17:1 and 15:16–17: that one righteous
man is better than the ample riches of many wicked men. Likewise, a mouthful
of dry food is better with peace than a house full of slaughtered animals
where there is contention. Likewise, a little with reverence for Jehovah is
better than a large and great treasure with tumult. Vegetable fare where
there is love is better than a fattened ox where there is hatred.

The reason for this is that the godly are heirs of the world and its rightful
possessors, and therefore can also partake of created things with great
cheerfulness. To this is added the fact that God supplies inwardly in the mind
what is lacking outwardly—see Daniel 1—just as in the highest gladness and
laughter of the wicked there is grief, and the end of joy is pain. Proverbs
14.

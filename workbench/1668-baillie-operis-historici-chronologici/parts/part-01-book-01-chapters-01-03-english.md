# BOOK ONE

## On the times from the creation of the world to the birth of Christ, and on
chronological difficulties in the Old Testament

## CHAPTER I

### On events from Creation to the Flood

In Psalm 8:4 the royal Psalmist, pondering the nature of the heavens, cries
out: *“What is man, that you are mindful of him, or the son of man, that you
visit him?”* The whole globe of the earth, when set beside the starry heaven—not
even the prime mover or the empyrean—is of so minute a bulk that it scarcely
counts as a point. Yet the heaven of heavens itself, the most spacious abode of
the blessed, exceeding the sphere of the stars by almost infinite measures,
stands incomparably farther from the immensity of the divine being than the
smallest point of the mathematicians. What we read in Isaiah 40:17 about the
nations compared with God is most true of the whole fabric of the world and of
all creatures gathered into one: *“All nations are before God as though they
did not exist, and are reckoned by him as nothing and emptiness.”* What, then,
becomes of man, whose proportion even to the earth, much less to heaven, is so
small? If you compare him with God, how immense must the distance be, sinking
below a point, below nothing!

The duration and time of man and of all other creatures are very like their
magnitude and bulk. In Moses’ own age, as Psalm 90 says, the usual bounds of
even the longest human life were contained in very few decades of years. Verse
10: *“The days of our years are seventy years, or, if by reason of strength,
eighty years.”*

The duration of the whole world down to this day has not yet reached 5,600
years—that is, scarcely an eighth part of one Platonic year. Just as the tenth
sphere produces the day, the eighth the month of the moon, and the sun the
ordinary year by their motions, so the ninth sphere, when its motion

[Printed p. 2 | IA leaf 32 | access PDF 32 | OCR object 31]

has been completed, is said to bring all the heavenly orbs and stars back to
the very points and precisely the same positions in which they stood at the
first moment of their creation. By completing its revolution once, it produces
the great Platonic year, which embraces 48,000 ordinary years. But even if you
multiply this greatest of years by the number of heaven’s stars or the sands of
the sea—or, if you wish, with the most inquisitive mathematician, by as many
grains of sand as would fill the hollow of heaven down to the earth’s
surface—this greatest duration that can readily be imagined does not count as
even a moment when compared with God’s eternal permanence. For what Moses says
of a thousand years applies to the proportion between eternity and any length
of time whatever, Psalm 90:4: *“A thousand years before your eyes are like
yesterday which has passed, and like a watch in the night, which are counted
as nothing.”*

I thought it well to note these things at the outset, to give courage to those
entering the great sea of time. However immense and vast the span of time from
the beginning of the world to this day may appear, since it does not reach an
eighth of one philosophical year or the least part of divine duration, it
ought not deter any sensible person from traversing it briskly. This is all the
more so because the first epoch before us, from the creation of the world to
its first destruction, consists of about 1,656 years—a greater stretch than we
count from the Lord’s birth and almost a third of the complete 5,600-year body
of time that the world has not yet attained—yet contains so few memorable
things, and no others than those related in the first few chapters of Genesis.
Amid the deep silence of all secular writers it is called by many empty and
almost destitute of things that can be narrated. We must indeed pass over those
first sixteen centuries and a little more in one or two short pages.

The sum of this epoch, namely 1,656 years, is gathered from Genesis 5 as
follows. Adam begot Seth in the 130th year of his age; Seth begot Enosh in his
105th; Enosh begot Kenan in his 90th; Kenan begot Mahalalel in his 70th;
Mahalalel begot Jared in his 65th; Jared begot Enoch in his 162nd; Enoch begot
Methuselah in his 65th; Methuselah begot Lamech in his 187th; Lamech begot Noah
in his 182nd; and the Flood occurred in Noah’s 600th year. Join these particular
sums and the total comes to 1,656 years.

In this epoch we shall mention only a few things under three heads: the
creation of things, the lives of the patriarchs, and some notable events in
those centuries recorded by Moses. Take the first head as follows.

After almighty God—three in one, Father, Son, and Holy Spirit—had lived from
eternity without the coexistence of any creature, in the most perfect
blessedness, namely the enjoyment of himself, he determined in time to create
the world and all its parts from nothing in the space of six days. On the first
day were created Chaos, that great Abyss and first matter of the corporeal
things afterward made; also light, whose setting of whatever kind brought the
first day to an end. According to many, besides Chaos and Light, the third
heaven, the abode of the blessed, and its first inhabitants, the angels, all
then good, were created.

The work of the second day was heaven: not merely the first, that expanse of
air separating the waters above the clouds from the lower waters of land and
sea, but also the second, embracing the elemental regions above the clouds and
upper waters and the whole framework of orbs in and through which stars,
whether planets or fixed stars, perform their motions. As for the third heaven,
it is commonly held to have been the work of the first day, as stated.

The third day gave earth and sea: the sea still empty of its later inhabitants,
the most prolific schools of fish, but the earth that day burdened with the
richest crop of every kind of herb, grain, shrub, tree, and fully ripened fruit,
yet without the work of any spring, summer, or autumn, or the concurrence of
the sun and stars which now cause those seasons. The fourth day placed light
in the lamps of the sun, moon, and other stars fixed in heaven’s vault. The
fifth filled the sea with fish and the air with birds. Finally, the sixth gave
the earth the various species of quadrupeds and reptiles, and after all else
man, lord of the rest: Adam, created in God’s image, to whom before that day’s
end his wife Eve was added as a helper.

On the seventh day God rested from further creation and consecrated to his
worship this day of his rest, appointed for mankind’s holy rest thereafter and
distinguished and separated from all other days.

As for the second head, the patriarchs’ lives, we learn their limits from
Genesis 5. The line of patriarchs before the Flood consists of eleven persons
in direct descent, each later one born from the one before.

First, Adam lived 930 years; second, Seth 912; third, Enos 905; fourth, Kenan
912; fifth, Mahalalel 895; sixth, Jared 962; seventh, Enoch, after 365 years,
was taken alive into heavenly blessedness without seeing death; eighth,
Methuselah passed 969 years; ninth, Lamech did not reach beyond 777; tenth,
Noah saw 950, 600 before the Flood and 350 afterward; eleventh, Shem reached
only 600, ninety-eight before and 502 after the Flood, as is clear from Genesis
11:10–11.

All these patriarchs except four passed 900 years; of those four Enoch was
taken into heaven before death, and Mahalalel lacked only five years of 900.
Methuselah outlived Adam by twenty-nine years, Noah by nineteen, and Jared by
only five. The first nine patriarchs all knew one another personally: the last
of them, Lamech, Noah’s father, lived 113 years with Enoch before his
translation and sixty-six with Adam before his death. Methuselah, who reached
the year of the Flood—the fifth year after his son Lamech’s death—lived 600
years with his grandson Noah, ninety-eight with his great-grandson Shem, 300
with his father Enoch, 735 with his grandfather Jared, and 243 with Adam.
Enoch lived 308 years with Adam and was translated fifty-seven years after
Adam’s death. Noah did not see three patriarchs,

Adam, Seth, and Enos, for he was born four years after Seth’s death, seventy
after Enoch’s translation, and 126 after Adam’s death. Whatever disadvantage
he could have suffered from this was amply repaired by personal association
with his other six ancestors, to all of whom those three had been intimately
known not only by sight but in their whole character and manner of life. Thus
Noah could easily obtain certain and infallible knowledge of everything that
had happened from the creation to the Flood and entrust it to the patriarchs
after the Flood to pass on to their descendants; for Noah’s days extended to
Abraham’s fifty-eighth year.

We can also show in another, still more convenient way how all necessary
teaching was transmitted to posterity. Shem spent ninety-eight years with his
great-grandfather Methuselah, who had lived 243 years with Adam and many more
with all the others. Whatever Shem learned from him, he could teach as fully as
he wished not only to Abraham and Isaac but even to Jacob, for his life extended
to Jacob’s fifty-first year.

Remember that from the loins of the first ten patriarchs, among whom chiefly
the church of God before the Flood consisted, we and all others now breathing
on earth, and all who have breathed anywhere among the nations since the Flood,
were born. We may regard each and all of them as our own true parents. After
the Flood, and to some extent before, the trunk of the human race first divided
into three branches and soon into countless twigs. Yet to confirm friendship,
society, and union among all mortals now living, divine providence caused the
tree of the entire human race—whether now alive, alive since the Flood, or to
live until the conflagration—to grow for 1,500 years from one root into a
single trunk.

Very little can be said under the third head; nevertheless, take this. The
foremost event of the first epoch after the fall of very many angels was the
fall of Adam and Eve in Paradise and the most wretched ruin of the human race
that followed it. Yet God’s mercy began to repair it in Paradise itself by the
most gracious promise of the coming seed and the new administration of the
second covenant. But every theologian says so much about this matter, to whom
it properly belongs, that we may safely pass it by in a chronological work.

Driven from Paradise, Adam was compelled by God’s command to draw bread for
the support of life from the soil of the now-cursed earth through the hard
labors of agriculture. While he labored at this work, behold, in his firstborn
Cain the poison of sin erupted into the dreadful wound of fratricide. Boiling
with envy at his full brother Abel, a most upright and godly man, merely because
of his godliness and integrity, Cain brutally murdered him contrary to God’s
express prohibition. Subjected to a new curse, he and all his posterity then
plunged into every kind of crime.

Nevertheless, they flourished in worldly affairs with no less success than any
of the godliest. In gifts of intellect and inventions for the conveniences and
ornaments of human life they surpassed the very best. Cain himself, if not the
first author of architecture, was certainly the first builder of cities. Among
his descendants Jabal is celebrated as inventor of tents and huts; his brother
Jubal of the lyre, organ, and musical instruments; their other brother
Tubal-cain, a true Vulcan, of ironworking; and their father Lamech of poetry
and songs.

While Cain’s offspring were wholly occupied with inventions for the adornment
and pleasures of the present life, other cares engaged Seth’s godly line. After
Enosh was born to Seth, men began to invoke God’s name more publicly. All of
Adam’s godly offspring who recoiled from the Cainites’ impiety joined together
in ecclesiastical assemblies, openly professing and displaying godliness and
the worship of the divine majesty before the world. To confirm their faith,
Enoch, one of their number, was taken alive into heaven soon after Adam’s death,
so that no godly person would tremble too much at worldly dangers and death,
being assured by this example that one day in the flesh, through the power of
the coming Messiah’s resurrection, he would enjoy everlasting blessedness in
heaven after death.

These public assemblies and churches for divine worship were the best defenses
against the scandals and temptations of profane people. Yet Satan’s force and
cunning finally broke through such defenses. Enticed by the same allurements
of carnal pleasure, the sons of God and of the godly were drawn into marriages
with the wicked. Hence a most extensive slaughter of godliness, decency, and
every virtue fell upon the church itself; all mortals wallowed indiscriminately
in the mire of every vice, and one man, Noah, was found free from the common
contagion of crimes. Hence came the most just decree to destroy the human race,
with very few exceptions, by a deluge of waters. Yet so that divine mercy might
shine in the midst of wrath and just vengeance,

[Printed p. 4 | IA leaf 34 | access PDF 34 | OCR object 33]

120 years were granted to mortals for repentance.

During them Noah acted as an unceasing herald of righteousness. To give his
words greater authority, at God’s command and in everyone’s sight he undertook
a work of astonishing bulk: the construction of the ark, by far the largest
vessel ever to move on land or water. It equaled Solomon’s temple in height,
but was more than twice its width and more than five times its length: 300
cubits long, fifty wide, and thirty high. Its size was astonishing, especially
if one understands, as Scripture often does, sacred cubits, which many hold to
be twice the ordinary size. The temple, however, did not exceed sixty cubits
in length, twenty in width, and thirty in height.

In the three decks of this enormous ship there could comfortably be distributed
Noah’s little family and, among terrestrial and aerial animals—for fish were in
no danger from the waters—two of each unclean kind, male and female, and seven
of each clean kind, that is, fourteen, seven males and as many females; or only
seven, three males and three females, with the seventh for the sacrifice Noah
offered after the Flood. So great a number of animals, with a year’s supply of
food and drink, could be distributed comfortably in so large a vessel. But how
light or air could be admitted while the window was closed, how excrement could
be discharged throughout the year, and all water excluded, we certainly do
not know, though one may form various conjectures and guesses.

It should be observed that twenty years after Noah began to foretell and preach
the world’s destruction because of sin, he began to beget children, and in the
500th year of his life begot Shem, Ham, and Japheth. This does not mean that he
produced all three at once, but that he first begot them then and had begotten
none of them before. Japheth was first and before the others, though named last
because his descendants had little intercourse with God’s people in Moses’
days. Ham is expressly called Noah’s younger son, Genesis 9:19; and Shem, when
he begot Arphaxad two years after the Flood, is said to have been one hundred.
Thus in the hundredth year before the Flood, when Moses expressly says Noah
begot sons, Japheth must have been born before the younger Ham and fully two
years before Shem, who was born two years after the beginning of that hundredth
year.

Genesis 10:21 expressly says Japheth was older than Shem, though the most
distinguished interpreters, ancient and modern, speak differently about the
true sense of the passage. The old Latin version attributed to Jerome, which
all Roman Catholics venerate as authentic, reads *“Shem, the elder brother of
Japheth.”* It is said to have the Chaldean paraphrast as predecessor in this
interpretation; Montanus’s Latin translation in the Royal Bible renders it
*“the elder brother of Japheth,”* though the Chaldean itself, *achuchi de
Jephet Rabba*, is better rendered *“the brother of the elder Japheth.”* So too
the Septuagint, ἀδελφῷ Ἰάφεθ τῷ μείζονι, that is, *“brother of the elder
Japheth.”* Among moderns our older English follows the Latin: “Schem the elder
brother of Japhet,” but the newest corrects it: “Schem the brother of Japhet
the elder.” The newest Dutch and Italian do likewise, following Tremellius:
*“to Shem, brother of Japheth, the eldest by birth.”*

Amid such diversity of interpreters, what will a man ignorant of Hebrew do? He
must languish in uncertainty. But one skilled in Hebrew sees that *Achi Jephet
haggadol* must be rendered “brother of the elder Japheth,” not “the elder
brother.” Apart from the fact that the adjective must be construed with the
immediately preceding noun—which is *Japheth*, not *brother*—the adjective here
has the demonstrative prefix *he*, binding it still more strongly to the noun
immediately preceding. Even if some ambiguity remained in the Hebrew itself,
the study of chronology would very easily and certainly distinguish and
demonstrate it; for the preceding calculation shows that Japheth was two years
older than Shem. By this example I wished incidentally to demonstrate how
necessary the study both of Hebrew and chronology is for the theologian and
everyone handling Holy Scripture.

I shall add no more about the events of the first epoch after making two
observations. First, Josephus’s account of two pillars, one stone and one brick,
on which the patriarchs inscribed what they wished to transmit to posterity,
appears fabulous. They allegedly used stone against the force of the waters
which they heard would one day overwhelm the earth, and bricks against the
force of flames, by which they were warned the earth would one day perish. For
we read of no writing or engraving before God’s finger inscribed his law on
stone tablets, or at least not before Moses’ time.

Second, what Manetho wrote about the antiquity of the Egyptian dynasties must
be utterly fictitious and false. Apart from the fact that the beginning of
those dynasties long predates the world’s creation, we read of no empire before
the Flood except paternal or household rule;

[Printed p. 5 | IA leaf 35 | access PDF 35 | OCR object 34]

indeed, the Egyptian nation began long after the Flood. We do not deny that
before the Flood, or even soon after creation, Egypt and lands much farther
from Paradise were filled with peoples, since in Abraham’s days, only 300
years after the Flood, Egypt and very many regions in Africa, Europe, and Asia
were widely inhabited by teeming nations descended from Noah alone. Yet we
maintain that Egypt’s dynasties cannot claim an age earlier than the nation’s
origin, when Egypt received its name and first settlers after the Flood. The
origin of that nation is placed in Genesis 10 in Mizraim, son of Ham and
grandson of Noah, who was born with all his brothers some time after the Flood.

## CHAPTER II

### Containing questions and difficulties concerning the preceding chapter

It is asked first how it may be demonstrated that, according to Scaliger,
3,950 years—neither more nor fewer—elapsed from the creation to Christ’s birth.
Answer: although chronologists usually answer that question here, both question
and answer should be postponed from the beginning to the end of the first
universal epoch. Once all parts of that period have been traversed, the manner
in which the total is composed will readily be demonstrated. To discuss at the
very outset difficulties touching every part of this period, middle and last
as well as first, would be the plainest irrelevance and disorder. Let each,
then, be relegated to its own place.

### QUESTION II

#### In what season the world was created

Second, it is asked in what season the world was created. I answer: it is
agreed that the sun and moon were created at some fixed position in the Zodiac,
either at one of the equinoxes, vernal or autumnal, or one of the solstices,
winter or summer. Though in a circle and the planets’ circular motions there
is truly no beginning, end, or middle, and nothing settled and fixed—what in
one respect is conceived as beginning may in another be called middle and in
another end—nevertheless, in relation to particular regions and inhabitants
of the earth, those four parts of the Zodiac are rightly marked as settled and
fixed for various uses in human life.

There are few authorities and fewer arguments for the sun’s creation at the
summer or winter solstice. But the whole nation of chronologists, leaders and
common ranks alike, stands for the equinoxes, if not for the exact points then
at least for the seasons: many for spring, more and weightier authorities for
autumn. We follow the latter, relying on these arguments.

First, autumn is the season when trees are laden with fruit; but when the world
was created the trees were laden with fruit, as appears from the account of the
fall in Paradise immediately after creation.

Second, the world was created and its first year began at the season when the
Israelites began their year before leaving Egypt. For in Egypt the Israelites
followed the reckoning of time learned from their fathers Jacob, Isaac, and
Abraham; these in turn were taught by their teachers and ancestors Shem, Noah,
and the other patriarchs back to Adam. It is thus very likely that Adam began
his year from his creation at the season when Abraham and his descendants began
theirs. That before leaving Egypt the Israelites began their year not in spring
but in autumn is clear from Exodus 23:16, where the feast of gathering all the
crops from the fields is said to be celebrated at the year’s end, and 34:22,
where that feast of Ingathering, Weeks, or Tabernacles is called the solemnity
when at the return of the year’s season everything is stored. Elsewhere this
feast is said to be celebrated on the fifteenth day of the Hebrews’ seventh
month, Tishri, whose first days fall in the Romans’ September and later ones in
October, the whole thus in autumn, which in the cited passages is said to close
and open the year. This seventh month was originally first for the Hebrews.
Nisan, whose earlier portion corresponded to Roman March and later to April,
was then seventh. But this seventh month Nisan, in which the Jews departed
Egypt, was by divine command made first of all months in sacred reckoning; and
Tishri, which before was first, was turned into the seventh, though Tishri
remained first and the year’s beginning in all civil computation, just as Nisan
became first in sacred computations after the exodus. The leading opponents
here, the Jesuits Petavius and Henry Philippson, do not deny this.

Third argument: the world was created, and Adam’s first year began, in the
season when the Jews’ sabbatical and jubilee years began. Every seventh year
was called sabbatical by the Jews, and the fiftieth after seven sevens—that is,
forty-nine years—was a jubilee. God appointed their beginning in autumn,

[Printed p. 6 | IA leaf 36 | access PDF 36 | OCR object 35]

after the sixth year’s harvest had been gathered. Cessation from all farming
then began in Tishri, the seventh sacred month; and Leviticus 25:9 expressly
appointed the jubilee trumpet’s blast on its tenth day. Had this differed from
the ancient Hebrew custom received from the first fathers, Moses would
doubtless have noted the change and its causes, as he did when God altered the
sacred computation by moving Nisan from seventh to first, to honor the exodus.

Fourth, for many centuries the Jews have agreed that the year originally began
in autumn. Thus the first synagogue reading, Genesis 1, was assigned to the
last Sabbath of the seventh month, both because their year then began and
because they believed the world was created then. The authoritative Chaldean
paraphrast on 3 Kings 8:2 says the autumn month was originally first. Egyptians,
Persians, and other Eastern peoples still begin in autumn; the ancient Etruscans
fixed their annual nail on the Ides of September. The opponents’ answer is so
feeble that I disdain to reply.

### QUESTION III

#### Whether Moses’ years equaled ours

Third, how can the years Moses assigns the patriarchs be shown to resemble and
not be far shorter than ours? Some construe them as months or quarters to avoid
granting the patriarchs longevity unknown today, but their calculations destroy
all chronology. We do not yet ask whether those years precisely equaled ours,
or whether they were lunar years eleven days shorter than solar ones, made up
by periodic intercalation. We need only repel those who turn biblical years
into months and uproot every chronological foundation.

First, Mosaic years had twelve months, each thirty or at least more than twenty
days; they therefore were not one, two, or three months but approximately our
years. Jeremiah 51:31 names the twenty-fifth day of the twelfth month; Genesis
8:14 the twenty-seventh of a month; and Genesis 7:11, 24 with 8:4 gives 150
days from the seventeenth of month two to the seventeenth of month seven,
exactly five thirty-day months—a plain proof to believers in Scripture’s truth.

Second, if patriarchal years were months or quarters, their longest lives would
not reach ages attained by many in our own and our fathers’ memory; Adam and
Methuselah would fall far short of one hundred.

Third, all Moses’ years must have one measure where he suggests no distinction.
Applying months or quarters to later patriarchs is ridiculous: Abraham, who
begot Isaac at one hundred, would be a decrepit child of nine or ten; most of
Jacob’s sons became fathers before twenty, Joseph just after thirty, making
them fathers at age two or three; and Jacob’s “deep old age” of 130 before
Pharaoh would be under thirteen. Enough of these absurdities.

### QUESTION IV

#### Whether the Fathers should be followed in ancient chronology

Fourth, why do so many learned Greek and Latin Fathers err so greatly in
counting years before the Flood, generally assigning that period more than
2,000?

[Printed p. 7 | IA leaf 37 | access PDF 37 | OCR object 36]

Answer: profound ignorance of Hebrew caused this and many other errors in
excellent men. Except Origen, Jerome, and a few others, the learned Fathers did
not go beyond the Septuagint, which scribal carelessness carried far from the
Hebrew source—even farther than today’s Greek Bible. The latter adds one
hundred to each patriarch’s age at fatherhood but subtracts it afterward, so
the total lives still agree fairly well with Hebrew. The Fathers’ copy adds
centuries to the total; Constantinople’s common copy adds 1,556 years. Hence
their errors, now undeniable even to boys drinking from the clear Hebrew
springs.

### Addition to Question IV

#### The Hebrew chronology defended against errors of the Greek version in the
antediluvian patriarchs’ years

I thought this old error buried until recent works of Louis Cappel, Jean Morin,
Isaac Vossius, Brian Walton, and others showed a powerful faction even in the
Reformed church striving to overthrow the purity and authority of the Hebrew
Bibles by imputing thousands of errors. Vossius attacks it haughtily in a
treatise appended last year to his father’s chronology; Walton does so more
modestly but earnestly at the end of his ninth Prolegomenon. This pains me,
because I esteem Walton’s splendid work on the London Polyglot and grieve that
so deserving a man is caught by the innovators.

Many Protestants and Catholics answered Cappel and Morin, above all Johannes
Buxtorf the younger, peerless in Hebrew; but he says nothing here because they
did not charge this error. Vossius and Walton added it, and, so far as I know,
none has replied; I therefore must oppose them according to my small measure.

Vossius’s words are harder than his arguments. He says Cappel’s *Critica Sacra*
reveals many Hebrew blemishes and gaps; readers should continue until their
cloud clears and they confess blindness in sunlight; the Samaritan rather than
Hebrew codex should be sacred; and he will conquer defenders of Hebrew purity
by arguments and witnesses. Such boasting promises many compelling proofs, but
he gives one argument and two authorities. From Augustine, *City of God* 15.12,
he argues that puberty bears a fixed ratio to total life: as it comes at fifteen
in a seventy-year life, it came around two hundred when men lived eight or nine
hundred. Hebrew codices placing fatherhood near one hundred must therefore be
falsified; Greek ones placing it near two hundred must be sound.

[Printed p. 8 | IA leaf 38 | access PDF 38 | OCR object 37]

Answer: so weighty a conclusion needed a weighty premise, lest a load on a
willow prop fall. Who judges its weakness better than Walton, who calls this
only Vossian argument a vain conjecture scarcely entering a sane mind?
Augustine ascribes it to absurd men who, denying longevity, made ten Mosaic
years one solar year and then added a hundred so fatherhood would fall at
sixteen or seventeen rather than five to seven. Augustine rejects this and
does not equate fatherhood with puberty. What impossibility is there in puberty
at two hundred when bodies were strongest? Adam was fit for fatherhood at
creation and begot Cain and Abel long before Seth. Augustine does not require
named patriarchs to be firstborn: *“Not the firstborn but those required by
the succession are recorded, so that one comes to Noah and then Abraham.”*
Nor is there a fixed ratio: Noah fathered at 500 [the witness elsewhere says
600], Mahalalel and Enoch at 65, Lamech at 182, Kenan at 70, Methuselah at 187;
yet Greek post-Flood patriarchs, though living half or a fifth as long, mostly
father at 130.

Vossius’s first authority is Josephus, who before the Flood follows Greek, not
Hebrew, years; hence he infers Josephus’s Hebrew codices agreed with Greek.
Answer: (1) Josephus follows Hebrew after the Flood, so if Vossius rejects him
there he cannot press him here. (2) His authority is small: Baronius, Vossius
and Walton’s leader here, prints “Josephus, a bad chronologist,” just as
Casaubon says of Baronius. (3) Petavius says Josephus’s total from creation to
Flood is 2,656 but his genealogies total only 1,660, a 996-year anachronism.
Vossius emends Greek “six hundred” to “sixteen hundred,” yet would also need
to alter “four hundred” to “eight hundred” against the best Geneva manuscripts.
(4) I will not call Josephus ignorant of Hebrew as learned men call Philo, but
he commonly cites Scripture through the Septuagint familiar to his Greek
audience. Chaldean paraphrases from his time nevertheless follow Hebrew.

Vossius’s other authority is *Bereshith Rabbah*, which says Adam did not know
Eve for 130 years after Abel’s death; hence Seth could not be begotten in
Adam’s 130th year. Answer: this proves nothing for 230; and Vossius admits the
book and this monstrous tale teem with fables worse than a fever dream. These
are his whole grounds for such boasting.

Walton first asserts the issue: Hebrew, not Greek, is corrupt. He says he does
not correct originals or compare translations with the Hebrew source, but
compares the Hebrew underlying the Septuagint with extant Hebrew, preferring
the former and its derivative versions. Answer: this is Cappel’s chief begging
of the question. That supposed Hebrew differs from today’s, neither appears nor
exists. They cannot prove it ever existed, but assume both that the Seventy
followed their Hebrew perfectly and that today’s Greek is precisely their
original deposited in Ptolemy’s library. Why grant this but not analogous
claims for Hebrew? Walton denies the Seventy were inspired prophets, though
Morin asserts it. Few think their version reached us uncorrupted; it is so
faulty that some attribute it to a later obscure Hellenist and Cleopatra’s,
not Ptolemy Euergetes’s, library. This is Ussher’s judgment, Britain’s foremost
critic of versions and originals; Walton should have answered rather than
accused him of calumny and untimely, false censure.

Bellarmine likewise calls the extant Septuagint so corrupted that it seems
another work—not merely to defend Trent’s Latin, but from manifest internal
corruptions, to which our Chamier adds more. Walton, Morin, and Cappel have not
answered them.

Walton argues Greek was corrupted neither accidentally, since this happens
often, nor deliberately, since there was no benefit and proposed motives are
vain. I answer the same fits Hebrew better. Augustine says Jews did not corrupt
their books, but Greek scribes deliberately corrupted this passage. Walton
objects that their reasons were vain; of course they were, since sound reasons
would make the alteration no corruption.

[Printed p. 10 | IA leaf 40 | access PDF 40 | OCR object 39]

Second, Walton says tampering with sacred Greek codices was too shameless and
would be exposed from Ptolemy’s autograph and many Hebrew copies by Jewish
priests and rulers. He cuts his own vines: this is truer of the more sacred,
accessible, and carefully guarded Hebrew.

Third, he rejects the claim that Egyptian translators lengthened the chronology
to please Egyptians, since even Greek dates fall short of Egyptian dynasties.
We need not defend their motive; yet it is not nothing, for Vossius prefers
Greek precisely because adding 1,600 years accommodates Egyptian dynasties
within creation, and Scaliger and Gerard Vossius even fit them after the Flood.

Fourth, omission is easier than addition, and Hebrew numerals once used small
single-letter signs easily omitted. Reply: (1) omitting many words in many
verses is not easier for a faithful copyist than adding them; (2) numerical
letters were no commoner in Hebrew than Greek; (3) all disputed Hebrew and
Chaldean texts use full words; (4) Hebrew, especially Samaritan, letters are
larger than Greek uncials; (5) whatever Jerome’s small handbook did, Jerome
and Augustine both correct corrupted Greek from Hebrew.

Fifth, Walton says Jews were careless custodians before Antiochus Epiphanes,
then became superstitiously exact; Pharisees chose readings most opposed to
the Septuagint they hated. Answer: reverence for Walton alone restrains my
indignation. Buxtorf never says custodians of God’s oracles corrupted them
worse than every version; Walton characteristically names no place. Yet we
accept that after Antiochus they preserved texts tenaciously. The interval
from Ezra and Nehemiah to Antiochus was too short for vast corruption,
especially after Eleazar supplied Ptolemy Euergetes an intact Hebrew copy. If
our codices are those of Antiochus’s time, they are those Christ and the
Apostles used without rebuking Pharisees or scribes. Others may not rebuke
them unless they prove them different, which Walton says is impossible.

[Printed p. 11 | IA leaf 41 | access PDF 41 | OCR object 40]

Nor did Walton prove Pharisaic hatred of the Septuagint. No variant supporting
his imagined choice appears in Walton’s Polyglot, Cappel’s *Critica*, or Morin.

Walton’s Josephus material was answered above. Greek writers after the
Septuagint and before Josephus add nothing: none saw or understood Hebrew; all
their Jewish chronology came from Greek and cannot prejudice unknown Hebrew.

His last argument is the universal church: East, West, and all Greek and Latin
Fathers but two deserted Hebrew for Greek. Answer: this Romanist weapon from
Baronius shows how far innovators press tradition against Hebrew authority.
From it they restore the whole hierarchy through the pope, defend Arminianism
against Augustine, install and venerate crucifixes and images, prayers for the
dead, saints’ invocation, and rejected Roman rites. Recent Leiden writings and
those of Grotius, Hammond, Percy, Forbes, and others reveal where authority
against Scripture’s sources leads. Prefer church to express Scripture and the
Reformed case against Rome is over.

Second, “universal consent” is false. Walton admits Jerome and Augustine—men
who defend the text from Scripture—against the world. Moreover Latin Bibles
have always followed Hebrew; is the West’s mind better gathered from a few
doctors than from Scripture perpetually read without censure? Origen gave the
East today’s Hebrew in his Hexapla, whose Septuagint column, Jerome testifies,
was conformed to it. The common Greek text varied greatly, explaining the
Fathers’ different totals observed by Petavius. Third, Fathers who followed
that adulterated Greek while neglecting Hebrew and the genuine Hebrew-consonant
Greek did so thoughtlessly; none who compared them can prefer a deviant version.
Fourth, Rome’s Martyrology and Breviary err in neglecting Hebrew and Latin
computation; Baronius defends it, but learned Catholics want correction.
Walton should not confirm a dying error or scandalize Catholics considering
Reformation.

Walton offers no more, only haughtily derides chronologists through the Jesuit
Spondanus as wasting labor in endless disputes. Differences are lamentable but
do not justify insulting the art or its masters, who largely agree from
Scripture in rejecting Walton’s chronology. Still fiercer is Petavius’s taunt
that they care not how false or absurd they are. It cannot touch grave scholars
who count 292 years from Flood to Abraham. Walton also says chronological
questions scarcely affect faith if textual authority remains; yet he stresses
a 1,600-year difference. Scripture containing so enormous an error could not
be from the God of truth.

Now for truth. First, uncontested Hebrew passages must not be disputed. Cappel
and Walton question too many places by variants; where none exist, attacking
Hebrew uproots all biblical authority. They admit no translation is authentic.
If even unvaried Hebrew is uncertain, no Bible is certain. The old Protestant-
Catholic dispute concerned Hebrew versus Latin authority; this removes
certainty from every Bible. What errorless divine Scripture or earthly rule of
truth remains? This sends many to the pope, more to Pyrrhonism and atheism,
leaving everyone to his mind as Satan desires. Walton’s huge catalogue has no
variant in our Genesis 5 passages.

Second, Hebrew passages followed by every ancient version except the
Septuagint should stand. Walton’s own Polyglot shows Chaldean, Samaritan,
Syriac, Arabic, and Latin agree with Hebrew against Greek, as do all modern
French, English, Dutch, Spanish, and Italian versions.

Third, Greek’s error is palpable. Its followers Josephus, Eusebius, Clement,
and Africanus disagree, implying variant Greek copies; moreover its Genesis 5
extends Methuselah fourteen or twenty years beyond the Flood without putting
him in the ark.

Walton angrily invokes Augustine, forgetting that Augustine says Greek must be
corrected from Hebrew. He saw three Greek and one Latin copy giving Methuselah
187 rather than 167 years before Lamech; does Walton follow him or correct
Hebrew from Greek? None of those copies survives, and Walton’s own best Greek
reads 165. A few cannot outweigh thousands.

Fourth, preferring Greek adds 1,500 years and ruins sacred chronology,
astronomical dates, and all modern calculations, enlarging disputes over days
or years into fifteen centuries. Fifth, friendship should have restrained both
men: Vossius’s father Gerard calls Greek absurd unless corrected by Hebrew;
Cappel, Walton’s praised friend, attacked many Hebrew places but left Genesis
5 untouched.

### QUESTION V

#### How long Noah remained in the ark

Answer: a full solar year. Genesis 7:11, 13 places entry on day seventeen of
month two in Noah’s 600th and the world’s 1,656th year; 8:15 orders exit on day
twenty-seven of month two next year. The parts agree: forty days’ rain, waters
prevailing to day 150—five thirty-day months—plus thirty days of month one and
sixteen of month two. The ark rested on Ararat as waters began decreasing,
when fifteen cubits still covered the peaks; its thirty-cubit hull could easily
draw fifteen. Latin need not add ten days before it rested.

[Printed p. 14 | IA leaf 44 | access PDF 44 | OCR object 43]

From month seven’s seventeenth to month ten’s, waters fell until peaks appeared.
Forty days later, on month eleven’s tenth, Noah opened the window and sent a
raven, which returned but did not reenter. After waiting vainly he sent the
dove in month twelve; after it stayed away on its third mission, he removed the
cover on New Year’s Day but did not step out until commanded on month two’s
twenty-seventh. The interval is a full year plus eleven days, which some add to
the lunar year then used by Jews to equal the solar; yet even a solar year of
twelve thirty-day months is at least five days shorter than ours.

## CHAPTER III

### Events from the Flood to Israel’s exodus from Egypt

This second epoch contains 797 years. Arphaxad, born to Shem two years after
the Flood, begot Salah at 35; Salah begot Eber at 30; Eber begot Reu at 32;
Reu begot Nahor at 30; Nahor begot Terah at 29; Terah begot Abraham at 70;
Abraham at 75 was called from Haran in Mesopotamia to Canaan. These total 367;
added to 1,656 they place Abraham’s migration and promise of the blessed seed
in world-year 2,023. Galatians 3:17 from Exodus 12:40 gives 430 years from then
to liberation.

The latter is also nearly reconstructed: twenty-five years to Isaac’s birth,
sixty to Jacob’s, and 130 to Jacob’s Egyptian descent total 215. After Jacob’s
death Joseph lived seventy-one years; Moses was eighty at exodus, totaling 151,
leaving sixty-four between Joseph’s death and Moses’ birth, compatible with
the ages of Levi (134), Kohath (133), and Amram (137). Thus 367 plus 430 is 797,
and with the Flood’s 1,656 makes 2,453 completed years, so exodus falls in
world-year 2,456.

Little else is memorable beyond Moses. Even with this epoch scarcely ended the
age called “unwritten,” that of Cecrops and others, and the “unknown” age.

[Printed p. 15 | IA leaf 45 | access PDF 45 | OCR object 44]

An ancient rabbinic maxim says the world lasts 6,000 years then burns: 2,000
empty, 2,000 law, 2,000 Messiah. The first description is so true that learned
pagans found little for centuries after year 2,000, better calling it *tohu*,
empty, obscure, and unknown. They called many later centuries mythical, full
of ancestors’ fables, and began history only after nearly all God’s prophets
died. Egyptians rightly told visiting Greek philosophers that compared with
the Jews, the oldest Greek, Latin, and pagan writers were children of late ages.

After Noah left the ark, sacrificed for deliverance, and received renewal of
God’s natural covenant with people and animals, sealed by the rainbow, his
first deed was a great fall. Drinking too freely the new, perhaps unknown fruit
of the vine, the venerable man lay indecently on the ground, mocked by his
offspring. Behold human depravity and even the best men’s ingratitude! This was
not immediately after the Flood, for Ham’s fourth son Canaan, unborn in the
ark, was old enough to join his father in mocking his grandfather and drawing
the curse upon himself.

Later fabulists made Noah Janus, named from *Janus’s wine* as first vintner,
with two faces because he saw both worlds. Humanity multiplied rapidly. From
the ark in Armenia the three continents were assigned, by oracle, counsel,
lot, or choice: chief Asia to Shem, Africa to Ham, Europe to Japheth. Scripture
is silent about America, though equal to the rest in size, riches, and people.
Europe and Africa have privileges, but Asia surpasses both in sky and soil;
it fell to the best, though not eldest, son.

Firstborn normally have privilege, but free grace often passes them over:
Abel then Seth; Shem; Shem’s third son Arphaxad; and likewise Isaac, Jacob,
Judah, Joseph, Moses, David, and Solomon were not firstborn.

Genesis 10 gives national origins. Japheth’s sons: Gomer, through Ashkenaz
ancestor of Germans, Gauls, and Britons, and through Riphath and Togarmah of
Tatars and Muscovites; Magog of northern China or Scythia and some Tatars and
Muscovites; Madai of Medes; Javan of Greeks; Tubal of Spaniards and Italians;
Meshech of Cappadocians and perhaps Turks; Tiras of Thracians. Ham’s Cush took
Babylonia, Arabia, and Ethiopia; Mizraim Egypt, and his Philistines Palestine;
Put Libya and Mauritania; Canaan his known seats until Israel extirpated his
cursed seed. Shem’s Elam fathered Persians, Asshur eastern Assyrians, and
Arphaxad’s grandson Eber had through Joktan thirteen sons, two of whom

[Printed p. 16 | IA leaf 46 | access PDF 46 | OCR object 45]

settled Havilah in India, Ophir, the Moluccas, and Philippines; thence peoples
spread to Japan and North America, then near northeast Asia.

Great families colonized especially around Peleg’s birth and Babel, when the
one language split—precisely seventy-two mother tongues I dare not affirm.
Nimrod seized unprecedented political rule over part of the scattered crowd
and founded Babylon’s first monarchy. Son of Cush, grandson of Ham—while Moses
also makes Asshur founder of Nineveh—he is pagan Belus and Assyrian Jupiter.
His small power grew until his son Ninus and daughter-in-law turned wife
Semiramis overran Asia.

If shadowy ancients were trusted, this empire began in world-year 1,718, year
62 after the Flood. Diodorus from Ctesias gives Assyria 1,360 years to
Sardanapalus and 313 more to Cyrus; subtracting 1,673 from Cyrus’s world-year
3,391 yields 1,718. Simplicius says Aristotle wrote that Callisthenes learned
Babylon had stood 1,903 years; subtracting these from its capture by Alexander
in 3,619 yields 1,716, nearly the same.

This is hard to digest. Nimrod’s authority followed Babel and linguistic
dispersion, which followed Peleg’s birth in year 101 after the Flood; prudent
chronologists defer it at least to year 190. Even if Nimrod then reigned
fifty-five years and Ninus fifty-two, to Abraham’s ninth year, it is scarcely
credible Noah’s descendants already supplied the immense armies reported. Let
alone Semiramis’s hosts, whence came Ninus’s 1,700,000 infantry, 200,000 horse,
and 60,000 scythed chariots against Zoroaster of Bactria? More in the Additions.

Historians told so much of Belus, Ninus, and Semiramis that almost nothing
remained of successors. Semiramis’s extraordinary prowess eclipsed men for
centuries. Her son Ninyas and thirty-three emperors to Sardanapalus lived as
half-men in women’s quarters, secure behind her victories, except two named
Balius who showed sparks of manhood. Some make Ninyas the king of Shinar,
chief of four kings Abraham defeated.

In this unknown, empty period, excluding fable leaves only revelation. Eusebius
collected ancient Assyrian, Egyptian, Sicyonian, and Argive king lists,

[Printed p. 17 | IA leaf 47 | access PDF 47 | OCR object 46]

but beyond names almost nothing remains; worms, Goths, Vandals, and devouring
time consumed it. We will not waste hours on useless nomenclature, though some
fill pages with pretty ancient fables.

In our fathers’ age Annius of Viterbo produced “Berossus the Chaldean,” used by
Funck and others, to great applause. Inspection turned the treasure to coals:
beyond genuine fragments all was Annius’s stale monastic fraud, hissed away by
scholars. As Italian painters and sculptors sold new works as ancient finds, so
this dealer forged his new-old Berossus. Idle cloistered men, especially
Jesuits, often offered products of their brains as ancient authors’ offspring,
usually ridiculously; criticism now has such sharp midwives that even supreme
artifice rarely deceives them.

Most post-Flood patriarchs died around Abraham’s promise of Messiah. From Adam
to Abraham the church and covenant descended in one direct line of twenty, but
before Abraham’s migration the promise was tied to no stock. It was then
applied to Abraham’s descendants, made Isaac’s possession and then Jacob’s,
remaining unlimited within twelve tribes until David, apart from Jacob’s Shiloh
prophecy; with David Messiah was restricted to his loins, Psalm 132:11, while
otherwise the promise remained free until the Virgin’s conception.

The patriarchs’ deaths cluster around Abraham’s 75th. Peleg died at 239 in
Abraham’s 48th; Nahor two years later at 148, seven years after Noah, in
Abraham’s 57th; Terah at 205 in Abraham’s 75th; then Reu at 239, Serug at 230,
and Arphaxad at 438. Shem, Salah, and Eber outlived Abraham: Shem to 600,
twenty-five years past Abraham, Isaac’s 111th and Jacob’s 51st; Salah one year
past Abraham at 433; Eber to 464 years after the Flood, Jacob’s 79th and
Isaac’s 139th. Isaac followed thirty-seven years later at 180, Jacob’s 120th,
Joseph’s 29th, world-year 2,228.

[Printed p. 18 | IA leaf 48 | access PDF 48 | OCR object 47]

Amid life’s miseries these patriarchs were greatly comforted by holy fellowship
and near hope of the approaching Messiah, their common confidence and true
spring of life amid death, promised from their own loins to bless nations.
These calculations chiefly follow Calvin; Cappel and others differ little
except over Terah, though minor disagreement is not worth pursuing here.

Melchizedek then reigned at Salem by Jordan, not Jerusalem at Zion. Inquiry
into his identity is futile, since the Spirit mystically hid his genealogy as
a type of Messiah’s ineffable generation. Noah died before Abraham defeated
the kings; Shem and Eber lived long after and surpassed Eastern kings in
holiness and authority, but their known lives and deaths exclude them. Men must
stop exposing what God hid.

Sodom and neighboring cities were destroyed by sulfurous fire, their fertile
region turned into the Asphalt Lake, an enduring example of divine vengeance
on lust.

At 77, Isaac being 137 and Abraham dead sixty-two years, Jacob fled, saw
Bethel’s ladder, and served Laban twenty years. He begot eleven patriarchs in
a few years: no more than six separate Reuben, born in year eight of service,
from Joseph, the eleventh (not Calvin’s sixth), born in year fourteen. After
Joseph and Dinah he stayed only six more years; on returning Joseph was six,
Reuben scarcely twelve, Judah ten.

At the Egyptian descent Jacob was 130, Joseph thirty-nine, Judah forty-three.
Judah then had two grandsons or quasi-great-grandsons through Perez, son of his
daughter-in-law Tamar; still more striking, Benjamin at twenty-three had ten
sons. This likely came through polygamy rather than premature marriage, though
that too was common.

There were 215 years from descent to exodus. Ephraim was about five at descent;
Joshua was born fifty-three years before exodus and died at 110 after forty
desert and seven Canaan years. Thus at most 169 years lay between Ephraim and
Joshua, yet 1 Chronicles 7:27 gives ten generations: Shelah, Beriah, Rephah,
Telah, Tahan, Ladan, Ammihud, Elishama, Nun, Joshua—requiring fatherhood by
seventeen. If Chronicles means two Shelahs, Joshua becomes eighth from
Ephraim,

[Printed p. 19 | IA leaf 49 | access PDF 49 | OCR object 48]

but then its sequence of eight generations before Beriah leaves scarcely
eighty years for nine after him, impossible even with earliest marriages.
Thus Shelah must be one, first from Ephraim, slain after fathering Bered and
apparently after Joseph died, since only Ephraim’s brothers comforted him. In
any case Beriah’s birth after Shelah’s slaughter and after Ephraim’s grandson
Bered shows very early marriage in Joseph’s family.

God governed Jacob and Isaac very differently. Jacob was tossed all life by
cares: flight from Esau, twenty years’ labor under Laban, pursuit by Laban and
threat from Esau, Simeon and Levi’s slaughter after Dinah, danger from
neighbors, Rachel’s unexpected death, Reuben’s incest, Joseph’s supposed
mauling, Er and Onan’s deaths, Judah and Tamar, famine, and Simeon’s detention.
After these waves he reached Egypt’s harbor and rested seventeen years in
Joseph’s bosom.

Isaac, apart from Ishmael’s childhood mockery and Abraham’s knife, flourished
to forty, then received noble and beautiful Rebekah without labor and lived
with her in concord. At sixty he had Jacob and Esau, his chief later cares. He
saw Ishmael die peacefully at 130 with twelve princely sons; saw Esau rule
widely and Jacob’s family prosper. Yet family tragedies and blindness imprisoned
him from 137 to 180. He died ten years before Jacob and eleven after Joseph’s
Egyptian descent.

Job seems to have lived then. Philo’s claim that he married Jacob’s daughter
Dinah lacks a good witness, but theologians commonly and plausibly put Job
before the exodus. After Jacob’s descent came the Greek floods of Ogyges in
Attica and Deucalion in Thessaly, and Phaethon’s predicted Ethiopian drought
and seeming conflagration. Cecrops is made Moses’ contemporary, yet one of the
first kings of Attica’s indigenous inhabitants.

No more about events, since they are either sufficiently known from sacred
history or fictitious and fabulous in profane history.

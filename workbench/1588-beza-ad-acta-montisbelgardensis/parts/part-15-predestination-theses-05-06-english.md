Thesis V of the Württemberg Doctors

The question, however, is whether God so predestined his elect to eternal life that, before they were born, he destined certain people—and indeed the greatest part of humanity—to eternal destruction and condemnation by an absolute and eternal decree, so that he does not will them to repent, be converted, and be saved.

Thesis VI of the Württemberg Doctors

We believe and confess that no hidden decree of God can be demonstrated from Scripture by which, without regard to any unworthiness and merely by the pleasure of his will, he destined anyone—much less the greatest part of the human race—to eternal condemnation, so that he does not will them to repent, be converted, and be saved.

Antithesis V and VI, Theodore Beza

We affirm what is denied here: that both vessels of wrath and vessels of mercy were destined from eternity. We do so not only because the relation of contraries is the same and, as we have said, the very word *election* proves it, but also because it is declared in God’s express Word, both in no few other passages and expressly by the Apostle in Romans 9:11. So far is this decree of God from being open to anyone’s charge of injustice that God would not have been unjust even if he had predestined no one to salvation, since we are all born children of wrath and he is debtor to no one.

Next, we say that the condemnation of those who by that eternal decree of God are left in their corruption is not rightly attributed to this decree. Although what God decreed cannot fail to happen, and therefore those who perish do not perish apart from this decree of God, nevertheless the cause of the decree’s execution—that is, of the condemnation of those who perish—is not that decree of God, but their native corruption in which they were born and the fruits of that corruption, from which it pleases God to deliver only his own, predestined to salvation. The matter itself proves that the number of those who perish has always been and remains very great. The Lord himself shows it when he says that many are called but few chosen, and that few enter through the narrow gate. Finally, God’s not willing them to be converted and saved must be understood in this way: not as though they will it and God resists their desire, but because they themselves do not will to be converted and cannot even will it, having been justly left in their impenitence.

1. Vessels of wrath are not made and prepared by God or his absolute will, but by the devil and their own will. Consequently they are destined to eternal condemnation by God’s own just judgment, though he never willed their condemnation. Therefore they perish by their own will against God’s will.

2. The relation is not the same here. For the one and only cause of the destruction of the ungodly is the devil and their own evil will. But the cause of the godly’s eternal salvation is God’s will revealed in Christ, as the voice from heaven declares: “This is my beloved Son, in whom I am well pleased; HEAR HIM” (Matthew 17), to whom the faithful and elect submit themselves.

3. The word *election* by no means proves it, because Holy Scripture nowhere teaches an absolute decree of God by which he created particular people for condemnation.

4. In Romans 9 Paul indeed teaches that believers are saved by God’s grace and mercy, but not by ABSOLUTE MERCY—rather, by mercy declared and exhibited in Christ. He teaches nothing, however, about an absolute justice of God by which he created and ordained the greatest part of the human race for eternal condemnation as persons whom he DOES NOT WILL to be saved.

5. God indeed could not be charged with injustice if he acted in this way; but Holy Scripture does not teach it. Rather, every page of sacred literature presses the contrary: namely, that God DOES NOT WILL THE DEATH OF ANY HUMAN BEING.

6. Those who are condemned are left in their corruption not by God’s eternal decree but because of impiety and contempt for the Word and the grace offered in Christ. Matthew 23: “How often I willed to gather your children, AND YOU REFUSED.”

7. This decree appears nowhere in Scripture. To speak in this way about the unrevealed God, overthrowing all consolation of the godly, is to set one’s mouth against heaven.

8. They said above that the first human beings sinned with God willing, and below it is said from their own mouth that man had to fall. How, then, would this hidden decree of God not be the cause of the execution of their condemnation? For if, according to their position, God had not willed them to sin, they would also have been free from this judgment and its decree. Whatever is the cause of a cause is also the cause of its effect.

9. The number of the condemned is indeed very great, but not from God’s absolute decree. For God wills no one to perish but everyone to be saved. Contempt for the grace offered in Christ and for his Word is the cause of destruction, which God does not will.

10. Because God does not pretend, but earnestly calls sinners to repentance, he therefore condemns no one by a hidden decree, but only those who oppose themselves to election as revealed in Christ: “Come to me, EVERYONE.”

11. This is a dreadful expression, found nowhere in Holy Scripture: that GOD BY AN ABSOLUTE WILL DOES NOT WILL human beings to be converted and saved.

12. This is a dreadful and abominable expression: that they neither will nor can be converted because they have been left in their impenitence by God’s hidden decree. It overthrows the whole doctrine of the gospel and the use of the sacraments, and takes away all consolation from troubled consciences.

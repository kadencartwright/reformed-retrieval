# On Certain Articles

This directory presents the complete Latin-form witness and a new English
translation of the consilium headed *Ad certa quaedam capita* in the *Calvini
Opera*. The body closes with the date 28 April 1556.

## Work and witness identity

| Field | Value |
|---|---|
| Historical responsibility | A French work attributed to John Calvin in CO |
| CO consilium | VII.4, *Ad certa quaedam capita* |
| Addressee | An unnamed “dearest brother” |
| Internal date | 28 April 1556 |
| Place | Not supplied; “this city” in one answer is not sufficient to insert a location |
| Language history | French original → Theodore Beza's Latin translation; the French wording has not been located here |
| Transcribed witness | Beza's Latin presentation printed by CO |
| Genre | Private pastoral counsel on financial and family cases, followed by exhortation |
| Topics | Interest-bearing loans and oppression; civil rate versus conscience; a third-party dispute; a widow and her children; a married woman's temporary flight from immediate danger; household piety |
| CO locator | X/1, pp. 264–266 |

CO attributes the work to Calvin and explicitly identifies the printed Latin
as Beza's translation from French. The edition therefore records the direction
French → Latin and does not call the Latin Calvin's own composition. The body
contains no recipient name, place, farewell, or signature.

This work must also be distinguished from CO consilium IV.4, *De
administratione ecclesiastica* (X/1, pp. 210–211). CO notes that an earlier
printing gave that separate document the generic title *Ad certa quaedam
capita*. Shared title wording does not make the two works identical; the
present edition is VII.4, beginning *Dilectissime frater, quia accidit ut
plurimum* and dated 28 April 1556.

## Source and transcription

The transcription follows Wilhelm Baum, Eduard Cunitz, and Eduard Reuss, eds.,
*Ioannis Calvini opera quae supersunt omnia*, vol. X, part 1 (*Corpus
Reformatorum* XXXVIII; Brunswick: C. A. Schwetschke, 1871), printed pp.
264–266. The volume is available through the University of Geneva's [complete
Calvin collection](https://archive-ouverte.unige.ch/unige:650) and as a [direct
volume X/1 PDF](https://access.archive-ouverte.unige.ch/access/metadata/f526a338-c95c-4f87-b600-04df723f502b/download).

The relevant openings were checked against 300-DPI renders of the UNIGE
facsimile:

| UNIGE PDF page | Printed CO pages | Text represented |
|---:|---:|---|
| 153 | 263–264 | Opening of VII.4 on p. 264, after the conclusion of VII.3 |
| 154 | 265–266 | Continuation and dated conclusion of VII.4 |

The downloaded PDF is 33,386,580 bytes and has SHA-256
`025973d6e4533769d0c2b870dd324497943bc14050eb2bcbcc4c964d4f2a1777`.
The transcription begins with *Dilectissime frater* and ends with the dated
body wording *re ipsa praestet. 28. April. 1556.* Every word in that interval
was collated against the page images. The CO heading, bibliographical footnote,
running heads, printed page numbers, terminal rule, and neighboring item are
excluded as editorial or collection matter.

CO's source note reads: “*Ex gallico versum Beza inseruit Tomo Epp.*” and
identifies the Geneva p. 354, Lausanne p. 370, Hanover p. 414, Chouet p. 487,
and Amsterdam p. 222 printings. CO gives no manuscript or surviving French
witness in this note and prints no variant apparatus within the body.

At the p. 264/265 turn the printer divides *fa-* / *ciente*. The normalized
Latin joins *faciente* and places the p. 265 marker before the complete word;
the English marker correspondingly precedes “by the Lord's action.” The p.
265/266 turn divides no word: it falls after *posterum* and before *enitendum*.

## Textual and interpretive cautions

- The legally fixed lending rate is not treated as automatic moral permission.
  The answer refuses a universal numerical rule and makes possible oppression
  of a poor debtor decisive.
- In *quem etiam fortasse ne audire quidem dignaretur*, the third party is the
  understood subject of *dignaretur*, but the referent of accusative *quem*
  is not beyond dispute. It most naturally points back to *me*, yet the
  English leaves “listen” without an object rather than turning that probable
  reading into an unmarked certainty.
- The widow who might take her children and the married woman considering
  flight are separate cases. In the second, *non animo divertendi a viro et
  illius deserendi* restricts the contemplated flight to avoiding immediate
  danger without an intention to separate permanently or desert the husband.
- The internal date is clear. No illegible word, supplied lacuna, or
  conjectural emendation was needed; the only page-divided word is documented
  above.

## Translation status and use

Mary D. Beaty and Benjamin Wirt Farley's copyrighted *Calvin's Ecclesiastical
Advice* (1991) translates the consilia collection. The project's bibliography
audit located no complete reusable public-domain English version of this
document. That modern translation was not consulted, and no wording from it
was used. [`english.md`](english.md) is a fresh translation from the normalized
Latin witness, not a reconstruction of the unlocated French.

This is an AI-assisted working edition checked against the complete
facsimile, not a peer-reviewed critical edition. Readers should preserve the
work/witness distinction, the title-identity caution, and the limits of the
surviving metadata.

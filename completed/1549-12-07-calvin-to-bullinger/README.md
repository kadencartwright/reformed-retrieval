# Calvin to Heinrich Bullinger, Letter 1324 (7 December 1549)

This directory presents the complete normalized Latin and a fresh English
translation of John Calvin's letter 1324 to Heinrich Bullinger. Calvin writes
about subscription to the agreement associated with the *Consensus
Tigurinus*, a supplement to a book on avoiding superstitions, the suppressed
ministerial colloquies at Lausanne, the rejection of François Hotman as a
deacon, and Martin Bucer's complaint against John Hooper.

> **Editorial status:** This is an AI-assisted draft transcription and
> translation checked line by line against high-resolution facsimile images,
> not a peer-reviewed critical edition. A qualified Neo-Latinist should
> independently collate it before formal publication or reliance on a
> disputed reading.

This is a letter, not a separate treatise or a copy of the *Consensus
Tigurinus*. The formula of agreement is discussed but not reproduced. Calvin
says that all his Genevan colleagues embraced it and offers to send a copy
signed by every one of them. The distinction matters because the signatures
and the formula are external documentary objects, while the promise to obtain
them is part of letter 1324.

## Edition, witness, and facsimile

The transcription follows:

> Wilhelm Baum, Eduard Cunitz, and Eduard Reuss, eds., *Ioannis Calvini opera
> quae supersunt omnia*, vol. XIII, *Corpus Reformatorum* XLI, *Thesauri
> epistolici Calviniani* IV (Brunswick: C. A. Schwetschke and Son, 1875),
> letter 1324, pp. 488–490.

CO states that its base witness is Calvin's autograph in the Zurich archives,
Pluteus VI, vol. 166, formerly *Epistolae* tome 24, fol. 14. Its editors also
list five copies: two in Geneva Codex 111 (Turretini's at fol. 242 and a much
later one at fol. 208), two in the Bern correspondence volumes (VI, p. 66,
and VII, p. 69), and one in Simler, vol. 71. `latin.txt` follows CO's edited
main text, not a new independent transcription of the Zurich autograph.

The public source is the University of Geneva's [complete *Calvini Opera*
collection](https://archive-ouverte.unige.ch/unige:650) and its [direct CO
XIII PDF](https://access.archive-ouverte.unige.ch/access/metadata/41532070-e825-447b-bbc7-584bb1347fa5/download).

## Authorial and editorial matter

The complete authorial text begins with the address *Clarissimo viro D.
Henricho Bullingero* and includes the abbreviated greeting *S.*, every body
paragraph, Calvin's Geneva dateline, and the subscription *Ioannes Calvinus
tuus*. The CO letter number and heading, italic synopsis, parenthetical witness
description, running heads, page numbers, footnote cues, and four footnotes
are excluded from `latin.txt`.

The excluded apparatus is documented as follows:

| Cue in Calvin's text | CO editorial information |
|---|---|
| after *magnam habeo gratiam* (p. 488) | A Bullinger letter appears to have been lost, leaving the immediately following allusions more obscure than the editors could resolve from other evidence. |
| after *priusquam longius spargeretur* (p. 488) | Cross-reference only: CO VI 641, for the material connected with the book on avoiding superstitions. |
| after *libellum vertit* (p. 489) | The unnamed translator called himself *Franciscus Villerius*, but CO identifies him as François Hotman, Seigneur de Villiers-Saint-Paul; the note adds a Simler anecdote, a 1700 biographical reference, and information about Hotman's father and siblings. |
| after *postremis literis* (p. 489) | Cross-reference to letter 1240, Bucer's letter to Calvin; that text and its apparatus identify Hopper as John Hooper and preserve the underlying ubiquity accusation. |

CO's synopsis likewise names the subjects—acceptance of the agreement,
Bullinger's addition to the book, the Lausanne colloquies, Hotman's rejection,
and Bucer's false denunciation by Hopper—but it is a nineteenth-century
summary rather than Calvin's opening paragraph.

## Files and collation

- [`latin.txt`](latin.txt) — complete diplomatic-normalized Latin of the
  letter with exact `[CO XIII, p. N]` markers.
- [`english.md`](english.md) — complete new English translation with matching
  locators and translator's notes.

The PDF text layer served only as a provisional aid. Every line of letter 1324
was manually checked against 600-DPI, 5000-by-6300-pixel renders of PDF pages
253–254. In accordance with
[`TRANSLATION_POLICY.md`](../../TRANSLATION_POLICY.md), printer wrapping is
removed, line-end divisions are joined, paragraph indentations are preserved,
and CO spelling and punctuation remain controlling. This includes *librarii*,
*quum*, *senatusconsulto*, *Zebedaeus*, *literis*, *Hoppero*, and the
unexpanded *Do.*

The only printed-page turn that divides a word is *salu-tari* at pp. 489–490.
The normalized text joins it as *salutari* and places the p. 490 marker before
the whole word. No reading remains illegible or conjecturally supplied.

## Translation and textual notes

The opening is necessarily less certain in reference than the rest of the
letter because the Bullinger letter on which it depends is lost. In
particular, *librarii censura*, the short deferred writing, and the fair cannot
be identified more precisely from letter 1324 alone. The English consequently
uses the cautious “printer's criticism” and explains the limitation rather
than inventing a title or event.

Other compressed expressions are preserved or annotated rather than silently
flattened: *in calculum ... veniat* uses the metaphor of entering a reckoning;
*heriles minas* casts the Bernese threats in master-and-servant terms;
*similes delitias* calls protected figures like Zebedaeus “similar
favorites”; and *traduci* describes Bucer's public exposure to reproach. CO
prints *Do. etiam Henrichum* without identifying the second Henry, so the
translation expands only the probable honorific (“Master”), not the person's
identity.

The English was translated afresh from `latin.txt` and checked against the
same images. No modern English translation was consulted or used as a source
for its wording.

RESPONSE.

“The production of things, insofar as it stands on God’s side,” he says, “is measured by eternity.”
But I say that, on your opinion, the production of things is measured by eternity not only as it is
from God but also as it is from secondary causes. I therefore do not grasp what the other member of
that distinction is. For Navarrete is accustomed to complete his distinctions in this fashion.
Again, he does not wish it to be conceded absolutely that God produces things in eternity, thereby
implying that this can nevertheless be conceded, though not absolutely. But he conceals whether it
can be conceded relatively or conditionally. I would gladly ask how things come to exist in eternity
if God does not produce them in eternity. It does not arise from the nature of things that they
exist in eternity, for this belongs only to things future, present, and past. Yet all things outside
God are naturally merely possible.

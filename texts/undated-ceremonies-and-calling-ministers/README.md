# On Ceremonies and the Calling of Ministers

This directory presents the complete French response printed under the CO
heading *Sur les ceremonies et la vocation des ministres*, together with a
fresh English translation made directly from the French.

> **Editorial status:** This is an AI-assisted draft transcription and
> translation checked line by line against 600-DPI facsimile images. It is not
> a peer-reviewed critical edition. A qualified specialist in sixteenth-century
> French should independently collate the manuscript witnesses before formal
> publication or reliance on an anomalous reading.

## Work identity

| Field | Value |
|---|---|
| Author | John Calvin; the response closes with his name |
| CO title | *Sur les ceremonies et la vocation des ministres*; supplied as the collected heading, not repeated in the signed body |
| Date | 25 December 1557, stated in the French close |
| Place | Not stated |
| Original language | French |
| Genre | Ecclesiastical counsel concerning ceremonies, feast days, and irregular ministerial appointments |
| Primary reported witness | Bibliothèque de Genève, Cod. 107, fol. 68, an old copy according to CO |
| Additional reported witness | The French conclusion copied again in Geneva Cod. 108, fol. 61v |
| Printed locator | CO X/1, pp. 208–210 |

The response first uses the collective “we” when describing imperfections that
must be borne and what “lies in us,” then repeatedly uses Calvin's individual
“I,” and concludes with the date and *Iehan Calvin*. Those shifts are retained;
the work is not recast as either an anonymous corporate minute or a uniformly
private letter.

## French witness and incomplete Latin parallel

The transcription follows Wilhelm Baum, Eduard Cunitz, and Eduard Reuss,
eds., *Ioannis Calvini opera quae supersunt omnia*, vol. X, part 1, *Corpus
Reformatorum* XXXVIII (Brunswick: C. A. Schwetschke, 1871), pp. 208–210.

CO identifies Geneva Cod. 107, fol. 68 as an old copy. It also reports that
Simler copied the French in his vol. 91 from a Bern archival copy which the CO
editors could no longer locate. The concluding portion absent from the Latin
was, according to CO, copied a second time in Geneva Cod. 108, fol. 61v. This
edition has collated CO's printed facsimile, not those manuscripts.

CO says the Latin printed in earlier *Epistolae et responsa* editions is a
free translation of only part of the text. Its p. 209 apparatus marks the
point after *en façon si confuse* where that Latin stops, while noting that the
Latin adds one further sentence about establishing sound doctrine and refusing
fraternity to those who tear it down. The French then continues with Calvin's
complaint that doctrine has been omitted, his contrast between scruples about
feast-day preaching and silence about election, providence, and the
sacraments, and his dated signature. Thus the historical Latin is neither a
complete witness nor the source of [`english.md`](english.md).

## Completeness, normalization, and apparatus

[`french.txt`](french.txt) begins with the internal topic line *Sur le poinct
des ceremonies* on p. 208 and ends with the signature *Iehan Calvin* on p.
210. It contains every word of the French body, including the date. It excludes
the CO heading, title-note cue, witness note, running heads, printed page
numbers, the two Latin-translation footnotes on p. 209, the preceding counsel,
and the following counsel.

Printer line-end divisions such as *con-formité*, *ediffi-cation*,
*imperfec-tions*, and *interroga-toires* are joined. Neither printed-page turn
divides a word: p. 208 ends *a tous* and p. 209 begins *bons et fideles*; p. 209
ends *de la doctrine* and p. 210 begins *Car c'est*. The English markers retain
those exact page changes even where the first divides a sentence.

Historical forms such as *poinct*, *toutesfoys*, *pource*, *assubietir*,
*advouer*, *advoüent*, *estez*, *recongnoissent*, *ouy*, *sestre*, and
*prescheurs* are preserved. Two conspicuous printed irregularities are also
retained rather than silently corrected:

- *Diou* in *la parole de Diou*;
- *en façon si. confuse*, including the unexpected point after *si*.

The English renders the contextual sense and calls attention to these forms in
its notes. It does not claim to establish what Calvin or the copyist originally
wrote behind CO's printing.

## Translation independence

Mary D. Beaty and Benjamin Wirt Farley published a copyrighted translation in
*Calvin's Ecclesiastical Advice* (1991). It was not consulted or used for the
wording here. The English was translated afresh from the complete normalized
French and checked against the facsimile; the free, incomplete historical
Latin supplied no translation wording.

Suggested citation:

> John Calvin, *Sur les ceremonies et la vocation des ministres*, 25 December
> 1557, French response, *Calvini Opera* X/1 (1871), 208–210, normalized French
> and draft English translation, *Reformed Retrieval*.

# William Ames, *Bellarminus enervatus*, TOME III (1632; bound issue 1633)

> **Translation preparation:** This directory contains an AI-assisted,
> diplomatic-normalized Latin transcription and an independent close English
> translation. Neither text has been independently reviewed. A qualified
> editor must compare both files with the facsimile before quotation or
> publication.

## Work and edition

- **Author:** William Ames (Guilielmus Amesius, 1576–1633)
- **Title:** *Bellarminus enervatus, sive Disputationes Anti-Bellarminianae*
- **Tome:** TOME III
- **Separate title-page imprint:** London, Humphrey Robinson, 1632
- **Issue represented by this workbench path:** the four-part London issue
  cataloged 1633; its first tome is dated 1633 and tomes II–IV have separate
  1632 title pages
- **Opening subject:** the nature, definition, causes, and effects of the
  sacraments

## Controlling public-domain witness

The controlling witness is the Lyon Public Library copy digitized by Google
Books as [`mhdPfwYZv0YC`](https://books.google.com/books?id=mhdPfwYZv0YC),
cataloged as volume 3 and available in full view. The 1632 TOME III title page,
dedication, and printed pagination govern this transcription; OCR is used only
as an aid and never over the page image.

The bibliographical relation of this separately dated tome to the 1633 issue
was checked against the freely accessible Koninklijke Bibliotheek copy at
Internet Archive, item
[`ned-kbn-all-00002889-001`](https://archive.org/details/ned-kbn-all-00002889-001),
whose catalog record states that part I is dated 1633 and parts II–IV are dated
1632. That copy has the same TOME III pagination and text.

## Editorial method

Long *s*, ligatures, line-end divisions, and typographic `u/v` are normalized.
Edition spelling and Ames's numbering are otherwise retained. Running heads,
signatures, catchwords, scan notices, and non-authorial marks are omitted.
Printed-page markers are retained in both files in an identical sequence.
Where the end of chapter II shares printed page 14 with the opening of chapter
III, Part 01 stops before the `CAP. III` heading. Likewise, where Book II and
Book III share printed page 59, Part 08 stops before `LIBER TERTIUS` and Part
09 begins with that heading.

The witness's printed chapter numbering is preserved without silent
correction. After chapter IV it prints `CAP. VI. De Charactere` and then prints
`CAP. VI. De Numero Sacramentorum`; the following chapters are numbered VII
and VIII.

The English is a full close translation of the paired Latin, not a summary. It
does not use a modern translation as a wording source. `Bellarm.` identifies
Bellarmin's position as stated by Ames; `Protest.` identifies Ames's Protestant
reply.

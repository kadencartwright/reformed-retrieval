[Book I, printed p. 68; BSB canvas 00094]

Nor is this consequence—“Antichrist does not exist; therefore he does not terminate God’s intuitive
knowledge”—an inference from what is restricted to what is unrestricted. I admit that the matter
stands as you say when you formulate it this way: “Antichrist does not exist; therefore he cannot
terminate God’s intuitive knowledge.” But it is corruptly formulated in that manner. For although
Antichrist does not yet exist, nevertheless insofar as he can exist, he can also terminate God’s
intuitive knowledge. Yet for as long as he does not exist, he cannot terminate God’s intuitive
knowledge. To terminate God’s intuitive knowledge is to be known by God as existing; but it is
impossible for what does not exist to be known as existing for as long as it does not exist.

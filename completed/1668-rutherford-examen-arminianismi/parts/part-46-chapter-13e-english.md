HEBREWS 6:4.

For it cannot be that those who were once enlightened, who tasted that
heavenly gift, and who were made partakers of the Holy Spirit, v. 5, and who
tasted the good Word of God and the powers of the age to come, v. 6, if they
fall away, should be renewed again to repentance.

From this passage the adversaries attempt to prove that those who are truly
regenerate and justified can fall from grace totally and finally. *Synodical
Writings*, article 5, p. 237.

[1668 KBNL/UvA witness | logical printed p. 571 | PDF p. 622]

We deny that the persons of whom the apostle speaks were truly regenerate.

1. For this passage, as our theologians rightly note after Athanasius, must be
understood of the sin against the Holy Spirit. This is not a simple sin, but
*parapiptein*—to fall away by total apostasy. Countless passages of Scripture,
as seen above, prove that this sin cannot befall those who are truly elect and
justified. Pareus notes the same in his commentary at this place.

2. For those of whom he speaks here did not have ears prepared and senses
trained for discerning good and evil, nor were they capable of solid food.
Indeed, they were persons who needed alphabet-level instruction in the first
elements of Christianity, as can be seen in chapter 5:12–14. But in verses 9
and 10 of this chapter the apostle says of the elect and true believers to whom
he writes that he is persuaded of better things that accompany salvation, and
of their laborious love by which they ministered to the saints.

3. For the whole argument has two affirmative premises in the second figure:

The regenerate were enlightened, tasted the heavenly gift, and were made
partakers of the Holy Spirit.

But these justified and regenerate persons to whom the apostle writes were
enlightened, tasted the heavenly gift, and so forth. Therefore, and so forth.

Otherwise the major premise, once converted, is manifestly false and is
the very point in question.

[1668 KBNL/UvA witness | logical printed p. 572 | PDF p. 623]

4. For although the apostle is not dealing here with the grosser hypocrites
but with somewhat more enlightened professors, it happens not infrequently
that people who were never regenerate taste with the tips of their lips the
sweetness of the Word and the happiness of the age to come. For *tasting* in
the Word is opposed to an inward and rooted impression of the thing upon the
heart, affections, and powers of the soul. Hence there is “tasting death” in
Matthew 16:28. Christ also is said to have tasted death, and yet we know that
he was not wholly subjected to death's dominion. And 1 Peter 2:3 signifies an
initial perception of grace which precedes the infant's first birth and
begetting. Tasting death is attributed to believers of whom it is denied that
they will die, John 11:26. Therefore, tasting the powers of the age to come
does not necessarily mean becoming a partaker of eternal life, as those who
have the firstfruits are, but being as those who are not partakers. Although
the material object of this tasting is the good Word of God and the powers of
the age to come, it is nevertheless not the formal object. For loving a wife
as a prostitute with a harlot's love differs greatly from loving her as a wife
with conjugal love. In the same way a heavenly and spiritual thing often
affects Herod, or a temporary believer, with joy as a pleasant good, but it
affects a true believer as a morally upright good—not under the same formal
aspect. For just as a spiritual person has spiritual joy in God over temporal
goods, so an unregenerate person can conceive natural joy over a

[1668 KBNL/UvA witness | logical printed p. 573 | PDF p. 624]

supernatural object. The affections and motions of the will and heart do not
receive their specification from the material object.

5. Participation in the Holy Spirit is a sharing of the common gifts of the
Holy Spirit in the sacraments and promises of the Gospel. But although these
excellent gifts are bestowed upon these temporary believers, there is deep
silence about remission of sins, justification, the sealing of the Spirit,
the firstfruits of future life, and gratuitous election to salvation—things
that essentially distinguish a believer from an unbeliever.

6. According to the adversaries' doctrines, the impossibility of renewal
cannot be attributed to any who are truly elect and believing, for they
maintain that universal and sufficient grace is available to everyone in this
life. They dream that *adynaton*, “impossible,” means “difficult” here, so that
sufficient grace is available to such people; but this makes the threat
pronounced against a far more atrocious sin into a light one, for such is the
punishment of a lighter sin.

7. “Crucifying the Son of God again” no more signifies that the Son of God was
once crucified for them than the fact that unworthy communicants and
participants in the Lord's Supper are said to be guilty of the body and blood
of the Lord signifies that they are persons for whom the Lord's body is said
to have been broken and his blood poured out on the wood of the cross, as Ames
observes in the *Antisynodical Writings*.

Rather, the apostle here alludes to the perverse Jews who crucified Christ
anew, just as their fathers once crucified him under Pontius Pilate, because
by maliciously opposing the Gospel and persecuting all who believe in Christ
they gave their assent to his ignominious death.

8. The sanctification attributed to them is: 1. Sacramental; 2. Inward,
indeed, but common; 3. Outward through profession of the Gospel.

9. It is false to call the truly regenerate the cursed land of verse 8, near
to a curse, whose end tends toward burning because it produces thorns and
briars. For all who are in Christ by faith—in whom all the nations of the
earth are blessed—are blessed with Abraham, Galatians 3:14.

10. The adversaries chiefly insist here that this threat would be futile for
the elect if apostasy is impossible for them. But that foundation of the
argument is slippery and weak. Joseph and Mary's flight into Egypt with Christ
while he was still an infant, lest the child be killed by Herod, was not
futile, although by God's absolute decree the killing of Christ in infancy was
just as impossible as the apostasy of the elect.

2 PETER 2:20.

For if, after they have escaped the pollutions of the world through the
knowledge of the Lord and Savior Jesus Christ, they are again entangled in
them and overcome, their last condition has become worse than the former.

[Printed p. 575 | Google/KBNL-UvA leaf absent; supplied from Google/BL PA575 | GB mLpoAAAAcAAJ]

Hence it is asked whether these persons were truly regenerate. The
Remonstrants affirm it. We deny it.

1. For, as Pareus observes in his commentary at this place, only historical
knowledge is ever attributed to them. First, they escaped the pollutions of
the world through knowledge of true doctrine and rejection of errors. In
verse 20, they escaped through knowledge of the Lord; and in verse 21, it
would have been better not to have known the way of righteousness.

2. For Judas, Herod, and Ahab escaped many pollutions of the world for a time,
but the devil returned with seven worse spirits. There is no fiducial resting
upon Christ here; the text says nothing about justification, redemption,
adoption, or their eternal election in Christ.

3. Although they are said to have escaped—the witness here prints `ὄντες`,
“being”—nevertheless, as Beza and Ames observe, `ὄντως`, “truly,” is not in
the Royal, Complutensian, or Plantin codices. Erasmus and Vatablus read
`ὄντως`; other codices read `ὀλίγως`, “scarcely.”

4. The whole sequence of the context cries out the contrary. Peter calls
those who escaped these pollutions of the world *psychas astēriktous*,
unstable souls; pigs, v. 12; irrational animals, v. 13; persons who will
receive the wages of unrighteousness; spots in their feasts; those whose eyes
are full of adultery; those who ensnare; and those whose hearts are trained in
greed, v. 15. Therefore, they professed Christ only outwardly and in the
*agapais*, the love feasts, joined themselves with

[1668 KBNL/UvA witness | logical printed p. 576 | PDF p. 625]

the saints, although they had no true sanctity in themselves, v. 13; Jude v.
12.

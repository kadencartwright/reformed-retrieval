CHAPTER XX

ON THE SOUL AND THE RESURRECTION OF THE FLESH

ACTS 7:59

“Lord Jesus, receive my spirit.”

It is asked whether souls are immortal. The Remonstrants and Socinians doubt
it. Joan. Geisteranus says in his *Confession* that he does not know whether
souls after death are alive or dead, mortal or immortal. Henricus Slatius says
in his *Open Declaration*, page 53, that he does not know whether souls survive
after they have been separated from the body; at least, this is not
comprehensible. In the *Apologia*, chapter 19, and *Confession*, chapter 19,
the Remonstrants teach that believers are to be raised and that they alone are
to be given a glorious and incorruptible body, contrary to Ecclesiastes 3:21
and 12:7; Matthew 19:28; and Acts 7:59.

It is asked whether the very same bodies numerically which were dissolved into
dust will rise again. The Remonstrants say in the *Apologia*, chapter 19,
folio 217, that the arguments on each side are probable and that the dispute
is to be decided by God in the age to come. Slatius says in his *Open
Declaration*, “These bodies of ours will not rise again.” The Socinians

[1668 KBNL/UvA witness | logical printed p. 754 | PDF p. 803 | split word joined: dicunt]

say that it is Muslim to believe that these bodies are to be raised for bodily
pleasures. But neither do we believe this.

Meanwhile, Scripture has taught us that these very same bodies numerically
will rise, and indeed to glory, heavenly joys, and eternal life. Job 19:26: “I
know that in my flesh I shall see God.” Verse 27: “I, the very same and not
another, shall see for myself, and my eyes shall behold, though my inward parts
are consumed within my bosom.” 1 Corinthians 15:52: “The dead will be raised
incorruptible, and we will be changed.” Verse 53: “For this corruptible must
put on incorruption.” Verse 54: “But after this corruptible,” τὸ φθαρτὸν τοῦτο,
“has put on incorruption,” καὶ τὸ θνητὸν τοῦτο, “and this mortal has put on
immortality, then what is written will come to pass: Death is swallowed up in
victory.”

2. Because Scripture speaks of the body as a thing which changes while
remaining numerically the same. 1 Corinthians 15:42: “It is sown in
corruption; it is raised in incorruption.” Verse 43: “It is sown dishonorable;
it is raised in glory. It is sown devoid of all strength; it is raised in
power. It is sown an animal body; it is raised a spiritual body.” The Holy
Spirit would say in vain that the body is buried and committed to the earth
with these or those qualities and rises with other new ones unless that which
was buried rose numerically the same.

3. We argue from the redemption of bodies. If the body has been bought by the
price of Christ's blood no

[1668 KBNL/UvA witness | logical printed p. 755 | PDF p. 804]

less than the soul, then the body no less than the soul must be restored for
that day which is the day of the redemption of our body, Romans 8:23; and he
will “transform our humble body to conform to his”—Christ's—“glorious body,
according to that effective power by which he is also able to subject all
things to himself,” Philippians 3:21. But the former is true; therefore so is
the latter. The major premise is clear from 1 Corinthians 6:14, where the
apostle proves that one must not fornicate in this body which we bear, because
“God, who raised the Lord, will also raise us by his power”—that is, our
bodies. 2. Because bodies are Christ's members. Verse 15: “Do you not know that
your bodies are members of Christ? Shall I then take Christ's members and make
them the members of a prostitute? By no means.” If the bodies of the saints
are Christ's members, they must be glorified with him. But they cannot be
glorified with him unless they are raised numerically the same. 3. Verse 19:
“Do you not know that your body is the temple of the Holy Spirit, who is in
you, whom you have from God, and that you are not your own?” Verse 20: “For you
were bought with a price. Therefore glorify God in your body and in your
spirit, which are God's.” What has been bought by Christ's blood and belongs
to God rather than us by right of redemption cannot utterly perish, but must
be raised with Christ. But these numerically identical mortal bodies which we
bear are not ours but God's by right of redemption. Therefore these same
bodies must be raised. The reason for the major premise is that what is not
redeemed by

[1668 KBNL/UvA witness | logical printed p. 756 | PDF p. 805 | split word joined: Christum]

Christ from destruction is not glorified with Christ—namely, something which
utterly perishes and for which something numerically different is substituted.

5. From God's justice and equity. If a numerically different soul, and not
this very soul which sinned in this life, were punished with eternal death,
God's justice would in this respect be violated. In the same way, if the body
which “was offered as a living sacrifice to God,” Romans 12:1, and was “an
instrument of righteousness,” Romans 6:12–13, were rewarded with no prize and
no recompense, we do not see how “each person may receive what he did in the
body according to what he has done, whether good or evil,” as the Holy Spirit
nevertheless says in 2 Corinthians 5:10.

6. God's omnipotence proclaimed by Scripture in the resurrection of the dead
would here be obscured. For omnipotence shines forth in this: those very same
bodies “which are in the graves will hear the voice of the Son of God and come
forth, some to the resurrection of life and others to the resurrection of
condemnation,” John 5:28–29. Souls, however, are not in the graves. Those who
“sleep in the dust” are to be raised, Daniel 12:2. And “the sea will give up
the dead which it held,” as will death and hell, Revelation 20:13. If bodies
numerically different from those we now bear are to be given to us in the
raising of the dead—and indeed created anew—they are no more to be created
anew from graves or the sea than from some other material. It would be a
curious limitation beyond

[1668 KBNL/UvA witness | logical printed p. 757 | PDF p. 806 | split word joined: Scripturas]

Scripture to restrict Jehovah's omnipotence to this material rather than that,
to the sea rather than the sepulchral dust in the earth, since he could form
or create different, new bodies from any material or from none. Nor do people
whose bodies will never be raised sleep in the dust any more than people who
were never begotten by parents do.

7. Since Christ is “true man,” Hebrews 2:16–17, “like us in every respect
except sin,” Hebrews 4:15, and since Christ presented his own numerically
identical body—marked by the same scar and trace of the nails by which it was
wounded on the cross—to Thomas and his disciples to be seen, felt, and
handled with their hands, John 20:20–29, it is necessary that he raise these
same bodies of ours which are liable to death and dissolved into dust.

8. Finally, the nature of resurrection supports the same truth. What is
created anew can no more be said to be raised from dust and graves than a son
newly begotten and brought forth into the light can be said to have been
raised from the dead. For a numerically different body created anew was never
dead or in the graves, and therefore is not to be raised.

Finally, this is not a curious and useless question, but one full of
consolation for us, who groan in this earthly dwelling of a clay tabernacle
while we

[1668 KBNL/UvA witness | logical printed p. 758 | PDF p. 807 | split word joined: expectamus]

await a heavenly home and a glorious body, 2 Corinthians 5:1–3. Nor did Calvin
in Letter 103 call it curious in itself,
as the Remonstrants suppose in the *Apologia*, folio 219, because there he
carefully confirms the orthodox position and expressly refutes the contrary
error. Rather, he deservedly called curious the question Laelius Socinus had
raised against manifest Scripture because of certain vain speculations about
the numerical identity of the raised body and about the supposedly impossible
or incredible manner of resurrection. And if at that time Calvin had not
still thought well of that man, who conducted himself as a brother and lived
with Pellican, the Zurich theologian, he could also have called it impious.

MATTHEW 25:41

“You accursed, depart from me into the eternal fire prepared for the devil and
his angels.”

It is asked whether the infernal torments of the damned are eternal, or whether
they will eventually have an end, so that the wicked will at last utterly
perish and be reduced to nothing. The Remonstrants do not shrink from this
Epicurean opinion. They indeed say in words of Scripture which they do not
explain, but which are open to a Socinian sense, that God will inflict infernal
torments and eternal punishments upon unbelievers—“when he casts them into
eternal fire together with the devil and his angels, so that they pay the
penalty of eternal destruction, expelled from God's presence and his glorious
power.” But in his *Commentary on the First Epistle of John*, chapter 2, page
104, Socinus explains this as total cessation and reduction to

[1668 KBNL/UvA witness | logical printed p. 759 (visible 259) | PDF p. 808 | split word joined: nihilum]

nothing. And when the most learned and illustrious Leiden Professors object to
the Remonstrants in their *Censure of the Confession*, chapter 20, last
section, page 264, that they do not clearly explain “whether they think those
infernal torments will endure forever and the wicked people themselves who
suffer those punishments will live forever in those pains,” because their
words are conceived ambiguously and in agreement with Socinus, the
Remonstrants answer this exceedingly weighty objection in their *Apologia*, in
which they discuss this *Censure*, as follows: 1. They do not answer even a
word or syllable at chapter 20 or elsewhere. 2. They deem the opinion of the
substantial destruction of the world far more probable than its contrary,
*Apologia*, chapter 19, folio 219. 3. They have nothing about the eternal state
of the damned. 4. In the *Confession*, chapter 19, section 2, they say, “For
then he will raise his believers and saints who have died from the dust of the
earth to eternal life, and will give them alone a glorious and incorruptible
body.” Henricus Slatius, their follower in every respect, says in the *Open
Declaration*, page 53, that the wicked will either not be raised or will be
reduced to nothing.

We, on the contrary, judge this opinion Epicurean and atheistic.

1. Because if the passages of Scripture for the eternity of infernal torments
can be evaded, then by the same reasoning the passages for the eternity of
eternal life can be evaded. It could be answered in both cases that life and
death are called eternal in the sense in which circumcision and ceremonies are
called eternal—that is, by a Hebrew idiom, because they are to endure for

[1668 KBNL/UvA witness | logical printed p. 760 | PDF p. 809]

a fixed time and eventually cease. But on this reasoning the soul would not
live forever but would utterly die, and Christ's kingdom would not be eternal
but would eventually perish. And what would prevent Christ's humanity from
eventually being reduced to nothing?

2. Because Scripture says the contrary. Isaiah 66:24: “For their worm will not
die, and their fire will not be extinguished.” Matthew 25:41: “Then he will
also say to those who are on the left, You accursed, depart from me into the
eternal fire prepared for the devil and his angels.” According to our
opponents, who reject our opinion, it follows from these passages, 1. that
“eternal life” in verse 46 is not eternal, and thus that good angels and
glorified people will eventually perish and be reduced to nothing or be
deprived of their blessedness and so become miserable; and 2. that the devils
and his angels will also be freed from eternal torments and either reduced to
nothing or attain eternal life or a state free from misery. Mark 9:43: “It is
better for you to enter life maimed than to go with two hands into Gehenna,
into the unquenchable fire.” Verse 44: “Where their worm does not perish, and
the fire is not extinguished.” Verses 56 and 48. Jude 6: “Devils reserved
under darkness in eternal chains for the judgment of that great day.” Verse 7:
“Sodom and Gomorrah suffer the punishment of eternal fire.” But it is never
said of circumcision that it will not cease, as it is said of that fire

[1668 KBNL/UvA witness | logical printed p. 761 | PDF p. 810]

that it will not be extinguished. And Revelation 20:11 says, “And the devil
who deceived them was cast into the lake of fire and sulfur, where the beast
and the false prophet were, and they will be tormented day and night for ages
of ages.”

3. Because there is no release from hell forever, according to Luke 16:26:
“And besides all these things, a great chasm has been established between us
and you, so that those who wish to cross from here to you cannot, nor can
anyone cross from there to here.”

4. Because every human soul is immortal, Matthew 10:28, and cannot be killed by
any mortal. Therefore souls will either live forever in eternal joy or eternal
torments, which is what has been established; or they will live forever in an
intermediate, neutral state, which is not written; or souls are to be
annihilated by God, which cannot be affirmed from Scripture.

As for their statement that Scripture asserts the dead do not exist: what is
denied of them in this way is not existence absolutely, but only existence in
a qualified sense—existence among people living in the flesh. But if this is extended to
absolute nonexistence, it could then be proved that Christ as a dead man was
utterly reduced to nothing, and that all who have died, both saints and
unbelievers, have utterly perished and are nothing with respect to both soul
and body.

THE END

# On a Certain Marriage Dispute

This directory presents the complete French response printed at CO X/1
238–239, including the final clause which CO identifies as an addition in
Calvin's hand, with a fresh English translation.

> **Editorial status:** This is an AI-assisted draft transcription and
> translation checked line by line against 600-DPI facsimile images, not a
> peer-reviewed critical edition. The case contains sensitive allegations
> about named historical people. A qualified manuscript specialist and legal
> historian should independently collate Geneva Cod. 145, fol. 141 before
> publication or prosopographical reliance.

The response says that “we” assembled in God's name and resolved the matter by
common agreement. It has no individual subscription. CO's statement that the
last words were added in Calvin's hand supplies direct evidence of his
intervention in the surviving witness, but not a reason to suppress the
collective voice or assign every preceding sentence to him alone.

## Title and textual layers

The transcription follows Wilhelm Baum, Eduard Cunitz, and Eduard Reuss,
eds., *Ioannis Calvini opera quae supersunt omnia*, vol. X, part 1, *Corpus
Reformatorum* XXXVIII (Brunswick: C. A. Schwetschke, 1871), pp. 238–239.

CO reports three materially distinct layers:

1. a contemporary copy of the response in Geneva Cod. 145, fol. 141;
2. the title *Sur un different de quelque mariage* placed at the copy's end;
3. the later marginal title *Avis de Calvin sur les promesses de mariage*.

CO moves the first title into its printed heading. Neither title is inserted
into [`french.txt`](french.txt) as the opening words of the advisers' address.
The later title is evidence of subsequent attribution and cataloguing, not a
contemporary authorial subscription.

CO further prints the final words *chascune partie sera mise en liberté de se
marier ailleurs* in a differentiated typeface and states that these last words
were added in Calvin's hand. The French file retains them at their syntactic
location under a supplied bracketed layer label. The English does the same.
The labels are project apparatus; the following historical words are the
addition.

## Boundaries, normalization, and completeness

The historical address begins *Treschers Seigneurs et freres* on p. 238. It
ends on p. 239 with Calvin's added release clause. Every body word between
those points is retained. The CO heading, source footnote, later marginal title
as quoted in that footnote, running heads, page numbers, preceding Latin work,
and following French work are excluded from the body and documented here.

Printer line-end divisions are joined. The pp. 238–239 turn divides no word:
p. 238 ends *la partie ny consentoit point* and p. 239 begins *Il est donc a
notter*. English markers follow the same boundary.

Historical spelling and legal phrasing are preserved, including *Treschers*,
*davoir*, *demenee*, *debvoient*, *sensuit*, *circonvenue*, *vefve*,
*tesmoings*, *donnation de corps*, *recepvable*, *consenty*, *apperceu*,
*dequite*, *laymast*, *dobvier*, *dadvis*, *confermer*, and *diffinitive*.
The names are transcribed as Iehan Focard, Margueritte Heberarde, Pierre
Focard, Alexis Myot, Simon Gaillard, Pierre Nemauso, and Pierre Havart.

## Evidentiary and translation cautions

- The response reports what Margaret, her mother and sister declared; what the
  aunt, servant, and three named witnesses allegedly said; the mother's
  confession; and John Focard's statements. This edition does not independently
  establish that any reported event or claim was true.
- *Donnation de corps* is retained in English as “donation of bodies,” a
  historical consent formula, rather than being silently replaced with a
  modern contract-law category.
- *Tant de rigueur que dequite* is compressed spelling for an appeal to both
  strictness and equity. The English renders that contextual construction
  without altering the French.
- *La tante delle vefve de Sire Pierre Focard le beau frère et son serviteur*
  lacks clarifying punctuation. The translation construes three deponents—the
  aunt (Pierre Focard's widow), the brother-in-law, and his servant—because the
  later witness list treats the aunt and brother-in-law separately. This
  relational parsing should not be used as genealogical proof without
  manuscript review.
- The singular *quelle aie a se purger* follows an instruction to summon both
  mother and daughter. The English keeps a singular “she” rather than silently
  identifying its antecedent.

The opinion reflects sixteenth-century procedure and doctrine concerning
coercion, consent, oaths, and marriage promises. It is published for historical
documentation, not as present-day legal or pastoral advice.

## Translation independence

Mary D. Beaty and Benjamin Wirt Farley published a copyrighted translation in
*Calvin's Ecclesiastical Advice* (1991). It was not consulted or used for the
wording of [`english.md`](english.md). The English was translated afresh from
the complete normalized French and checked against the facsimile.

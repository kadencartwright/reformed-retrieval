## Chapter VII. On the Comparison of the Sacraments of the New Testament with One Another

We teach that no question should be raised about inequality among the
sacraments, because they have the same efficient cause; matter equal in its
spiritual significance; the word of the same authority and efficacy; an
entirely similar relation; and, as to the substance of the matter, the same
end and use. The Tridentine papists, however, in session 7, canon 3,
anathematize those who make the seven sacraments of the new law equal. Our
writers have received this canon with ridicule, because we make only two
sacraments equal, not seven. Bellarmine defends it with certain observations
which we must consider.

2. Bellarmine. The council does not place the force of its statement in the
number seven but in the character of a sacrament.

Protestant reply. It therefore does violence to the character of a sacrament,
for that character applies equally and to an equal degree to all sacraments.
Otherwise, properly speaking, *sacrament* would not be the genus of all the
sacraments.

3. Bellarmine. Under various aspects, there is no sacrament which does not
excel some other sacrament.

Protestant reply. There is no sacramental aspect under which matrimony and
orders can be compared with baptism and the Eucharist. Those two, Bellarmine
says in chapter 27, are more universal.

4. Bellarmine. Baptism excels all the others with respect to its effect of
remitting sins.

[1632 witness | printed p. 38]

Protestant reply. The remission of sins is effected equally in the Eucharist.
For its word—and, as Bellarmine interprets it, the form of the Eucharist—is:
“This is my blood of the new covenant, which is poured out for many for the
remission of sins.” Matthew 26:28.

5. Bellarmine. Confirmation excels all the others with respect to the effect
of grace for doing good; it is the perfection and consummation of baptism.
Luke, final chapter, verse 49.

Protestant reply. 1. Through baptism we are enabled to walk in newness of
life, Romans 6:4, and the grace of remission is never separated from the grace
of doing good. 2. To call baptism imperfect without episcopal confirmation is
not free from blasphemy. 3. Bellarmine is ridiculous when he imagines that in
Luke, final chapter, verse 49, the apostles—who were already participants both
in the Lord's Supper and in the highest ecclesiastical order—were waiting for
the sacrament of confirmation.

Bellarmine 6. The Eucharist excels all the others with respect to the very
substance of the sacrament.

Protestant reply. The substance of a sacrament consists of matter and form;
the matter is the thing and the form is the word, according to Bellarmine,
Book 1, chapter 18. But in the Eucharist, according to the papists, the thing
or perceptible element is only the accidental species of bread and wine, which
does not excel in substance. And no word can be more divine than that commonly
used in baptism. Therefore, with respect to substance, the Eucharist does not
excel baptism.

7. Bellarmine. Penance excels the others in necessity, baptism alone excepted.

Protestant reply. 1. Necessity considered in itself is no more excellent than
contingency or indifference. 2. The necessity of auricular confession and
priestly absolution, of which the sacrament of penance consists, is not as
great as the necessity of the Eucharist.

9. Bellarmine. Extreme unction in some manner excels penance with respect to
the effect of grace, for it presupposes the whole effect of penance, adds more
abundant grace to it, and removes the remnants of sins.

Protestant reply. 1. On this reasoning it also excels baptism and confirmation.
2. If these claims are true, it excels them to such a degree that it even does
away with purgatory itself.

9. Bellarmine. Orders excels all the others except confirmation with respect
to the minister, for it can be conferred only by a bishop. It also surpasses
the others because it establishes people in a rank more exalted than that of
other Christians.

Protestant reply. 1. Whenever people please, on the same reasoning they may
assign any other thing, whether sacred or not, to their bishops—as they have
also done with basilicas, with preparing chrism, and with consecrating
vestments and vessels—

[1632 witness | printed p. 39]

—and then under that aspect it will excel baptism and the Eucharist. 2. The
rank of ministers is not more exalted than the rank of Christians on
sacramental grounds, but only with respect to office and function.

10. Bellarmine. Matrimony excels in signification, for it signifies the union
of Christ with the church.

Protestant reply. 1. Matrimony no more signifies the union of Christ and the
church than do the conjunction of the vine and branches or of the head and
members. 2. Baptism signifies our closest union with Christ and our communion
in his death and life. Romans 6.

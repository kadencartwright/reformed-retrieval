Verse 29. Have you seen a man diligent in his work? He will stand before kings; he will not stand before obscure men.

The purpose is to stir us to diligence in our calling by setting before us God's providence in rewarding the industrious.

It is not enough for a Christian to be occupied in doing his own work unless he also brings diligence and industry to it. For it is the industrious hand that is commended. Accordingly, in this passage we are commanded to discharge the task committed and entrusted to us morning and evening, in season and out of season, in cold and heat.

Next, it is required that each person attend to his own affairs. For Αλλοτριοεπίσκοποι, people who meddle in others' affairs, are condemned in 1 Peter 4:15 and 2 Thessalonians 3:11. Of these it may be said in earnest, “I do not like the excessively diligent.”

When he says “Have you seen?” he teaches that we ought to observe and imitate those who discharge their duties industriously and diligently. Thus Paul set his own diligence before the Thessalonians for their imitation, 2 Thessalonians 3:7–8. But some people habitually take the most indolent as their models in carrying out their affairs, imagining that they have conducted their business energetically and seriously if they are not the most idle of all.

The promise follows that a man diligent in his work will stand before kings. This is also amplified by denying the contrary: he will not stand before men of low rank. Thus the industrious hand is said to enrich, Proverbs 10:4 and 21:5. The same hand will also rule, Proverbs 12:24. This is confirmed by many clear examples, Genesis 47:6; that of Rebecca, Genesis 24:18, 19, 20; that of Jeroboam, 1 Kings 11:28; and that of Ruth, who, although a poor woman, nevertheless secured through her diligence and industry a marriage to Boaz, a man very rich and powerful in authority. But the sluggish and idle, although born into an honorable position and occupying a station in life that is not to be despised, nevertheless through their own indolence either fall headlong or sink gradually into misery and disgrace.

Now if it is an incentive to exercise diligence in human affairs that the diligent will stand before earthly kings, though this is not altogether certain, since it can be, and often is, hindered in many ways, how much diligence ought we to employ in divine affairs, so that we may stand forever before the King of kings in the highest heavens! This reward is greater by an infinite difference and, in view of God's certain will and unquestionable power, assured and secure.

### Translator's note

The Greek Αλλοτριοεπίσκοποι is retained in the form printed here and glossed in the translation. The following saying about the “excessively diligent” refers to their intrusive attention to other people's affairs. The source's abbreviated *Thessal.* after *Paulus* means the Thessalonians, the people to whom Paul presented his example.

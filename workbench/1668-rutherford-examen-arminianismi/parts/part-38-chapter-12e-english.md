1 JOHN 1:7.

“And the blood of his Son Jesus Christ cleanses us from every sin.”

[1668 KBNL/UvA witness | logical printed p. 535 | PDF p. 586]

Hence we have it that justification is the full remission of absolutely all
sins. Hence

It is asked whether our justification is one complete and continuous act, and
thus an act not to be repeated; or whether justification is interrupted and
broken off whenever justified people sin, and whether they are justified anew
whenever they believe and repent. Our adversaries teach that justification is
successive, is interrupted and cut off, and is advanced and perfected by its
degrees. Arminius, Disputation 48, thesis 12; Episcopius, Disputation 22,
thesis 12. We, however, assert that justification is a single indivisible act,
the sense of which can be renewed daily, but that justification itself is not
perfected by any degrees; rather, the whole is posited in an indivisible
instant.

1. Those who are justified have all their sins washed away by Christ's blood.
First John 1:7. Their sins are blotted out like a cloud. Isaiah 44:23. They are
all cast into the depth of the sea. Micah 7:19. Thus, if Israel's iniquity is
sought, it is not found, and none exists. Jeremiah 50:20. Therefore, their
sins, once remitted, are never imputed anew in such a way that a new
justification is necessary.

2. The kind of remission that Christ obtained upon the tree of the cross is
the kind he confers upon a believer in justification. But he obtained upon
the cross the full remission of each and every sin. Therefore, and so forth.
The reason for the major premise is that it is `ἄγραφον`—unwritten—and absurd
that Jesus Christ should have made satisfaction upon the tree of the cross
for some of believers'

[1668 KBNL/UvA witness | logical printed p. 536 | PDF p. 587 | split word joined: peccatis]

sins, while the believers themselves will expiate other sins, one after
another, in hell. For in this way Christ will be a half-Savior and an imperfect
Redeemer of such people. But Christ “obtained eternal redemption,” Hebrews
9:12, and “made peace through the cross,” Colossians 1:20. Therefore, he
removed every war and cause of estrangement.

3. From this position it follows that God's special favor in Christ is cut
off, that the decree of election is retracted, changed, and rescinded from
hour to hour. For if the elect are justified whenever they believe and repent,
they must also be predestined whenever they do so. Romans 8:30. And whenever
they sin, justification is retracted just as often, the decree of election is
rescinded, and a decree of reprobation succeeds in its place. There would thus
be almost infinite revolutions and rotations of God's contrary decrees and of
his immanent acts of loving and hating, of imputing sins and not imputing them,
together with the changeable and inconstant acts of our inconstant free
choice.

4. The Covenant of Grace is called eternal in Jeremiah 31, and the continuance
and perseverance of those in covenant within the Covenant is eternal.
Jeremiah 32:40; Isaiah 54:10–11; Isaiah 59, last verse. It is therefore not cut
off and interrupted. Otherwise, it would follow that true believers fall away
from this Covenant and that not all whom God predestines, calls, and justifies
will be glorified. Yet the Apostle says this expressly in Romans 8:30.

[1668 KBNL/UvA witness | logical printed p. 537 | PDF p. 588 | split word joined: Sequitur]

5. It follows that no one is completely justified and that no one's sins are
all cast into the depth of the sea until he completes his course in faith and
repentance. This conflicts with innumerable passages of Scripture.

6. Either a justified person falls out of justification because of any light
sin committed from weakness—or, as the Sophists call it, a venial sin—or only
because of more heinous offenses. If the former, justification would have to
be repeated and reiterated a hundred times every day, which Holy Scripture
never intimates. If a justified person does not fall out of justification
because of lighter sins, there will be no need for remission in Christ's blood
to wash those lighter sins away. Therefore, they are not sins at all; and if
they are sins, the justified person does not fulfill God's Law and is not
justified by good works. If he falls out of justification only because of more
heinous sins, those more heinous sins either have been expiated by Christ's
blood or have not. If the former, Christ's satisfaction and the remission
obtained by Christ are apprehended by the believer in parts: one part after
another, a third after the second, a fourth after the third. Therefore, if
someone dies before he is rejustified following his exclusion from
justification, he is justified only by half. Satisfaction has been made for
his sins and one part of the sins has been fully remitted, but the other part
is to be paid by that person forever in hell. And one who once truly believes
is condemned forever,

[1668 KBNL/UvA witness | logical printed p. 538 | PDF p. 589]

which is absurd. But if the more heinous sins have not been expiated by
Christ's blood, they are simply unpardonable, like the sins of devils.

The whole difficulty of this question appears to depend upon this: sins seem
to be remitted before they are committed, although a regenerate person must,
after each sin—or at least after the more heinous sins—apprehend and apply to
himself anew the remission of sins in Christ. But the difficulty is easily
removed. The saints indeed pray daily for the remission of sins, but, having
been justified once, they pray for a sense and renewal of the remission once
already apprehended and applied. Nor is it absurd; indeed, it is necessary
that all the sins of the regenerate, both those already committed and those to
be committed hereafter, were long ago expiated by Christ's one sacrifice and
have already been blotted out in God's mind and intention, and that the sense
of that expiation already made should be apprehended and applied again and
again through new acts of believing. For this must be firmly maintained: the
remission of sins by no means causes sins no longer to be sins or a sinner no
longer to be a sinner, as regards sin's physical and real inherence in its
subject. It causes only that sins are not imputed—that is, that the moral
obligation to punishment satisfying divine wrath is removed. This is just as
a remission granted by an earthly king, from the prince's mere grace, to a
traitor guilty of treason against his majesty

[1668 KBNL/UvA witness | logical printed p. 539 | PDF p. 590 | split word joined: indulta]

does not cause the person to whom it is granted to cease being a traitor. It
causes only that he ceases to be a traitor who must be punished by beheading
and that he is freed from every legal obligation by which, before remission
was granted, he was bound to undergo the capital punishment of treason. For a
royal remission does not retract by any physical action the acts of treason
committed by the person who would have had to be beheaded.

Hence sins are removed in two ways. 1. Legally and *in fictione juris*, as they
say—that is, by a purely judicial and forensic removal. In this manner sins are
removed in justification, not so that they cease to exist, but so that they
are not imputed, as Augustine says. Therefore, justification introduces into
the person justified not some physical change, but only a moral or legal one,
by which a person is released from obligation to punishment. This release is
made all at once in God's mind and is apprehended all at once by the believer
when he is first justified; its sense is renewed and repeated daily.

2. Sins are removed really and physically with respect to their physical
inherence in their subject. They are removed in this way in sanctification,
though in this life only inchoately and imperfectly, just as our sanctification
in this life is imperfect and inchoate. Therefore, sanctification expels sin
by physical action only in parts and successively, just as morning light, or
the first light of dawn, expels the darkness of night

[1668 KBNL/UvA witness | logical printed p. 540 | PDF p. 591 | split word joined: non nisi]

only successively and by degrees.

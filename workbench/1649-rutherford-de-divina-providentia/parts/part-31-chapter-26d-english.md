18. GOD is our friend; but the nature of friendship requires one efficaciously
to will good things for one’s friend, as Aristotle says. Therefore GOD does not
predetermine to an act from which the loss of friendship and liability to
eternal punishment inseparably follow. A father, king, husband, or host would
not predetermine his own people to such an evil. It belongs to a sordid host to
will that those whom he kindly invites should not eat. Therefore GOD does not
will to predetermine to acts leading to inescapable destruction. Likewise, GOD
forsakes no one unless he is first forsaken by him, as Scripture and the
Fathers teach. But if God determines someone to the material act of sin before
the free will of the human being is supposed to determine itself to that
action, then God forsakes the justified person before he is forsaken by that
person.

[Marginal reference: Ruiz, disputation 34, section 1; Aristotle, *Ethics*, book
8, chapter 2, and book 9, chapter 5.]

[Marginal reference: 2 Chronicles 15.]

[Marginal summary: Although God is called Friend, Father, Bridegroom, King,
Host, and so forth, he is nevertheless not bound by the same law not to concur
toward the material acts of sin by which earthly human beings—who are also
friends, fathers, and so forth—are bound, because GOD concurs wholly freely and
from no legal debt.]

I reply. All these titles—Friend, Father, Husband, King, and Host—are attributed
to GOD in a mode far more eminent than to human beings and with the divine
freedom to have mercy on whom he wills preserved. Nor, because he is Father and
Husband, does he therefore will well to his children and wife by an impulse of
nature. A father, friend, husband, king, and host are obliged by the debt of an
office required by the law of nature to will well to their own; but God wills
well to his own from no debt of law, not even of his holy nature. This is clear
from the Scriptures, Isaiah 40:14–15; Romans 11:35: “Who first gave to him, and
it shall be repaid to him?” Matthew 20:15: “Is it not lawful for me to do what I
will with what is mine?” But is it lawful for a mother to refuse her breasts to
her newborn infant, or for a father to refuse food to his little son? Is it
lawful for a king to deny judgment and justice to his subjects, or for a host
to deny food to his guest? Is a father a father to his children because he has
mercy when he wills and denies good things when he wills—a father to his son, a
husband to his wife, a king to his subject? But GOD has mercy on whom he wills,
Romans 9:15. Nor does God sin by not showing mercy, whereas an earthly father,
by not showing mercy to his chil-

[Chapter XXVI, Part D; printed page 425; IA PDF page 483; BSB canvas 477]

-dren, and a mother, by denying milk to her infant, would sin with a detestable
`ἀστοργία`, lack of natural affection. 2. To discharge the office of the first
cause, either by predetermining or by codetermining toward an act, is not to
will badly but to will well. That human beings abuse predetermination is not
GOD’s fault but theirs. 3. If these titles are pressed, neither a father ought
to permit his offspring, nor a king his subjects, nor a husband his bride, nor
a friend his friend, to rush into sin—which is the road to eternal
destruction—if they could prevent it, as God can. It is therefore necessary
that something be lawful here for GOD which is not lawful for an earthly
father, king, or husband. 4. The office of Father, King, and Bridegroom is
preserved in GOD toward those for whom he is Father, King, and Bridegroom in
CHRIST, by his predetermining their wills to final perseverance in the state of
grace. 5. By his approving will, God wills even for the reprobate the enjoyment
of spiritual feasts, although by his decreeing will he decreed that they would
never enjoy the banquets to which he invites them. God invites all to life and
justification through the works of the Law, Romans 10:5: “The one who performs
those things will live by them.” Yet he decreed that no mortal would live by
those works, but that the just would live by faith, Habakkuk 2; Romans 1:17.
6. GOD’s discharging the office of the first cause by concurring toward an act
is not forsaking. For forsaking is the withdrawal of grace and of GOD’s
gracious concurrence; but the bestowal of concurrence cannot be the denial of
concurrence. 7. But it is well indeed that our adversaries, without any regard
for divine grace, should want us in every supernatural act, through the action
of free choice, to precede GOD’s grace and always to forestall the influx of
most efficacious grace, so that our freedom becomes mistress and grace her
attendant. What is Pelagianism, if this doctrine is true? 8. The passage in 2
Chronicles 15:2 concerns GOD’s forsaking in war, not the denial of concurrence.
Nor does the passage in 1 Chronicles 28:9 favor them: “If you seek him, he will
be present to you; but if you forsake him, he will reject you forever.” That is,
if you worship GOD sincerely, all will go well with you and you will be blessed,
as your father David was; but if you fall away from the fear of GOD and true
religion into idolatry, then God will reject you as he rejected Saul. Its
meaning is certainly not this: if you first cooperate with grace by the influx
of free choice and determine GOD’s concurrence, GOD will supply concurrence to
you; but if not, God will deny it to you. 9. I do not deny that Augustine says
that GOD does not forsake unless he is forsaken, and that Prosper says the same.
But both speak of the holy life of the regenerate in the visible Church,

him.]

[Marginal reference: Augustine, *On Nature and Grace*, chapter 26.]

[Marginal reference: Prosper, response to the seventh objection of Vincentius,
and to that chapter 79, *On Nature and Grace*, of the Gauls.]

whom God freely rewards with further grace when they are watchful and live
piously, while from defectors he deservedly withdraws the grace previously
given. Yet each teaches, against the Pelagians, that grace always precedes free
choice. I also know that Chrysostom and the Greek Fathers, who were less
practiced in the controversies concerning grace—Augustine alone is here for me
in place of them all, being sounder, more polished, and more learned than the
rest—taught that human beings therefore do not obey God when he calls because
they were unwilling, not because of a defect in GOD’s grace. We concede this
in a sound sense. For whatever causality of non-conversion and sin the Fathers
attribute to human beings, they remove from God; but they attribute to human
beings only the moral causality of non-conversion and sin. Therefore the
Fathers’ intention and aim was only to deny that God is the moral cause of
non-conversion and sin and to maintain that the culpable cause of obstinacy
must be laid upon human beings. This we embrace with open arms.

Ruiz: *To predetermine to the material acts of sin is contrary to the bowels of
mercy by which GOD wills all to be saved.* The College of Salamanca replies
that this does not follow, because the predefinition—to which I add
predetermination—that is not given for avoiding sin is not among the things
required for the giving of sufficient aids, nor is it owed here and now; and
although GOD predefines toward the material element of sin, he at the same time
has a will to give sufficient aids. But this solution must be rejected, because
it is contradictory for GOD to will salvation for all, even the reprobate, and
yet to predefine and predetermine them to the material acts of final
impenitence, on the supposition of which God must necessarily will them to
perish eternally. Thus he would will salvation for them and not will it. 2.
Predefinition and predetermination for avoiding the act of final impenitence is
necessary—not absolutely, but on the hypothesis the Dominicans posit, namely,
if God wills each and every person to be saved and wills the aids sufficiently
necessary for obtaining salvation. 3. I say that God’s predetermining to the
material acts of sin is not contrary to GOD’s free mercy, by which he has mercy
on whom he wills. Nor is predetermining toward acts an evil; it belongs to the
free goodness of the first cause. Indeed, Suárez himself says: *Thus GOD does
not simply and absolutely will these evils of fault to occur, although he does
not absolutely will these acts not to occur. But after he foresees that they
will be future, he takes pleasure in them insofar as they are from him, which
is only to take plea-*

[Marginal reference: College of Salamanca, treatise 4, *On the Will of GOD*,
disputation 10, doubt 7, §12, no. 206.]

[Marginal reference: Suárez, *Metaphysics*, volume 1, disputation 22, no. 27.]

[Chapter XXVI, Part D; printed page 427; IA PDF page 485; BSB canvas 479]

*sure in the concurrence he supplies toward them.* But I note, first, that he
says hesitantly that GOD does not will simply and absolutely that the evils of
fault occur. What, then, is the other member of the distinction? Namely, that
he wills the evils of fault in a qualified and conditional way. Yet in this he
inclines strongly toward our position. 2. He says that *GOD does not absolutely
will these acts not to occur.* Therefore, according to sound logic, he
absolutely wills these acts to occur. 3. I note that he wants God to take
pleasure in evil acts after they exist. But why would he not take pleasure in
them before they exist? For they are as evil after they exist as they are
before they exist.

Ruiz: *Whatever end GOD procures and causes in the administration of things was
also intended in the creation of those same things. But the material actions
of sinners are not an end intended by God in the creation of free choice and
of other things. Therefore the material actions of sinners are not an end
procured and caused in their administration; hence, if they are not physically
predetermined, neither are they efficaciously procured. The end in the creation
of free choice was that human beings should know and love GOD. If GOD’s end was
that some should elicit material actions of unbelief and hatred of GOD, since
the number of the damned is greater than that of the elect, the intention of
GOD which concerns a far greater multitude of people should rather have been
affirmed; and it would have to be said absolutely that human beings were
created for the material actions of idolatry, heresy, hatred of GOD, and so
forth, which is contrary to Scripture. This is confirmed because the maker of
an instrument intends that use of the instrument for which he employs it more
frequently and for the most part. It is also confirmed thus: no wise craftsman
makes an instrument intending an abuse of the instrument contrary to his art;
but free choice is an instrument, and the material actions of sinners are a
positive abuse of free choice. A human being, however, who was created in the
image of GOD could not have been created by GOD for such actions.*

[Marginal reference: Ruiz, disputation 35, section 3.]

I reply: 1. I deny the minor premise of the first syllogism. For although the
material actions of sin are not the end of free choice—namely, the end of the
work, since a human being abuses so distinguished a work of the Creator as
free choice when he performs acts of sin, for the nature of free choice tends
toward exercising good acts—nevertheless those actions are the end of God as
agent, who created all things for himself. For God is also the final cause of
those things of which he is the efficient cause. But God is the efficient
cause of material acts. Therefore he is their final cause also, and hence God
intends those acts as an intermediate end,

[Marginal summary: In what respect GOD intends even evil instruments and their
use for his own sake.]

[Chapter XXVI, Part D; printed page 428; IA PDF page 486; BSB canvas 480]

and himself and his glory as the ultimate end. 2. It is no wonder that the
knowledge and love of GOD are called the end of the human race in general,
because the human race was instituted and created for that end by the nature
and native condition of the rational human workmanship, although most do not
attain that end, by the intention of GOD who permits them to stray from it for
his own glory. For the designation comes from the end of the work, not from the
end of the agent. And the Jesuit is bold who would not blush to teach the Holy
Spirit how to speak in the Scriptures. Nowhere in Scripture is it written, not
even by a hundredth-order consequence, that GOD intends the obedience of all
human beings as an end. It is well known that in Scripture a designation is
taken from the more excellent part, even though its number is far the smallest:
Job 1:29; John 3:16; John 6:45; John 12:32; Romans 11:12, 15; Isaiah 40:5; Joel
2:28. 3. It is the bold presumption of the flesh to say that God would not be a
wise maker of an instrument—that is, of free choice—if he intended the material
actions of sin, that is, the abuse of the instrument. For the positive and
entitative actions underlying malice are not the abuse of free choice but its
use and an entitatively good act, which becomes evil and irregular through the
human being’s deviation from the norm of the law. 4. It is true that a human
being created in GOD’s image could not have been created for the material acts
of sin as the end of the work; I deny it if they are taken as the end of the
agent. The end of the sun is that it should move and illuminate the earth, and
this is the end of the work itself, or of the solar workmanship, which by its
nature tends toward motions and illuminations as its end. Yet no one, I think,
will say that the intention of GOD as agent and creator of the sun was not that
the sun should stand motionless at Joshua’s prayers and, while the Savior was
suffering, should not illuminate the earth even during the day. Otherwise
miracles would happen contrary to and beyond GOD’s intention. Likewise, if we
now consider nature in its fallen state, the end of the work in creating lions
is that they should devour animals, and of fire that it should consume all
combustible things. But the end of GOD as agent is both that lions should
devour and that they should refrain from devouring Daniel, and that fire
should burn and that it should suspend its act of burning the three youths in
the Babylonian furnace. One thing, therefore, is the end of the nature of the
workmanship; another is the end of GOD the workman. The end of the element of
water is that it should float over the earth on every side as an element
lighter and rarer than the heavier and denser earth; but the end of GOD in
creating the waters is that they should contain themselves within channels and
pas-

[Marginal summary: The end of the work and the end of the agent in God’s
creation.]

[Chapter XXVI, Part D; printed page 429; IA PDF page 487; BSB canvas 481]

-sages of the earth, with an opposing boundary so that they do not cover the
earth, Psalm 104:9; Jeremiah 5:22. Those, therefore, who want the end of the work
and the end of the agent to be one and the same here say that all miracles,
changes of nature, deviations, and excesses or defects are beyond GOD’s
intention and therefore happen by chance, which is absurd. I therefore say that
the end of the work of a human being created in GOD’s image is that he should
serve the Creator and enjoy him forever. But the end of GOD as agent is very
different: namely, that in some he should reveal the glory of punitive justice,
in others that of sparing mercy.

18. Ruiz: *If GOD predetermines to the material actions of sin, the complaint
of human beings would be proved true when they say that GOD, from hatred and a
passion for punishing, disposed and arranged all their affairs—even those which
seemed especially to be signs of benevolence—so that the people would say truly,
Deuteronomy 1, “The Lord hates us, and therefore he brought us out of the land
of Egypt to deliver us into the hand of the Amorite and destroy us.” And Judas
was admitted to familiarity with Christ for this end: that GOD might
predetermine him to the most intense material affection of avarice, by which he
would in reality esteem money more highly than Christ and be eternally
punished. Likewise, GOD neither condemns nor punishes what he himself has done;
but GOD condemns and punishes the material actions of sin, as in Matthew 16,
“He will repay each according to his works”; Matthew 10, “Whoever denies me,”
and so forth; Matthew 23, “You devour widows’ houses,” and so forth. This is
proved: punishment must be proportioned to the punished action under the
formal aspect under which it is punished; but punishment is proportioned to
glorification and delights, which, insofar as they are such, are real and
positive actions. Therefore real and positive actions are punished under some
real and positive aspect—namely, that of delight and the enjoyment of temporal
goods. Malice is punished primarily, and the material action as its origin and
subject.*

[Marginal reference: Ruiz, *On the Will of GOD*, disputation 35, section 4,
no. 1.]

I reply: 1. Those who deny that GOD has disposed all things from a passion—or
rather, from the will to declare his glory—both by punishing and by forgiving
sins, deny providence itself. For he made all things for himself. And it cannot
be denied that, among the reprobate, GOD ordains even things that are signs of
benevolence to their ruin: for example, their table, Psalm 119; Psalm 69:22,
and the preaching of the word of the gospel to them so that they may be
hardened, Matthew 13:13; Isaiah 6:9. 2. Nor, on that account, does the complaint
in Deuteronomy 1, “The Lord hates us,” cease to be unjust. For God brought his
church out of Egypt by gratuitous love in order to give them the land promised
to Abraham,

[Chapter XXVI, Part D; printed page 430; IA PDF page 488; BSB canvas 482]

and not from hatred and an intention to destroy them through the Amorites, as
they unjustly complained. Nor was it false that God freed some from Egypt in
order to lay their corpses low in the wilderness. For their slaughter occurred
either by GOD’s counsel or by fortune; and that the impious objected this to
GOD as unjust when he was nevertheless free of injustice was an unjust
complaint. And who besides an atheist most hostile to GOD’s providence would
dare deny that, because God held Esau in eternal hatred and rejected him before
he had done anything evil, life was therefore bestowed on him and he was
permitted to sin, Romans 9; and that the dignity of the apostolate was bestowed
on Judas so that it might issue in ruin for him, and this by GOD’s wise counsel?
Meanwhile Judas’s ruin was only an intermediate end of GOD. I do not add here
more than what I said above concerning this matter. 3. I utterly deny that the
material actions of sin are formally either punished by God or hated by GOD.
God does not punish or hate what is good and a positive entity; but such are
the material actions of sin. 2. What God does not formally prohibit, he does
not formally punish; but it was proved above that such acts are not formally
prohibited. 3. The passages of Scripture prove nothing. GOD repays each person
according to works insofar as they are evil and under the formal aspect of
malice, and punishment is proportioned to Babylonian glorification and
delights insofar as those acts were evil and unlawful. Therefore the minor
premise is false: malice formally and alone is condemned and punished, while
the material action is not punished even as its subject.

19. Ruiz: *In the material action of sin, does GOD initiate the action only
insofar as it is an abstracted and prescinded action that does not include a
positive and real action contrary to right reason and GOD’s law? Or does GOD
initiate the common character of volition by impressing upon the will an
inclination to good, and if any other character can be found in the aforesaid
volition which does not conflict with reason and the divine law, does GOD
initiate that also? But GOD does not initiate the material action of sin
according to the specific character of the action by which it really conflicts
with reason and GOD’s law, because in that way he would by that very fact
initiate the malice which follows from the aforesaid real contrariety, not
accidentally, but altogether of itself and necessarily. This is proved: the
Semi-Pelagians are rightly refuted because they said that merit, obtaining, and
disposition to grace are given from the powers of nature, thus furnishing from
the powers of nature the beginning of predestination and justification,*

[Marginal reference: disputation 36, section 1.]

[Chapter XXVI, Part D; printed page 431; IA PDF page 489; BSB canvas 483]

*and yet they did not connect grace with works necessarily and of itself. For
they said that only because of divine mercy was the attainment of grace from
human works decreed beyond the worthiness of the works. But not even divine
omnipotence can prevent malice from following from the material act of sin;
therefore, whoever determines the created will to the material action of hatred
of GOD supplies the beginning of sin, even under the aspect by which it really
desires any evil whatsoever contrary to the divine law.*

I reply: 1. It accords with the principles of neither logic nor metaphysics to
say that volition in general, and insofar as it is a prescinded action, does not
conflict with God’s law, and yet that this volition limited and contracted to
this particular volition that conflicts with GOD’s law does conflict with
GOD’s law. Namely, in this way God by his concurrence is the cause of volition
but not of this material volition; he is the cause of a particular entity but
not the cause of this entity; and he is the cause of the material action of sin
but not the cause of this material action of sin. He is the cause of volition,
but not of this volition. Therefore, in material and positive acts God will be
the cause of entity, the cause of action, but not of this action. In that case
he is likewise the author and creator of Peter insofar as Peter is an entity,
a substance, and a body, but he is not Peter’s author and cause insofar as
Peter is a human being produced and begotten through culpable generation. Alas,
how greatly the Lord has made foolish the wisdom of this world! But this
material act of sin is a positive entity. Therefore it will either be no less
causally from God than action in general and entity in general, or else it will
be some positive effect of a second cause which is not from the first cause.
But this is Manichaean. 2. None of us teaches that *GOD initiates the material
action of sin according to the specific character by which it really conflicts
with GOD’s law.* For in that way God would initiate the act under the formal
aspect of malice. But we teach that *GOD initiates the material action of sin
according to the specific character of a positive and entitative act.* If the
Jesuit denies this, he will be a Manichaean who must posit an evil principle as
its first cause. But we deny that *GOD initiates the material action of sin
according to the specific character of malice and insofar as the act is
contrary to God’s law.* From this the Jesuit proves only that God furnishes the
physical beginning of sin, not its ethical and moral beginning. This gives our
adversaries cause to blush, since they teach: 1. that the created will furnishes
the physical beginning of faith, hope, love, and all supernatural acts; 2. that
the same created will is the first cause and furnishes the beginning of all the
actions of the second cause.

[Marginal summary: GOD predetermines to the material act insofar as it is this
act, and not insofar as it morally conflicts with God’s law.]

[Chapter XXVI, Part D; printed page 432; IA PDF page 490; BSB canvas 484]

Thus they dismiss the first cause from the office of first cause. 3. It is no
wonder, as I taught above, that GOD cannot even by omnipotence prevent malice
from following from the real contrariety of the act and the divine law. For if
the act is contrary to GOD’s law, it cannot, without contradiction, fail to be
evil; and from an evil act malice cannot fail to follow. The Semi-Pelagians are
rightly condemned, implicated in the same charge as the Jesuits, because both
groups attributed to the human being the determining beginning of supernatural
acts. But it is utterly false either that malice follows an act of itself and
necessarily or that GOD simply cannot procure the act and prevent the act’s
malice by efficacious grace.

20. Ruiz: *The Fathers and Councils prove from this that all operations useful
for justification are supernatural and require GOD’s supernatural concurrence
and operation: because it is said of them all, “Without me you can do nothing”;
our sufficiency is from GOD, not from ourselves; GOD works in us to will and to
accomplish; the will is drawn by GOD; GOD precedes and rouses us; we have
nothing which we have not received; and so forth. But if GOD predetermines to
acts, then GOD can say, “Without me you cannot sin,” and GOD will work the
willing to hate God. No efficacious argument for proving the necessity of
supernatural concurrence would then be drawn from those testimonies.*

I reply. Those testimonies must not be understood of God’s predetermination,
which belongs to the natural order, but of what is plainly supernatural. And it
would be false to say, “Without me”—as the root into which you must be engrafted
through faith like branches, as in John 15—“you cannot sin.” 2. It is false
that God precedes, rouses, and draws wills and brings it about that they will to
hate GOD. This by no means follows from GOD’s predetermination to the material
acts of sin except by this utterly empty consequence: God predetermines to the
material acts of sin; therefore he also predetermines to the malice of those
acts. Although we require GOD’s predetermination for the act, we do not require
it for the malice.

21. Ruiz: *The material action of hatred of GOD is altogether determined to an
evil use. Therefore GOD is not its author. And physical predetermination is
also determined to an evil use. And the material action of hatred of GOD is in
its quiddity the evil use of free choice itself.*

[Marginal reference: Ruiz, disputation 38, section 1.]

[Chapter XXVI, Part D; printed page 433; IA PDF page 491; BSB canvas 485]

*And no one can convert it to a good use. And it is impossible to abuse
predetermination more disgracefully and inordinately than GOD in fact abuses
it.*

I reply: 1. The material act of hatred of GOD is essentially evil, and God does
not predetermine to that act as such. Rather, he predetermines to the entitative
act which underlies hatred of GOD, and that act is not an evil use of free
choice. 2. The Jesuit’s assertion that *the material act of hatred of GOD is in
its quiddity an evil use of free choice* completely overthrows his argument.
For we do not teach that God predetermines to an act that is evil in its
quiddity. And it is no wonder that an act which essentially includes malice
cannot be converted to good. Our thesis, however, is that God predetermines a
merely entitative and physical act in the physical order; the Jesuit,
deliberately misleading us, argues from the act in the moral order. 3. It is
true that God is not the author of a thing which of itself and by its own nature
is determined to an evil use; but God may certainly be the author of a thing
which is determined to an evil use accidentally, through the fault and abuse
of free choice that converts that thing to evil. 4. It is also true that, on
the hypothesis and by modal necessity, the human being cannot abuse that same
individual predetermination more disgracefully than he in fact abuses it. Yet,
speaking absolutely and by God’s grace, he could have used that predetermination
when elevated and qualified by God’s grace far more honorably. And it is
blasphemy to assert that GOD abuses his own predetermination, for he uses it
most rightly in the office of the first cause by promoting the act, while the
human being abuses it most foully by promoting the act’s malice. This argument
also harms our adversaries’ cause. For, on the hypothesis that the depraved
will has determined God’s concurrence to that abuse, God cannot use his
universal concurrence otherwise than toward hatred of himself and blasphemy of
his own name.

# William Ames, *Bellarminus enervatus*, Tome II (1633)

> **Working edition in progress:** This directory contains an AI-assisted,
> diplomatic-normalized Latin transcription and an independent close English
> translation. Neither file has been independently reviewed and neither is
> publication-ready. Page images, not OCR, control the text.

## Work and edition

- **Author:** William Ames (1576–1633)
- **Title:** *Bellarminus enervatus, sive Disputationes Anti-Bellarminianae*
- **Unit:** Tome II only
- **Edition used:** London, Humphrey Robinson, 1633
- **Tome structure:** six books, on councils, the Church militant, the members
  of the Church militant, the Church suffering in purgatory, the Church
  triumphant, and the sacraments

The 1633 title page describes the edition as revised and enlarged by the
author (`Ab Auctore recognitus & auctus`). The present directory does not use
or reproduce any modern English translation.

## Controlling public-domain witness

The controlling witness is the 1633 EEBO microfilm copy made openly
downloadable by the Internet Archive as
[`bim_early-english-books-1475-1640_bellarminus-enervatus-_ames-william_1633`](https://archive.org/details/bim_early-english-books-1475-1640_bellarminus-enervatus-_ames-william_1633).
It supplies 851 scan frames, a downloadable page-image PDF, individual scan
images, DjVu XML, and OCR. Ames died in 1633 and the witness is in the public
domain. The scan images alone govern this transcription; OCR is used only for
navigation and as a transcription aid.

The scan is a composite of all four tomes. Tome II begins at IA scan `n218`:

- IA scan `n218`: Tome II title page, dated 1633; excluded from Part 01.
- IA scan `n219`: dedication to Ernest Casimir, dated Franeker, 20 November
  1625; excluded from Part 01.
- IA scan `n220`: Tome II, Book I begins on printed p. 3.
- IA scans `n220`–`n231`: the complete opening Part 01 unit, printed pp. 3–14.
- The remainder of Tome II after the Chapter III heading on scan `n231` is
  outside the present unit.

The source catalogue labels the OCR language incorrectly as Middle English.
The work itself is Latin, with occasional Greek. The microfilm has intermittent
edge shadow and show-through, so individual letters were checked against the
full-resolution scan rather than accepted from OCR.

The public-domain 1629 Harvard/Google scan at
[`bellarminusener00amesgoog`](https://archive.org/details/bellarminusener00amesgoog)
was consulted only as a comparison witness where automated OCR was especially
poor. No comparison reading was adopted unless confirmed in the 1633 image;
the 1633 witness remains controlling throughout.

## Editorial method

Latin long *s*, ligatures, line-end divisions, and typographic `u/v` and `i/j`
are normalized. Substantive wording, spelling, numbering, headings, Greek,
citations, and the alternating `Bellarm.` / `Protest.` argument structure are
retained. Running heads, signatures, catchwords, printed page numbers, and
ornaments are omitted from the prose but represented by exact source markers.

The English is a full close translation of the paired Latin, not a summary.
Bellarmine's stated arguments and Ames's replies are both translated in full.
No modern translation was used as a wording source. Both files remain
AI-assisted and unreviewed.

## Part 01

**Part 01 is complete as an AI-assisted working draft.** It contains the
opening coherent twelve-page body unit of Tome II: Book I, Chapters I–II, on
the convocation of a council and voting rights in a general council. It begins
at `TOMUS II. LIBER PRIMUS. DE CONCILIIS.` on printed p. 3 / IA scan `n220`
and ends on printed p. 14 / IA scan `n231` with `Iudicium populi coniuncti est
ut a nonnullis vocatur populicum, atque adeo publicum, non privatum.` The
`CAP. III. De Iudicio Concilii quale sit` heading and all following text on the
shared final page are excluded.

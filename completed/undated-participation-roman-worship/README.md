# Whether Participation in the Worship of the Roman Synagogue Is Permissible

This directory presents the complete Latin and a new English translation of
the consilium headed *Qui liceat participare cultui Romanae synagogae* in the
*Calvini Opera*.

## Work identity

| Field | Value |
|---|---|
| Author | John Calvin (collection attribution) |
| CO consilium | II.6, *Qui liceat participare cultui Romanae synagogae* |
| Internal date | 21 June 1558 |
| Place and addressee | Place and name not preserved; addressed to a “best and dearest brother,” with a plural final blessing |
| Principal presentation form | Latin; CO gives no translation notice or manuscript witness for this item |
| Genre | Epistolary theological and pastoral advice |
| Principal topics | Participation in Roman worship, the Jewish temple and synagogue, apostolic prayer, Paul's vow in Acts 21, the abrogation of Mosaic ceremonies, adiaphora, weak believers, sacrifice and expiation, papal holy water, and baptism |
| CO locator | X/1, pp. 189–192 |

Although the consilia collection often suppresses dates, this document is not
chronologically undated: its final sentence gives 21 June 1558. It does not,
however, preserve Calvin's place, the recipient's name, or a closing signature.
The singular address and plural blessing permit the inference that the
individual recipient belonged to a wider group, but do not identify them.

“Roman synagogue” is the heading's polemical name for the Roman church. The
body's discussion of the Jerusalem temple and Jewish synagogues is a
historical and theological comparison; it does not make the title a reference
to Jewish worship. The work asks whether apostolic participation in still
valid, originally divine Jewish rites can justify sharing papal prayers.
Calvin's answer distinguishes the origin and content of the actions rather
than treating mere physical presence as decisive.

## Edition, heading, and publication state

The transcription follows Wilhelm Baum, Eduard Cunitz, and Eduard Reuss,
eds., *Ioannis Calvini opera quae supersunt omnia*, vol. X/1 (*Corpus
Reformatorum* XXXVIII) (Brunswick: C. A. Schwetschke, 1871), pp. 189–192.

CO's sole source note records earlier printings in the Geneva *Epistolae et
responsa*, p. 328; Lausanne, p. 435; Hanover, p. 487; Chouet, p. 450; and
Amsterdam, p. 205. It cites no manuscript, alternate language state, or
textual variant for this item. “Latin-form” here therefore describes the
witness CO prints; it does not by itself prove that no lost vernacular draft
ever existed.

The displayed heading and bibliographical footnote are editorial or
publication paratext and are excluded from the historical body. The body
begins *Quia de quaestionibus quas moves* on p. 189 and ends with the blessing
and date on p. 192. Running heads, printed page numbers, and the terminal rule
are excluded. The embedded Acts, 2 Timothy, and Romans locators belong to the
main text and are retained.

This item begins on the same CO page on which *Ut se pius homo a papatus
superstitionibus subducat* ends. They are separate letters: the earlier item
has its own conclusion and signature before the new heading, while this item
has a new opening, argument, farewell, blessing, and independent 1558 date.

## Facsimile and source audit

The public facsimile is supplied by the University of Geneva's [complete
*Calvini Opera* collection](https://archive-ouverte.unige.ch/unige:650) and
its [direct CO X/1
PDF](https://access.archive-ouverte.unige.ch/access/metadata/f526a338-c95c-4f87-b600-04df723f502b/download).
The captured 362-page PDF is 33,386,580 bytes with SHA-256
`025973d6e4533769d0c2b870dd324497943bc14050eb2bcbcc4c964d4f2a1777`.

The exact one-indexed mapping is:

| UNIGE PDF page | Printed CO pages | Relevant contents |
|---:|---:|---|
| 116 | 189–190 | End of the preceding consilium; this item's title, source note, and p. 189 opening; continuation on p. 190 |
| 117 | 191–192 | Remainder, farewell, blessing, date, and terminal rule |

All four printed columns were collated line by line against 300-DPI,
2500 × 3150-pixel renderings. Extracted text served only as orientation. The
p. 189/190 boundary falls between *angustiis* and *quibus*; the p. 190/191
boundary falls between *et* and *papales*. Neither divides a word. The p.
191/192 turn divides *eximo-rent*. The transcription joins *eximerent* and
places the p. 192 marker before the complete word. Latin and English both
preserve the exact marker sequence 189, 190, 191, 192.

No authorial reading is illegible or conjectural. Because CO prints no variant
apparatus for the body, the project's principal textual crux is syntactic, not
paleographical: at p. 192 the scope of *non* in *non ideo ... se expiare, aut
voto eucharistico ... defungi* can appear to prohibit both sacrificial
expiation and Paul's thanksgiving vow, while p. 190 calls temple entry and a
sacred offering permissible when used to attest worship of Israel's God. The
English retains the surface coordination and documents the tension rather
than emending *aut* or silently narrowing the negative.

The final classification also requires care. The grammar groups together
rites pure in origin but later abolished and rites that degenerated through
superstitious use; it sets both apart from sheer superstitions fabricated to
corrupt divine worship. Treating all three as morally identical would erase
the argument.

## Translation status

Mary D. Beaty and Benjamin Wirt Farley's copyrighted *Calvin's Ecclesiastical
Advice* (1991) translates the complete 46-item *consilia* corpus. The audit
located no complete reusable public-domain English version of this document.
No wording from the modern translation was consulted or reused;
[`english.md`](english.md) is newly translated from the normalized Latin and
checked against the facsimile.

The English is an **AI-assisted scholarly draft**, not a peer-reviewed critical
edition. It preserves Calvin's distinctions among a rite's divine or corrupt
origin, the intent and setting of participation, expiatory sacrifice,
thanksgiving, temporary weakness, and ceremonies he treats as indifferent.
Readers making exact claims should cite the Latin and printed page.

Suggested citation:

> John Calvin, *Qui liceat participare cultui Romanae synagogae*, 21 June
> 1558, *Calvini Opera* X/1 (1871), 189–192, normalized Latin and draft English
> translation, *Reformed Retrieval*.

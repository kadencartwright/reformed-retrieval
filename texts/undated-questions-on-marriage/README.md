# Questions on Marriage

This directory presents the complete Latin text and a new English translation
of the consilium headed *Ad quaestiones coniugales* in the *Calvini Opera*.

## Work identity

| Field | Value |
|---|---|
| Author | John Calvin (collection attribution) |
| CO consilium | V.1, *Ad quaestiones coniugales* |
| Date | Undated |
| Language/form presented by CO | Latin |
| Genre | Four short responses on marriage law and pastoral discipline |
| Subjects | Consanguinity and affinity; remarriage after adultery; incapacity and nullity; minor betrothals |
| CO locator | X/1, pp. 231–233 |

CO places the item beneath the divisional heading *Quaestiones matrimoniales*
and the more specific heading *Ad quaestiones coniugales*. These are
organizing titles in the printed edition, not titles verified against an
authorial manuscript. The body supplies no addressee, signature, dateline, or
other basis for assigning a date.

The response structure also requires care. The first answer is not printed
with a numeral; the next three are explicitly labeled `II.`, `III.`, and
`IV.`. The Latin preserves that state rather than silently inserting `I.`.
The English likewise leaves the first answer unnumbered and retains the three
printed numerals.

## Edition and transmission

The transcription follows Wilhelm Baum, Eduard Cunitz, and Eduard Reuss,
eds., *Ioannis Calvini opera quae supersunt omnia*, vol. X/1 (*Corpus
Reformatorum* XXXVIII) (Brunswick: C. A. Schwetschke, 1871), pp. 231–233.

CO reports the item in the Geneva *Epistolae et responsa* at p. 359 and in
the Lausanne, Hanover, Chouet, and Amsterdam collections at pp. 671, 754,
493, and 225 respectively. The note cites no autograph, manuscript, variant,
or date. This project therefore presents the CO main-text edition state and
does not claim access to Calvin's authorial document.

The public facsimile is supplied by the University of Geneva's [complete
*Calvini Opera* collection](https://archive-ouverte.unige.ch/unige:650) and
its [direct CO X/1 PDF](https://access.archive-ouverte.unige.ch/access/metadata/f526a338-c95c-4f87-b600-04df723f502b/download).
The relevant surrogate pages are:

| PDF page | CO material |

| 137 | logical pp. 231–232, beneath the divisional heading |
| 138 | p. 233, upper left column |

The running numbers are suppressed on the divisional spread at PDF p. 137;

## Scope and collation

`latin.txt` includes every word from *In quibus gradibus consanguinitatis* on
logical p. 231 through *parentes de ipsis male transegerant* on p. 233. It
excludes the divisional and item headings, CO's source footnote, running
furniture, and the following French consilium beginning on p. 233.

Every line and page turn was checked against 600-DPI renderings of both
facsimile pages. Printer line wrapping and line-end divisions are removed;
CO's spelling, capitalization, punctuation, biblical locators, and response
numbers are otherwise retained. The p. 231–232 turn divides *si-mul*. The
normalized transcription joins it as *simul* and places the p. 232 marker
before the complete word. The p. 232–233 marker falls between the separate
words *Itaque / lusus*. No reading is illegible or conjecturally supplied.

## Translation cautions

- The kinship terms *patruelis* and *consobrinus* distinguish cousin
  relationships through paternal and maternal lines. The English explains
  the distinction rather than pretending that one modern English kinship
  label reproduces every Roman-law nuance.
- In response II, the grammar identifies a condemned and an innocent party
  but does not name the persons or facts of a particular divorce. The English
  does not supply a case history.
- Response III's *frigidos et eunuchos* is translated “impotent men and
  eunuchs.” Its account of sex, disability, consent, and marital purpose is an
  early-modern theological-legal argument, not present-day medical or legal
  guidance.
- Response IV rejects binding children to marriages contracted before
  puberty. Its vocabulary of parental authority, financial penalties, and
  legal actions is translated historically and should not be mistaken for a
  statement of current law.

## Translation status

John H. Leith's edited volume *Calvin's Ecclesiastical Advice*, translated by
Mary D. Beaty and Benjamin W. Farley (1991), contains a copyrighted modern
translation of the *consilia*. It was not consulted and supplied no wording
or interpretation here. No complete public-domain English version of this
item was located in the research audit.

`english.md` is an **AI-assisted scholarly draft**, translated afresh from
the normalized Latin and checked against every printed line. It has not
received independent review by a human Latinist. Cite the Latin and CO page
for exact-wording claims and do not treat this historical translation as
legal advice.

Suggested citation:

> John Calvin, *Ad quaestiones coniugales*, undated, *Calvini Opera* X/1
> (1871), 231–233, normalized Latin and draft English translation, *Reformed
> Retrieval*.

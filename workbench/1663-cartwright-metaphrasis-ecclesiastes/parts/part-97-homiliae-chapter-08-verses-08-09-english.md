VERSE 8. In this whole matter I saw, when I applied my mind to every work done
under the Sun, that there is sometimes a time when one person rules over
another to his own harm.

First Solomon prefaces this whole treatment in order to gain greater
credibility for what he says, when he states that he applied his mind so that
he might understand the whole matter thoroughly. The faithful have several
books, and books of great variety, to wear out and unfold: the book of
creation; the book of divine administration and government, both in the
universe and in the Church; and further, the book of the administration of
that same world and Church insofar as each is administered through human
beings. Solomon professes in this place that he has occupied himself with
this book. Finally, there is Sacred Scripture, which is chief of them all;
skill in it alone opens the eyes to judge all these other things better, and
without its aid we must necessarily be blind and go astray in the rest.

Therefore, when weariness with reading one book creeps upon us, we are
permitted to pass to another. But in them all let us remember to apply our
mind; unless we bring it to the study of wisdom, our labor will be empty. For
it is clear that the holy Prophets of God, although so inspired by the Spirit
of God that they could not stray even in the smallest degree from the truth
(since the Holy Spirit directed both their tongues and their pens),
nevertheless employed effort and diligence to search out and investigate the
things of which they spoke. See 1 Peter 1:10–11; 2 Peter 3:15–16; 2 Timothy
4:13. How much more must we do this, who are liable to errors both in speaking
and in writing?

Let us now come to the first part, concerning the unjust dominion of certain
Princes and Prelates, as is evident from what follows in verses 9–10. This
wickedness of princes, cruel toward human beings and ungrateful toward God, is
all the more abominable because God bestows upon them this honor: he sets them
over others and subjects to them people made from the same clay. It is a great
honor, as the Prophet relates, that God subjected the beasts of the field,
the birds of heaven, and the fish of the sea to the human being. But to
subject a human being made in God's image is an act of almost incredible
munificence.

Yet these offices of rule were established for different ends. People are
placed over mute creatures for their own particular advantage, but they are
placed over human beings not so much for their own good as for the good of
those over whom they hold authority. Hence they obtain the appellation of
Fathers, and even of gods, provided that they rule with moderation. Thus God
also shares this honor and honorable appellation with them so that they are
called gods. Those, however, who use that power for Tyranny are turned into
savage and ferocious beasts—or rather, into Demons. Thus far concerning the
first part.

There follows the judgment concerning this administration, which is
twofold: 1. First, the judgment of the Holy Spirit, as far as verse 13. 2.
Then, the judgment of corrupt flesh in the next two verses.

Solomon first sets forth the Holy Spirit's judgment in his customary way. It
is this: Rulers practice Tyranny to their own particular harm. It is plain
and established in common understanding that they hold dominion to the harm
of their subjects, partly by plundering them and partly by leading them into
error through their laws and their own most wicked example. Yet that this is
done to the harm of the Princes themselves seems scarcely probable;
indeed, almost everyone believes that this Tyranny redounds to their benefit.
Although the appearance and mask of good dazzles their eyes, Solomon,
nevertheless, by the prompting of the Holy Spirit declares that it is to
their harm. He said this in words, but it is God

who will give it effect in deed and in fact. They indeed set their own power,

VERSE 9. For I observed the ungodly who had ruled in this manner being buried
after they died and departed from the holy place, and being given over to
oblivion in the city where they had acted so. This also is vanity.

This is a prolepsis. Its objection is the point that the Prophet introduces,
which appears to conflict diametrically with the preceding proposition. Its
answer is found in verses 10 and 11 and in the latter part of verse 9. The
objection is this: we see those Tyrants expire at a ripe age and be buried
magnificently and with great display; epitaphs are composed for them, statues
are erected, and funeral praises are delivered. I understand the word
“burial” in this sense here, Luke 16, where the rich banqueter is said to have
been buried, while mention of Lazarus's burial is passed over—not because he
was not buried, but because no expense was incurred for his funeral.

This is just as if mourning took place because the wretched common people had
been relieved of the burden of Tyranny, or as if the dead man had been some
Josiah worthy to be adorned with the people's tears. For just as to be
deprived of burial and exposed like a donkey in a field to birds' beaks and
dogs' teeth is a curse, so to obtain burial—and indeed one splendid and full
of dignity—is rightly reckoned among God's blessings.

For this is the point of Solomon's statement that they were buried after they
had departed from the holy place—that is, from their office and dominion. By
this he indicates clearly enough that these Tyrants were not removed from
their dignity and greatness as they deserved, but died while still holding
power. Someone may therefore ask how their Tyranny is harmful and destructive
to them, since they remain in the highest honor to the final breath of life
and after death do not lack the honor of burial, and indeed of august and
splendid burial.

Thus far concerning the objection; now let us come to the answer. First,
however, let us note the expression that the Holy Spirit used in describing
the Principate, when he calls the Prince's tribunal and throne a “holy
place.” Hence in Deuteronomy 1:17 judgment is said to be the Lord's, and in 2
Chronicles 19:6 judgment is said to be not human beings' but God's own; and in
Psalm 82 God himself is said to be present in the assembly of Judges.

Therefore, in the holy place within the Commonwealth, care must be taken
concerning Princes: profane people must not be appointed; those already
appointed must be removed from their seat; and people strong in holiness and
justice must be placed in authority—people who will judge without respect of
persons and who are devoted to and lovers of God's worship. Let them protect
the Ministry and preserve and defend the Schools, the seedbed of the Ministry
and Magistracy.

The answer is that this pomp and display of burial will do them no good in
rescuing their name from oblivion. For they are nevertheless given over to
oblivion in that very city where they practiced injustice and were adorned
with burial. The memory of the righteous endures forever, but the name of the
ungodly will stink, Proverbs 10:7. This single oracle of God will prevail
against every effort and ingenious contrivance, against all the splendor and
display by which ungodly people attempt to preserve their name.

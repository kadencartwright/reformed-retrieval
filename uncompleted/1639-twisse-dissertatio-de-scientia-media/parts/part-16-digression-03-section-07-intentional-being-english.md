Nevertheless, to admit the truth, at last he completes the whole matter with an illustration that
is not only familiar but exceedingly apt, saying that a material thing is said to be elevated under
the mode of existing in the intellect. For by this mode of elevation it is said to be immaterial. I
would indeed readily admit that future things are elevated in this manner and made intellectually
present to God—not only in known being but also in willed being—and thereby become knowable as
future things. Just as things are called immaterial by merely extrinsic denomination insofar as
they are in the intellect, so things not yet existing are said by merely extrinsic denomination to
exist in eternity: namely, insofar as they are in God’s eternal will as willed by him and in his
eternal intellect as known by him. In this way they are simultaneously in eternity and in
themselves, not according to their real being, but only according to intentional being. For it is
altogether impossible that they possess a proper measurable entity, since in the preceding
argument you already admitted that Antichrist at this instant has no proper entity and is not
anything.

It is asked: Did Arminius teach rightly in his letter to Hippolytus that it
cannot happen that God imputes Christ's righteousness to us as righteousness,
because Christ's obedience is righteousness itself, taken according to the
most severe rigor of the Law, whereas the sinner's righteousness is
gratuitous? Likewise, did Episcopius teach rightly in disputation 22, thesis

[1668 KBNL/UvA witness | logical printed p. 507 | PDF p. 558]

4: “Therefore, it is not properly Christ's righteousness, much less his active
or passive righteousness, or that which Christ merited by each of them and we
obtain through Jesus alone. For Christ's righteousness is not, properly
speaking, that which is imputed, but that on account of which righteousness is
imputed to us who believe in him”? That is: Is Christ's righteousness by no
means imputed to us, but, on account of Christ's righteousness which he has by
dying and making satisfaction for us, has it come about that our good works,
and therefore the act of believing, are imputed to us as righteousness by
God's gracious valuation? The Arminians say yes. Thus Arminius himself,
*Response to Article 31*: “If a Creditor to whom one hundred florins are owed
accepts ten as full payment and thus imputes payment to the Debtor.” The
Papists together with the Socinians assert the same thing. Socinus, *Dialogue
on Justification*, pages 16 and 19; Smalcius, disputation 4 against Frantzius.

We, however, believe that Christ's righteousness is imputed to us and that by
it we are justified before God.

1. Isaiah 53:5: “For he himself is afflicted with grief because of our
defections; he is crushed because of our iniquities; and the chastisement of
our peace is laid upon him, and by his bruise healing is brought to us.” Verse
11: “He himself will carry their iniquities.” Verse 12: “For he himself will
bear the sins of many.” Galatians 3:13: “Christ redeemed us from the curse of
the Law, having been made a curse for us.” Philippians 3:9: “That I may be
found in him, not having my righteousness which is from the Law, but that
which is through faith in Christ, that is, the righteousness which is from God
through faith.” Romans 3:11: “But now

[1668 KBNL/UvA witness | logical printed p. 508 | PDF p. 559]

apart from the Law, the righteousness of God has been manifested.” Verse 22:
“The righteousness of God through faith in Christ, unto all and upon all who
believe.” Verse 23: “For all have sinned.” Verse 24: “As those who are
justified freely, that is, by his grace, through the redemption accomplished
in Jesus Christ.” Now this righteousness in verses 21–22 is directly opposed
to the inherent righteousness in verse 23 which is stained by sin. But the
former is solely the imputed righteousness of Christ.

2. For our justification consists in this imputation. Romans 4:6: “Just as
David also declares that person blessed to whom God imputes righteousness
apart from works,” saying in verse 7, “Blessed are those whose iniquities are
remitted and whose sins are covered.”

3. For by dying Christ made satisfaction to God in the place and stead of
sinners, just as if they themselves had undergone death in their own person.
For by dying and obeying the Father Christ acquired a righteousness not merely
personal but surety-righteousness. For the one who exactly pays the penalties
owed for sin and fulfills God's law by obeying is righteous. But he paid not
for himself but for others, and he obeyed the Father both for himself and for
us. And surety-righteousness and sponsor-righteousness belong properly to
those for whom the pledge and suretyship are undertaken.

4. Scripture says expressly that Christ was made our righteousness by dying
for us and obeying the Father, and that for us he was made a victim, a curse,
and an offering for our sins. Second Corinthians 5:21; Romans 3:25; Romans
5:96, 7, 10; John 15:

[1668 KBNL/UvA witness | logical printed p. 509 | PDF p. 560]

13; 1 Peter 3:18; Isaiah 53:12. But it is nowhere written that Christ died and
obeyed the Father for the purpose that our good works, performed by the powers
of grace, might satisfy the righteousness of the Law and be regarded as our
righteousness before the Tribunal of the fearsome Judge—as though Christ's act
of justifying, redeeming, and saving consisted in this, that through his
merits Christ made us the Justifiers, Redeemers, and Saviors of ourselves. For
then the power of Christ's merits would be that he had communicated to our
works the power of meriting justification, remission of sins, and eternal
life. This fruit of Christ's death appears nowhere in all Scripture.

5. For that sole argument of Arminius to the contrary does not establish its
conclusion. Christ's righteousness is surety-righteousness, entirely
conformed to God's Law and also gratuitous. It is conformed to God's Law
because Christ most perfectly satisfied the rigor of the Law in every respect.
And insofar as it is imputed to us—and formally, so that it becomes ours by
God's active imputation or reckoning and by our faith graciously apprehending
it—it is altogether gratuitous. These things do not conflict. For if a Sponsor
pays a thousand florins in full for a friend, so that the debtor owes nothing
more, then by legal justice that payment is perfect, rigorously and according
to the rigor of the law. But insofar as that payment is imputed to the
bankrupt, who was unable to pay, it is a gratuitous payment.

ROMANS 4:3.

“For what does Scripture say? Abraham believed God, and it was imputed to him
for righteousness.”

We acknowledge here a metonymical and figurative expression. For that which
we apprehend is imputed to us as righteousness, just as someone says, “My hand
made me rich”; that is, “The riches acquired by the industry and labor of my
hands made me rich.” Hence

It is asked: Is the act of believing imputed to the believer, not figuratively,
correlatively, and metonymically, but properly, so that it is our formal
righteousness before God's Tribunal? Arminius affirms this in his disputation
*On Justification*, as does Bellarmine, *On Justification*, book 1, chapter 17.
We deny it.

1. For we are not justified from any work in us. Ephesians 2:5: “By grace you
have been saved.” Titus 3:5: “Not from works of righteousness which we had
done.” Romans 11:6: “If it is from works, grace would no longer be grace.”

2. For “God's judgment is according to truth,” Romans 2:2. God cannot regard
the act of believing as the perfect fulfillment of the Law, because to believe
is not exactly and in every respect to keep the Law.

3. We are justified “through God's grace and the redemption which is in
Christ,” Romans 3:24, and through Christ's imputed righteousness. Therefore,
we are not at the same time justified through any acts in us.

4. It is Ἄγραφον—unwritten or unscriptural—that Christ merited that our faith
should merit the remission of

[1668 KBNL/UvA witness | logical printed p. 511 | PDF p. 562 | split word joined: peccatorum]

sins and eternal life, since faith, actions, and all our sufferings are not
worthy of that glory which is to be revealed. Romans 8:18; 2 Corinthians
5:17–18.

5. The righteousness in which we must be clothed when we appear before God's
Tribunal is free from sin and cannot coexist with sin. Romans 3:9, 22–23;
Galatians 3:10–13. But the faith of the elect coexists with sin. First John
1:8; Psalms 130:3 and 143:2; Proverbs 20:9.

1 JOHN 1:8.

“If we say that we have no sin, we deceive ourselves, and the truth is not in
us.”

It is asked: Can we, through the grace of God which he bestows upon the elect
and faithful in this life, keep the Law perfectly? The Remonstrants, together
with the Papists and Socinians, say yes. We deny it.

1. Besides the Apostle John asserting the contrary in the passage cited, it
is said in Proverbs 20:9, “Who can say, ‘I have purified my soul; I am clean
from my sin’?” James 3:2: “In many things we all offend or stumble.” First
Kings 3:46: “There is no one who does not sin.” Ecclesiastes 7:20: “For there
is no righteous person upon earth who does good and does not sin.”

2. For “cursed is everyone who does not remain in all the things written in
the book of the Law, to do them.” Therefore, those who seek Justification from
the works of the Law are cursed, because the rationale of the inference is
none other than this:

[1668 KBNL/UvA witness | logical printed p. 512 | PDF p. 563]

no one, whether regenerate or unregenerate, can remain in all the things
written in the Book of the Law.

3. For a culpable defect and inability to fulfill the Law inheres in everyone,
and a cause which is morally impure and defective cannot produce effects which
are morally pure and perfect. And Paul, a chosen vessel, says truly of himself
in Romans 7:14, “The Law is spiritual, but I am carnal and sold under sin.”
Verse 18: “For I know that no good dwells in me, that is, in my flesh.” Verse
19: “But I see another Law in my members, warring against the Law of my mind
and making me captive—to the Law of sin which is in my members.”

4. Fallen human beings who have not yet been fully reformed to God's image and
who are bound to love God with all their heart, all their soul, and all their
powers cannot keep the Law perfectly, even through God's grace granted in
this life. But all the regenerate are such persons and are bound to love God
in that way, because this is the sum and end of the Law, to which all mortals
without distinction are obligated. The rationale for the major premise is
that no one after the fall can love God most intensely in this life, because
there is no love of God here which cannot be increased and perfected.

The Adversaries' inventions are empty and false: that human beings are bound
to love God above all things objectively, by willing for God the

[1668 KBNL/UvA witness | logical printed p. 513 | PDF p. 564 | split word joined: maximum]

greatest good, and above all things by valuation, by preferring God to all
other things; but that we are not bound to the end of the Law except in
intention and endeavor—that is, that the Law does not bind us to love God
above all things intensively, because this is impossible in this life. But the
Law does not bind anyone to the impossible.

Response 1. This distinction is empty and deceptive. For from a greater
valuation of some person or thing than of himself, the Savior, Christ infers a
less intense love of the Savior. Matthew 10:37; Luke 14:26–27. For the one who
loves God with less love than created things, and therefore makes less of and
values God less than parents and children, loves God too slackly and tepidly,
according to the Savior's testimony, and loves the creature more intensely
than the Creator. And the one who loves God above all things by valuation and
thinks that he should be preferred to every lovable object loves God most
intensely. For the degree of valuation of the thing loved is followed by the
degree, measure, and intensity of love. But there is none of the saints in
this life who does not often, by a disordered and excessive love of himself,
his own, or even sin, violate this love of God and show in practice that he
makes more of those things than of God—as godly Eli did in 1 Samuel 2:29, and
as all the saints do when and as often as they sin. And thus the members of
this distinction coincide.

2. It is false that the end of the Law does not bind; nor is the rationale
valid that the end is impossible, because

[1668 KBNL/UvA witness | logical printed p. 514 | PDF p. 565 | split word joined: impossibilitas]

this impossibility has been culpably contracted by us. Nor is it an
impossibility simply and absolutely so called, but only qualifiedly and
conditionally.

3. If we are bound to love God with all our powers, then a slack exertion of
our powers as three, if the Law demands an intensity of our powers as eight,
will be a violation of the Law.

4. For no one, not even the most righteous of mortals, dares to appear before
God's Tribunal in his own inherent righteousness. Psalms 143:2 and 130:3; Job
42:5 and 9:3; 1 Corinthians 4:4.

5. All who keep the Law obtain eternal life under the title of legal
obedience. But no mortal obtains or can obtain eternal life under this title.
Therefore. The major premise is evident from Romans 2:13 and Galatians 3:12:
“The one who does these things will live in them.” The minor premise is
evident from the whole Gospel. Romans 8:3–4; Galatians 3:21: “If a Law had
been given which can give life, righteousness would be from the Law.” Likewise
verse 22: “Scripture confined all under sin.” Likewise Galatians 2:21: “If
life is through the Law, Christ died in vain.” Likewise Galatians 3:10: “As
many as are from the works of the Law are under the curse.”

6. For “if anyone offends in one thing, he has become guilty of all,” as James
says in chapter 2, verse 10. But there is no mortal who does not offend in one
thing. Therefore, and so forth.

7. The one who seeks righteousness and life through fulfillment of the Law
has been “made a debtor to the whole Law,” Galatians 5:3–4. But the regenerate
person has not been made a debtor to the whole Law; rather, he is “under grace,
not under the Law,” Romans 7:6.

8. Those who

[1668 KBNL/UvA witness | logical printed p. 515 | PDF p. 566]

can fulfill the Law through God's grace have been given the Spirit in such a
measure that infused grace is no longer imperfect and capable of increase but
is perfect and sufficient for loving God with all the heart, all the soul,
and all the powers. But grace has not been given in that manner to any mortal.
1. For it belongs to everyone to “grow in grace,” 2 Peter 3:18; Philippians
3:12; 2 Thessalonians 1:3. 2. For “the Spirit distributes to each one just as
he wills,” 1 Corinthians 12:11.

9. For Scripture says that the Law is “a yoke which neither we nor the Jews
can bear,” and that it is impossible to observe because of the inability of
the flesh, so that room might be made for the fulfillment of the Law through
Christ. Romans 8:3; compare Romans 7:14–17 with verse 24 and chapter 8, verse
1.

11. The Adversaries admit that human beings cannot so avoid all sins that they
do not need to say every day, not only humbly but truthfully, “Forgive us our
sins”; and they say that there is no one who can avoid at least venial sins.
But every sin, however venial they call it, is a violation of the Law.
Therefore, those who cannot avoid such things through grace cannot fulfill the
Law through grace.

# Calvin and the Genevan Ministers, *Response to the Polish Brothers on Christ the Mediator* (1560)

This directory presents the complete Latin text and a new English translation
of the Genevan ministers' response to the Polish churches concerning
Francesco Stancaro's claim that Christ is mediator only according to his human
nature. Calvin wrote in the corporate voice of his colleagues, and the
surviving manuscript tradition preserves their dated subscription and names.

> **Editorial status:** This is an AI-assisted draft transcription and
> translation checked word by word against high-resolution facsimile images,
> not a peer-reviewed critical edition. A qualified Neo-Latinist should
> independently collate it before formal publication or before relying on the
> English alone for a disputed historical or theological claim.

The work is Calvinian without being a sole-signed personal letter. CO
expressly identifies Calvin as the author of the two Stancaro responses,
writing in his colleagues' name; this first extant response identifies the
pastors and teachers collectively and then supplies each signatory. Its
corporate form and witness history should not be flattened into either “an
anonymous Genevan document” or “a private letter signed only by Calvin.”

## Polish and Stancaro context

Stancaro argued in the Polish churches that Christ is mediator only according
to his humanity. He maintained that calling Christ mediator according to his
deity would make the Son inferior to the Father and would absurdly have him
intercede with himself. The Genevan response argues instead that mediation
belongs to the one person of Christ according to both natures. Particular
actions may concern one nature directly, but their saving effect belongs to
the whole person; this does not confuse the distinct properties of the
natures. The Son's subjection in the office of mediator is explained through
the economy or ordered dispensation of God's counsel, not as an inferiority of
the divine essence.

The opening mourns the recent death of Jan Łaski (John a Lasco), whose loss
left still-young Polish churches more exposed to controversy. It praises the
Polish ministers for having answered Stancaro in godly agreement and presents
the Genevan argument as support for churches whose “crop of pure doctrine”
was still scarcely beyond the blade.

This is not every response Calvin ever made concerning Stancaro. The CO
prolegomena infer an earlier short answer, carried through Sebastian, from a
Polish letter of 14 March 1560; that answer has not survived. The present,
fully extant corporate response is dated 9 June 1560. A second, longer
Genevan-ministers response from March 1561 is a distinct work at CO IX
345–358. The present directory neither reconstructs the lost answer nor
combines the 1561 response with this one.

## Work, witness, and publication identity

CO based the present text on an old Zürich archival copy, but calls that
witness merely an apograph rather than an autograph. A derivative copy is in
the Simler collection, vol. 97. CO reports two addresses in the principal
apograph. The address printed here—“To the faithful ministers of Christ ...
our brothers worthy of honor”—stood at the end of that copy, as addresses
often do in manuscript transmission. A second routing inscription at its
front, in Francesco Lismanino's hand, addressed ministers in Zürich, Bern,
Basel, and Strasbourg. CO inferred that Lismanino circulated the Genevan
response among neighboring Swiss churches before carrying or sending the
autograph to Poland. The Lismanino routing inscription is witness history,
not part of the response printed by CO, and is not inserted into `latin.txt`.

The old apograph also preserves the dated corporate subscription and names.
Both the address used by CO and the subscription were omitted from later
printed editions. They remain part of the fuller manuscript state and are
included here; the shorter printed state is not allowed silently to determine
the work's extent.

CO finds no basis for the claim that the response had a separate 1560 print.
Its earliest confirmed printing appeared in January 1561 at pp. 101–106 after
Calvin's work against Heshusius, later known as the *Dilucida explicatio*.
The response was reprinted in the 1576 *Tractatus*, pp. 939–941; the 1597
*Tractatus*, pp. 790–792; the Stoer edition, pp. 682–683; and the Amsterdam
edition, pp. 587–588. CO also records French states in Beza and Stoer. The
bracketed tags `[pag. 102]` through `[pag. 106]` in CO's body align its text
with the early printed pagination; they are not words written to the Polish
recipients.

## Authorial and editorial matter

CO IX 333–342 contains the following distinguishable layers:

- Pp. 333–334 are a CO title leaf and pp. 335–336 are blank. The repeated
  title above p. 337 is an editorial heading. The title is recorded as work
  metadata but is not counted as a sentence of the corporate response.
- The authorial text begins with the manuscript-preserved address on p. 337
  and ends with the last signatory, *Ioannes Tagautius artium*, on p. 342.
- CO's starred source note identifies the old Zürich apograph. It is
  nineteenth-century apparatus, not a sentence addressed to Poland.
- The bracketed earlier-print tags `[pag. 102]`, `[pag. 103]`, `[pag. 104]`,
  `[pag. 105]`, and `[pag. 106]` are excluded.
- The p. 337 note says the address is absent from the printed editions; the
  p. 342 note says the corporate subscription and names are absent from them.
  Those notices are excluded, but the fuller manuscript main text to which
  they refer is retained.
- At p. 339 CO's main text reads *secundum utramque naturam esse et censeri
  mundi iudicem*; the apparatus says the codex inserts *summum*. The insertion
  is not blended into `latin.txt`.
- At p. 340 CO prints *in quo manifestanda perfecte erat divinitatis suae
  gloria*. Its apparatus reports the codex's *erat perfectae*. The normalized
  text follows the CO main reading, and the alternative is explained in the
  translation notes.
- Running heads, printed page numbers, gathering marks, rules, and footnote
  cues are excluded.
- Parenthetical Scripture locators printed in CO's main text are retained.
  The transmitted Ephesians 1:2 locator is not silently repaired, although
  Ephesians 1:22 fits the immediate claim about Christ's headship more closely.

## Transcription and collation

The PDF text layer and OCR were used only as orientation aids. Every word and
punctuation decision in the Latin body was then checked against the native
300-DPI images of UNIGE PDF pages 248–250. The normalized transcription joins
only divisions caused by printer line endings, retains CO spelling and
punctuation, removes apparatus cues with their excluded notes, and supplies
an exact marker for every numbered CO page.

The page turns were inspected individually. P. 337 ends *captasse* and p. 338
begins *Negant*; p. 338 ends *alteri* and p. 339 begins the parenthetical
Isaiah locator; p. 339 ends *carne* and p. 340 begins *manifestatus*. The
p. 340/341 turn divides *descen-deret*, so the normalized p. 341 marker is
placed before the complete word *descenderet*. The p. 341/342 turn similarly
divides *Do-minus*, and the p. 342 marker precedes the complete word
*Dominus*. No illegible or conjecturally supplied word remains.

## Translation provenance and modern English

The English was translated afresh from `latin.txt`, with the page images
rechecked wherever syntax, technical vocabulary, or the page division
affected the sense. No published English translation was copied, revised,
paraphrased, or used as a base.

Joseph N. Tylenda published a complete modern translation with introduction,
“Christ the Mediator: Calvin Versus Stancaro,” *Calvin Theological Journal* 8,
no. 1 (April 1973), 5–16. The journal's
[official issue record](https://digitalcommons.calvin.edu/ctj/15/) documents
the article. That translation is copyrighted and therefore does not satisfy
this project's criterion of a public-domain English version. No wording from
Tylenda's article or translation was consulted or used here; only its
bibliographic existence was recorded.

The new translation preserves the distinctions among Christ's one person,
two natures, mediatorial office, and the ordered economy of the divine
counsel. It renders *creatum* at p. 340 as “appointed” in its predicative
office-bearing context and explains that decision, rather than allowing the
English word “created” to suggest the very ontological inferiority Calvin is
arguing against. The technical *communicatio* is retained as “communication”
and glossed from its immediate context.

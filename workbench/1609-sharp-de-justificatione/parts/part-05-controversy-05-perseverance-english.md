## Fifth controversy

The fifth controversy is: **Can those who truly believe fall away from faith?**

Bellarmine, book 3 *On Justification*, says that justifying faith, once
conceived, can be extinguished and lost, and that it can be recovered after it
has been lost. He falsely ascribes to us, however, the error of Jovinian, who,
according to Jerome, book 2

[1609 e-rara witness | canvas 73 | image 15575045 | printed p. 71]

*Against Jovinian*, and Augustine, *Heresy* 82, said that the righteous could
not sin after baptism, or that, if they sinned, they had been washed with water
but had not received the Spirit of grace. We acknowledge that faith has its
increases and suffers an eclipse when it is shaken and obscured by storms of
temptations, as in David, Psalm 32:3, and in Peter when he denied Christ three
times, and so forth. Yet faith is never entirely extinguished, much less
finally. It is indeed often shaken through our fault, but the grace of God on
account of Christ does not fail completely. The Roman Catholics, therefore—
perhaps following Bernard’s error, for he used to say that the apostles and
all other Christians fell away from Christ, that through that sorrowful
Sabbath the Virgin alone stood in faith, and that the church was preserved in
the Virgin alone—assert that true believers can lose true faith entirely and
recover it after it has been lost.

We, however, assert that believers cannot fall away from faith entirely,
relying upon these arguments.

### Arguments

1. From Psalm 1:3 and Ezekiel 47:12: believers are compared to a tree planted
by streams of water, which always has moisture in its root and remains alive
so that it yields its fruit in its season. But this moisture is Christ
apprehended by faith, who says that he is life. Therefore those once grafted
into Christ by faith cannot lose it.

2. From Isaiah 9:6–7: if Christ establishes his kingdom and all the citizens
of his kingdom and confirms them forever, then no member of Christ can fall
away from faith. But the former is true from the cited passage. Therefore the
conclusion follows.

3. If Christ’s covenant with the whole church and with each of its members is
inviolable, and if he has promised

[1609 e-rara witness | canvas 74 | image 15575046 | printed p. 72]

that he will give each of them a new heart and one way so that they may fear
him all the days of their life and may not withdraw and fall away from him,
then no believer can fall away from faith. But the former is true from
Jeremiah 32:39–40. Therefore the conclusion follows.

4. Those who are betrothed—that is, joined to Christ by an indissoluble bond,
and that by faith forever—cannot lose faith. But each true believer is
betrothed to Christ forever by the bond of faith, Hosea 2:19–20; Ephesians
5:23, 30. Therefore the conclusion follows.

5. Those who are planted by God in the church—that is, those who are truly
grafted into Christ by faith—are not pulled up or rooted out. But each
believer is planted by God in the church. Therefore the conclusion follows.
Proof of the major premise: 2 Samuel 7:10; Amos 9:15; John 13:13.

6. From Psalm 37:24: those who are not cast down when they fall because
Jehovah sustains them may fall, but they cannot fall away entirely. Each
believer is of this kind according to the cited passage. Therefore the
conclusion follows.

7. Those who are built upon Christ as upon a rock and are closely joined to
Christ as their head cannot be torn from Christ by any temptations, nor can
the gates of hell prevail against them. But all and each of the elect are
built upon the rock and closely joined to Christ as their head. Proof of the
major premise: Matthew 7:24–25 and 16:16. Proof of the minor: Colossians 2:7,
19; Ephesians 4:12–13, 16. Therefore the conclusion follows.

8. Those for whom Christ prays that their faith may not fail cannot fall away
entirely. But Christ prays for all and each of the faithful that their faith
may not fail and that eternal life may be given to them, John 17:12 and
following; Luke 22:32. Augustine, volume 7, *On Rebuke and Grace*, chapter 12,
teaches that this passage is to be understood of every believer who is built
upon Christ the rock.

9. From Matthew 24:24: if it is impossible for

[1609 e-rara witness | canvas 75 | image 15575047 | printed p. 73]

the elect to be led astray, then they cannot fall away entirely. But the
former is true from the cited passage. Therefore the conclusion follows.

10. If those who receive Christ by faith perpetually retain the Spirit of
regeneration; if the water—that is, the Holy Spirit—becomes in them a spring
of water leaping up to eternal life; and if those who once live from this
water never thirst, then those who are given faith cannot lose it. But the
former is true from John 4:14; 6:36; and 7:38. Therefore the conclusion
follows.

11. No one in whom Christ abides and who abides in Christ falls away
entirely. But all and each of the faithful abide in Christ. Therefore the
conclusion follows. Proof of the minor premise: all who eat Christ’s flesh and
drink his blood abide in Christ, and Christ abides in them, John 6:16. But all
believers eat the flesh and drink the blood of Christ. Therefore they abide
in Christ and consequently cannot fall away.

12. From John 6:37: if those who are given to Christ by the Father and who
come to Christ are not cast out, then no one who believes can fall away
entirely, for the person who believes in Christ comes to Christ. But the
former is true from the cited passage. Therefore the conclusion follows.

13. Those who never perish and cannot be snatched from Christ’s hand cannot
fall away entirely. But all and each of Christ’s sheep—that is, believers—are
such, John 10:28. Therefore the conclusion follows.

14. If the gifts which God gives his own, the chief of which is faith, are
*ametamelēta*—irrevocable—and if nothing can separate believers from the love
of God in Christ, then they cannot fall away entirely. But the former is true
from Romans 8:38 and 11:29; John 13:1. Therefore the conclusion follows.

15. If God completes until the end the good work which he begins in the
elect, Philippians 1:6 and 2:13,

[1609 e-rara witness | canvas 76 | image 15575048 | printed p. 74]

and if faith is the first work of grace in us, John 6:29; 1 John 3:23, then
faith cannot be lost and believers cannot fall away. But the former is true
from the cited passages. Therefore the conclusion follows.

16. If true justifying faith could be lost, there would be no distinction
between temporary and justifying faith. But the latter is absurd. Therefore
the former is also absurd.

### Testimonies of the Fathers

1. Hilary on Matthew 5: “There is no justification if faith is doubtful.”

2. Augustine, volume 7, *On Grace and Rebuke*, chapter 11: “Through this grace
of God there is in us, in receiving the good and perseveringly retaining it,
not only the ability to do what we will but the willing of what we are able to
do. This was not present in the first man. One of these was present in him,
but not the other. Namely, he did not need grace in order to receive the good,
because he had not yet lost it. But in order to remain in it he needed the aid
of grace, without which he could not do so at all. He had received the ability
to do so if he willed, but he did not possess the willing of what he was able
to do. If he had possessed it, he would have persevered.”

3. The same author a little later: “He therefore makes them persevere in the
good who makes them good. Those who fall and perish were not in the number of
the predestined.”

4. The same author afterward: “God not only gives aid such as he gave to the
first man, without which they cannot persevere if they will, but in these he
also works the willing. Since they will not persevere unless they are both
able and willing, by the bounty of divine grace there is given to them both
the ability and the will to persevere. Indeed, their will is so greatly
kindled by the Holy Spirit that they are able because they so will, and they
so will because God

[1609 e-rara witness | canvas 77 | image 15575049 | printed p. 75]

works in them to will.”

5. The same author in the same place a little later: “The infirmity of the
human will was aided so that it might be governed undeviatingly and
inseparably by divine grace, and therefore, although weak, it might not fail
or be overcome by any adversity.”

6. And at the end of the chapter: “Since Christ therefore teaches, ‘I have
prayed for you that your faith may not fail,’ let us understand that it was
said to the person who is being built up. Thus the man of God not only has
obtained mercy so that he may be faithful, but faith itself also does not
fail.”

7. The same author, *On the Good of Perseverance*, chapter 2: “When God speaks
in this way, ‘I will put my fear in their heart,’ what else does this mean
than, ‘My fear will be of such a kind and so great, when I put it in their
heart, that they will cling to me perseveringly’?”

8. Chrysostom on Romans 5: “He said well, ‘in which we stand,’ for such grace
of God has no end and knows no limit, but advances toward greater things. It
is not so among human beings. For example, someone has received a principate,
dignity, and power, yet he does not always continue in them but quickly falls
from them. Even if no human being takes them away, death comes upon him and
removes them altogether. But the things which belong to God are not of this
kind. For neither a human being, nor time, nor a change of affairs, nor the
devil himself, nor approaching death cannot drive from them those who, when
we die, possess them more certainly and, always advancing, enjoy more things.
Therefore, if you distrust future things, strengthen your faith in them also
from present things and from the things you have already received.”

### Objections

#### Objection 1

From Luke 8:13: if many believe only for a time, then believers can fall away
from faith. But the former is true from the cited passage. Therefore the
conclusion follows.

Response 1: there is an equivocation in the word “faith,” for Christ speaks of
temporary faith, which does not have perseverance joined to it, and not of
justifying faith.

2. The major premise is false. It does not follow, “Some temporary believers
believe for a time; therefore some believers can fall away.”

#### Objection 2

Scripture says that some make shipwreck concerning the faith and apostatize
from the faith, 1 Timothy 1:19; 4:1; and 6:21. Therefore believers can fall
away from faith.

Response 1: there is a fallacy from the equivocation of “faith,” because in
these passages faith is taken not for justifying faith but for the doctrine
of faith, as it is also in this sense in Romans 1:8.

2. In the first passage he speaks of Hymenaeus and Philetus, who possessed
only a hypocritical profession for a time.

An instance: but Timothy was a true believer, and the apostle exhorts him not
to do the same. Therefore true believers can make shipwreck of the faith.

Response 1: I deny the inference. He exhorts Timothy to preserve faith and a
good conscience, not because he could fall away from faith, but so that he
might be more eager to perform these things and might actually retain them.
It is therefore a fallacy that takes as a cause what is not a cause.

2. The same passage teaches that Timothy cannot fall away, for it mentions
the prophecies concerning him which foretold that he would be such as Paul
requires him to be.

#### Objection 3

From Ezekiel 18:18: the person who turns away from righteousness

[1609 e-rara witness | canvas 79 | image 15575051 | printed p. 77]

and commits iniquity so as to die in it can fall from grace. But such persons
are righteous or believers. Therefore the conclusion follows.

Response 1: there is an equivocation in “the righteous” and “righteousness.”
In this passage “a righteous person” does not signify one who is truly
righteous by the righteousness that justifies before God, and therefore it
does not signify a true believer. It signifies the person who appears
righteous and covers himself with a hypocritical mask and the appearance of
righteousness. Many such people are and have been in the church, 1 John 2:19.

2. Even if it be granted that he speaks in that passage about those who are
truly righteous, nothing follows, because the discourse is instructional, and
instructional statements posit nothing in the actual existence of the thing.
For it does not follow, “If he does this, therefore he does that.”

3. God speaks in this way: (1) to rouse us so that we may proceed with an
upright step and be eager; (2) to show us our infirmity and how prone we are
by nature to falling, so that he may thus keep us in filial fear; (3) by
speaking this way, to show the miserable condition of hypocrites, so that he
may thus keep us at our duty and so that we may devote ourselves to prayer
and holiness of life; and (4) to teach what we must do. Augustine therefore
rightly says in *On Rebuke and Grace*: “In the command, recognize what you
ought to do; in rebuke, recognize that through your own fault you do not
possess it; in prayer, recognize from where you may receive what you wish to
possess.”

#### Objection 4

From John 15:6: if the person who has been grafted into Christ can wither, be
cast out, and be thrown into the fire, then a true believer can fall away from
faith and be condemned. But the former is true from the cited passage.
Therefore the conclusion follows.

Response 1: I deny the minor premise, because he does not say absolutely that
the branch which bears fruit is cast out, but conditionally, if it does not
bear fruit. Yet it is impossible for the person who does not bear fruit to be
truly grafted into Christ. It is therefore a conditional threat intended to
rouse us, as above.

[1609 e-rara witness | canvas 80 | image 15575052 | printed p. 78]

2. Many appear to be branches and bear leaves—that is, hypocrites who are
cast out—but none of the true elect are such.

#### Objection 5

From Matthew 12:43 and Luke 11:24: if the devil, after being cast out, can
return with seven others, then a believer can lose the gifts of the Holy
Spirit. But the former is true from the cited passages. Therefore the
conclusion follows.

Response: I deny the major premise, because the cited passages speak not
about true believers but about hypocrites. They possess temporary faith and
some common gifts of the Holy Spirit, and afterward lose them again. Thus
they adorn a dwelling for the devil, who was cast out in appearance. The
contrary holds concerning believers. Where the Spirit of regeneration is,
the temple of the Holy Spirit is not empty but is adorned with spiritual
virtues by his efficacy. Or Christ alludes to the condition of the Jewish
people, who, because the law was excepted, appeared to have been freed from
the demon who securely possessed the other nations. Yet they did not keep the
law, and they despised and opposed the gospel when it was preached. Thus they
prepared a dwelling for the devil, and for that reason he threatens judgment.
They, however, were not true believers but hypocrites.

#### Objection 6

From Matthew 24:12–13: those whose love grows cold do not persevere to the
end. But according to Christ, the love of many grows cold. Therefore the
conclusion follows.

Response 1: this does not conclude what is in question. We do not deny that
many fall away, and Christ foretells that there will be many apostates who
will be led astray by false prophets. But the question concerns true
believers, whom verse 24 teaches cannot be led astray.

2. By the name “love” he understands external Christian virtues which true
believers have in common with hypocrites. Not only true believers but also
hypocrites possess these virtues, and hypocrites can lose them.

[1609 e-rara witness | canvas 81 | image 15575053 | printed p. 79]

#### Objection 7

From Matthew 25:29: those from whom the talent—that is, gifts of the Holy
Spirit—is taken away lose faith. But the talent which believers possessed—
that is, faith—is taken from them because they used it badly. Therefore the
conclusion follows.

Response: I deny the minor premise. He does not speak of believers who truly
possess the gifts of regeneration, but of hypocrites who possess common gifts,
whether natural gifts or gifts of ecclesiastical office. Yet they have not
used these rightly, just as people who possess the best gifts are often empty
of true godliness. The interpretation, “They possess a talent; therefore they
possess justifying faith,” is consequently false.

#### Objection 8

From 1 Corinthians 9:29: if the apostle brought his body into servitude lest,
when he preached to others, he himself should become reprobate, then true
believers can be reprobate. But the former is true from the cited passage.
Therefore the conclusion follows.

Response 1: there is an equivocation in “reprobate,” because in this passage
a reprobate person is not opposed to one elected to eternal life, nor does it
signify a condemned person guilty of eternal death. It is opposed to
“approved” and signifies a person whom his life shows not to be such as he
ought to have been, especially when he prescribes to others a rule of life
which he does not keep. The sense is this: when I preach to others and my life
ought to be a rule for others, I therefore strive so to conduct myself that
my life may correspond to my doctrine, lest, if I myself neglect the things I
preach to others, I should be a stumbling block to them and be rejected by
them.

2. Even if he speaks about reprobation unto death, the proposition is false,
because he does not teach that this occurs in believers, but that it does not
occur, 2 Corinthians 13:6. Nor does it follow, “Paul labored lest he become a
reprobate; therefore he could become a reprobate.” For that concern does not
conflict with the certainty of election, because, just as God wills the
perseverance of the regenerate to be firm, so he wills that this

[1609 e-rara witness | canvas 82 | image 15575054 | printed p. 80]

perseverance be nourished and fostered by godly concern and diligence.

#### Objection 9

From 1 Corinthians 10:12: “Let the person who stands take heed lest he fall.”
Therefore believers can fall away from faith and salvation.

Response 1: I deny the inference, because it does not teach what occurs but
what does not occur. It is therefore a fallacy that takes as a cause what is
not a cause. By this very exhortation and similar ones God wills to foster and
preserve faith and salvation, and not to entrust perseverance to their own
powers.

2. Nothing follows from the passage except that believers can fall and slip.
But falling is one thing and falling away from faith entirely is another. The
latter does not happen to believers, as is taught in verse 13 of the same
chapter.

3. He does not speak only of believers but more particularly of hypocrites.
Thus he does not say “the person who stands” but “the person who appears to
stand.” By the example of the Israelites he warns the puffed-up Corinthians
not to commit sins against God and deserve a similar judgment.

4. By this and similar exhortations he rouses our sluggishness and wills that
we not be idle in the course of salvation. Moreover, he warns us of our
weakness and that we are prone to falling. Finally, he rouses us to implore
God’s aid.

#### Objection 10

Galatians 3:1 and 5:4: the Galatians were believers and had received the
Spirit. But according to the apostle, they were made void of Christ and had
fallen from grace. Therefore the conclusion follows.

Response 1: not all the Galatians to whom he writes were believers by
justifying faith, but only by profession. Such people can fall, and only such
were those who fell.

2. They all received the Spirit, not the Spirit of regeneration but
temporary, spiritual gifts which are common to the elect and the reprobate.
They began in the Spirit when they embraced by profession the spiritual
doctrine of the gospel. They ended in the flesh when they wished to mix this
doctrine with the carnal

[1609 e-rara witness | canvas 83 | image 15575055 | printed p. 81]

ceremonies of the law. Or by “the flesh” he understands doctrine devised by
human wisdom and the prudence of the flesh. There is therefore an equivocation
in “believer” and “Spirit.”

3. As to the minor premise—“But these were made void and fell from grace”—the
apostle does not speak about each person in the church, but about the external
face of the church. The visible church as such can fall away, although those
in the church who are endowed with true faith do not fall away entirely but
cling to God in Christ.

4. By the name “grace” is understood the doctrine of grace; thus there is an
equivocation. The expression *katērgēthēte apo tou Christou* signifies that
Christ is made empty for them if they join the ceremonies of the law to
Christ. He says that they have fallen from grace because those who do this
show that in reality they have not embraced grace, although for a time they
professed the doctrine of grace—that is, the gospel—by an external
profession.

#### Objection 11

From Romans 11:20: if true branches are cut off on account of unbelief, and if
Gentiles who do not remain in faith will be cut off, then believers can fall
away. But the former is true from the cited passage. Therefore the conclusion
follows.

Response 1: this is a fallacy of missing the point, because he does not speak
of individual believers but of Gentiles and Jews collectively.

2. He speaks only of the visible church, and Abraham is set down in verse 1 as
the trunk of the visible Jewish church. But members of the visible church can
fall away, and indeed the whole visible church as such can do so. There is
therefore an equivocation in “trunk” and “branches,” because the apostle does
not speak of Christ as the spiritual root and of spiritual members.

[1609 e-rara witness | canvas 84 | image 15575056 | printed p. 82]

#### Objection 12

From Hebrews 6:4 and 10:26: those who are enlightened, taste the good gift of
God, are partakers of the Holy Spirit, and are sanctified by the blood of the
covenant are believers. But, according to the apostle, such people fall from
grace and cannot be renewed again. Therefore believers finally fall from
grace.

Response: I deny the major premise, because the apostle speaks about
hypocrites who professed for a time, afterward apostatized, and sinned against
the Holy Spirit. Thus they are said to have tasted the heavenly gift, not to
have perceived it in a saving manner or to have eaten and drunk Christ by
true faith. They are also called partakers of the Holy Spirit because they
possessed gifts common to the elect and reprobate, such as external holiness,
temporary faith, and in some cases perhaps the gift of prophecy and miracles.
They did not, however, possess the Spirit of regeneration and justifying
faith. The enlightenment of the mind signifies the knowledge which they
possessed concerning God’s word and the mysteries of salvation, which is
common to the godly and the reprobate.

An instance: but they were sanctified by the blood of the covenant—that is,
Christ’s blood. Therefore they were true believers.

Response: there is an equivocation in the word “sanctification,” for
sanctification and washing have three meanings. (1) It is said of those who
are such in reality before God. This sanctification is true and real and
proper to believers, concerning which Romans 7:6 speaks. With respect to them,
all who are in the same church are called holy by synecdoche, as in Romans
1:7; 1 Peter 2:5.

2. It is said of those who are not truly regenerate but are so only in
appearance. They hold Christ only on their tongue and in their understanding,
profess the gospel, and acknowledge it to be good. The seed of the word only
grows green in them and does not produce true fruit. Because of the
appearance,

[1609 e-rara witness | canvas 85 | image 15575057 | printed p. 83]

however, they are called regenerate and holy.

3. It is said of those who only with their mouth, concerning whom John 4:22.
In the cited passages the apostle speaks of the second class, who are called
sanctified by the blood of the covenant because of their profession and
appearance of godliness and their external communion with the other members
of the church in the word and sacraments. Through these the blood of the
covenant is shared with those who apply it, but in different ways: to true
believers truly and unto salvation, but to hypocrites in appearance and unto
condemnation, 1 Corinthians 11:27. That they were not truly sanctified by
Christ’s blood is evident from the similar expression in verse 29 of the same
chapter, where he says that they trample the blood of the covenant underfoot;
yet they do so not in reality, but only in appearance and as far as it lies in
them.

#### Objection 13

From 2 Peter 1:9: those who can become blind in the knowledge of Christ and
forget that they were cleansed from sins can fall from grace and faith. But
believers can do this. Therefore the conclusion follows.

Response 1: I deny the minor premise, because the passage does not say that
these things befall believers; it speaks about hypocrites. Scripture commonly
calls those who are baptized and profess Christ “saints” and “cleansed from
sins.” There is therefore an equivocation in “cleansing.”

2. He ascribes to them the knowledge of the gospel’s doctrine and departure
from it because these things apply to apostatizing hypocrites, as is taught in
verse 21 of the same chapter. This, however, does not befall believers, as is
taught in verse 23 of the same chapter.

#### Objection 14

From 2 Peter 2:20–21: if those who once fled from the pollutions of the world
and were washed can return to their vomit and wallowing place, and if their
latter condition can become worse than the former, then believers can fall
away. But the former is true from the cited passages. Therefore the
conclusion follows.

[1609 e-rara witness | canvas 86 | image 15575058 | printed p. 84]

Response: he does not speak about what happens to true believers, but about
what happens to hypocrites. They appeared to have been washed and, by pursuing
external holiness, put off in part the pollutions of the world, but they were
not truly regenerate.

#### Objection 15

From Revelation 2:4: the pastor of the church at Ephesus was a believer
because he possessed true love. But he fell from it. Therefore the conclusion
follows.

Response: I deny the minor premise. The passage does not say that he fell
away entirely; on the contrary, it praises him in many respects. Rather, he
fell far from his former zeal, which often befalls believers. This is
therefore a fallacy from what is said in a qualified sense. It does not
follow, “Believers sometimes fall in part from faith, love, and so forth;
therefore they fall away entirely.”

#### Objection 16

The angels were righteous before the fall, but they fell. Therefore the
righteous can lose righteousness.

Response: this does not conclude what is denied, for the question concerns
not every kind of righteousness, but faith and the righteousness of Christ by
which we are justified. The angels were righteous and possessed the
righteousness and grace of creation, while we possess the grace of redemption.
In a mutable state they possessed mutable grace and righteousness, but
believers received [immutable grace], both with respect to the promises and
with respect to [vo]cation and Christ’s mediation. Finally, they were
reprobate, and therefore fell; but true believers are elect. It therefore
does not follow, “The angels, established in a mutable state and reprobated,
lost the grace of creation; therefore true believers who are elect can lose
the grace of regeneration.”

#### Objection 17

Adam was a true believer before the fall and fell, and for that reason was
punished so severely. Therefore the conclusion follows.

Response: the condition of Adam before the fall differed from that of Adam
himself and of us after the fall, because in a mutable state he received only
the grace of creation. But afterward, he, together with

[1609 e-rara witness | canvas 87 | image 15575059 | printed p. 85]

believers, received the immutable grace of regeneration, which is greater and
stronger than the former grace.

2. It is true that he sinned grievously, but he did not fall entirely. He
retained the fear of God. He was also punished grievously, but he deserved a
greater punishment. It is therefore a fallacy from what is said in a
qualified sense. It does not follow, “He fell; therefore he fell entirely.”

#### Objection 18

Saul received the Spirit and was among the prophets, 1 Kings 15, 16. Simon
Magus believed, Acts 8. Judas was given to Christ, John 17. But these men
perished. Therefore the conclusion follows.

Response 1: these men were only members of the visible church for a time, and
such members can fall away. They were not, however, true believers.

2. In Saul’s example there is an equivocation in “Spirit.” It is sometimes
taken for gifts of office, such as prudence, fortitude, and prophecy, as it is
in this passage. At other times it is taken for the Spirit of regeneration,
whom Saul did not possess. He was therefore not a believer.

3. The faith of Simon Magus was only temporary, not justifying.

4. Judas was given to Christ not unto life, salvation, and justification, but
unto the apostolic office. Ecclesiastical offices are given both to the
reprobate and to the godly, and to those who do not possess true faith. Christ,
however, speaks about those who are given by the Father unto salvation.
Finally, Judas is excepted in verse 22 of the same chapter.

#### Objection 19

Psalm 51:12–13: the person who asks that the Spirit be renewed and not be
taken away has lost the Spirit and can lose him. But David asks these things.
Therefore the conclusion follows.

Response 1: this is a fallacy from what is said in a qualified sense, because
he asks that the Spirit be renewed, not with respect to his existence, but
with respect to his efficacious operation. The Spirit had lain hidden

[1609 e-rara witness | canvas 88 | image 15575060 | printed p. 86]

for a time with respect to his efficacious operation, but had not been
extinguished.

2. It does not follow, “He asks that the Spirit not be taken away; therefore
the Spirit can be taken away.” Rather, this follows: therefore he had not been
taken away. This is evident from David’s prayer, from the repentance which
followed, and from the struggle between flesh and spirit at the time of
temptation, as Psalm 32 teaches.

#### Objection 20

Aaron and Solomon were idolaters, and Peter denied Christ. But those who do
these things fall away entirely. Therefore the conclusion follows.

Response 1: I deny the minor premise, because they did not fall with their
whole mind and will, but from infirmity and not without a struggle.

2. They did not fall finally, as their subsequent repentance teaches.

3. Peter did not lose faith, for Christ prayed that his faith would not fail,
Luke 22:32, and without doubt obtained what he asked. Finally, many believers
sin grievously but do not fall away entirely.

#### Objection 21

From Galatians 5:9; Ephesians 5:5; Colossians 3:5–6: those who commit the works
of the flesh are excluded from God’s kingdom and lose grace. But many
believers do this. Therefore the conclusion follows.

Response: I distinguish the proposition. It is true of those who commit the
works of the flesh without repentance and with the full impulse of the will.
But no believers sin in this way or persevere in sin.

#### Objection 22

Those who can be blotted out of the book of life can lose faith. But
believers can be blotted out of the book of life, Exodus 32:32–33; Psalm
69:29. Therefore the conclusion follows.

Response 1: I deny the minor premise, nor do the testimonies presented prove
it. Moses was not blotted out, nor could he be. He received a negative answer
from God, for God says, “I would blot out not you, but the person who sins.”

[1609 e-rara witness | canvas 89 | image 15575061 | printed p. 87]

2. Moses asks this not absolutely but conditionally—namely, rather than that
God’s whole people should be blotted out and God’s glory endangered. There is
therefore a twofold fallacy, both from what is said in a qualified sense and
from taking as a cause what is not a cause.

3. What God says about the people he does not say absolutely but in relation
to Moses’ words: “If I were to blot out anyone,” he says, “I would rather blot
out the people who sin.” The inference, “Therefore someone can be blotted
out,” is likewise a fallacy from what is said in a qualified sense.

4. In the psalm he speaks about hypocrites who were enemies of God and David,
and he desires them to be blotted out of the book, not the book of eternal
life, but the number of those living here. Furthermore, hypocrites are said
to be blotted out of the book of life, not because they were truly written in
it, but because they appeared to be written in it on account of their
profession. Thus Augustine on this passage of the psalm: “They are said to be
written because they so think and are so regarded. Nor,” he says, “ought we
to understand it in such a way that God writes anyone in the book of life and
then blots him out again. If a man said, ‘What I have written, I have written,’
does God write and blot anyone out? Afterward this was said according to their
own hope, because they thought themselves written there. They are blotted out
so that it may become evident to them that they are not there—that is, they
are declared truly not to be there.”

#### Objection 23

From Romans 9:3: the person who can be accursed from Christ can lose faith.
But Paul could be, or he wished it in vain. Therefore the conclusion follows.

Response 1: I deny the minor premise. Nor does it follow that he therefore
wished it in vain. This is a fallacy that takes as a cause what is not a
cause, because he wished it for another reason—namely, to show his affection
for their salvation.

2. It is also a fallacy from what is said in a qualified sense, because he
does not ask this absolutely, but with an implicit condition: if it could be
consistent with God’s will that the people

[1609 e-rara witness | canvas 90 | image 15575062 | printed p. 88]

of Israel, about whose rejection he was afterward to speak, should not be
rejected.

#### Objection 24

Faith is not an act but a habit. A habit, however, does not necessarily
prevent a contrary act. Therefore a person who possesses an act of faith can
commit an act of unbelief. But according to Scripture, the person who commits
an act of unbelief loses the contrary habit, because from this he simply
becomes an unbeliever and an enemy of God. Our opponents also say that by
every sin justifying faith is shaken out and the Holy Spirit poured away. But
everyone commits an act of unbelief. Therefore the conclusion follows.

Response 1: faith is an infused habit, and an act of unbelief can coexist
with faith, though not with full impulse, but with a struggle of faith against
unbelief and not without subsequent repentance.

2. He falsely asserts that either Scripture or we affirm that an act of
unbelief removes the contrary habit of faith, or that one sin removes either
faith or the Holy Spirit. Sins indeed hinder the operation of the Holy Spirit
and of faith, but they do not remove them.

An instance: if believers who sin do not lose grace and faith, then God is not
angry at sins, because God’s wrath and grace cannot exist together. But God
is angry at sins. Therefore they lose grace entirely.

Response 1: the major premise is denied.

2. As to the proof, God’s grace and wrath cannot exist together in the same
respect. In believers, however, they do not have the same respect. God is
angry at them insofar as they sin and therefore chastises them insofar as they
sin. But because and insofar as they believe, they are children of God, and
he pursues them with grace.

3. God’s grace and love coexist with his

[1609 e-rara witness | canvas 91 | image 15575063 | printed p. 89]

wrath, but not with his hatred. For God is angry at believers who sin, but
does not pursue them with hatred. Thus, like natural parents, he chastises in
anger but not in hatred.

#### Objection 25

All who are baptized are justified and receive faith. But many of these fall
away and are condemned. Therefore the conclusion follows. He proves the
major premise from our testimony and because we assert that some who are not
baptized possess faith.

Response 1: the major premise is false, and he falsely affirms that we assert
this. Baptism does not give faith, but is only a sacrament added to the
covenant of grace. Nor do all who are baptized receive faith, just as some
who are not baptized also do not possess faith, Acts 8. The eunuch first
believed and afterward was baptized.

2. No baptized people who believe are condemned. Accordingly, if the minor
premise concerns believers, it is false.

#### Objection 26

If faith could not be lost, there would be no heretics, because this is the
difference between heretics and pagans: pagans did not possess faith, while
heretics possessed it. But there are many heretics. Therefore the conclusion
follows.

Response 1: I deny the major premise, because heretics who persist finally in
heresy never possessed faith.

2. That difference between heretics and pagans is false. They differ from
pagans by a profession of the truth before manifest heresy; after heresy they
differ only in part, or are tenuous in name, but they do not differ in
justifying faith.

#### Objection 27

If believers cannot lose faith, exhortations and prayers

[1609 e-rara witness | canvas 92 | image 15575064 | printed p. 90]

for the forgiveness of sins would be empty. But the consequent is absurd.
Therefore the antecedent is also absurd.

Response 1: I deny the major premise, because exhortations are employed for
other reasons—namely, so that zeal for godliness and flight from sin may be
roused in believers—and not because they can fall away, but lest this happen.
It is therefore a fallacy that takes as a cause what is not a cause.

2. Nor are prayers empty, although we cannot fall away: (1) because of God’s
command; (2) so that we may acknowledge God as the giver of every good,
worship him, love him, and depend upon him—but so that, having been heard, we
may be grateful; and (3) because of the promises, since he does not promise
that he will give anything unless we ask for it in prayer.

#### Objection 28

A doctrine which renders everyone secure must be rejected. But the doctrine
of final perseverance does this. Therefore the conclusion follows.

Response: I distinguish the major premise. A doctrine which renders people
secure with carnal security must be rejected, but not one which renders them
secure with spiritual security. This doctrine renders us secure not in
ourselves but in God, not in the flesh but in the Spirit, because through it
we are certain of God’s final mercy and of salvation. Although by our
infirmity and ingratitude we provoke God to forsake us, and insofar as it lies
in us we forsake him, nevertheless, on account of the immutability of election
and the certainty of the promises, he does not forsake us. Thus we must always
judge concerning ourselves according to the saying, “Let the person who
stands take heed lest he fall,” but concerning God we must determine with
certain confidence according to the saying, “God is faithful, who will not
allow you to be tempted beyond what you are able,” 1 Corinthians 10:13.
Finally, if any abuse this doctrine unto carnal security, that arises not
from a fault in the doctrine, but from the fault of those who abuse it. To
determine with the Roman Catholics, however, that

[1609 e-rara witness | canvas 93 | image 15575065 | printed p. 91]

the saving grace of God, once given, can be lost is to torture consciences on
an intolerable rack; and to imagine that losing or receiving grace lies in our
hand is to spread a cushion for the license and lust of the flesh and sin, and
to trifle with God.

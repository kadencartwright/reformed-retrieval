[Various Questions 1–7; printed page 584; IA PDF page 658; BSB canvas 634]

Now let us raise several brief questions concerning this matter which may also
pertain to the whole doctrine of providence.

[Marginalia: To what extent God’s justice is referred to his free will.]

Question 1. If GOD’s justice had been contrary to that which is revealed in the
law and Gospel—that is, if God had commanded the contrary—would it have been
just to the same degree as what is revealed in the law and Gospel?

I answer: if we except that justice which necessarily arises from GOD’s
intrinsic and natural justice, then, had God commanded the contrary, it would
have been equally just.

Question 2. Can everything just that God commands creatures ultimately be
traced back to GOD’s constancy and immutability as its origin and fountain,
rather than to a connatural suitability and proportion between one thing and
another—for example, between bread and nutrition rather than between a stone
and nutrition? For a certain person was born in England who emerged from the
womb holding a stone wrapped in his hand and

[Various Questions 1–7; printed page 585; IA PDF page 659; BSB canvas 635]

lived on stones alone. The same question applies between good works and eternal
life. I answer affirmatively, subject to the judgment of the learned, if we
speak of the ultimate resolution.

Question 3. Therefore, although it is free for God to punish sins, is
corrective justice an accident in God, like *its raining or not raining
tomorrow*, as Socinus says? I answer that the most learned men Sibrandus and
Andreas Essenius very properly determine the question negatively. I add that,
although on the hypothesis of a decree concerning tomorrow’s future rain it is
contrary to GOD’s immutable nature not to will the future rain, God must not
therefore be said to hate the nonfuture rain from a principle of internal
natural justice in the way in which he is *GOD who does not will iniquity*,
Psalm 5. Consequently, although corrective justice, which is called relative,
could have failed to exist in God, it is nevertheless no accident in God.
There is in God a natural equity and fittingness which inheres in him
essentially and is the foundation of corrective justice. But corrective justice
adds something beyond GOD’s free decree concerning future rains and the like:
namely, a moral pleasure in the duty and punishment of the sinful creature.

[Marginalia: To what extent and in what manner corrective justice, which is
referred to creatures, is not essentially in God. Sibrandus, ibid., p. 11;
Andreas Essenius, book 1, section 2, chapter 2.]

Question 4. Is it properly and strictly justice for God to substitute his Son
as surety for sinners, the innocent for the guilty? I answer that this belongs
to grace wholly gratuitous—John 3:16; Titus 2:11—not to justice properly so
called. Love drew the Son to give surety; once he was drawn, the necessity of
justice, as it were, compelled payment of the ransom. See John 10:18; 1 Timothy
1:15; Luke 19:10, and compare Luke 22:42; Romans 3:15; Matthew 26:42.

[Marginalia: God gave the surety from grace, not justice.]

Question 5. According to Deuteronomy 8:3, are all means therefore means to the
end intended by God because God uses them, and not because they are means in
themselves, indeed prior to GOD’s decree? I answer: some are intrinsic means
with respect to an intrinsic end. Thus the rational soul is directed toward
reasoning as an intrinsic end; means of this kind are means prior to the
decree. But other things are means because God uses them. Even if there is an
intrinsic congruity between bread and nutrition, that congruity wholly and
originally derives from GOD’s free decree. Thus Deuteronomy 8:3 says, *A person
will live upon every utterance from the mouth of GOD*—that is, upon every word.
This is not in the text, though Jerome correctly gives it, as does the Chaldean
paraphrase: *But by every word which proceeds from the Lord’s mouth.* The
Septuagint reads: *But by every word proceeding through God’s mouth a person
will li-*

*—ve.* The Syriac translation reads, *but a person lives by every thing which
the Lord’s mouth brings forth*; the Arabic translation, *but a person lives by
every utterance of God.* It signifies GOD’s voluntary decree. Genesis 24:50:
*The word came from the Lord*—that is, the decree—*we cannot speak good or evil
to you.* The Hebrew is הַדָּבָר; the Septuagint, *the matter*. Genesis 19:22:
*For I shall not be able to do the matter.* See also Joshua 9:24; Judges 6:39;
Deuteronomy 17:5; 1 Chronicles 2:7–8. “Behold, these women were to the sons of
Israel בִּדְבַר בִּלְעָם.” By Balaam’s word or decree, transgression was to be
committed against Jehovah. The Targum, as Shimler says, has בְּעֵצָה, *by
counsel, by decree*. Esther 1:12: *Queen Vashti refused to come.* The Targum has
בִּדְבַר הַמֶּלֶךְ, *by the king’s decree*. Hence the inner sanctuary, דְּבִיר,
1 Kings 6:19, 21: the oracle from which God discloses decrees and commands.
Genesis 18:14 in the Septuagint: *Is any word impossible with God?* See also
Genesis 30:34 and 44:2.

[Marginalia: “The word of God” signifies God’s decree. Numbers 31:16.]

Question 6. Prior to GOD’s decree, is it just for a creature to render obedience
to the Creator? I answer: by the law of gratitude between lord and servant, it
is. But by a law binding to punishment it is not just unless a penal law first
commands obedience under such a penalty. For God does not punish by necessity
of nature; when a creature sins, he does not act according to the creature’s
demerit except because he freely decreed to act in that way. Likewise, from
grace or freely—not from a necessity of natural justice—he rewards even the
most perfect obedience in Adam or the elect angels. The creature’s merit cannot
be founded upon GOD’s essential justice without blasphemy. For by the nature of
the thing there is no condign proportion between a work and a reward which
determines God by necessity of nature to reward; indeed, he is not compelled
even to confer temporal things. If he gives existence, he is not bound to
preserve that existence except by a qualified and hypothetical necessity.

[Marginalia: What kind of law exists before the decree.]

Question 7. Once GOD’s decree is posited, does it remove any power from created
agents? I answer: it removes no created or physical power, nor any part of it.
First, because GOD’s decrees, as decrees, do not really flow into second causes.
Second, because the first cause does not diminish the entities of things either
by concurring or by establishing concurrence, but rather preserves them. Hence
God’s decree does not remove pure power; it does, however, remove modal power,
which concerns the event and efficacy of the thing. First, because God himself,
the uncreated Angel, says in Genesis 19:22 that he cannot destroy Sodom and
Gomorrah unless Lot departs from there. The denied power is modal power

[Marginalia: God’s decree removes modal power, not physical power.]

[Various Questions 1–7; printed page 587; IA PDF page 661; BSB canvas 637]

by reason of the decree by which God graciously appointed to preserve Lot after
his rescue. But if we speak of pure power, God could have overthrown Sodom at
every distinction of time. Likewise Genesis 24:50: *Then Laban and Bethuel
answered and said, “This word, this decree, is from the Lord,”* הַדָּבָר. *We
cannot* (לֹא נוּכַל) *speak good or evil to you*, because this marriage is a
decree or affair sanctioned by GOD’s decree. We cannot speak to the contrary;
that is, modal power to resist is lacking.

I take Numbers 22:18 in the same sense: *If Balak gave me a house full of silver
and gold, I could not*—לֹא אוּכַל—*transgress the mouth of the Lord my God to do
either great or small.* Modal power is denied there, both because of GOD’s
decree, by reason of which GOD’s people could not fail to be blessed, and
because GOD’s hand drew the willing false prophet toward blessing. Acts 27:31:
*Unless these men remain in the ship, you cannot be saved.* They could not be
saved by reason of modal power, since God had decreed to link the salvation of
those who were in the ship with Paul to the sailors’ remaining in the ship.
Otherwise, speaking simply, they could be saved; indeed, they could not perish
by reason of modal power.

Second, all the passages of Scripture and arguments we have adduced to prove
that God immutably decrees all contingent and free acts prove this. For if God
decreed that the Medes and Persians should devastate the Chaldeans or other
nations, they cannot fail to devastate them by reason of modal power, since
GOD’s decrees and counsels cannot be broken. Isaiah 14:24–27; 46:10–11.

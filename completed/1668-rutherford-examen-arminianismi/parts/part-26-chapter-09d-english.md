Another question allied to this is whether Christ intercedes generally for
all, but specially only for believers. The Remonstrants say so in the
*Synodical Writings*, article 2, page 319, and Corvinus in *Against Molina*,
chapter 28, section 24. They teach that in those words of John 17:9, “I do not
pray for the world,” Christ does not exclude others from his prayers, but only
prays for the Apostles in this prayer. For elsewhere Christ prays for those
crucifying him: “Father, forgive them.” They therefore will have Christ pray
and intercede for all from a general and antecedent affection, but only for
believing elect persons by a consequent will. We teach the contrary.

1. They do not prove from any passage of Scripture that Christ prays for all.
In Isaiah 53:12 he prays for

[1668 KBNL/UvA witness | logical printed p. 412 | PDF p. 463]

transgressors. An indefinite proposition in contingent matter is equivalent
to a particular one. For he does not pray for all sinners, because he does not
pray for those sinning against the Holy Spirit, nor for the finally unbelieving
and contumacious as such. For those for whom he did not die as Mediator he does
not intercede as Advocate so that his death may profit them unto eternal
salvation. But the adversaries deny that Christ died for the finally
unbelieving as such.

2. Christ is always heard whenever he prays to the Father, John 11:42: “For I
knew that you always hear me.” But Christ's prayers for each and every person
have not been heard, because each and every person does not believe to the end
and is not saved. Yet it is blasphemous that Christ's prayers should not be
heard: 1. They would thus rest on the piety and merits of the human beings for
whom he prays, and not on his own merits, if Christ's prayers are heard for
each and every person provided that each and every person believes and
perseveres. 2. Christ intercedes by praying that the faith of those for whom he
prays may not fail, Luke 22:31.

3. It belonged to the typological Priest not only to sacrifice for the people,
but also to enter the Holy of Holies with blood once every year. The Holy
Spirit applies both of these to Christ in Hebrews 5:1, 2, 3; 8:3, 4; 9:7, 11;
and 10:19, 20. He not only offered himself as a sacrifice to God the Father,
but by the merit of his blood entered the Holy

[1668 KBNL/UvA witness | logical printed p. 413 | PDF p. 464]

of Holies—that is, heaven. But the Arminians deny that Christ intercedes
in heaven or enters that heavenly Holy of Holies with his blood for each and
every person, but only for believers.

4. According to the Arminians, Christ can intercede for no one. Therefore, he
cannot intercede for all. The antecedent is proved: according to them, Christ
did not merit faith, regeneration, and perseverance in faith by his death.
Therefore, he cannot pray for our faith and perseverance. The reason for the
consequence is that the prayer of Christ the Priest rests on the value and
merit of the perfect sacrifice offered by him to God.

5. They do not dare assert that Christ now prays at the Father's right hand
for all the reprobate, not even generally. For then he must generally have
risen for them, ascended into heaven for them, and now intercede for them at
God the Father's right hand. But they do not teach this, since they say that he
rose only for believers.

1 CORINTHIANS 1:4.

I always give thanks to my God for you because of the grace that was given to
you in Christ Jesus.

Hence it is clear that Christ is the one on account of whom, as the meritorious
cause, all grace was given to us. Therefore:

It is asked whether the Remonstrants rightly teach in the *Apology*, chapter
8, folio 25, that for no mortals did Christ

[1668 KBNL/UvA witness | logical printed p. 414 | PDF p. 465]

by his death merit faith and regeneration. We deny it.

1. In Ephesians 1:3, God “blessed us with every spiritual blessing in Christ.”
Therefore, he blessed us with faith and regeneration as well.

2. We are God's new workmanship in Christ Jesus, whom he created for good
works, Ephesians 2:10. Therefore, he also created us for faith, which they call
a good work and Christ calls the work of God in John 6:29.

3. Paul gives thanks to God on this account in 1 Corinthians 1:4–5: “I always
give thanks to my God for you because of the grace that was given to you in
Christ Jesus, because in everything you have been enriched in him.” Therefore,
he gave thanks that they were rich in faith in him.

4. All God's promises in Christ are “Yes and Amen,” 2 Corinthians 1:20,
because he merited all the things promised. Chief among these are
regeneration, circumcision of the heart, Deuteronomy 30:6, a new heart and a
new Spirit, and consequently the generation of faith. All these things are
promised to us in the New Covenant, Ezekiel 36:26; Ezekiel 11:19; Jeremiah
31:33; and Zechariah 12:10. Indeed, it is absurd that the things promised in
the New Covenant should not have been obtained for us by the merit of the
blood of the New Covenant and the death of the Surety of the New Covenant, as
Christ is called in Hebrews 7:22.

5. The spirit of regeneration was poured out upon us through Christ Jesus our
Savior from the kindness and `φιλανθρωπίᾳ`, love for humanity, of God, Titus
3:4, 5, 6.

[1668 KBNL/UvA witness | logical printed p. 415 | PDF p. 466]

For since faith and regeneration are God's gifts, it is absurd that these
should be given to us except on account of Christ's merits. Otherwise, God
would give these gifts to us just as the Creator of the world gives food to
ravens. What—what could be more contrary to the Scriptures?

8. The Arminians therefore deny that Christ merited faith and regeneration for
us, lest they be compelled to say that faith and regeneration are given by the
necessity and power of Christ's merits. 2. It would be absurd for Christ to
have acquired faith and regeneration for all by his merits, just as he died
for all, since very few believe and are regenerated. 3. For them faith is not
a gift of grace obtained by Christ's merit, nor is regeneration—if they had
learned to speak sincerely—but a work of free choice. 4. They think it absurd
that something required from us as a duty and a condition to be freely
performed under the title of free obedience should be obtained by Christ's
merits. For then that which we nevertheless perform freely, not necessarily,
would necessarily have to be given to us.

JOHN 15:13.

No one has greater love than this: that someone lay down his life for his
friends.

It is asked whether Christ embraced those for whom he died with the greatest
love, or whether any love greater than that with which he pursues those who
actually believe and are saved could have been given. The Remonstrants teach,
especially in the *Hague Conference*, page 184, and Corvinus in

[1668 KBNL/UvA witness | logical printed p. 416 | PDF p. 467]

*Against Molina*, chapter 28, section 17, that Christ in this passage does
not signify the greatest love absolutely, but only in a qualified respect.
For, they say, it is a greater love not only to merit salvation but actually
to confer it. We deny this.

1. A distinction must not be made where God's Law does not distinguish. But
Christ expressly says that no one has greater love than this.

2. This love is such that if God gives the Son for us—which is the greater
thing—he cannot fail to give all other, lesser things, and consequently the
grace of believing and persevering and eternal salvation, Romans 8:32.

3. This love of Christ is so great in length, breadth, depth, and height that
it surpasses all knowledge, Ephesians 3:18–19.

4. The love of Christ laying down his life for his friends cannot even be
called love according to the Arminians, because this love is fully compatible
with hatred, with a peremptory and irrevocable decree of Reprobation, and with
the eternal destruction of all whom he embraces with such love. Indeed, it is
compatible with such a denial of means as God foreknows would effectually
deliver them from God's eternal wrath and destruction.

5. Paul argues in this way in Romans 5:10: “For if, when we were enemies, we
were reconciled to God through the death of his Son, much more, having been
reconciled, we shall be saved through his life.” It follows that reconciliation
through the death of God's Son is an act of greater

[1668 KBNL/UvA witness | logical printed p. 417 | PDF p. 468]

love than the glorification of those reconciled. But if an antecedent
reconciliation through Christ's death is posited as common to all, elect and
reprobate, as the adversaries will have it, no mortal need be saved.

6. They cannot say that God could not have shown a greater love in that
kind—namely, antecedent love toward enemies—but did show a greater love toward
friends in whom the Father makes his dwelling, as Corvinus replies in the same
place, section 19. 1. Christ is speaking about love toward the friends
themselves, John 15:13. 2. There is one love in God with which he has embraced
his own from eternity. Here he speaks of God's love with respect to affection,
as it is manifested to us most fully in this: that Christ died for us.

It is asked whether the Remonstrants rightly define Reconciliation in the
*Apology*, chapter 8, folio 98, as the active production of a will to save in
place of a will to destroy. We deny it.

1. There is no efficient cause of God's Will among creatures.

2. A Will to destroy is not mutable, migrating into a Will to save.

3. Reconciliation is an external and temporal act in God by which God declares
that he regards one who believes in the Son of God as pleasing and accepted.
It presupposes Christ's death and a human being's faith in Christ, and differs
from

[1668 KBNL/UvA witness | logical printed p. 418 | PDF p. 469]

Justification in reason, not in reality. Reconciliation on our part is also an
act of obedience commanded of us, 2 Corinthians 5:20: “We entreat you in
Christ's name: be reconciled to God.” Therefore, it is not essentially such a
will.

4. It is false that in God a will to save the reprobate was substituted
through Christ's death in place of a will to destroy. 1. There is no peace for
the reprobate. 2. There could not have been a will in God to destroy the
reprobate before the Redeemer was spurned, as they themselves teach. But to
destroy those who do not believe in infancy is iniquity; to destroy them
because of Original Sin is to punish those who are not sinners, according to
them; and to destroy them because of sins intervening between infancy and a
sufficient presentation of the gospel is `ἄγραφον`, unwritten or unscriptural.
For those who sin before grace is given through the preaching of the gospel
labor under invincible ignorance, since they are not yet capable of the gospel
sufficiently proposed.

ROMANS 8:34.

Who is he who condemns? It is Christ who died, and moreover, who was also
raised.

From this passage a solid argument of Christian consolation is available to
all those for whom Christ died. Hence:

It is asked whether the solid foundation of Christian consolation, hope, and
certainty lies in this, that Christ died for each and every person, while the
foundation of despair and perpetual doubt lies in this, that he

[1668 KBNL/UvA witness | logical printed p. 419 | PDF p. 470]

died only for those absolutely elected and for believers, since no one knows
from the Scriptures that he is elect and believes. The Remonstrants say so. We
deny it.

1. Truly Christian hope and consolation are proper to the elect and believers
and laid up for them in heaven, Colossians 1:5; 1 Peter 1:3; and Romans 8:24.
Both objective and subjective certainty of salvation, and the certainty of
adherence, as they call it, is proper only to believers, Romans 8:38–39; 2
Corinthians 5:1–2; Colossians 2:2; Timothy 1:12; Romans 5:1; and Romans 8:16.
We therefore profess that our doctrine by no means sews cushions under every
armhole for the ungodly and unbelieving.

2. Those who teach as follows provide miserable hope and empty consolation to
poor souls: granted that Christ poured out his life and blood for me and each
mortal, including Judas and Pharaoh, yet unless my Freedom—more pivoting than
a hinge—does this one thing and makes me and all mortals participants in
salvation, it is possible for me and all mortals to perish together eternally.
Indeed, this consequence is just as valid: “Christ died for sinners; therefore,
the Devil will be saved,” as this one: “Christ died for each and every person;
therefore, Judas the traitor, be of good cheer: you will be saved, and
sufficient consolation is available to you.”

3. The Arminians teach nothing about the movement of human hearts according to
God's foreknowledge, as the Jesuits do, and nothing about an effectual and
undeclinable bending

[1668 KBNL/UvA witness | logical printed p. 420 | PDF p. 471]

of the heart to obedience to Christ, as the Dominicans teach. But according to
their doctrine, Free Choice rules over God's providence and over all grace
acting most powerfully. It rules over `τὸ` willing or not willing, `τὸ`
believing or not believing, and `τὸ` persevering or not persevering in grace.
Hence there arises a suspended, doubtful, wavering, uncertain hope, shaken at
every hour by dread of final ruin and apostasy; and from this comes empty and
slippery consolation. How miserable these comforters are!

4. More than enough hope, certainty, and consolation underlies this syllogism:

Whoever believes in Christ will be saved.

But I believe in Christ.

Therefore, I will be saved.

The major premise is Scripture's. The minor premise can be proved from the
Scriptures, Romans 6:16, and from those infallible signs by which someone can
be certain that he believes. These can be discerned with divine certainty,
partly from the Holy Scriptures, partly from the testimony of the Holy Spirit,
and partly from the sense of Christian experience.

5. The Arminian foundation of consolation and certainty is universal election,
or a universal intention to save all and to die for all, which has nothing
solid in itself. For unbelievers, unless they presume and nurture an empty
hope, then have material for despairing of eternal salvation and for suspecting
that they are not among the

[1668 KBNL/UvA witness | logical printed p. 421 | PDF p. 472 | ends before Quæritur, An reprobi, morte Christi]

number whom God destined to glory by a complete, irrevocable, and peremptory
decree of election. But we certainly command no one to seek the foundation of
hope and consolation in God's secret intentions and hidden decrees, but in
God's revealed will compared with each person's conscience.

ACTS 10:43.

“All the prophets bear witness to him that everyone who has believed in him
will receive remission of sins through his name.”

From this passage it is evident that faith is the one instrument for
apprehending Christ. Yet serious questions concerning the nature of faith
exist between us and the Arminians.

It is asked whether they teach correctly in the *Apology*, chapter 10,
section 1, that in the business of justification faith is considered only
insofar as by its natural property it includes obedience—that is, conversion,
repentance, and the observance of the commandments—and not correlatively,
insofar as it apprehends Christ's righteousness. We deny this, and we maintain
that faith justifies only correlatively.

1. This is evident from the context just cited and from Romans 4:6: “Just as
David also declares blessed the person to whom God imputes righteousness
apart from works.”

2. If faith essentially includes works in this act, then blessed will be the
person to whom works are imputed apart from grace. Romans 11:6: “But if it is
by grace, it is no longer from works; otherwise grace is no longer grace. But
if it is from works, it is no longer from grace; otherwise work would no
longer be work.”

3. For if faith is considered here as including repentance, faith justifies
insofar as it is repentance; thus repentance itself justifies, which is
absurd.

[1668 KBNL/UvA witness | logical printed p. 541 | PDF p. 592 | split word joined: Quia]

4. For this is pure Socinianism. Socinus says in *Lectures*, chapter 17:
“Faith is not the apprehension of any satisfaction, but obedience to Christ's
precepts under the hope of future immortality.” And by this reasoning, if
repenting and reforming one's life under confidence of attaining eternal life
is faith, then obeying the Law under confidence of obtaining eternal life
through works of the Law will be legal justification. Thus, in this sense,
people would be justified by faith under the Law just as they are under the
Gospel.

# An Exhortation and Apology Addressed to a Papist

This is a fresh English translation of the complete Latin presentation at CO
X/1 181–184. CO says that Theodore Beza translated this item, like the letter
before it, from French. The unlocated French wording has not been reconstructed:
the translation below is accountable to Beza's printed Latin witness. Biblical
language is translated directly from that Latin, and no wording from Beaty and
Farley's modern translation or a modern English Bible supplied the English.

## Translation

### [CO X/1, p. 181]

Although I am unknown to you by face, my having written to you will not seem
absurd if I cite your children before you as the originators of this plan. I
also wish this fact to serve the following purpose: that without any circuit
of excuses I may address you here at once. For I have learned this about you:
if I were to write anything to you, not only would you receive it calmly, but
neither would it be troublesome for you to read it through and then ponder and
weigh it for yourself.

This inspired such hope in me concerning you that, since I see a seed of piety
lying within you, I concluded that once cultivation was at last applied, fruit
would in its time follow from it. Since, therefore, this writing concerns the
cultivation of your own self, I wish you first to consider this: nothing is
more fitting for a Christian than, while he can and ought to observe palpable
abuses and corruptions in the worship of God as it is practiced, also to take
care not to allow himself to wallow in that mire by indiscriminate consent. He
should instead learn from this that he must inquire into God's truth, so that
he may rest safely in it.

I add a second request: hear calmly what I shall add concerning our doctrine.
I will be brief, so that you may recognize all the more easily what road we
follow and where it also

### [CO X/1, p. 182]

leads. First, it is sufficiently established that very many people cling to
ignorance and superstition, since they cannot even bring themselves to open
their eyes to the light offered them. I state openly that I count you among
their number, so that I need not employ a longer exhortation to demonstrate or
persuade you of that fact.

Yet one thing above all makes a Christian: that he resolves he must take care
to follow a sure manner of worshiping God, lest he be deceived willingly,
especially in a matter of such importance, where the soul's eternal salvation
is at stake. It is well known that Christianity has been adulterated and
corrupted by the church's pastors—by their inactivity as well as their
ignorance, ambition, and greed.

I do not restrict this degeneration to their life and conduct. Foremost within
it is the transformation of God's own truth into falsehood and impostures, the
contamination of God's worship by the introduction of countless
superstitions, the extreme disruption of the church's order, and the
defilement of the use of the sacraments—to conclude in one phrase, an
astonishing disorder surpassing every power of perception. If few people
observe this, it is because the Lesbian rule alone functions here as the
standard.

Now, if the pure and native institution of God is set against that Catholic
religion, the same agreement will be found there as exists between light and
darkness. Therefore, for anyone to know and judge these matters truly, he must
stand neither on a ruler's authority, nor on the prescription of custom, nor
on his own perception. The Lord's Word must be consulted, both what he
commands there and what he forbids. For the Lord has not spoken in a corner;
he wished his will to be disclosed to the highest and the lowest alike.

Once you have prevailed upon yourself to lend a patient ear to God when he
speaks, to rest in the things he has handed down to us in his Word, and to
resolve that the one way of salvation must be sought and received from him
alone, you will have prepared for yourself a broad entrance to knowing fully
the things which it is expedient for each of us to hold.

I come to our doctrine. Very many people condemn it unheard and unexamined by
a prejudice of their own, possessed, no doubt, by some opinion or other which
completely dulls their mental sight in this matter. I do not touch on the
insults, and even crimes, which are charged against us so that everyone may be
deterred from so much as tasting our teachings.

Yet only this can be charged against us: we strive to recall all those who are
counted by Christ's name, but still wander miserably, to those standards which
are their own—namely, the Word of God. At the same time, we also prove that
every controversy about God's worship must be settled from his Word, so that
each person may hold what is established to be from God.

What do our adversaries do here? They falsely plead “the Church,” using it like
Ajax's shield so that they may hide safely beneath its mask. The situation of
the prophets and apostles was no different when they had to deal with people
who, in the same bad faith, also usurped the church's name and supreme
authority within it. What, then? We know the church which rests upon a sure

### [CO X/1, p. 183]

foundation of prophetic and apostolic doctrine, whose immovable cornerstone
Christ is and remains. But that church in which God's Word does not reign is
counterfeit.

With this established, we infer that God's worship must be ordered from his
command. Nothing handed down or introduced by human agency can be borne here.
It belongs to God alone to fasten a law upon consciences. The right to
prescribe what he wishes us to do belongs to him alone.

From this come those complaints: “You have utterly destroyed the statutes of
holy mother church.” For, following Isaiah and on Christ's authority, we
testify that God is worshiped in vain by human commandments; likewise, from
James, that there is one lawgiver in whose power it is to save and destroy.
Indeed, if you examine more intently whatever it is that these people adorn
with the name of God's worship, you will find there a sheer invention fashioned
from the human brain.

Now, if faith and invocation are at issue, Scripture commends us to Christ's
grace alone and places before our eyes our miserable condition, which carries
the most certain condemnation with it. Following Scripture, however, we recall
people to the acknowledgment and proclamation of the grace of Christ alone.
That position cannot in turn be established without the most absurd fiction
of merits for earning eternal life collapsing in an instant.

From this also come those denunciations: “You remove from our midst every
doctrine of, and zeal for, good works.” As though that slander against us could
stand, when our adversaries' entire manner of teaching these subjects in their
sermons is more than empty if it is brought into comparison, even from afar,
with the serious and exact instruction in holy living which is delivered and
inculcated through our ministry.

We attend to this so constantly, however, that lest anyone deceive himself by
some boasting about his works, we teach plainly that we can do nothing good
unless God's Spirit directs us. We also teach that even for those who live so
holily—since our defects are innumerable, everything in us is frail in itself,
and nothing in us has any weight for exhibiting righteousness before God—no
other foundation exists upon which a solid righteousness may stand for us
than that we embrace with both hands the cross and death of Christ alone,
casting every other thing behind our backs.

To this foundation of faith we join true invocation in such a way that we show
both that our prayers must be directed to God alone when he summons us to
himself, and that, lest awareness of our unworthiness turn us away from him,
we must run with sure confidence to the throne of grace through Christ alone,
as he is offered to us as mediator with the Father.

From this also come those tears: “You are insulting toward God's male and
female saints! You have abolished their worship and honor!” Indeed! We retain
the memory of God's holy people with sincere devotion and pursue it with honor
by holding it in the place which the Lord teaches us to give it. But we cannot
endure that the saints themselves, transformed into idols,

### [CO X/1, p. 184]

are regarded in the place of God or Christ. Indeed, we dare also affirm that
if those godly and now blessed souls perceive any of the things by which the
world in its customary madness—always fastened upon creatures—carelessly
despises the Creator, if, I repeat, they perceive any of them, they are wholly
inflamed with zeal to summon God's judgment upon those sacrilegious people who
worship them.

As for the sacraments, since both their use and their purpose are to confirm
us in the fear and reverence of God and in faith in his mercy, it cannot be
said how shamefully everything has here too been inverted and corrupted. Yet
when we strive to restore them to their purity—as it were, to their true
origins—it is still objected to us that we empty the sacraments.

Let our entire practice be weighed against the Lord's one institution, so
that anything which differs from it may be marked. The mass, as it reigns
there, is indeed banished from here. But the Lord's Supper has returned by
right of restoration—the Lord's Supper itself, I say, as it was once for all
instituted by him, which not even the adversaries themselves can deny. As
their entire defense, they put forward only their own rite and the received
“divine service,” as they call it. But we rest upon a better and firmer right:
the command of the Lord himself, which must stand inviolate until the end of
the age.

“By doing this,” Paul says, “you will proclaim the Lord's death until he
comes.” No one, therefore, can change anything here without being held before
the Lord on a charge of rebellion.

I would become too lengthy if I touched on the remaining matters, which I
shall instead omit. It will now be enough that from the things you have here
you have received some taste which, when a fuller treatment is added, may lead
you to a richer knowledge.

Farewell in the Lord. May the Lord govern you by his Spirit, from whom you may
learn to answer to his will, and may he be pleased to provide for your
salvation and tranquility. Geneva.

## Translator's notes

1. The recipient is an unnamed adherent of the Roman church whom Calvin says
   he does not know by sight. The recipient's children prompted the letter and
   supplied Calvin's expectation that it would be read patiently. The closing
   gives Geneva but no date, recipient's name, or signature.
2. CO's source note makes the Latin a Bezan translation of a French work. The
   English therefore translates the Latin presentation without treating its
   individual turns of phrase as recoverable French wording.
3. The “Lesbian rule” is the ancient image of a pliable rule which bends to
   the object measured instead of supplying a fixed standard. Here it names
   the shifting norm which Calvin contrasts with Scripture; it has nothing to
   do with the modern sexual sense of the adjective.
4. “Ajax's shield” evokes the great shield behind which Ajax could shelter.
   Calvin charges his opponents with pleading “the Church” as protective
   cover while evading the prophetic and apostolic criterion by which he
   identifies the church.
5. *A nostris degustandis* leaves the substantive plural unstated. “Tasting our
   teachings” follows the immediate transition to *nostram doctrinam* without
   importing a more specific list of writings or doctrines.
6. The three biblical propositions attributed to Isaiah and Christ, James,
   and Paul are translated from Calvin's Latin rather than copied from a
   modern English Bible.
7. CO prints the future *compellet* in “when he summons us to himself.” The
   transcription preserves it rather than regularizing the form to a present
   subjunctive. “By right of restoration” renders *postliminio*, the Roman-law
   image of recovering one's former standing after return from exile.

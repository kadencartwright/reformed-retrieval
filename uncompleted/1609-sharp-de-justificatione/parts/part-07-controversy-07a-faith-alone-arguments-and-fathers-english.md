## Seventh controversy

The seventh controversy is: **Are we justified by faith alone?**

The Roman Catholics’ position on justification amounts to this. They teach
that before justification there occurs a preparation, which they call an
action, arising partly from the Holy Spirit and partly from the natural power
of free choice. By it a person is prepared for future justification, in which
they consider two things:

1. The foundation and beginning, namely faith.

[1609 e-rara witness | canvas 99 | image 15575071 | printed p. 97]

2. The things proceeding from this foundation, namely these six acts: fear,
hope, love, repentance, the resolve to receive the sacrament, the resolve to
undertake a new life, and observances of God’s commandments. When this
preparation has been made, they say that justification follows. As they
maintain, it is twofold: either the first justification, when a sinner becomes
good from being evil, for the accomplishment of which two things are required,
the forgiveness of sins and the infusion of inherent righteousness; or the
second, when someone becomes more righteous from being righteous. They
maintain that this righteousness proceeds from works of grace, because the
person who is righteous by the first righteousness can bring forth good works
by whose merit he can make himself more righteous.

Our position, however, is that a person cannot in any way prepare himself by
the natural powers of free choice for future justification, as will be shown
in the chapter on free choice. Next, faith is considered in justification not
as a preparation or the beginning of preparation, but as the instrumental
cause. Nor does anything in us contribute either as preparation or as cause
except faith alone, as the instrument which apprehends Christ’s merit; and in
this sense we assert that we are justified by faith alone. Those other six
acts are not causes of justification or antecedent preparations, but its
effects and consequences. We also maintain that the division of justification
into first and second is false, since justification is an indivisible act of
God by which he makes and pronounces us righteous on account of Christ. We say
also that our inherent righteousness and works pertain to sanctification, the
effect of justification, and not to justification itself as its cause.

On this question, **Are we justified by faith alone?**

[1609 e-rara witness | canvas 100 | image 15575072 | printed p. 98]

the Roman Catholics’ position is as follows. Bellarmine, book 1 *On
Justification*, chapter 12, asserts that this proposition, “We are justified
by faith alone,” is false. But from the judgment of the Council of Trent,
session 6, chapter 6, he enumerates those seven acts by which the ungodly are
disposed toward righteousness. Thus, he says that we are justified by faith,
but are prepared for justification by faith together with the other virtues.
In chapter 13 he grants that the ungodly person is justified through faith,
but only as through a disposition and as the first beginning and root, and
that it acts in the business of justification in such a way that it leaves
their place to the remaining virtues. In chapter 17 he says that faith
justifies by its own dignity and merit, not correlatively because it
apprehends and receives the things promised.

We, however, assert with Scripture that we are justified by faith alone. The
meaning of this proposition is that nothing in us, even when we have been
regenerated, causes justification except faith alone. This is not by its own
power or merit, or insofar as it is a quality in us, but correlatively and
insofar as it is an instrument which receives and applies the grace of God in
Christ. For in the covenant of grace there are two things: (1) the substance
of the covenant, which is that the righteousness and salvation of the church
are given in Christ; and (2) the condition annexed to the covenant, if the
members of the church believe. The giving on God’s part cannot be effectual
without reception on our part. It is therefore necessary that we have this
instrument for receiving and applying what God gives by grace. Accordingly we
are said to be justified *pistei* and *dia tēs pisteōs*—by faith and through
faith—but not *dia tēn pistin*—on account of faith. The exclusive particle
“alone” is added not to separate the other virtues from faith, or from the
person justified by faith, from whom they can no more be separated than light
from the sun or heat from fire. Rather, it separates them from causing
justification, either as

[1609 e-rara witness | canvas 101 | image 15575073 | printed p. 99]

preparations or by their own merit and dignity. We assert that a person is
justified by faith alone, relying on these arguments.

### Arguments

1. The promise, or the things promised, can be received only by faith alone,
Romans 4:16; Galatians 3:22. But the promise is forgiveness of sins,
reconciliation, and justification. Therefore justification takes place by
faith alone.

2. We are justified in the same way in which we obtain forgiveness of sins,
both at the beginning and in the progress of justification, because in Romans
4:7 forgiveness of sins, as a part of justification, is put synecdochically
for the whole. But we obtain forgiveness of sins by faith alone in Christ, not
on account of the other virtues or works. Therefore the conclusion follows.
Proof of the minor premise: we obtain forgiveness of sins in the same way in
which we apprehend Christ, because sins are forgiven on account of Christ,
Romans 3:25; Acts 10:45. But we apprehend Christ the mediator by faith alone,
Hebrews 4:15–16. Therefore we obtain forgiveness of sins by faith alone and,
consequently, are justified by faith alone.

3. We are justified through that alone by which Christ’s righteousness is
imputed to us. But Christ’s righteousness is imputed to us through faith
alone, Romans 3:22, 28; 4:5, 24. Therefore the conclusion follows.

4. We are justified through that alone by which we have access to the Father
and to the grace in which we stand, whether one regards the beginning or the
progress of justification. For to have access to the Father in Christ, to
obtain grace, and to stand in grace is to be justified in Christ; and to stand
in grace signifies the continuation of justification. But by faith alone in
Christ we have access to the Father and to the grace in which we stand, Romans
5:2. Therefore the conclusion follows.

[1609 e-rara witness | canvas 102 | image 15575074 | printed p. 100]

5. We are justified through that alone by which Abraham, the father of the
faithful, was justified, for there is one and the same way and the same means
by which all believers are justified, Romans 4:23. But Abraham was justified
by faith alone, Romans 4:2–3. Therefore the conclusion follows.

6. If Abraham was justified by faith—not merely at the beginning of
justification or by faith as preparation, but if his justification before God
is continually attributed to faith both at the beginning and in the progress
of faith—then faith alone justifies, and this not merely as the beginning of
justification. But the former is true, Hebrews 15; Genesis 15. Therefore the
conclusion follows.

7. Those who are justified only through faith in Jesus Christ are justified
by faith alone. But no one is justified except through faith in Jesus Christ,
Galatians 2:16. Therefore the conclusion follows.

8. That alone and always justifies which Christ says that he alone requires
on our part for receiving his benefits and grace. But he requires faith alone
for receiving these things, Mark 5:36; Luke 8:50; Matthew 9:20. Therefore the
conclusion follows.

9. We are justified through that alone and always by which we become children
of God and have peace with him. But we have these things through faith alone,
John 1:12; 3:15–16; Romans 5:1; 1 John 5:1. Therefore the conclusion follows.

10. We are justified through that alone, and not merely as the beginning of
justification, which in us receives the gospel unto salvation and through
whose continuance in us we obtain salvation and eternal life. But faith
receives the gospel unto salvation, Romans 1:16, and through faith we obtain
salvation, John 3:16; Romans 10:8, 10; Habakkuk 2:4; Romans 1:17. Therefore the
conclusion follows.

11. We are justified in the same way in which the righteousness presented to
us in the gospel is revealed and

[1609 e-rara witness | canvas 103 | image 15575075 | printed p. 101]

manifested as being presented to us. But the righteousness of God is revealed in
the gospel as being presented to us from faith to faith—that is, so that it
may be believed solely and continuously through the advances of faith—Romans
1:17. Therefore we are justified by faith alone, not as though by the
beginning of justification, but by faith which continuously apprehends the
promises more and more.

12. The doctrine which excludes the Jews from God’s righteousness, and which
the apostle dissuades them from embracing, must be avoided; conversely, the
doctrine by which we attain God’s righteousness must be embraced. But the
doctrine which teaches that we are justified not by faith alone but through
the works of the moral law which we perform is that which excludes the Jews
from God’s righteousness, and so forth, Romans 10:3. And the doctrine of
justification by faith alone is that which the apostle commends in verse 4 and
the following verses of the same chapter. Therefore the conclusion follows.

13. If Scripture excludes from the beginning, progress, and completion of
justification everything which is in us except faith alone, then we are
justified by faith alone. But the former is true, Romans 3:20, 27–28; 4:2;
Galatians 2:16, 21; Ephesians 2:8; Philippians 3:8. Therefore the conclusion
follows.

14. The person of the sinner must please God before his work does, because no
one can bring what is clean from what is unclean, John 14:5; 15:14. But the
person of the sinner cannot please God before he is fully justified. Therefore
before justification no one can prepare himself for justification by inward
virtues, nor is there anything in us besides faith which causes justification.
Therefore the conclusion follows.

15. From John 3:14–15: the bronze serpent in the wilderness was a type of
Christ, and the people’s healing by looking at the serpent was a type of the
manner of our healing from sin through Christ. But the people were healed only
by looking at the serpent, not by the works of physicians or in any other way.

[1609 e-rara witness | canvas 104 | image 15575076 | printed p. 102]

Therefore we too are healed from sin—that is, justified—only by the gaze of
faith upon Christ, that is, by faith alone in Christ.

### Testimonies of the Fathers

1. Chrysostom, homily 3 on the epistle to Titus: “If you believe,” he says,
“why do you introduce other things to faith, as though faith alone were not
sufficient to justify?”

2. The same author, homily *On Faith and the Law of Nature*: “No one had life
without faith. But the thief only believed and was justified.”

3. Hilary, canon 8 on Matthew: “Christ forgave what the law could not release,
for faith alone justifies.”

4. Basil, homily *On Humility*: “This,” he says, “is perfect and complete
glorying in God: when someone does not vaunt himself on account of his own
righteousness, but knows himself to be unworthy of true righteousness and is
justified by faith alone in Christ.”

5. Ambrose, commentary on Romans 3: “They are justified freely,” he says,
“because, doing nothing and rendering nothing in return, they are justified by
faith alone, by the gift of God.”

6. The same author on Romans 4: “There is therefore no need for the law when
the ungodly person is justified through faith alone.”

7. The same author in the same place: “Thus it was decreed by God,” he says,
“that, when the law ceased, they should require faith alone by the grace of God
for salvation.”

8. In the same place: “Blessed,” he says, “are those concerning whom God has
determined this: that without labor and any observance they are justified
before God by faith alone.”

9. The same author on Romans 9: “Faith alone has been appointed for salvation.”

10. Augustine, book 1 *Against Two Letters of the Pelagians*, chapter 21:

[1609 e-rara witness | canvas 105 | image 15575077 | printed p. 103]

“However great the virtue you may proclaim the righteous ancients to have
possessed, only faith saved them.”

11. The same author, *Eighty-Three Questions*, question 76: “If someone should
depart from this life immediately after believing, the justification of faith
remains with him, not because of preceding good works, since he came to it not
by merit but by grace.”

12. Theophylact, commentary on Galatians 3: “Faith alone has in itself the
power of justifying.”

13. Bernard, sermon 22 on the *Song of Songs*: “Whoever,” he says, “is pierced
with remorse for his sins and thirsts for righteousness, let him believe in
you who justify the ungodly, and, justified through faith alone, he will have
peace from you.”

14. The same author, letter 77, on the words “The one who believes and is
baptized will be saved”: “Carefully,” he says, “he does not repeat, ‘But the
one who has not been baptized will be condemned,’ but only, ‘But the one who
does not believe,’ plainly implying that faith alone is sometimes sufficient
for salvation, and that without it nothing is sufficient.”

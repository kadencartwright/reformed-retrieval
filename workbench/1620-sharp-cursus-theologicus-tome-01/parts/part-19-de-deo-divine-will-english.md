On the Will of God

Will is a communicable attribute of God by which God is signified to will
freely, most simply and infinitely, to approve or disapprove what he wisely
knows. He wills most simply because God’s will is purest act, not potency.
Indeed, it is the very essence of God, and what he wills he wills by his
essence. He wills infinitely because he wills without beginning and end,
without succession or change, and freely, impelled by no cause, Matthew 11:25
and 20:15, 1 Corinthians 12:11, Ephesians 1:11, and Romans 9:18. Thus he
neither begins to will what he did not will, nor conversely, nor can he be
prevented from executing what he wills, Numbers 29:19,[^1] 1 Samuel 15:29,
Psalm 33:11, Proverbs 29:21,[^2] Malachi 3:6, James 1:17, and so forth. Nor can
he will contradictory things, because he is always like himself, Psalm 89:34
and Ezekiel 9:28.[^3]

Will has three meanings:

1. It signifies the power of willing.

2. The act of willing.

3. A decree of the will, as a testament is called the will of the testator.
These are distinct in creatures, but in God they are one most simple will of
God.

Although God’s will is one in itself, it is nevertheless said to be manifold:

1. Because of the different kinds of things which God wills.

2. Because of the different modes in which he appears to will the things he
wills. Hence the various divisions of God’s will arise:

1. Into antecedent will, which concerns God’s decree, and consequent will,
which concerns the execution of the decree; thus Damascene, *On the Orthodox
Faith*, book 2, chapter 46.

2. It is divided into the will of good pleasure, which is so called because
of the cause that moves God to will the things he has willed, namely his good
pleasure, and the will of sign, by which he has signified his decree to men,
and that in the word; or, as others would have it, it is distinguished into a
will of good plea—

[1620 Tome I | col. 198 | BSB frame 00115]

—sure, which is so called by reason of good effects, whose end and means God
intends primarily and in themselves, and a will of pleasure, which is so
called by reason of evil effects, in which God intends the end primarily and
in itself, but not the means themselves except for the sake of the end;
thus Lombard, *Sentences*, book 1, distinction 145.[^4]

3. Into absolute and conditional will.

4. Into revealed and unrevealed will.

5. Into the will concerning what God wishes to do with us and the will
concerning what he wishes to be done by us.

6. It is divided into efficacious and inefficacious will, which is called
conditional. They call it inefficacious because it depends upon a condition.

All these divisions come down to the same thing.

The object of the will is twofold, good or evil; the good is twofold:

1. God himself, who before all things wills his own glory, Isaiah 48:11,
Proverbs 16:4, and 1 Corinthians 10:32.[^5]

2. All other natural, moral, and divine things, which he wills, loves, and
approves.

There is no agreement among everyone about how God wills evil, and on this
matter there are four opinions:

1. The opinion of the Libertines, who say that God so wills sin that he is
the author and cause of sin. They therefore maintain that sins are not to be
reproved, but this opinion is blasphemy.

2. The opinion of the Manichaeans, who used to say that God could in no way
be said to will sin. They therefore established another principle of evil and
a supreme evil contrary to God; this opinion too is impious.

3. Others hold a middle way between those extremes and say that God wills sin
and is its cause, yet that the fault is not in God but in men, because when he
wills and performs sin, he acts justly.

4. The opinion of Augustine and others of our time, who deny that God wills
sin but say that he willingly permits it to occur.

The true opinion, however, is this: E—

[1620 Tome I | col. 199 | BSB frame 00116]

—vil is twofold, the evil of fault or of punishment. God wills the evil of
punishment as its efficient cause and as Judge. For every good is from God,
and punishment considered as punishment is a good because it is a work of
justice. The evil of fault, or sin, is considered in two ways:

1. Insofar as it is properly something at variance with the law of God.

2. Insofar as it is the punishment of a prior sin, for God punishes sins with
sins, Romans 1:26 and 2 Thessalonians 2:11; and in this sense God wills sin.
Next, three things are considered in sin:

1. Its subject, which is an internal or external action. But every action,
insofar as it is an action, is good, and in this way God wills sin by reason
of its subject.

2. The deformity of the action that departs from the rectitude of the divine
law. In this sense, since it is only a mere privation of the good, God cannot
will sin, as in Psalm 5:5, Habakkuk 1:43,[^6] and Romans 9:14, because, as the
supreme good, he cannot will sin as such.

3. Liability, or obligation to punishment. Although this liability has its
foundation in the very deformity of the action, it nevertheless arises from
divine justice. This liability is called sin when God is said to forgive
sins, that is, the liability or obligation to punishment; and in this sense,
insofar as sin is an obligation to death arising from the justice of God, God
wills sin.

But it is asked whether he in any way wills sin insofar as it is a deformity
departing from the rectitude of the law.

Response. The verb *to will* is considered in two ways:

1. Properly and in itself. In this sense everything we will is desirable
and thus good; and in this sense God does not will sin.

2. Improperly and for the sake of another thing, that is, because of the good
that follows from it. He does not will it in itself, and thus God wills
the deformity of sin not because he loves and approves it, but because of the
good ends

[1620 Tome I | col. 200 | BSB frame 00116]

which he draws from it. Finally, the things God wills he wills freely, yet in
such a way that from eternity he has willed in one constant act the things he
wills; and the things he wills can neither be changed nor prevented, because
he is immutable.

This single will of God,[^7] according to the diversity of the things presented
to it, has different names and is called truth, goodness, love, hatred,
justice, mercy, wrath, chastity, and so forth.

THE

Cause of God *against* the Anti-Sabbatarians.

BOOK TWO.

On Worship and Solemn Time.

Because in this discussion about the *Sabbath* many matters occur which concern
*the solemn worship of God* and *solemn time*—for when the Sabbath is discussed,
some particular and solemn time is discussed, and when the sanctification of
the Sabbath is examined, the worship of God is also under discussion—we have
therefore thought it necessary, in order to make our way easier, to set out in
this book some more general matters concerning both worship and time, solemn
time, that is. Accordingly:

*Chapter I.* The nature of *worship* in general is explained to some extent,
and some of its divisions are proposed.

*Chapter II.* *Solitary worship* is explained, and it is shown how it is solemn.

*Chapter III.* *Domestic worship* is treated in particular.

*Chapter IV.* Some general matters concerning *time* are proposed for
consideration.

*Chapter V.* These general matters concerning *time* are applied more
particularly to religion, or divine worship.

*Chapter VI.* It is shown how useful and necessary it is to religion in general
that *time* be determined for the worship of God.

*Chapter VII.* It is shown how useful the determination of time for the worship

[1674 logical printed p. 234 | IA leaf 252 | PDF p. 252]

of God is with respect to *duration* and *frequency*, both separately and
together.

*Chapter VIII.* Whether and how the determination of *time* is useful to
religion with respect to *when*, or its *beginning*, is investigated.

*Chapter IX.* The manner of determining various aspects of *time* in relation
to religion is treated.

*Chapter X.* The *principal solemn time* and the necessary determination of it
for the worship of God are treated.

*Chapter XI.* It is demonstrated that a human being is not permitted to
determine this *principal solemn time*.

*Chapter XII.* It is shown by firm arguments that the church is not permitted
to determine this *principal solemn time*.

*Chapter XIII.* It is proved that the church is not permitted to institute
*annual feast days*.

*Chapter XIV.* It is proved by many arguments that the power to determine this
*principal solemn time* belongs to God alone.

*Chapter XV.* It is demonstrated that the observance of *solemn time* is a part
of divine worship.

*Chapter XVI.* The contrary opinion of Dr. *Waleus* and his arguments are
examined.

*Chapter XVII.* It is proved by arguments that all time is not now equal with
respect to holiness.

*Chapter XVIII.* The arguments for the contrary opinion are examined.

*Chapter XIX.* Some general matters concerning *place* in relation to religion
are proposed, and its *necessity* and *utility* are investigated.

*Chapter XX.* It is proved by many arguments to be utterly false—that much
repeated dictum of the adversaries—that *place* and *time* are equal
circumstances of religion.

# CHAPTER VI (continued)

*Objection 16.* 2 Corinthians 3:6, 7, 11, *where the law is distinguished
from the Gospel in that it is called the letter that kills, the ministry of
death, and something formed upon stones, and is now removed as useless.*

*Answer.* Here the apostle indeed compares the legal ministry with the
evangelical ministry and prefers the latter to the former, because, while
the former was the ministry of the letter and of death, the latter is the
ministry of the Spirit and of life, and of righteousness rather than
condemnation. That is, the law and its administration under that old economy
were ineffectual for giving salvation. For it was merely engraved on stones
and set forth by Moses to be read and known, but it did not change the heart
or grant the strength to obey. It thereby convicted and condemned as guilty
of sin all who were disobedient,

[Printed p. 69 | IA PDF 77 | IA leaf 77 | IA decausadei02brow]

and showed no way of escape. By contrast, the Gospel has the Spirit of
Christ joined to it. He gives life and produces in the hearts of the elect
the faith by which they are justified and obtain forgiveness of sins. The
Gospel thus demonstrates the way to obtain salvation clearly and openly.
[Beza says on the passage: *“With respect to its hearers, the law has no more
power than a piece of writing which is in itself devoid of every power,
unless it is among people by whom it can be fulfilled. But since the law
does not find in us the capacity for such fulfillment, but rather contrary
darkness and a mind wholly opposed to it, its external preaching merely
teaches what God demands and what he prohibits. It not only fails to correct
that fault of ours but even provokes it, and from this comes a headlong leap
into death unless a remedy is sought elsewhere. But the Gospel, or new
covenant, insofar as it is opposed to the law considered in and of itself,
is called Spirit for many reasons. The first is because of the old temporary
ceremonies, which were placed in gross and corporeal things. The next
reason—and the one to be regarded as the most important for us in this
passage—is that the subject matter, content, and finally the minister of the
Gospel is Jesus Christ. He no longer teaches what one ought to do and what
one ought to avoid (which is the office of the law), but freely offers
himself to believers unto eternal life; nor does he merely offer himself,
but actually conveys himself to them. For the Gospel does not offer
righteousness under the condition of faith in the same way as the law
offers blessing with the condition of works added. (In that case both the
law and the Gospel would offer not life but death, since by ourselves we can
neither act nor believe.) Rather, they do so in entirely different ways. The
law requires works, and indeed perfect works, but cannot provide the capacity
to perform them, and therefore condemns those lying in death. The Gospel,
however, through the Spirit of Christ whom it has joined to it, also gives
the elect the faith which it requires from us, so that, justified and
sanctified in Christ apprehended by faith, they may attain eternal life.”*]
Yet nothing can be drawn from this by which it could be proved that the moral
law has been so abolished that it is no longer a rule for those who are
justified. The ceremonial law, which was part of the economy of that
covenant, has indeed been abolished. The whole law has also been abolished,
not insofar as it is the rule of life and conduct, but insofar as it was set
in opposition to the Gospel, or the new dispensation of the covenant. That
is, it has been abolished insofar as it was the letter that kills and the
ministry of death and condemnation, or insofar as it exposes everyone
without exception to the curse (Galatians 3:10), and insofar as it was then
administered with the full array of ceremonies joined to it.

2. Bullinger thinks that the apostle speaks here about the law insofar as it
contained the covenant of works and its observance was urged by false
teachers. For he speaks as follows: [*“It is therefore evident from both this
and Paul's other epistles that certain obscure wanderers, whom some called
Nazarenes and others Mineans, violently disturbed churches which had already
been properly established by the apostles. By secretly entering them, these
people were compelling them to Judaize, mixing legal observances with the
Gospel as though the grace of Christ were not sufficiently effectual for
attaining salvation. On the authority of the letter of the law, therefore,
they taught that circumcision and the observance of the whole law were
absolutely necessary for attaining salvation. The written law of Moses
seemed to lend them very strong support, since it strictly required
circumcision, sacrifices, and weighty works. The apostle therefore does not
call the entire law, or the meaning of the law, the letter, but the whole
kind of doctrine which was delivered by these people. By Spirit he means*

[Printed p. 70 | IA PDF 78 | IA leaf 78 | IA decausadei02brow]

*the preaching itself of the Gospel and of God's grace, which attributes
everything to Christ alone and nothing to legal ceremonies, works, or our
own powers.”*] A little later he also adds these things, which should be
carefully noted: [*“Thus as far as the Old Testament is concerned, there is
no less Spirit and life in it than in the New, provided we remember that it
contains very many types which must be expounded by faith. Again, the New
Testament writers, when speaking about the law, frequently used synecdoche.
The unimpaired authority of Scripture also still remains. In short, Christ
is the Spirit in both testaments, while the letter is the veil itself, a
precept without faith, a type without the reality and the Spirit—indeed,
whatever the flesh imagines for itself outside Christ and his Spirit.”*]
These things, then, should not be understood absolutely, nor should it be
said absolutely that the moral law has been abolished, but only with respect
to a particular aspect.

3. Let us hear Calvin as quoted by Marlorat: [*“By the name of the letter,”*
he says, *“Paul means external preaching which does not reach the heart; by
the Spirit, however, he means living doctrine which works effectually in
souls through the grace of the Spirit. The letter is therefore equivalent
to dead and ineffectual preaching which is received only by the ears. The
Spirit, however, is spiritual doctrine; that is, doctrine which is not only
uttered by the mouth but effectually penetrates all the way into souls with
a living perception. For Paul had in view the passage in Jeremiah 31:33. For
this reason he calls the law the letter, because its preaching is dead in
itself, but the Gospel the Spirit, because its ministry is living—indeed,
life-giving. We must next answer that these things are not predicated
absolutely either of the law or of the Gospel, but insofar as the one is set
in opposition to the other. For the Gospel is not always Spirit, but when
the two are compared it is truly and fittingly said that the nature of the
law is to teach people literally, so that it does not penetrate beyond the
ears, whereas the nature of the Gospel is to teach spiritually, because by
the grace of Christ it is an instrument.”*] And on verse 8 he has this:
[*“Let us now examine briefly those attributes of the law and the Gospel. But
we should remember that he is not speaking about the entire doctrine (note
well) which is contained in the law and the prophets, nor is he treating
what happened to the fathers under the Old Testament. He merely touches on
what pertains particularly to the ministry of Moses. The law was engraved
on stones and therefore was a literal doctrine. This defect of the law had
to be corrected through the Gospel, because it was necessarily fragile as
long as it was recorded only on tablets of stone. The Gospel, therefore, is
a holy and inviolable covenant, because it was ratified with the Spirit of
God as its sponsor. It will also follow from this that the law was the
ministry of condemnation and death. For when people are taught about their
duty and hear that all who do not satisfy God's righteousness are cursed
(Deuteronomy 27:26), they are arraigned for sin and death. Thus from the law
they carry away nothing but guilt of this kind, because there God demands
what is owed to him but does not confer the ability to perform it.”*] Let the
other writers in Marlorat also be read.

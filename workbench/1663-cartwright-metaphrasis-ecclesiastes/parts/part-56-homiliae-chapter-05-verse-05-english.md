VERSE 5. It is better that you do not vow than that you vow and do not pay.

He further confirms this doctrine by a comparison with the lesser case:
because it is better not to vow than not to pay vows, or to defer and postpone
them. Deuteronomy 23:22. The person who has not vowed is said not to sin,
although the person who makes vows and performs them does better. So too in 1
Corinthians 7:38, the man who gives his daughter in marriage does well, while
the man who does not give her does better.

Yet what is written in Psalm 76 seems to conflict with this: “Vow to the Lord
and pay the Lord your God; let all who surround him bring a gift to the
fearsome one.” There a command seems to be given both to make and to pay vows.
I answer that it is not an unqualified command, but one similar to “Be angry
and do not sin.” It does not command us to be angry. Rather, if we become
angry, it forbids us to continue in anger or to let the sun go down upon our
anger. Alternatively, it may be called counsel which is to be obeyed only when
times arise that are suitable and opportune for us to vow. See 1 Corinthians
7:25–26.

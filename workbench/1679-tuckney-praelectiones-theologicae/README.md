# Anthony Tuckney, *Praelectiones theologicae* (1679)

> **Working edition in progress:** This directory contains an AI-assisted,
> diplomatic-normalized Latin transcription and an independent close English
> translation. Both are unreviewed working texts and are not publication-ready.

## Work identity

- **Author:** Anthony Tuckney (1599–1670)
- **Title:** *Praelectiones theologicae, nec non determinationes quaestionum
  variarum insignium in scholis academicis Cantabrigiensibus habitae; quibus
  accedunt exercitia pro gradibus capessendis*
- **Edition used:** Amsterdam, Stephanus Swart, for Jonathan Robinson and
  George Wells, 1679
- **Form:** a posthumous anthology of theological lectures, determinations,
  degree exercises, and sermons, transcribed by the editor from Tuckney's
  autograph manuscripts

## Controlling witness and rights

The controlling witness is the Early English Books Online microfilm scan at
the Internet Archive,
[`bim_early-english-books-1641-1700_prlectiones-theologicae_tuckney-anthony_1679`](https://archive.org/details/bim_early-english-books-1641-1700_prlectiones-theologicae_tuckney-anthony_1679).
The catalog identity is independently confirmed by the
[Folger Shakespeare Library record](https://catalog.folger.edu/record/430688).
The 1679 book and Tuckney's underlying text are public domain. Internet
Archive page images control the transcription; its page-structured OCR is
used only as a navigation and collation aid.

The scan contains 678 image leaves and several independent pagination
sequences because the volume is an anthology, not a single continuous
treatise. Parts therefore follow complete lectures or other coherent items,
not a volume-wide page quota.

## Editorial method

Long *s*, ligatures, line-end divisions, and typographic `u/v` are normalized.
Substantive spelling and wording are retained. Greek and Hebrew are retained
where legible; a damaged or genuinely doubtful reading is marked explicitly
rather than silently invented. Running heads, printed page numbers,
signatures, catchwords, ornaments, and copy-specific stamps are omitted.
Marginal source citations are retained in bracketed blocks. Source markers
give the 1679 printed page and zero-based Internet Archive leaf number.

The English is a fresh, full translation from the paired Latin, not a summary.
No modern English rendering was used as a wording source. Both files remain
AI-assisted and unreviewed pending complete comparison by a qualified human
editor.

## Part 01: complete probationary lecture

Part 01 is Tuckney's complete `Praelectio probatoria`, his probationary or
inaugural lecture on John 17:3 and the true deity of Christ. It begins with
the heading and prayer on printed p. 1 / IA leaf 9 and ends with the lecture's
final sentence on printed p. 19 / IA leaf 27. The next item, a lecture on
Romans 1:17, begins on printed p. 20 / IA leaf 28 and is excluded.

## Part 03: second unit of the Romans 1:17 lecture

Part 03 continues with the proofs that the scriptural meaning of

printed p. 51 / IA leaf 59. The next sentence is `Et primo quod ad
Redemptionem attinet, cum Redemptio in genere sit ...`; it begins the distinct
discussion of redemption on that same printed page and is excluded.

hactenus in Controversiae hujus statu constituendo Adversariorum tantum
exposuimus sententias ...` on printed p. 88 / IA leaf 96. The next paragraph,
`4. At 4. in loco nobis expedita est fides omnia significari ...`, states
Tuckney's own position and is excluded.

## Part 07: sixth unit of the Romans 1:17 lecture

Part 07 states Tuckney’s own position that every kind of work is excluded
from constituting justification, invokes Cardinal Contarini on resting upon
Christ’s imputed righteousness, and establishes the first of two foundations
for evaluating the opposing positions: the safer interpretation assigns less
to us and more to grace. It begins with `4. At 4. in loco nobis expedita est
fides omnia significari ...` partway down printed p. 88 / IA leaf 96 and ends
with `Nostra ergo quae totum hoc gratiae tribuit ... congruentior` partway
down printed p. 90 / IA leaf 98. The next sentence, `2. Secundum fundamentum
hoc esto`, begins the second foundation and is excluded.

## Part 08: seventh unit of the Romans 1:17 lecture

Part 08 begins Tuckney's second foundation for adjudicating competing accounts
of justification: Paul's argument must be understood from the historical aim
and genuine state of the controversy. It completes the first of two inquiries,
identifying both the open opponents who set law against gospel and the
pseudo-apostles and seduced believers who combined law with gospel, and then
applies that distinction against the Roman Catholic inclusion of works done
from faith and grace. It begins with `2. Secundum fundamentum hoc esto` on
printed p. 90 / IA leaf 98 and ends with `at suis operibus ... confisos` at
the end of printed p. 93 / IA leaf 101. The next paragraph, `Quod vero alicubi
respondet Bellarminus ...`, begins a subsidiary answer concerning Abraham on
printed p. 94 / IA leaf 102 and is excluded.

- Latin: `parts/part-08-romans-01-17-justification-07-latin.txt`

## Part 09: eighth unit of the Romans 1:17 lecture

Part 09 answers Bellarmine's attempt to restrict Paul's exclusion of a
believer's works, then completes Tuckney's second inquiry into the principal
aim of Paul's argument. Augustine, Ambrose, Jerome, and Protestant expositors
are marshalled to show that Paul's chief thesis is gratuitous justification
by faith in Christ without works of any kind. It begins with `Quod vero
alicubi respondet Bellarminus ...` on printed p. 94 / IA leaf 102 and ends
with `Et primo de priore` partway down printed p. 98 / IA leaf 106. The next
centered question, `An cum Apostolus nos ex operibus legis justificari negat,
sola opera Ceremonialia excludat`, begins the separate examination of the
first Roman Catholic restriction and is excluded.

## Part 10: ninth unit of the Romans 1:17 lecture

Part 10 opens the separate inquiry whether Paul's exclusion of works of the
law reaches ceremonial works alone. After three preliminary concessions,
Tuckney contrasts the continuing office of the moral law with the abolished
ceremonial law and presents ten positive arguments that Paul excludes moral
as well as ceremonial works from justification. It begins with the centered
question `An cum Apostolus nos ex operibus legis justificari negat, sola opera
Ceremonialia excludat` on printed p. 98 / IA leaf 106 and ends with the two
brief corroborating appeals to Romans 4 and Philippians 3 partway down printed
p. 106 / IA leaf 114. The next sentence, `Restant tandem ad hanc absolvendam
controversiam quaedam Adversariorum argumenta diluenda`, opens the answers to
the opposing arguments and is excluded.

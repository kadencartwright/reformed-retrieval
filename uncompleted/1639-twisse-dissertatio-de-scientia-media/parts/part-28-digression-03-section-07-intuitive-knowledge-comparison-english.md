RESPONSE.

But nothing terminates our intuitive knowledge except what exists simply. Hence, although the
certainty of divine cognition possesses the mode of certainty belonging to created cognitions, as
Navarrete himself had said in Section 6, Conclusion 12, God’s intuitive cognition itself is not
conformed to the likeness of human cognition.

Secondly, nothing terminates the angels’ intuitive knowledge except what exists simply. It follows
that God’s intuitive cognition is by no means conformed to angelic intuitive cognition. But God
also has intuitive cognition of himself, and indeed as existing in eternity. Will it therefore
follow that God by no means exists simply? Certainly the intuitive cognition of things will be in
no way conformed to God’s intuitive cognition by which God understands himself; yet it was commonly
said that by knowing himself God also knows all things.

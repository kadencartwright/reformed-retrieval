CHAPTER XVI

ON

THE MINISTERS OF THE WORD

ROMANS 10:15

“How, then, will they preach unless they are sent?”

The sending of ministers is twofold. One kind is immediate and extraordinary,
as was that of the apostles. The other is mediate, carried out in the ordinary
way through the church. We have a dispute with the Remonstrants concerning
both kinds of sending. The former was extraordinary because the apostles were
immediately moved and inspired by the Holy Spirit, so that they could not err.

Hence it is asked whether the divine inspired communication with which the
apostles were endowed by God was the norm and rule according to which the Old
Testament, insofar as the subject contained in that communication was
concerned, had to be interpreted; or whether all their inspired communications
had to be tested against the norm of the Word. The Remonstrants say that these
communications had no need of examination by God's Word.

We frame the matter differently. Distinctions are needed here. 1. One account
applies to those who are divinely inspired, and another to their hearers. 2.
The authority of these inspired communications in and of themselves is one
thing, and their authority in relation to us is another. 3. The account of
these

[1668 KBNL/UvA witness | logical printed p. 682 | PDF p. 731 | split word joined: afflatuum]

inspired communications in act or exercise is one thing, and the account of
them in a habitual state is another.

Hence Assertion 1. While the apostles themselves were being inspired by these
communications in act and exercise, they did not need to examine and test
them, with respect to the subject contained in them, by the law of nature or
by other Scriptures. The reason is that Abraham, when addressed by God's
inspiration, did not need to examine whether the slaughter of his son was
conformed to the law of nature when God commanded it. For that inspired
command to slaughter his son essentially included a revelation made to
Abraham that the command was lawful and divinely inspired. Hence the angel's
address to Zechariah and the voice directed to Peter in Acts 10—both of which
they resisted—were not immediate inspired communications, but a bare
communication of God's will sent to them through an angel or a heavenly voice.

Assertion 2. But when prophets or apostles were not in the actual exercise of
their inspired communications, they could doubt and therefore examine those
communications by God's Word. Hence Jeremiah, when calling into question
whether he himself ought to prophesy in chapter 20:9—“I said, I will not make
mention of him, nor will I speak any more in his name”—needed, in the light of
God's Word revealed to him in chapter 1:7–10 in a divine inspired
communication, to examine whether this was rightly said (“I will not speak any
more in his name”), and also to examine by God's inspired communications
whether he had been called to

[1668 KBNL/UvA witness | logical printed p. 683 | PDF p. 732 | split word joined: prophetandum]

prophesy. Likewise, if the apostles had doubted Christ's resurrection, they
would have had to examine by the Scriptures the inspired communications by
which they had once believed that article.

Assertion 3. But the hearers of the apostles and prophets needed to examine,
by the rule of sacred Scripture, the law of nature, and the Decalogue, whether
what the prophets and apostles said conformed to Scripture. For the apostles'
immediate inspired communications, although in themselves no less authentic
than the canon of Scripture, contain God's very own will formally and are in
every respect the same as God's Word; and God's Word in itself is God's Word
and αὐθεντικὸν, authoritative. Nevertheless, in relation to us those
communications are not formally a rule of faith that regulates, but in
relation to us a rule that is regulated. Hence the hearers needed to examine
them; indeed, they could and ought to test by God's Word whether those inspired
communications were, with respect to their subject, God's Word. This is proved
against the Remonstrants.

1. 1 John 4:1: “Beloved, do not believe every spirit, but test the spirits,
whether they are from God.” 1 Thessalonians 5:21: “Test all things; hold fast
what is good.”

2. The Bereans are praised as more noble in Acts 17:11 because they examined
the Scriptures daily to see whether the things announced by Paul under divine
inspiration were so. Therefore, they were not bound to regard Paul's immediate
inspired communications, however divinely inspired in themselves, as a
regulating and

[1668 KBNL/UvA witness | logical printed p. 684 | PDF p. 733 | split word joined: immotâ]

immovable rule, but to examine them from God's Word.

3. The apostles—and even Christ—prove what they preached not by divine
inspired communications but by the Scriptures of the Old Testament, by the
law and the prophets, 1 Corinthians 15:4; Acts 26:22.

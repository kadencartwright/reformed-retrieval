# How a Godly Man May Withdraw from the Superstitions of the Papacy

This directory presents the complete Latin-form witness and a new English
translation of the consilium headed *Ut se pius homo a papatus
superstitionibus subducat* in the *Calvini Opera*.

## Work and witness identity

| Field | Value |
|---|---|
| Historical author | John Calvin (collection attribution and closing *Tuus Calvinus*) |
| CO consilium | II.5, *Ut se pius homo a papatus superstitionibus subducat* |
| Date | 15 June; year not preserved |
| Place | Geneva |
| Addressee | Unnamed “dearest brother” |
| Authorial language | French, according to Theodore Beza as reported by CO |
| Witness edited here | Beza's early Latin translation, reprinted by CO |
| Genre | Pastoral and sacramental advice in letter form |
| Principal topics | Conversion from error, union with Christ, resurrection, the Lord's Supper and the mass, idolatry, confession of faith, and religious dissimulation |
| CO locator | X/1, pp. 188–189 |

The language-state distinction is essential. CO's only source note says that
Beza is the authority for the claim that this item “was also written in
French” and that he published it in Latin. CO does not print a French
archetype here, and this directory does not retrovert one from Beza's Latin.
`latin.txt` is therefore a normalized transcription of a historically early
translation witness; `english.md` is a new translation of that Latin witness,
not a claim to reproduce Calvin's lost French wording.

The item is also distinct from the preceding French advice *De l'émigration
pour cause de religion* at CO X/1 187–188. That preceding work has a separate
manuscript history and a separately recorded Latin translation entitled
*Quae ratio a pio viro teneri possit ubi religionis causa domo migrare
cogetur*. Similar themes and their sharing p. 188 do not make the two texts a
single document.

## Edition, heading, and earlier print

The transcription follows Wilhelm Baum, Eduard Cunitz, and Eduard Reuss,
eds., *Ioannis Calvini opera quae supersunt omnia*, vol. X/1 (*Corpus
Reformatorum* XXXVIII) (Brunswick: C. A. Schwetschke, 1871), pp. 188–189.

CO records Beza's Latin in the Geneva *Epistolae et responsa* (1575), p. 347,
and in the Lausanne, p. 657; Hanover, p. 739; Chouet, p. 477; and Amsterdam,
p. 217 editions. The displayed title and CO's numbered publication note are
paratext, not part of the address to the recipient. The historical body begins
*Gratias ago Domino, frater carissime* and ends with the place, day and month,
and *Tuus Calvinus*. The year and recipient's name are genuinely absent and
are not conjecturally supplied.

The work's opening thanksgiving and closing signature establish its epistolary
form. Its central answer is conditional: participation in the Lord's Supper is
permitted only if the rite at issue really is Christ's Supper. The contrast
that follows denies such identity to the papal mass. Extracting the initial
*licere omnino* without its condition and argument would reverse the advice.

## Translation status and cruxes

Mary D. Beaty and Benjamin Wirt Farley's copyrighted *Calvin's Ecclesiastical
Advice* (1991) translates the complete 46-item *consilia* collection. The
bibliographical audit located no complete reusable public-domain English
version of this item. Their wording was not consulted or used;
[`english.md`](english.md) was translated afresh from the Latin transcribed
here.

The Latin translator's *iam ex hoc mundo cum Domino ... unitus* compresses the
relation between separation from this world and present union with Christ;
the English preserves rather than silently resolves it. The later *tibi ...
omnem licentiam praecidas simulandi* forbids the recipient to allow himself
an outward appearance of approving the impiety under discussion. Both places
are annotated in the translation.

This is an **AI-assisted scholarly draft** checked against every printed line,
not an independently reviewed critical edition. Cite the Latin witness and CO
page whenever exact wording or the relation to the lost French archetype is
decisive.

Suggested citation:

> John Calvin, *Ut se pius homo a papatus superstitionibus subducat*, French
> work in Theodore Beza's Latin translation, Geneva, 15 June [year unknown],
> *Calvini Opera* X/1 (1871), 188–189, normalized Latin and draft English
> translation, *Reformed Retrieval*.

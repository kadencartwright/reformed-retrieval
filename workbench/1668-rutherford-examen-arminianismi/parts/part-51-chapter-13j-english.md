MATTHEW 16:18.

Upon this rock I will build my church, and the gates of hell shall not prevail
against it.

In these words we have Christ's promise concerning the indefectible
perseverance of the saints, so that

[1668 KBNL/UvA witness | logical printed p. 594 | PDF p. 643]

those built upon the rock—that is, Christ or Peter's confession—cannot perish.
Hence:

It is asked whether God promises believers perseverance not absolutely, but
conditionally, provided that the believers themselves watch, pray, and
perform their duty. The Remonstrants affirm this in article 5, p. 293. We deny
it.

Meanwhile, we caution that, according to the state and condition of those who
lay hold of it, the promise of perseverance may be called either dissolute or
absolute. The dissolute promise belongs to carnal people who turn the grace
of God into licentiousness. The adversaries falsely fabricate such a promise
for us, as though God promised perseverance to someone who is sluggish and,
indeed, walks according to the flesh. We acknowledge no such promise. The
other is absolute, as distinguished from a conditional promise. For although
everyone in whom God fulfills the promise of perseverance necessarily
watches, prays, and performs his duty, watching, praying, and performing duty
is not a condition qualifying the person in whom the promise of perseverance
is fulfilled. It is the very thing promised.

Again, one condition is the condition of the person, or the condition of the
subject, or the passive condition: namely, if someone is elect in God's
intention, the promises of perseverance are made to him absolutely. Another
is the condition of duty, or the active condition. In this sense the promise
of perseverance is not made absolutely and indiscriminately to someone who is
negligent and sluggish in his duty.

[1668 KBNL/UvA witness | logical printed p. 595 | PDF p. 644]

Against the Remonstrants, we maintain with the Scriptures that the promise of
perseverance is not conditional but absolute.

1. If God promises perseverance in faith provided that people pray, watch,
and perform their duty, then God promises perseverance provided that they
persevere, because we are commanded to persevere in praying, watching, and
performing our duty, 1 Thessalonians 5:17; Matthew 26:41; Ephesians 6:18; and
Galatians 6:9. Indeed, on this account either God promises perseverance in
faith even to devils and to those who have fallen into the sin against the
Holy Spirit—which is absurd—or else God promises only the power to persevere,
not perseverance in actuality, contrary to Jeremiah 32:40 and 33:35–37 and
Isaiah 54:10–12 and 59:21–22. This also conflicts with the prayers of the
saints, who ask God for actual, not potential, perseverance. Potential
perseverance is non-perseverance, and such prayers of the saints would be
heard even if every believer became a most abandoned apostate, Matthew 6:13
and Psalm 71:9. It is also contrary to Christ's prayers, Luke 12:31; John
17:15, 20; and Romans 8:38. Finally, the power to persevere is merely the free
capacity either to persevere or finally to fall away. God cannot promise this
unless he would delude us.

2. For the question will return whether, by the very fact that God promises us
perseverance, he also

[1668 KBNL/UvA witness | logical printed p. 596 | PDF p. 645]

promises the grace to persevere in praying, watching, and performing our duty,
since performing one's duty is perseverance itself. If he promises the grace
to watch and perform one's duty, then he promises it either conditionally or
absolutely. If conditionally, there will be an infinite regress. If
absolutely, we have what was to be proved. If he does not promise the grace to
persevere in performing one's duty, then that perseverance occurs without any
grace of God promised to us and is a matter purely of free choice.

3. It is pure Pelagianism to make actual perseverance depend upon the acts of
our free choice. For in this way our free will is established as the
efficient cause, mistress, and independent craftswoman of election and
reprobation, salvation and damnation.

It is asked whether Christ chose no one to persevere and graciously
predestined no one to it from eternity. The Remonstrants affirm this, so that
no one may be called decisively and irrevocably elect to glory except the
person who finally perseveres and dies in true faith. Perseverance is thus
said not to depend upon God's gracious predestination but to hang upon the nod
of slippery, inconstant, and changeable free choice. We teach the contrary:
namely, that perseverance is an effect of gratuitous election to glory.

1. According to Ephesians 1:3–6, he chose us before the foundations of the
world were laid to live blamelessly and to the praise of grace. Therefore, he
also chose us to final holiness.

[1668 KBNL/UvA witness | logical printed p. 597 | PDF p. 646]

2. The intention and counsel of Christ as he died and gave himself for his
church was that at the last judgment he might present the church glorious,
not having spot or wrinkle or anything of this kind, but that it might be holy
and blameless, Ephesians 5:27. Therefore, from eternity he destined it for
this perfection and consummate holiness.

3. God predestined us through the sanctification of the Spirit unto
obedience, 1 Peter 1:2, and in verses 3–4 he begot us again unto an
incorruptible and undefiled inheritance reserved for us in heaven.

4. God appointed us not to wrath but to obtain salvation through our Lord
Jesus Christ, 1 Thessalonians 5:9. Therefore, he predestined us to the
necessary means of that salvation, and this means is perseverance in faith.

5. God works in us both to will and to accomplish according to his gracious
good pleasure, Philippians 2:13. Therefore, according to that gracious good
pleasure, he decreed from eternity to work this willing and accomplishing
constantly in us unto the end.

6. If perseverance depended upon free choice rather than the grace of
election, then salvation would be from works, from us, and from our free
choice.

7. We are God's workmanship, created for good works for which he prepared us,
Ephesians 2:10. Therefore, we were created for

[1668 KBNL/UvA witness | logical printed p. 598 | PDF p. 647]

continued good works and final perseverance.

8. The faith which is called the faith of the elect is so called because it
is given to the elect alone from this sole source: that they are elect.

9. If, according to that golden chain in Romans 8:30, God glorifies us because
he predestined us from eternity, then he also continues our effectual calling
unto the end because he predestined us.

It is asked whether Christ acquired perseverance for the elect by the merit
of his death. The Remonstrants deny this, lest they be forced to say that the
elect, who cannot be deprived of the benefits purchased by Christ's death,
cannot fail to persevere. We teach the contrary.

1. God blessed the elect with every spiritual blessing in Christ, Ephesians
1:3. Therefore, he also blessed them with perseverance in Christ.

2. Christ gave himself to death for his church in order that at last he might
present it glorious and blameless to God the Father, Ephesians 5:27.
Therefore, by that death of his he merited that we should be finally and
consummately sanctified. For because he died so that we might have the
remission of sins in his blood, it is properly concluded that remission was
obtained for us by the merits of Christ's death.

3. Christ bore our sins in his body upon the tree, so that we, being dead to
sins, might live unto righteousness, 1 Peter 2:24.

[1668 KBNL/UvA witness | logical printed p. 599 | PDF p. 648]

4. Through Jesus Christ we have been brought by faith into this grace in which
we stand and rejoice under the hope of the glory of God, Romans 5:2.

5. God cannot fail to grant us all things together with Christ, who was given
over to death for us, Romans 8:32. Therefore, he also grants us perseverance.

6. In all these things we are more than conquerors through him who loved us,
and this includes conquering sin, Romans 8:37.

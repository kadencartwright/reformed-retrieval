[Marginalia: Confirmation. Testimonies of Scripture. The prophets.]

We have lingered much longer than was fitting in explaining our thesis while entangled in our adversaries' thickets. I shall dispatch its confirmation in as few words as possible.

As for the testimonies of Scripture:

1. Even when the prophets act most fully as evangelists and speak most explicitly about the grace of the gospel, they nevertheless uphold both the honor and the authority of the law. Jeremiah presents the new covenant of the gospel, but foretells that under it God's law will be inscribed on believers' hearts, chapter 31:33. Ezekiel promises that under the same covenant God's Spirit will be placed within the saints, but immediately adds as its end or effect that they should walk in his statutes and observe his ordinances, chapter 36:27. Finally, when Malachi was about to seal the canon of the Old Testament and foretold that the Sun of righteousness would shortly arise under the gospel, he wished them even then to remember the law of Moses, final chapter, verses 2 and 4.

[Marginalia: Christ.]

2. As for Christ himself, what could be clearer than Matthew 5:17–18? “Do not think that I came to dissolve the law. I did not come to dissolve it, but to fulfill it. For truly I say to you, until heaven and earth pass away, not one jot or one stroke shall pass from the law,” and so forth. This testimony is so evident and illustrious that when the Manichaeans could not evade its force, they were driven to deny that Christ had ever said it and therefore to claim Matthew's Gospel was spurious. A fine achievement indeed: unable to untie the knot, with monstrous audacity they cut away the entire Gospel with the sword.

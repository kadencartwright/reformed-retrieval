# David Calderwood, *Altare Damascenum* (1623)

> **Working edition in progress:** This directory contains an AI-assisted,
> diplomatic-normalized Latin transcription and an independent close English
> translation. Neither has received human review, and neither is
> publication-ready.

## Work identity

- **Author:** David Calderwood (1575–1650), writing in the first edition under
  the anagrammatic pseudonym “Edwardus Didoclavius”
- **Title:** *Altare Damascenum, seu Politia Ecclesiae Anglicanae obtrusa
  Ecclesiae Scoticanae*
- **Edition used:** 1623, without stated place or printer
- **Structure:** Calderwood's large examination of English ecclesiastical
  polity, with Daniel Tilenus's *Paraenesis* quoted and refuted at relevant
  points, followed by separately authored or anonymous Scottish-polity
  documents

## Controlling witness and rights

The controlling witness is the 1623 first Latin edition in the Princeton
Theological Seminary Library, digitized by the Internet Archive as
[`altared00cald`](https://archive.org/details/altared00cald). The volume and its
scan are public-domain source material. Internet Archive page images, not its
OCR, control the transcription.

The cleaner Leiden reset of 1708,
[`altarasce00cald`](https://archive.org/details/altarasce00cald), is a comparison
witness only. It explicitly identifies David Calderwood and calls itself an
edition “more elegant and corrected than the former,” but the digitized copy
is textually defective near the beginning: it jumps from printed p. 3 to
printed p. 6. The p. 3 catchword `Capituli` does not join p. 6 (`Hoc caput
alterum`), so printed pp. 4–5 are absent. Nothing is silently supplied from
that reset.

The 1621 English
[*Altar of Damascus*](https://quod.lib.umich.edu/e/eebo/A17571.0001.001?view=toc)
is a much shorter antecedent, not a translation of this expanded Latin work,
and it has not been used as an English wording source. The selected modern
translation of propositions identified in the
[research inventory](../../research/untranslated-latin-treatises.md) is both
partial and CC BY-ND; it likewise has not been used as a wording source.

## Composite map and attribution warning

The controlling volume has 1,088 access-PDF pages (1,090 IA frames). Its
principal boundaries are:

- PDF pp. 1–4: cover, binding, and ownership matter, excluded.
- PDF p. 5: 1623 title page; PDF p. 6 is its verso.
- PDF pp. 7–12: Calderwood's unnumbered address `Lectoribus benevolis`.
- PDF pp. 13–967: the main *Altare Damascenum*, printed pp. 1–955.
- PDF pp. 968–973: index to the main work.
- PDF pp. 974–976: Andrew Melville's separately attributed
  `Anti-Tami-Cami-Categoria`.
- PDF pp. 977–992: the separately attributed letter `De regimine Ecclesiae
  Scoticanae`, by “Hieronymus Philadelphus.”
- PDF pp. 993–1084: the anonymous *Vindiciae* of that letter against John
  Spottiswood.
- PDF pp. 1085–1088: blank or binding matter, excluded.

The main work is itself composite in presentation. Calderwood incorporates and
refutes Daniel Tilenus's *Paraenesis ad Scotos*, and the final chapter directly
addresses Tilenus's taunts and accusations. Quoted adversarial matter must not
be attributed to Calderwood merely because it appears within his book. The
letter on Scottish polity, Melville poem, and anonymous *Vindiciae* are also
distinct documents, not silently part of Calderwood's continuous authorial
prose. Parts 01–12 contain none of the appended documents and do not reach the
embedded Tilenus refutation. Parts 07–12 do, however, include long passages
that Calderwood expressly quotes from Thomas Brightman, Tilenus, Eusebius,
Scaliger, patristic writers, imperial and conciliar legislation, English
canons, canonists, and other named writers; the paired files retain their
quotation boundaries and attributions.

## Editorial method

Latin long *s*, ligatures, line-end divisions, and typographic `u/v` are
normalized. Edition spelling and substantive wording are retained. Running
heads, signatures, catchwords, ornaments, and bare printed page numerals are
omitted. Marginal references and quoted documentary material are retained;
source markers record printed page, IA leaf, and access-PDF page.

The English is a fresh, full translation of the paired Latin, not a summary.
It has not been copied from either the short 1621 English antecedent or a
modern selection.

### Part 08 bounds and evidence

Chapter IV occupies printed pp. 112–302, so a full-chapter unit would be far
larger than the preceding synchronized parts. Part 08 therefore follows the
chapter's first complete internal argument. It begins on shared printed p. 112
/ IA leaf 124 / PDF p. 124 with the centered heading:

`CHAP. IV. Episcopus & Presbyter sunt eiusdem gradus.`

It includes Calderwood's account of Beza's divine, human, and satanic forms of
episcopacy, the distinction between presiding and princely bishops, the
subheading `De intensiva principis Episcopi potestate`, and the whole first
argument alleged for apostolic institution. It retains Calderwood's quoted
material from Tilenus, Downham, Augustine, Cyprian, Socrates, Tertullian,
Vincent of Lérins, Whitaker, Hegesippus, Eusebius, and Spalatensis, all visible
marginal citations, and the Greek `ἀντιβαλλόμενα`, `πολιτευσαμένῳ`, and `μικρὰς
προφάσεις`.

Part 08 ends in the upper part of printed p. 120 / IA leaf 132 / PDF p. 132 at:

`...progrediamur ad secundum gradum ipsa nempe Apostolorum tempora.`

The immediately following `Secundum argumentum` is excluded. This gives Part
08 nine identical Latin and English markers, p. 112 partial through p. 120
partial. The access-PDF images for PDFs 124–132 were freshly rendered at 240
dpi and audited page by page against both files; OCR and the 1708 reset were
used only as reading aids. The anomalous 1623 reading `interruptam ac
perpetuam successionem` is retained and translated literally; the 1708 reset
has `non interruptam ac perpetuam successionem`, but no emendation has been
made.

### Part 09 bounds and evidence

Part 09 begins in the upper part of shared printed p. 120 / IA leaf 132 / PDF
p. 132 at the explicit heading:

`Secundum argumentum.`

It contains the complete second argument alleged for apostolic episcopacy.
Calderwood examines early episcopal succession lists; the reports about Peter,
Mark, and other supposed apostolic bishops; Epaphroditus and the “apostles of
the churches”; and the angels of the seven churches in Revelation. The pair
retains the argument's quoted material, all visible marginal citations, the
Greek text, and the Syriac/Hebrew transliterations `Schelichim` and `Scheliach
tsibbur`.

### Part 10 bounds and evidence

Part 10 begins exactly where Part 09 ends, in the lower part of shared printed
p. 137 / IA leaf 149 / PDF p. 149, at the explicit heading:

`3 Argumentum.`

The third argument as a whole is unusually long. Calderwood expressly divides
its proof between tradition and Scripture: after announcing that bishops
ordained by the apostles are proved `partim ex traditione, partim ex
Scripturis`, he first examines the alleged ancient evidence for Evodius,
Ignatius, Linus, Clement, Mark, Anianus, Polycarp, James the Just, and Simeon
son of Cleopas. Part 10 contains that complete traditional-witness unit,
including its attributed quotations, all visible marginal citations, and the
Greek `τὸ ἀξίωμα τῆς ἱερωσύνης`, `Ἑβραιοζήλας`, `διασπορᾷ`, `πρωτεῖα`,
`σχέσει`, and `γρῦ`.

It ends at the foot of printed p. 144 / IA leaf 156 / PDF p. 156 with
Calderwood's explicit transition:

`Atque haec de Episcopis quos ex veterum monumentis probant ab Apostolis
constitutos, pergamus ad Timotheum & Titum, quos ex sacra scriptura Episcopos
ab Apostolis ordinatos esse contendunt.`

The scriptural case beginning `Timothei & Titi Episcopatum` on printed p. 145
is excluded. Part 10 therefore does not claim to complete the whole third
argument; it stops at the author's stated boundary between its traditional
and scriptural proofs. The pair has eight identical markers, p. 137 partial
through p. 144. Printer-divided words at the p. 142/143 boundary are joined,
with the incoming marker placed before the complete word and the English
marker placed at its corresponding phrase.

The controlling access-PDF images for PDFs 149–156 were freshly rendered at
240 dpi and inspected page by page against both files. The IA OCR and the 1708
Leiden reset assisted reading only; the 1623 page images governed retained
wording, typography, quotations, Greek, marginalia, and page joins.

### Part 11 bounds and evidence

Part 11 begins at the top of printed p. 145 / IA leaf 157 / PDF p. 157 with
the scriptural case announced at the end of Part 10:

`Timothei & Titi Episcopatum, ut didicit a Saravia, sic probat ex scripturis
Tilenus.`

It retains Tilenus's and Downham's full opening arguments, Calderwood's stated
division into three questions, and his complete treatment of the first two:
whether the cited Pauline commands necessarily imply judicial jurisdiction,
and whether their execution was entrusted to Timothy and Titus alone. It
preserves the quoted material from Tilenus, Downham, Beza, Anselm, Calvin,
Piscator, Whitaker, and the bishop of Ely, all visible marginal citations, and
the Greek `κατὰ πρόσκλισιν`, `χωρὶς προκρίματος`, `πρότυπα`, `οὓς δεῖ
ἐπιστομίζειν`, `τὸ παραγγέλλειν`, `διαγγέλλειν`, `παραγγέλλειν`, and
`ἔλεγχε`.

It ends in the upper portion of printed p. 148 / IA leaf 160 / PDF p. 160 at:

`...non ipsos quidem Apostolos ordinariam iurisdictionem solos exercuisse
postea videbimus.`

The immediately following explicit transition, `Quod ad tertium attinet`,

Part 11 four identical Latin and English markers. The printer-divided

### Part 12 bounds and evidence

Part 12 begins exactly where Part 11 ends, in the lower portion of shared
printed p. 148 / IA leaf 160 / PDF p. 160, at Calderwood's explicit transition:

`Quod ad tertium attinet.`

It contains his complete third question: whether the matters commanded or
permitted to Timothy and Titus belonged to them as evangelists or as bishops.
The discussion treats their journeys and temporary stations, the force of
`προσμένειν` and `ἐπιδιορθώσασθαι`, the date and subscriptions of the Pauline
epistles, extraordinary vocation and gifts, the seventy disciples, Barnabas,
Philip, the laying on of hands by Paul and the presbytery, ordination and
jurisdiction, succession, and the alleged patristic evidence. The pair retains
the extended attributed quotations, all visible marginal citations, and the
Greek, including `συνεργούς`, `ὡς καὶ ἐγώ`, `ψιλὴν`, `ἀμέσως`, `ἁπλῶς`,
`κατ᾽ ἐξοχήν`, `διὰ`, `μετὰ`, `λόγος σοφίας`, `λόγος γνώσεως`,
`κατ᾽ ἀποκάλυψιν`, `ἅπαντες`, `οἱ ἀπόστολοι`, `ἀποκαλυφθῇ`, `κατ᾽ ἰδίαν`,
and `κατὰ συνεκδοχήν`.

It ends in the upper portion of printed p. 179 / IA leaf 191 / PDF p. 191 with
Calderwood's explicit close and Bilson quotation:

`Atque haec de Timotheo & Tito ... divino aliquo iure aut scripturarum
testimonio non licebit.`

The immediately following paragraph, `Ex consequenti quomodo probaverit
Dounamus...`, begins a new argument about bishops' alleged institution
immediately by Christ and is excluded. This gives Part 12 thirty-two identical
Latin and English markers, p. 148 partial through p. 179 partial. Printer-split
words at page boundaries are joined, and each incoming marker is placed before
the complete Latin word and the corresponding English word or phrase.

# When a Minister Had Been Dismissed Without a Hearing

*A fresh translation of the complete Latin-form witness in CO X/1 223–224.*

## Translation

### [CO X/1, p. 223]

Since we had heard both about the disturbances that troubled your church and
about the change of pastor that followed, and since N. also testified here
among us that he did not know why he had been dismissed from the ministry by
you, we thought it most appropriate to admonish you briefly about the matter.
We are confident that it will be received in the same spirit in which it is
written by us. And just as Christian zeal, sincerity, and true brotherly love
move us to do this, so you in turn must apply that spirit of gentleness which
Scripture commends above all to the task of hearing us.

There is no need for us to debate at length how that disorder threatens the
church with imminent ruin when matters have reached the point that the civil
magistrate, acting with unrestricted authority and on its own, either
dismisses or appoints ministers.
Since matters are nevertheless sometimes brought to that point when
contentions and disturbances rage and ordinary remedies can no longer have a
place, you ought to have been taught by experience for the

### [CO X/1, p. 224]

future not to tear one another apart in such a way that you yourselves furnish
the occasion for overturning order. It is unnecessary to treat the matter at
greater length with you, since there is no one who does not perceive both the
magnitude of the evil and what follows from it. And since this is not the
first time your church has incurred that ruin, those who gave occasion for the
evil are all the less excusable.

We therefore ask you to attend seriously to yourselves in the future, so that
you guard against Satan's schemes. For when he feels himself pressed by the
power and brilliance of the truth which the Lord has restored to the world,
he directs all his effort toward exposing all the godly to reproach and
disgrace by overturning the church's order.

As for that dismissal from the ministry, according to what the brother
reported to us in writing, the pulpit was forbidden to him even though he had
been accused by no one, and no reason at all was stated. This manner of
proceeding plainly appears utterly foreign to us, as indeed it is. Nor did we
think that what we learned from him should be
concealed from you. If anything was done wrongly here in the manner of
proceeding, it should be corrected, so that both his just cause for complaint
and the scandal joined with it may be removed.

Yet we do not wish this admonition of ours to bring prejudice against you. It
aims only at discharging our duty. This alone is what we had to write to you
about a matter that is not sufficiently clear to us. This is not because your
integrity comes into doubt with us, but so that a mark of fickleness should
not be fastened upon us if we were to proceed farther without the matter
having been adequately investigated.

Nor, in turn, do we wish to press you to tell us any more about what the facts
are, except that we would wish such light to be brought here as would
immediately drive away every harmful rumor. Let that be in your hands. It
will be enough for us to have exhorted you as brothers to see that the church
entrusted to you admits nothing alien to unity and concord, and consequently
that the sacred order which ought to prevail is not ruined by disputes and
quarrels. For you know that God's children ought to be kept in true moderation
by a spirit of gentleness.

Meanwhile, we pray that God, once those disputes which had such power to tear
you apart have been removed and completely extinguished, may protect you by
his defense, grant you prosperity in every good, and increase his gifts in
you, so that he may be glorified in you all the more.

*Geneva, 10 December.*

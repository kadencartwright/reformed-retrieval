## CONTROVERSY III: On the Relics of the Saints

The Papists adore certain relics with religious worship, such as Christ’s
shroud, with these words: *Holy shroud, pray for us and deliver us from plague
and a sorrowful death*, and so forth. Likewise, the cloth of Veronica, on
which Christ’s face was imprinted by Veronica, with these words: *Hail, holy
face imprinted upon the little cloth; cleanse us from every stain of vices and
join us to the fellowship of the blessed.* Likewise, they invoke the wood of
the cross on the feast of the finding and exaltation of the holy cross, and
ask from it what ought to be asked from God alone, with these words: *Save the*

[1620 BSB Tome II | canvas 36 | printed col. 72 | bsb10175568]

*company summoned and gathered in your praises. O cross, hail, sole hope;
increase righteousness in the godly and grant pardon to the guilty*, and so
forth. Likewise, they worship the cross as they do other relics, with temples,
altars, offices, feast days, and so forth.

Bellarmine, however, book 2, chapter 2, *On the Church Triumphant*, attempts to
excuse this idolatry when he says that the Papists indeed honor relics and
kiss them as sacred pledges of our patrons, but do not adore them as God or
invoke them as saints, but venerate them with a lesser worship than the
spirits of the saints, and so forth. In chapter 3 he says that relics are to
be honored with worship.

There is therefore a twofold question between us and the Pontificians about
relics: 1. about relics in general; 2. about the cross. On the first question
we say that the relics of the saints, such as bodies, bones, and so forth, are
to be treated with due honor, that is, burial, embalming, and so forth, as was
Christ’s body, John 19, and Stephen’s, Acts 8:2, and so forth, but not with
religious worship. This is proved by the following arguments.

Argument 1. The worship of relics has neither a command nor an example in
Scripture. Therefore it does not arise from faith, but is
ἐθελοθρησκεία—self-devised worship—and consequently a sin, Romans 14:23;
Colossians 2:23. Therefore.

Argument 2. If religious worship was not rendered to the relics of the
patriarchs, prophets, Christ, the apostles, and others until after the Church
had been corrupted by idolatry and superstitions, then it is not now to be
rendered to any relics, because no bodies can be worthier than their bodies.
But their bodies were honored by no honor other than burial, embalming, and so
forth, as Scripture everywhere teaches. Therefore.

Argument 3. What Christ condemns in the Jewish scribes and priests, he also
condemns in the Papists. But in them he condemns the superstitious adornment
of the

[1620 BSB Tome II | canvas 37 | printed col. 73 | bsb10175568]

monuments of the prophets, Matthew 23:29; 11:42. Therefore.

Argument 4. In the Old Testament God commanded burial; indeed, he himself hid
the body of Moses lest it be adored, the last chapter of Deuteronomy, and
under the law contact with dead bodies was uncleanness and sin. Therefore
worship was not rendered to them, nor were the bones of the saints dug out of
tombs to be kept somewhere for the sake of worship, as occurs in the Papacy.

Argument 5. For a Christian no one is to be acknowledged in worship according
to the flesh, 2 Corinthians 5:16. Therefore.

Argument 6. If relics are to be adored and worshiped with religious worship,
then this is either by an elicited and immediate act of religion, flowing
immediately from religion itself, or mediately. But neither can be said. Not
the first, because by its primary act religion leads us to the one God alone
and regards God alone; not the second, because a mediated act of religion is
an act commanded by religion, James 1:27, and performed through the service of
the actions of other virtues. But the act of worshiping relics is not
commanded by religion. Furthermore, in this way honor given to parents is a
religious act, and yet children do not worship their parents with religious
worship.

Argument 7. If what the Papists do in honor of the saints by preserving
bodies and parts of a body without burial is the very thing which God
denounces as a punishment against enemies, then the Papist preservation of
relics is not an honoring of the saints. But the former is true, Isaiah 14:19;
Jeremiah 8:1. Therefore.

Argument 8. Almost all the Papists’ relics are spurious, and the same parts of
one saint’s body are displayed in several places. Thus Augustine, book 28,
*On the Works*

[1620 BSB Tome II | canvas 37 | printed col. 74 | bsb10175568]

*of Monks*, after saying that certain monks carry and advertise the limbs of
martyrs, adds, *If indeed they are martyrs’ limbs*; how much more so now.

Argument 9. Jerome, against Vigilantius, when Vigilantius objected that
lighting candles before relics was idolatry, could answer nothing except that
only little women who had zeal but not knowledge did it. The Council of Elvira
says that the spirits of the saints are disturbed by candles of this sort.

### OBJECTIONS

Objection 1. From the examples of Exodus 13 and the last chapter of
Deuteronomy: Moses honorably conveyed Joseph’s bones into the land of Canaan;
God himself honored the body of Moses. See similar things in 2 Kings 13 and
chapter 23. Therefore.

Response. This does not conclude what is in question, namely, that relics are
to be adored, because the cited passages treat only of the honor of burial,
not of religious worship. Furthermore, the conveyance of Joseph’s bones was
only a sign that God would give the promised land to the people; similarly,
the miracle performed through contact with Elisha’s bones was a sign that the
Israelites would have victory over the Syrians.

Objection 2. Isaiah 11:10 predicts that the Lord’s tomb will be honored by
everyone. Therefore.

Response. It does not conclude that relics are to be adored with worship.
Furthermore, this is a false interpretation of the passage, because it treats
not of the tomb but of the Church in which the nations rest.

Objection 3. The woman who touched the hem of Christ’s garment was healed,
Matthew 19; certain people were healed through Peter’s shadow and Paul’s
handkerchiefs, Acts 15 and 19. Therefore God was confirming relics even by
miracles.

[1620 BSB Tome II | canvas 38 | printed col. 75 | bsb10175568]

Response. It does not conclude, because the question concerns worship.
Furthermore, the cited passages concern the living, but the question concerns
the relics of the dead.

2. If these things are to be adored because they belonged to Peter and Paul,
then much more were Peter and Paul themselves to be adored with worship. But
they did not admit religious worship, Acts 10 and 14. Finally, by the same
reasoning it will follow that everything through which God has performed
miracles is to be adored, which is absurd.

Objection 4. Miracles are often seen in the bodies of the dead, as in the face
of Stephen, Acts 6. Therefore.

Response. What is said about Stephen happened while he was alive, but the
question concerns the relics of the dead. Furthermore, what the Papists say
about miracles is false. Finally, this does not follow: therefore they are to
be adored with religious worship.

Objection 5. The bodies of saints, such as Gervasius, Protasius, and others,
were found and raised up by divine agency. Therefore.

Response. The inference is denied: they were found, therefore they are to be
adored with religious worship. Furthermore, what they say about the finding
is false, as was said above from Augustine.

Objection 6. Relics are honored by the use of candles and lamps, and light is
a sign of honor, not superstition, even if it is used before an inanimate
thing while the sun is shining, as the lampstand was before the ark, Exodus
25. Therefore.

Response. The use of candles and lamps is disapproved no less than the worship
of relics. Furthermore, the use of the lampstand before the ark had a command
and was a Mosaic ceremony, but the use of light in the Papacy is bad
imitation.

Objection 7. Temples of the Holy Spirit are to be honored, but the bodies of
the saints are temples of the Holy Spirit. Therefore.

Response. The proposition is true of lawful honor, but it does not conclude
what is in question, namely, that they are

[1620 BSB Tome II | canvas 38 | printed col. 76 | bsb10175568]

to be adored with religious worship. Furthermore, living believers are
temples of the Holy Spirit, but are not to be honored with religious worship.

Objection 8. The angels in Jude 9 honored the bodies of the saints, as also
irrational creatures did in 1 Kings 13:21; Daniel 3:25; and Daniel 6:21.

Response. It does not conclude: therefore they are to be honored with worship
by the superstitious preservation of bones, and so forth. Furthermore, the
angels honored the body of Moses only with burial. Finally, Daniel 3 and 6
concern the bodies of living people which were not adored with worship, but
the question concerns the relics of the dead.

## QUESTION II: On the Cross

There is a twofold question about the cross between us and the Papists: 1.
about the adoration of the cross; 2. about the sign of the cross.

On adoration Bellarmine says in book 2, chapter 26, that the Lord’s cross is
to be venerated partly among relics and partly among images; and in chapter 25
that the true cross of Christ, the nails, the thorns, and so forth, are to be
adored analogically and reductively with the same kind of worship with which
Christ is adored, that is, latria.

We, however, say that Tertullian in the *Apologetic* and others affirm that
the wood of the cross was set before Christians, but only so that it might be
a symbol of Christian profession and so that they might show themselves not
ashamed of the cross but rather glorying in it. We therefore refute the Papist
adoration of the cross with the worship of latria by these arguments.

Argument 1. What is done without faith is sin. But adoration of the cross is
done without faith,

[1620 BSB Tome II | canvas 39 | printed col. 77 | bsb10175568]

because nowhere in Scripture is there a command or example for it.

Argument 2. Whatever is to be adored with latria is God himself, as the
adversaries concede. If, therefore, the cross is to be adored with latria, it
will be God at least analogically and reductively, because, as Bellarmine
maintains, it is reductively adored with latria.

Argument 3. If the cross on which Christ suffered such dreadful things is
reductively adored with latria because of contact with the Lord, then much
more is Mary, who bore Christ in her womb for nine months, because her contact
was natural, while the other was violent. But the Papists deny the latter as
absurd. Therefore they must also deny the former.

Argument 4. If the cross is to be adored with latria because of contact with
Christ, then much more is the donkey upon which he rode when he entered
Jerusalem with such celebration, as are also the beds, tables, boats, and
countless other things which Christ touched while he was here. Indeed, why
should not the lips of Judas, with which he kissed Christ, and the hands of
the officers also be adored, since on the part of the Jews they were no less
instruments of that wicked crime than the cross was? But the latter is absurd.
Therefore.

Argument 5. The Papists attribute more to the cross than to Christ’s
resurrection in these words: *We adore your cross; we glorify the
resurrection*, and so forth. Likewise, they attribute to the cross what
belongs to God alone, namely, the remission of sins, as is evident from the
prayers of Pope Gregory.

Argument 6. If they worship crosses because they bear the likeness of Christ’s
cross, then they ought to adore all mangers, spears, nails, thorns, and so
forth, because they bear the same likeness. But the latter is absurd.
Therefore.

[1620 BSB Tome II | canvas 39 | printed col. 78 | bsb10175568]

Argument 7. If Christ’s humanity separated from the divinity is not to be
adored with latria, much less is the cross. But the former is true, as we
showed above and as Augustine teaches at greater length in homily 38, *On the
Words of the Lord*. Therefore.

Argument 8. If the cross is adored with latria because it is a sign and image
of Christ, as they maintain, then it will follow that images are to be adored
with the same worship as the things signified, and thus that it is lawful to
erect temples, altars, and so forth to the cross and other images. But
Augustine refutes the latter in the cited passage. Therefore.

Argument 9. If the Holy Spirit is present in baptism, as they concede, and yet
baptism is not adored, then much less, because of contact with Christ’s body,
are all crosses representing Christ’s cross to be adored. But the former is
true. Therefore.

### OBJECTIONS

Objection 1. Scripture testifies that Christ willingly chose the cross, which
was prefigured by various figures in the Old Testament, such as the erected
stone, Genesis 20; the tree of life in paradise; Noah’s ark; Aaron’s rod; the
bronze serpent; and so forth. Hence in Galatians 6:14 we are said to glory in
the cross. Therefore the wood of the cross is venerable.

Response. It does not conclude what is in question. Furthermore, this is a
fallacy of equivocation in “cross,” because in the cited passage it signifies
not the wood of the cross but death on the cross. Third, it is false that
those figures of the Old Testament signified the wood of the cross, because
they signified Christ himself, who hung upon the wood of the cross.

Objection 2. The wood of the cross contained many mysteries within itself.
Therefore.

Response. The inference is denied, because not everything that contains
mysteries within itself is

[1620 BSB Tome II | canvas 40 | printed col. 79 | bsb10175568]

to be adored. For the figures of the Old Testament, the Word, and the
sacraments contain mysteries within themselves, and yet are not to be adored.
Furthermore, the mysteries Bellarmine adduces are fabrications of the human
mind.

Objection 3. The cross was found when the enemies had been defeated, at a time
when it could be worshiped—namely, in Constantine’s time—and God showed by
miracles that it was the true cross. Therefore.

Response. The inference is denied, because many things have been found and
many also made illustrious by miracles which are not to be adored.
Furthermore, they are inconsistent about the finding, for Bellarmine
maintains that it was recognized by a miracle and distinguished from the two
crosses of the thieves found at the same time, but Ambrose, in his oration on
Theodosius, and Chrysostom, homily 84 on John, say that it was recognized from
the inscription.

Objection 4. If the place in which an angel stood was holy, Exodus 3 and
Joshua 5; if Paul’s handkerchiefs were honored; and if the sacraments are
honored, then much more is the cross which, sprinkled with Christ’s blood, was
the instrument of redemption. But the former is true. Therefore.

Response. The cited passages treat not of the presence of angels but of
Jehovah. Furthermore, it does not conclude what is in question, namely, that
the cross is to be adored with the worship of latria. Finally, if the cross
was holy and to be adored because it was the instrument of redemption, then
by the same reasoning Judas, the officers, and all Christ’s enemies would be
holy and to be adored, because they were instruments of Christ’s death.

Objection 5. A most radiant cross appeared to Constantine in the air, with
the words, *Conquer by this*, ἐν τέτω νίκα. Likewise, a cross appeared in the
time of Constantine, Julian, Arcadius, and Leo.

Response. The inference is denied: therefore it is to be

[1620 BSB Tome II | canvas 40 | printed col. 80 | bsb10175568]

adored with latria, because many other things have appeared extraordinarily
and yet are not to be adored. Furthermore, the credibility of the apparitions
is doubted, because Sozomen, book 1, chapter 3, and other writers say that the
cross appeared to Constantine in a dream.

Objection 6. Signs of holy things are holy and venerable. But every cross
bears the image of Christ crucified, than whom nothing holier can be conceived.
Therefore.

Response. I distinguish the proposition. They are holy and venerable, but not
to be adored with the worship of latria. Thus, when the serpent erected by
God’s command was adored with the worship proper to the thing signified, it
was broken. Furthermore, not every sign is holy and venerable, but only those
divinely instituted; the cross is not such a sign. Finally, the assumption is
denied.

## QUESTION III: On the Sign of the Cross

Bellarmine, book 2, chapter 29, says that the sign of the cross which is drawn
on the forehead or in the air, or traced with a finger over any things
whatever, is sacred and venerable.

We, however, say that the sign of the cross displayed for civil reasons on
coinage, a military standard, or in another manner is lawful. Nor do we
condemn the use of the sign of the cross in Constantine’s time, by which
Christians distinguished themselves from Jews and Gentiles and testified that
they were Christians. But we deny that this sign has such efficacy, or is to
be made on the forehead or elsewhere for the ends the Papists imagine: namely,
on the forehead to signify the Father, on the belly the begotten Son, and in
the middle the love of the Holy Spirit, of the Father and the Son; and to
drive away the Devil, and so forth. We therefore prove by these arguments that
it is not a sacred and

[1620 BSB Tome II | canvas 41 | printed col. 81 | bsb10175568]

venerable religious sign.

Argument 1. The sign of the cross has neither a command nor an example in
Scripture. Therefore it is not to be received by the Church as a sacred and
venerable religious sign.

Argument 2. If the sign of the cross was humanly instituted only as an outward
sign distinguishing Christians from others, then it is not a sacred and
venerable religious sign. But the former is true, as is evident from history.

Argument 3. If outward signs instituted by God are to be removed when they are
instruments and an occasion of superstition or idolatry, then much more are
humanly instituted signs, such as the sign of the cross, to be removed. But
the former is true, as is evident from Hezekiah’s deed, 2 Kings 18:4.
Therefore.

Argument 4. The sign of the cross weakens the cross of Christ, because it
attributes to the cross what belongs to Christ alone. For they attribute to it
the power of driving away demons, sanctifying, and so forth, which the Apostle
attributes to Christ alone, 1 Corinthians 6:11; Ephesians 5:26; 1 Timothy 4:5;
and elsewhere. Therefore.

### BELLARMINE’S OBJECTIONS

Objection 1. From the figures of the Old Testament: the blood of the lamb,
Exodus 12; that tau which is commanded to be inscribed upon the foreheads of
those who groan, Ezekiel 9; likewise Genesis 48, when Jacob blessed Joseph’s
sons with crossed hands; and so forth, signified the sign of the cross on the
foreheads of Christians and the blessings which would come through the sign
of the cross. Therefore.

Response. The antecedent is denied. For in Exodus 12 the sprinkling of blood
on the doorposts of the houses signified the sprinkling of Christ’s blood, by
which we are delivered from death and the devil.

2. In Ezekiel 9 the letter tau does not represent the sign of the cross, and
signifies only a

[1620 BSB Tome II | canvas 41 | printed col. 82 | bsb10175568]

sign indefinitely. It speaks of a sign marked only upon the elect and known
to the angel striking the people. But what has this to do with the Papist sign
of the cross, which is marked upon everyone indiscriminately? Finally, nothing
of this sort can be gathered from Genesis 48, nor does it follow: “He blessed
with crossed hands; therefore it was a figure of the sign of the cross.”

Objection 2. From Revelation 7:3 and 14: the sign and name on the forehead is
the sign of the cross. Therefore.

Response. 1. The antecedent is denied. Rather, he means the spiritual mark of
faith by which God distinguishes his people from unbelievers, with which mark
outward confession is joined.

Objection 3. Many miracles have been performed through the sign of the cross.
Therefore.

Response. The inference is denied. Furthermore, what the Papists relate about
these miracles is fabulous and consists of the dreams of monks.

Objection 4. Soldiers ought to have a military sign by which they are
distinguished from others. But all Christians are soldiers, Ephesians 6:11.
Therefore they too ought to have a sign, and consequently the sign of the
cross.

Response. I deny that consequence, because they have other signs, namely, the
inward sign of faith, outward confession, and participation in the Word and
sacraments, and so forth.

Objection 5. In the sign of the cross there is a great mystery, and it
contains within itself the sum of those things contained in the creed, such
as the Trinity, Incarnation, Passion, and so forth. Therefore.

Response. The antecedent is denied, and the inference is also denied, because
if God had wished to teach us through the sign of the cross, he would have
commanded its use. But he did not command it.

Objection 6. If the benefits of the sign of the cross are great, then it ought
to have a place in the Church as a venerable sign. But the former is true,
because it drives away demons, arouses our hope and us to the imitation of
Christ, and so forth. Therefore.

[1620 BSB Tome II | canvas 42 | printed col. 83 | bsb10175568]

Response. The proposition is denied, because many very useful things are
given which are not received as venerable signs in the Church. Furthermore,
what they relate about the efficacy of the cross is fabulous.

## QUESTIONS

### QUESTION 1: Whether Sacred Pilgrimages Are to Be Made to Sacred Places and the Relics of the Saints

Bellarmine, book 3, *On the Church Triumphant*, affirms it. We argue to the
contrary as follows.

Argument 1. Those pilgrimages are made without a command or example in
Scripture. Therefore they are ἐθελοθρησκεῖαι—self-devised acts of worship.

Argument 2. If pilgrimages are to be made, as they maintain, because God
performed miracles and exceptional works in those places, then it will follow
that we seek ordinary works fixed to places resembling extraordinary ones,
and that we think God’s power is bound to the holiness of some place. But the
latter is absurd, because even the holiest places can be profaned, as Bethel,
of which Genesis 35:15 speaks, became Bethaven, 1 Kings 12:29; Hosea 4:15.
Therefore.

Argument 3. Those who make pilgrimages in the Papist manner think that God is
more propitious in one place than another, that they merit pardon through
pilgrimages, and that they bind God’s grace to a particular place. But God
wishes to be adored in every place, John 4:23, and is near to all who invoke
him, Psalm 145:18. Therefore.

Argument 4. Pilgrimages to Jerusalem began only in Constantine’s time, which
Gregory of Nyssa refutes in a long oration, as does Jerome in his *Letter to
Paulinus*.

Argument 5. There are many bodily and spiritual dangers in pilgrimages of
this sort, both for men and women. Thus Bellarmine in the cited book maintains
that they are suitable only for mature men.

[1620 BSB Tome II | canvas 42 | printed col. 84 | bsb10175568]

### OBJECTIONS

Objection 1. In Deuteronomy 16 God commands the Jews to make a pilgrimage to
the temple three times a year; indeed, the nations came to adore at Jerusalem,
Acts 8 and 20. Therefore.

Response. The inference is denied, because there was a command for those
pilgrimages, but none exists for Papist pilgrimages. Furthermore, the reason
for the command was that it was not lawful to offer sacrifices except in that
one place, but now it is lawful to adore God everywhere. Finally, the ordinary
sign of God’s presence was established there, but not in the places observed
by the Papists.

### QUESTION 2: Whether Feast Days Are to Be Observed by Christians

Bellarmine, book 3, chapter 10, proposition 2, says that the received feasts of
Christians are celebrated not only for the sake of order but also of mystery,
and that those days are holier than others; finally, that they are a part of
divine worship.

We, however, say that it is lawful for the Church by common consent, without
superstition or idolatry, to establish certain days and dedicate them to
divine worship: for hearing God’s Word, conceiving extraordinary prayers to
avert God’s judgments, or giving thanks for a singular benefit received, as
Augustine says in letter 128 to Januarius. But we refute the Papists’ position
by these arguments.

Argument 1. The observance of feast days is forbidden under the New
Testament, Romans 14:5–6; Galatians 4:10; Colossians 2:16. Therefore.

Argument 2. Both in the Old and New Testament feast days were instituted in
honor of God alone, such as Passover and others under the Old Testament, and
the Lord’s Day in the New Testament.

Argument 3. There is no command

[1620 BSB Tome II | canvas 43 | printed col. 85 | bsb10175568]

or example in the New Testament for the observance of a feast day in honor of
dead saints; indeed, they were humanly instituted only in idolatrous ages.

Argument 4. Because of the holiness of a feast day, the Papists think that
their works, such as Masses and so forth, are more pleasing to God, and that
the works they then perform are divine worship. Indeed, they attribute to
feasts a power through which they obtain salvation, protection, and eternal
life, as they pray on the vigils of the apostles: *Grant that the venerable
solemnity may increase devotion and salvation for us*; and on the common feast
of the martyrs: *Grant that through these feasts we may obtain eternal joys.*

Argument 5. The observance of Passover, Pentecost, and so forth, is the
observance of the feasts of the Old Testament, because in the New Testament it
has neither a command nor an example. But according to Bellarmine, chapter 10,
proposition 1, it is in no way lawful to observe the festivals of the Old
Testament. Therefore.

Argument 6. If the use of feast days instituted by the Church in honor of the
saints has ceased, then the feast days themselves have also ceased. But the
former is true, because they were instituted in times of persecution to
strengthen the saints in the steadfast profession of faith, and only in those
places where martyrs were put to death, and so that they might give thanks to
God for them, according to Jerome in Eusebius, book 4, chapter 14.

Argument 7. Temples, altars, feast days, sacrifices of the saints, and so
forth, pertain to latria. But latria is owed to God alone, as they concede.
Therefore.

Argument 8. The multitude of feasts was part of the ceremonial yoke. But we
have been delivered from the ceremonial yoke. Therefore we have also been
delivered from the observance of Papist feasts, for they observe more feast
days than the Jews.

### OBJECTIONS

[1620 BSB Tome II | canvas 43 | printed col. 86 | bsb10175568]

Objection 1. Everyone celebrates the Lord’s Day, but it is celebrated in
memory of the Resurrection. Therefore feasts are celebrated not only for the
sake of order but also for the sake of mystical signification.

Response. There is more in the conclusion than in the premises, because the
case of the Lord’s Day, which had an apostolic institution in place of the
Sabbath, Acts 20:7; 1 Corinthians 16:2; Revelation 1:10, differs from that of
feast days, which have neither an apostolic command nor example. Furthermore,
we do not celebrate the Lord’s Day because it contains a mystery within
itself. For Scripture does not say that it is a figure of eternal life, as
they maintain, but we celebrate it for the sake of the Church’s order, so that
we may be reminded that the shadows of the law have ended, meditate upon the
work of redemption, and give thanks to God for it. Finally, that day is not in
itself holier than others, but only by reason of the worship rendered to God
on that day.

Objection 2. The observance of feasts which is commanded in Exodus 20 pertains
to the worship of God, because it is a commandment of the first table. But
that commandment is in some measure natural and pertains to us. Therefore we
ought to have some days whose observance pertains to the worship of God.

Response. In Exodus 20 no observance of days is commanded except that of the
Sabbath, which indeed contains something natural that pertains to us, namely,
that one day out of seven is to be dedicated to divine worship. Therefore
from this passage it can be concluded that there ought to be no other feast
days besides the Lord’s Day; and Bellarmine in the same place concedes that
the determination of this or that feast day was made by human beings.

Objection 3. By being born Christ consecrated the place, that is, the manger;
by dying, the cross; by rising, the tomb; and so forth.

[1620 BSB Tome II | canvas 44 | printed col. 87 | bsb10175568]

Therefore he also consecrated those days on which he was born, suffered, rose
again, and so forth. Therefore.

Response. The antecedent is denied, because Scripture says nothing of this
sort. By his death, burial, and so forth, he indeed sanctified for us death,
the tomb, afflictions, and so forth, and removed the curse from them.
Furthermore, this does not follow: he consecrated those times which, as they
maintain, remain numerically the same; therefore he consecrated that passing
time which can no longer exist.

Objection 4. If temples, altars, and so forth are called holy because they are
consecrated to God, then days which are consecrated to divine worship will
also be holy. But the former is true. Therefore.

Response. The proposition is true of places and days consecrated by God. But
this does not follow: places assigned to worship by God’s command are called
holy; therefore days instituted by human beings are also called holy.

The remaining things Bellarmine adduces are unworthy of a response.

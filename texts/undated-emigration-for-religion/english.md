# On Emigration for the Sake of Religion

*A fresh translation of the complete French consilium in CO X/1 187–188.*

## Translation

### [CO X/1, p. 187]

If a married man living under the Pope's tyranny is compelled to withdraw
because he is not permitted to serve God faithfully in the purity he ought, I
would counsel him first of all to take pains to win his wife over so that she
agrees with him. Since he is her head, it is right that he conduct her along
the way of salvation insofar as he is able. Thus it would not be lawful for a
Christian man to abandon his wife under the pretext of following the Gospel.
Instead, let him labor by every means to draw her to our Lord Jesus, so that
both may obey him with one accord. He should not do so for only a single day,
but continue even if he finds her hard and rebellious.

Moreover, let him spare no effort, and while doing his duty to teach and
exhort her, let him pray that God touch her by his Spirit. God must indeed work
in the matter, or all human labor will be useless. If it is asked how long such
a man ought to persevere, I cannot assign any limit. I would only admonish him
to proceed with such courage and zeal that he makes all the more progress in a
few days, just as God also makes such forthright earnestness effective.

In any event, the husband must not grow weary of the long wait or lose courage
when he sees hardness in his wife, provided that in the meantime he abstains
from all idolatry. In order to draw his wife to God, he must not himself draw
away from God. For the rest, when he has done his whole duty, if he cannot live
safely in the country without involving himself in the pollutions of the
papacy but is compelled by persecution, threats, or otherwise to leave it, he
will always be excused, because it will not have been his fault that he

### [CO X/1, p. 188]

has not brought his wife with him. Nevertheless, although his departure should
not amount to withdrawing from her, he should instead endeavor while absent to
bring her out of the mire from which he has been delivered, so that they may
bless God together. In doing this he cannot be reproached for having made a
voluntary divorce or separated from his spouse. Where necessity constrains,
there is no longer such an obligation toward one's wife that, in order to
gratify her in any way whatever, one must turn aside from God's service. Thus
a man will be free when, after trying what was possible for him, he leaves
captivity.

If a man has children and is compelled to leave the country, whether of his
own accord or by force, in order to flee idolatry, I counsel him to bring them
with him as the finest treasure God has given him. If he leaves them, they
will immediately be seized and held closely in such bondage that he will
regret having left them for the rest of his life. Nor would this do anything
to preserve his property, unless he emancipated them and gave them their
share—something he must by no means do. For by doing so he makes them captives
until the age of twenty-five.

Even if the appointed guardian is well disposed, he will not dare undertake
anything; nor will it be in his power to sell or alienate anything. Even with
annual income, because he will be accountable for it, perhaps he will hesitate
to give any of it to the father. In short, whoever thus leaves his property in
his children's name gives them a cord, or entangles them in such a way, that
they cannot escape the abyss even if they desire to do so.

On the Communicable Attributes

Communicable attributes are those of which something similar and analogous
inheres in created things. They are called communicable, not because they are
in themselves communicated to other things and inhere in them, but because
similar effects are observed in creatures; and these indicate the divine
essence to us *a posteriori*.

Concerning these attributes, this rule must be maintained: all communicable
attributes are to be assessed according to the incommunicable, so that they
are predicated of God most simply, infinitely, eternally, immensely, and
immutably; and in this manner these attributes can be called incommunicable.
In creatures, however, these attributes are diverse accidents, existing
finitely, imperfectly, and mutably; but in God they are the one most simple,
infinite essence of God. Only in accommodation to our capacity does God
attribute to himself these diverse names, drawn by us from likeness, because
as they are in God we cannot compre—

—hend them, and so that we may consider what sort he is toward us.

The communicable attributes are reduced to these four: life, wisdom, will,
and power.

On the Life of God

The life of God is a communicable attribute by which God, or the essence of
God, is signified to live and to be active in itself most simply and
infinitely—that is, to move itself and act: Genesis 16:14 and 24:62,
Deuteronomy 5:26, Joshua 3:10, 1 Samuel 17:26, Matthew 16:16, Acts 14:15, and
Romans 9:26. Life is attributed to God most simply because it is the very
essence of God, and God is living, and God in his entirety is his life, 1 John
5:20. It is attributed infinitely because this life is eternal and immutable,
Deuteronomy 32:40 and John 5:20, and immense—that is, without increase or
decrease. Hence he is called life, the fountain of life, and immortal, Psalm
36:10, 1 Timothy 6:16, John 11:25, and 1 John 1:2. Thus he alone has life from
himself, John 5:26, while all things have it from him only by communication,
Genesis 2:7 and Deuteronomy 30:20.

That life, however, is not in God as it is in creatures is evident, because
in them the soul is the cause of life and life is movement, but those are not
so distinct in God.

The use of the doctrine. We are taught:

1. What sort of God we worship and how far he differs from fictitious gods
and from creatures, which have life only by communication.

2. Someone rightly compares God to the light created on the first day and
placed in the sun on the fourth day: namely, in Christ, so that it may be
communicated to others. Since, therefore, this life has been communicated to
Jesus Christ, the nearer we approach Christ by faith, the more we live.

3. Since in Christ we have been made partakers of the divine nature, 2 Peter
1:4, and life is in the divine nature, therefore we shall live forever.

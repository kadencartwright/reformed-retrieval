XXX. Thus far we have seen how faithfully *Wolzogen* conducted himself in his
first little book, in which he set forth his position without adornment. We
must now see how he conducts himself when he advances against an enemy, and
whether he has defended the cause hitherto received by the Reformed orthodox
and faithfully championed against enemies of every kind with the candor of
mind that be-*

[Page 139 | ONB canvas 193 | ABO Z176010803 image 00000193]

*fitted him, with that sincerity, and with the faithfulness that was fitting.
But here too it will become no less clearly evident that he betrays the cause
he had undertaken. We shall make this manifest by proceeding through the
chapters of the *Paradoxical Exercise* and weighing what *Wolzogen* replied.
*Paradox* begins his little book in this way: [From the very beginnings of the
Reformation, there has flourished between the Papists and the Evangelicals a
serious and momentous question concerning the right and the infallible norm
for interpreting the sacrosanct Scriptures], and after certain things: [Nor do
the divided members of lacerated Christianity seem to me capable of being
united, and so many heads brought back to the same opinion, by any better
remedy than the same one by which the disease could have been forestalled and
prevented: namely, a certain and by no means fallacious norm and rule, to which,
as to a touchstone, the truth of every interpretation may be brought for
testing, investigated, and judged. Therefore the labor of those who have
strained the powers of their talent to find it and have consecrated part of
their studies to it is by no means to be despised, much less attacked, but is
to be extolled with the greatest praises and promoted. Hence neither shall I,
although a newcomer----be ashamed that I have spent some time and labor in
this investigation.] From these things it is sufficiently clear what matter
the *Paradoxical Exercitator* raises here: namely, not the means by which the
true meaning of Scripture is investigated, for as far as those are concerned,
hardly any controversy exists between the Papists and the Evangelicals. And if
the question were instituted about these alone, it would not be of such great
moment as it actually is now. For then the question between *Paradox and
Wolzogen* would be only this: Is Reason or Philosophy the sole means of
discovering the true meaning of the Scriptures? Or, besides it, are knowledge
of the usage of discourse or speech, examination of the context, antecedents
and consequents, and other things of that ki-*

[Page 140 | ONB canvas 194 | ABO Z176010803 image 00000194]

*nd required, which *Wolzogen* refers to the use of reason? In this question,
as far as I conjecture, he would have found the *Paradoxical Exercitator* not
greatly disagreeing with him. But the question which *Paradox* raises here is
the same as that which flourished between the Papists and the Evangelicals
from the very beginnings of the Reformation: namely, To whom does the supreme
right of interpreting the Scriptures belong? And what is to be regarded as the
infallible norm for interpreting them, to which, as to a touchstone, we can and
ought to bring the truth of every interpretation for testing, judge it, and
investigate it? And since it is beyond the hazard of controversy that all the
Reformed ascribe this right and privilege, and this prerogative, to God's own
word and to the Spirit speaking in the word, who will not affirm that
*Wolzogen* betrays the cause when he either turns aside from the question
raised by his adversary and disputes about a very different one, thereby
avoiding the pitched battle with his adversary which he had solemnly promised
that he would not unwillingly engage on page 90; or openly affirms that this
right and prerogative must be attributed, not to the Holy Spirit speaking
through Scripture and in Scripture, or to Scripture itself, but to usage of
speech and reason, and thereby abandons all the Reformed and the cause hitherto
championed by them? This is what I had to demonstrate. But besides this, let
his own words on pages 94–95 be read: *[We nevertheless confess*, he says,
*that labor which is not useless can be spent in setting forth more explicitly
than has previously been done the whole of that by which we must be guided in
this path of interpretation lest we err. To this our author too must
necessarily return: he does nothing else than show that reason is the arbiter
of the true meaning. If he had professed this as fully as he endeavored to
establish it in actual fact, there would have been nothing concerning
this distinction of the interpreter which we would judge deserving of
criticism.]* Here he openly declares that he stands more on the side of
*Paradox* than on the side of the Reformed, since he wishes Rea-*

[Page 141 | ONB canvas 195 | ABO Z176010803 image 00000195]

*son to be the arbiter of the true meaning.

# Against Menno

This directory presents the complete Latin-form text and a new English
translation of the consilium headed *Contra Mennonem* in the *Calvini Opera*.

## Work identity

| Field | Value |
|---|---|
| Author | John Calvin (collected-print attribution; see below) |
| Addressee | Micron, conventionally identified as Marten Micron |
| Adversary | Menno Simons |
| CO consilium | I.7, *Contra Mennonem* |
| Date | Undated; CO cautiously proposes 1556 |
| Language/form presented by CO | Latin |
| Genre | Doctrinal response in epistolary form |
| Subject | Christ's human descent and conception, the two natures, and a refutation of Menno's Christology |
| CO locator | X/1, pp. 167–176 |

The work answers questions or arguments about Menno's account of Christ's
flesh. Its opening, *Calvinus Micronio* (“Calvin to Micron”), and direct
address to a “most upright brother” give it an epistolary frame. The displayed
heading *Contra Mennonem* is CO's organizing title, not an authorial title
attested by an autograph. The repository title similarly identifies the work
without claiming an original title page.

CO's note says only *Pertinere videtur ad a. 1556* (“It appears to belong to
the year 1556”). That is an editorial inference, not a dateline, so this
directory retains “undated” as the work's secure state. Attribution to Calvin
is explicit in the transmitted address and in the collected-print tradition,
but CO cites no autograph or manuscript for the item. Authorship, addressee,
date, original formatting, and CO's printed witness must therefore not be
collapsed into a claim that the author's document survives.

## Edition and transmission

The transcription follows Wilhelm Baum, Eduard Cunitz, and Eduard Reuss,
eds., *Ioannis Calvini opera quae supersunt omnia*, vol. X/1 (*Corpus
Reformatorum* XXXVIII) (Brunswick: C. A. Schwetschke, 1871), pp. 167–176.
This is the edition/witness represented here, not the lost authorial document
as such.

In the footnote attached to the heading, CO reports the item in the Geneva
*Epistolae et responsa* at p. 298 and in the Lausanne, Hanover, Chouet, and
Amsterdam collections at pp. 590, 664, 408, and 186 respectively. It records
no manuscript variant or autograph. Those citations establish a
collected-print transmission history; they do not independently establish
the proposed year.

The public facsimile is supplied by the University of Geneva's [complete
*Calvini Opera* collection](https://archive-ouverte.unige.ch/unige:650) and
its [direct CO X/1 PDF](https://access.archive-ouverte.unige.ch/access/metadata/f526a338-c95c-4f87-b600-04df723f502b/download).
The five relevant surrogate pages are:

| PDF page | Printed CO columns |
|---:|---:|
| 105 | 167–168 |
| 106 | 169–170 |
| 107 | 171–172 |
| 108 | 173–174 |
| 109 | 175–176 |

The downloaded PDF was 33,386,580 bytes with SHA-256
`025973d6e4533769d0c2b870dd324497943bc14050eb2bcbcc4c964d4f2a1777`.
On PDF p. 109 the right-hand running number is misprinted **167**. Its logical
number is **176**: it follows 175, completes this work, and is followed by 177
on the next spread. Both text files therefore use `[CO X/1, p. 176]` and this
note makes the editorial correction inspectable.

## Scope and collation

`latin.txt` includes the transmitted address *Calvinus Micronio* and every
word of the prose from *Tu vero, integerrime frater* on p. 167 through *nihil
petulantius hoc cane* on logical p. 176. The separately set address is retained
as transmitted paratext; without an authorial witness, its precise original
status cannot be proved. The file excludes the CO heading and its editorial
footnote, running heads and numbers, the end of *Contra Osiandrum* sharing p.
167, and the following consilium beginning on p. 176.

Every line and page turn was checked against 600-DPI renderings of all five
facsimile pages. Printer line wrapping and line-end divisions are removed;
CO's spelling, capitalization, punctuation, biblical locators, and Greek are
otherwise retained. At page turns, the marker precedes the whole normalized
word that begins on the new page. This affects:

- pp. 168–169: *crea-tiones* becomes *creationes* on p. 169;
- pp. 169–170: *Ma-ria* becomes *Maria* on p. 170; and
- pp. 172–173: *na-tura* becomes *natura* on p. 173.

The pp. 171–172 and 173–174 turns fall between the separate words *Nec /
quidquam* and *sua / perperam*. No authorial word is illegible or
conjecturally supplied.

## Textual and interpretive cautions

- CO clearly prints `παραβολὴν` on p. 174. Hebrews 11:11 and the surrounding
  argument would lead one to expect `καταβολὴν`, but the Latin file does not
  silently emend the witness. The English flags the reading in a translator's
  note.
- The citation “Galatians 4 (v. 4)” on p. 169 is retained as printed even
  though the immediately following claim about one seed is ordinarily
  associated with Galatians 3:16. Biblical wording is translated from
  Calvin's Latin rather than copied from an English Bible.
- The compressed *qualis futura erat ante lapsum* on p. 175 is rendered with
  *generatio* as its antecedent: “such as it would have been before the fall.”
  The Latin remains available for readers who construe the reference
  differently.
- The tract contains Calvin's forceful insults, including *nebulo*, *canis*,
  and *asinus*. The translation preserves rather than sanitizes this rhetoric.
- Its discussion of “female seed,” menstrual blood, sperm as coagulated blood,
  and fetal formation records early-modern reproductive assumptions. These
  claims are part of the historical argument and are not presented here as
  modern medical fact.

## Translation status

John H. Leith's edited volume *Calvin's Ecclesiastical Advice*, translated by
Mary D. Beaty and Benjamin W. Farley (1991), contains a copyrighted modern
translation of the *consilia*. It was identified bibliographically but was
not consulted and supplied no wording or interpretation here. No complete
public-domain English version of this item was located in the research audit.

`english.md` is an **AI-assisted scholarly draft**, translated afresh from
the normalized Latin and checked against every printed line of the facsimile.
It has not received independent review by a human Latinist. For exact-wording
claims, cite the Latin and its CO page; identify the English as this project's
draft translation.

Suggested citation:

> John Calvin, *Contra Mennonem*, undated (CO proposes 1556), *Calvini Opera*
> X/1 (1871), 167–176, normalized Latin and draft English translation,
> *Reformed Retrieval*.

# CHAPTER XX

## Whether sin is the punishment of sin, and whether GOD wills sin insofar as
it is the punishment of sin. Bellarmine, Diego Ruiz, Leonard Lessius, Gabriel
Pennotto, Lodovico Maeratius, Gabriel Vásquez, Alfonso Curiel, André Duval,
Luis de Molina, Philippe de Gamaches, and the Arminians are summoned to the
controversy.

Our fifth argument is this: *Whoever wills sin insofar as it is the punishment
of sin wills sin to occur by his permission.*

But GOD wills sin insofar as it is the punishment of sin.

Therefore God wills sin to occur by his permission.

[Marginal heading: Fifth argument.]

[Marginal summary: The third Reformed distinction is vindicated: between sin
in itself and in relation to God's law, according to which sin is sinful; and
sin insofar as it is the punishment of sin, in relation to the sinner, for whom
it is a penal evil.]

The major premise is certain, because a punishment is not inflicted unless a
judge decrees and wills it, pronounces a penal sentence from his just will, and
determines this rather than that as the punishment.

The whole question concerns the minor premise. For the Jesuits and Arminians
deny that God wills sin as punishment because they deny that sin is
punishment. The question, therefore, is whether sin is the punishment of sin.
I will accordingly (1) show in what senses sin is the punishment of sin; (2)
prove our position, namely, that sin is the punishment of sin; and (3) answer
the arguments brought to the contrary.

1. That sin is the punishment of sin is understood in various ways. (1) On
the side-*

[Chapter XX; printed page 262; IA PDF page 322; BSB canvas 314]

*of the cause, which is the removal of grace, sin appears to be a punishment
only demeritoriously and morally, because by its own demerit it removes the
grace that would prevent sin.

[Marginal summary: The ways in which sin is the punishment of sin.]

(2) On the side of the act, because by its frequent exercise it brings
weariness and affliction, according to Horace's line:

> The Sicilian tyrants found no torment
> greater than envy.

(3) But the present question does not concern these senses. Sin can be
regarded as the punishment of sin according to the disgrace or harm that
follows sin. And certainly, if God wills sin insofar as it is disgraceful and
harmful to the sinful creature, he cannot fail to will sin in some manner. On
this matter, see Thomas, Gregory of Valencia, and Vásquez.

[Marginal reference: Thomas, I–II, question 87, article 2.]

[Marginal reference: Gregory of Valencia on I–II, disputation 6, article 17,
point 3.]

[Marginal reference: Vásquez on I–II, tome 1, disputation 140, chapter 3,
number 12.]

2. Johann Gerhard says that God punishes sin with sin in two ways: *“(1) When
grace is lost on account of sin; once grace is lost, a person can do nothing
but plunge into crimes. (2) When GOD hands the sinner over into Satan's hands,
who drives him into various crimes.”* This is right. But does it not follow
from this that God does something from which it inescapably follows that the
person thus deprived of grace and handed over to Satan cannot avoid sinning?
Yet Gerhard asserts that, in our doctrine, this makes GOD the sinful author of
sin. Away with such blasphemies.

[Marginal reference: Gerhard, *Commonplaces*, part 1, chapter 19, on
providence, p. 140.]

3. Chemnitz says that sin is the cause and punishment of sin in four ways:
*“(1) As an accidental efficient cause, when sin drives out faith and grace.
(2) As a disposing cause, by of itself inclining the inclination to sin, as
drunkenness inclines to fornication. (3) As a material cause, as gluttony is
the cause and punishment of lust. (4) As a final cause, when someone commits
murder in order to fornicate.”*

[Marginal reference: Chemnitz, *Commonplaces*, part 1, p. 636.]

4. Aquinas says that sin is the punishment of sin in three ways: *“(1) On the
side of the cause removing what restrains—namely, God's grace. (2) On the side
of the substance of the act, which induces affliction, as anger and envy do.
(3) On the side of the effect.”* Nevertheless, he denies that sin is punishment
in itself.

[Marginal reference: Aquinas, I–II, question 87, article 7.]

5. Scotus says: *“The fault itself is from the will as from a deficient active
cause, and thus it is a fault because it is voluntary. The fault itself is in
the will as in a subject—not a subject of inherence but of denomination—and by
the fault that will is deprived of a fitting good. That good was owed insofar
as the will was able to act with due rectitude and did not do so; and in this
way the fault is a punishment.”*

[Marginal reference: Scotus, book 1, distinction 37.]

[Marginal reference: Cajetan, commentary on 2 Samuel 12:11.]

7. Peter of Ledesma, professor at Salamanca, says: *“There is no absurdity in
GOD's causing sin—not indeed insofar as it is sin, but insofar as it assumes a
certain character of punishment—in such a way that he causes and predetermines

[Marginal reference: Ledesma, *On the Helps of Divine Grace*, Salamanca,

8. Augustine says: *“Is it not both sin and the punishment of sin? For the LORD
mixed for them a spirit of slumber,”* and so forth. In the same place, by the
most invincible testimonies of Scripture, he demonstrates against Julian the
Pelagian—who defended with the Jesuits and Arminians the same evil cause of an
idle and indolent providence of GOD concerning sin—that the same sin is both
formally sin and the punishment of sin. Gregory likewise says: *“That which is
committed through a just blindness inflicted from above in vengeance for a
preceding sin is deservedly called the punishment of sin.”*

[Marginal reference: Augustine, *Against Julian*, book 5, chapter 3.]

[Marginal reference: Gregory, *Moralia*, book 25, chapter 12.]

9. The Jesuits—such as Molina, Lessius, Maeratius, Ruiz, Pennotto, Suárez,
Vásquez, and Alfonso Curiel—and those who conspire with them against the glory
of divine providence, the Arminians and Socinians—such as Jacob Arminius,
Arnoldus Corvinus, the Remonstrants in the synodical writings, Episcopius,
Socinus, Ostorodt, and Smalcius—maintain (1) that God in no way willed or
decreed that sins should occur; (2) that idle permission is the punishment of
sin; and (3) that sin formally considered is in no way the punishment of sin
and that GOD cannot will it. Indeed, the Arminians and Socinians say that God
does not even foreknow future sins, will them as punishments, or have the power
to will any penality inhering in them.

[Marginal reference: Jacob Arminius, *Anti-Perkins*, pp. 121–122.]

10. Finally, our position is that sin in relation to GOD's law, whose formal
violation it is, is the evil of fault and is contrary to GOD's holy-*

[Chapter XX; printed page 264; IA PDF page 324; BSB canvas 316]

*will—namely, it is repugnant to that approving will of God by which moral
evil is detested. Yet in relation to the subject, or efficient cause, of sin,
it is something penal.

Hence the first assertion: if the discussion concerns that bare permission by
which GOD does no violence to the creature's free choice, but lets it act in
its own connatural manner and according to its natural indifference—with
respect to willing or refusing, acting or not acting—then that permission is
not punishment. This is proved: (1) Punishment does not precede sin; it follows
sin as an effect follows its demeritorious cause. But this permission was in
Adam before any sin. For if he had not been permitted to sin in this way, he
could not have sinned at all. (2) Such permission, or non-violation of the free
will, has a place in the free choice of the good angels, whose free will was
not, and is not, violated so as to prevent them from willing or refusing,
acting or not acting, in their own connatural manner. No punishment has a
place in the good angels; and the same can be said of the free choice of our
Lord CHRIST. (3) Such non-violation of the free will is a common benefit by
which the Creator concurs with all secondary causes, preserving their natures
and tempering his concurrence to the character and disposition of causes,
both necessary and contingent. But no benefit of the Creator is a punishment
of GOD the judge. Pereira says in *On Exodus*, chapter 11, disputation 3: *“If
God hardens because he permits someone to be hardened, why is God not likewise
said to steal because he permits a person to steal?”*

Second assertion. Hence the Arminians' permission cannot be punishment. (1)
That permission leaves the will in its own indifference, with the aid of
sufficient grace, so that it can sin or not sin. But the permission of which
Scripture speaks is such that the one permitted could not avoid sinning,
because those punished by God cannot avoid being punished, as is clear from
these passages: Job 11:19–20; 1 Kings 22:22–23; Isaiah 6:9–11; Matthew
13:14–15, 15; Romans 1:25–28; Romans 11:8–9. Therefore Arminian permission is
not the kind of punishment described in Scripture as being blinded, being
handed over to a reprobate mind, being struck by a spirit of slumber, and being
abandoned to an efficacious delusion. (2) That which supplies matter for
greater praise is not inflicted by God as the gravest punishment upon those
who, because of preceding crimes, are blinded, hardened, and handed over to
foul affections. But Arminian permission is in itself *matter for praise and
greater merit,* because, as Arminius testifies, *to abstain from sin where
enticements and occasions for sinning are present is the greatest praise.*

[Marginal reference: Arminius, public disputation 10, thesis 8.]

[Chapter XX; printed page 265; IA PDF page 325; BSB canvas 317]

But all who are permitted to sin by the Arminian and Jesuit permission can
refrain from sinning. For they are furnished with sufficient grace, by which,
even when permitted, they are equipped to avoid sin and so acquire greater
praise and merit. Otherwise sin is not sin for them, if they do not have ready
at hand indifferent freedom to sin or abstain and sufficient grace.

[Marginal summary: Arminian and Jesuit permission is not the punishment of
sin.]

Third assertion. Nevertheless, I would not deny that a permission which
includes the denial of habitual grace—even grace of the more abundant kind—is
a punishment. For every privation of a good by which a person is rendered
more inclined, ready, and prone to sin is something harmfully evil to the
rational creature. But such a privation of more abundant grace is something
of this kind. (2) The bestowal of more abundant grace is a benefit graciously
granted to a person. Therefore the removal of such a benefit is a penal evil
for the person from whom it is removed.

[Marginal summary: Permission that includes the denial of grace is a
punishment.]

Fourth assertion. The adversaries err in thinking this to be the essential
difference between the evil of fault and the evil of punishment: that the evil
of fault is the privation of a morally upright good, whereas the evil of
punishment is the privation of a pleasant good and merely natural good. (1)
For, God favoring, I will prove from Scripture that sin is formally the evil of
punishment and, consequently, that punishment is the privation of a morally
upright good. (2) The formal character of penal evil requires that it be a
privation of a good, whether natural or moral. Therefore the essential and
specific difference between fault and punishment is this: fault is a
transgression of the divine law and a privation of the rectitude that GOD's
law requires, whereas punishment is the privation of that same legal
rectitude insofar as it is something harmful and penal to the rational
creature—or it is the privation of a natural good. Hence fault and punishment
are distinguished in that fault is essentially referred to GOD's law as
contrary to it, while punishment is referred to the rational creature as
something harmful to it. (2) The character of punishment requires that it be
a harmful evil inflicted by a judge with reference to sin by way of demerit.
And this latter feature adds nothing real to the evil, but only God's relation
by which he wills it to be inflicted in connection with sin. Hence every sin
is its own punishment, because GOD willed that sin—which in itself is harmful
to the rational creature—should be unfitting to it by way of demerit, by the
very fact that the creature sins-*

[Marginal summary: The differences between the evil of fault and punishment.]

[Chapter XX; printed page 266; IA PDF page 326; BSB canvas 318]

*. As Augustine beautifully says: *“It is fitting that the iniquity of sin
should not exist even for a moment without its due punishment.”*

Fifth assertion. We cannot help marveling at the Remonstrants' assertion,
namely: *“GOD predetermines, previously determines, and foreordains the
stubborn and exceptionally contumacious to sin by some penal judgment of GOD,
although those sins are not reckoned among sins so as to increase guilt.”*
From this it follows: (1) GOD is the author of blasphemy, sin against the Holy
Spirit, and all the parricides, adulteries, and poisonings committed by the
exceptionally stubborn, who have already deserved to be punished through the
commission of such crimes. The reason is that GOD predetermines the stubborn
to commit them. Therefore he is the author of all these things. The antecedent
is theirs, as is the consequence with which they argue repeatedly and always
against us. (2) It follows that, in that state, the exceptionally stubborn are
subject to no law of GOD. For although they heap abominable transgressions
upon transgressions, nevertheless, because they commit them without any guilt
or liability to punishment, the Arminians absolve them of every sin in such
crimes and pronounce them innocent. (3) It follows, contrary to the Apostle
John's determination, that not every transgression of GOD's law is sin. (4) It
follows that contumacy absolves some people from punishment, guilt, and every
law of GOD. Therefore, you who have already repeatedly treated the evangelical
commands and threats as nothing, and who are now hardened and stubborn,
proceed to blaspheme, worship idols, lie, perjure yourselves, commit adultery,
and heap parricides, poisonings, burnings of living persons, the devastation
of towns, and murders of old people, infants, and pregnant women upon your
other crimes: the Arminians freely pronounce you innocent henceforth, declare
that *these sins will not enter the reckoning of sins,* and say that they
*will not increase guilt.* (5) Murderers and parricides who have now reached
this fatal endpoint of fatal contumacy are not to be rebuked or corrected, nor
punished by the magistrate, since *such crimes are not reckoned among sins so
as to increase guilt.* For those who do not sin are not to be punished by God,
much less by the civil magistrate. (6) It follows-*

are reckoned among sins.]

*that those now stubborn are not to repent of such crimes, are to be touched
by no pang or sting of conscience, and are by no means to heed any such pangs
of conscience in a son who killed his aged father and stabbed his pregnant
mother, since they are mere fantasies. Such criminals do not need pardon and
the remission of sins in CHRIST. For a conscience that portrays, accuses, or
torments people over crimes *which are not reckoned among sins so as to
increase guilt* is delirious and insane. Nor is there any need for CHRIST to
wash sinners from such stains with his blood. (7) GOD, when inviting such
persons among a mixed multitude to repentance and to CHRIST the Mediator,
acts with malicious deceit, assumes a double and deceptive character—away
with the blasphemy!—and lies. For he commands all hearers of the gospel to
abstain from parricide and blasphemy—which is GOD's revealed will obligating
them—while by his hidden, predetermining and previously determining decree he
wills the stubborn to perpetrate such crimes. (8) GOD does not will the
lighter sins of tractable people to occur, but wills the gravest sins of the
stubborn and of devils to occur. (9) Because the Remonstrants, conquered by
the force of truth, see in some persons (1) that sin is the punishment of
preceding sins and (2) that the exceptionally stubborn sin by the penal
necessity of divine judgment, they therefore prefer to say that the deeds
which the stubborn perpetrate against GOD's law, natural conscience, and the
light of the gospel are not sins, rather than to confess with us and with
God's word that another sin is the punishment of sin. They prefer this to
admitting (2) that sin can be necessary and inevitable and nevertheless be a
sin deserving punishment; (3) that freedom and necessity in a qualified sense
can coexist; and (4) that there is sin which people do not have sufficient
grace and a corresponding ability to avoid.

Sixth assertion. Sin is the punishment of sin. This is proved (I) from
Scripture: *Thus says Jehovah: “Behold, I will raise up evil against you from
your own house, and I will take your wives before your eyes and give them to
your neighbor, who will lie with them in the sight of this sun. Because you
did it secretly, I will do this before all Israel and before the sun.”*

[Marginal reference: 2 Samuel 12:11–12.]

[Marginal summary: That sin is the punishment of sin is formally proved from
Scripture and from reasons deduced from it.]

Here four verbs are attributed to GOD as judge: (1) קום, to rise, and in the
Hiphil הקים, to raise up; (2) לָקַח, to take or receive; (3) נָתַן, to give,
render, apply, or place; and (4) עָשָׂה, to effect, prepare, or bring about.
God attributes these four actions to himself in punishing-*

[Chapter XX; printed page 268; IA PDF page 328; BSB canvas 320]

*David's adultery through Absalom's abominable incest. Hence I argue as
follows: whoever punishes David's adultery through incest as something penal
to David wills the incest insofar as it is David's punishment; and thus the
incest is punishment. But GOD punishes David's adultery through incest.
Cajetan says this, and Bellarmine confesses the same: *“GOD caused that evil,
not insofar as it was Absalom's fault, but insofar as it was David's
punishment.”* The Jesuits' and Arminians' chief answer here is: *“Nothing here
was penal except bare permission; the incest itself was not formally the
punishment.”* But this has been refuted. (1) David's punishment was the public
disgrace and harm inflicted upon David *before the sun and all Israel,* while
the impious counselor Ahithophel recommended this crime as GOD's instrument.
It was also the pollution of David's bed. But GOD's permission (1) was not
formally David's harm; (2) was not enacted publicly before the sun, but
secretly in Absalom's impure heart, when GOD loosened the reins upon his vile
lust; and (3) was not enacted in Absalom's heart at Ahithophel's urging as an
instrument, but immediately by God. Nor is the permission formally corrective
of David; the permitted incest is. (2) The very words of the threat prove
this, for in this passage GOD does not threaten David with permission as the
punishment, but with the thing permitted—namely, the harm and disgrace of his
polluted bed. For by the law of retaliation, the words contain a just
proportion of GOD's compensatory justice between sin and punishment: *“You
violated the one wife of an innocent man; I will hand over your many wives to
be violated. (2) You committed this adultery secretly, while Israel and the
husband did not know; I will punish you openly, with heaven and the sun as
witnesses before Israel. (3) You harmed your neighbor through adultery; I will
that you be punished by the harm of incest.”* Indeed, anyone here sees that God
repays like for like and punishes a similar sin with a corresponding
punishment. Yet the proportion between David's adultery and the permission by
which Absalom is left to himself so that he can either commit or refrain from
incest is feeble and incongruous. There is no compensatory justice here unless,
just as David had polluted Uriah's bed by adultery, David's own bed was in turn
polluted by the actual violation of his wives. (3) If the adversaries'
exposition prevails, then the threat will have this blasphemous and heterodox
sense: *“I both will this evil of David's polluted bed as the punishment for
Uriah's polluted bed and vow in this threat that I will inflict that evil; yet
at the same time, by this prohibition of the law—‘you shall not pollute your-*

*father's bed’—I intend and will Absalom to abstain from this abominable
pollution of the bed of his father and prince. That is, I will that this incest
occur”—for he wills what he either threatens or promises—“while at the same
time I prohibit it and therefore will that it not occur.”* Does God not then
speak contradictions and falsehoods? In his threat he says both that he wills
this and that, with the same will, he does not will but prohibits it. (4)
Punishment is a certain compensation for sin, but Absalom's being permitted to
violate is no compensation for the injury done to Uriah unless the actual
violation is added. (5) This permission is either a punishment of sensation
for David or a punishment of loss. It is not a punishment of sensation,
because this permission—by which God loosened the reins on the impious man—was
enacted while David neither knew nor perceived it and was many miles away.
Nor is it a punishment of loss, because this permission removes neither a
natural good nor a good of grace from David, any more than it does from other
holy men who ought to grieve over the deprivation of grace in their children.
(5) In this judicial act of violation David most justly suffers the infamy and
harm of violation. For it is just that the wives of the man who violated an
innocent man's wife after killing him should be violated. Thus David suffers
this most justly from the supreme judge, because every work of justice is from
the first just one; and everything just by participation is from the first one
who is just in himself and essentially just. Therefore either Absalom is the
first just judge here, or fortune, fate, or the stars are. It is not Absalom,
because against every human and divine right and against the law of nature he
pollutes his father's bed. Nor is it fortune, fate, or the stars, unless GOD
and GOD's providence are banished—which is absurd. Therefore GOD himself is
the first just judge, ὅπερ ἔδει δεῖξαι—what had to be demonstrated. You will
say that GOD as permitting is the principal judge. But to answer in this way
is sophistry, because the justice is in the actual violation, not in the
permission of the violation. (2) According to the Jesuits and Arminians, for
GOD to punish while permitting a penal evil is for GOD to punish unwillingly,
since for them to permit is the same as to be unwilling or not to will. But no
just judge performs a work of justice unwillingly and without willing it. (2)
No work of justice is contrary and evil to the nature and will of a just
judge, but good insofar as it is such a work.

The same point is proved thus. If GOD commanded—that is, ordained by hidden
providence—that Shimei should revile David his prince, insofar as those insults
were David's chastisements, then GOD wills sin-*

*insofar as it is punishment. But the former is true: *“Let him revile, for
the LORD has commanded him.”* GOD commanded by a command of providence, not a
command of precept; by a physically directive command, not a morally
illuminative one; by a judicial command of the will of good pleasure, as a
just judge punishing and chastising, not by an approving command of the will
of sign, as a legislator commanding and directing; or by a so-called command
in an improper sense, not a properly so-called command which it would have
been right and just for Shimei to obey. For both physical and moral dominion
belong to God. Thus Augustine says: *“The Lord commanded Shimei to curse David,
not by commanding him”—where obedience would be praised—“but because by his
just and hidden judgment he inclined Shimei's will, which was evil through its

bringing about that cursing through Shimei—not insofar as it was sin, but
insofar as it was David's punishment.”* What do Augustine and Cardinal Cajetan
teach here other than what our theologians teach? Our own Pareus likewise
says: *“GOD therefore inclined Shimei's evil will to this sin permissively
insofar as it was sin, but positively insofar as it was GOD's judgment or
David's punishment.”* First answer. Bellarmine says: *“GOD inclined Shimei's

I answer: (1) Who here is the investigator of hidden providence and the secret
inclinations of Shimei's heart? Was this revealed to Bellarmine by GOD's word?
Certainly, that wretched dog—if his powers had equaled his malicious will—
wanted the king and his family cut off from their very root and would have
accomplished it. The Jesuit therefore guesses badly that Shimei's will was
closed to other crimes but open to this one. (2) Either the way was closed to
his corrupt will by bare permission of GOD or by a positive action of GOD. If
the former, Shimei's will was not closed to other crimes, because Jesuit and
Arminian permission leaves the will indifferent to willing and perpetrating
all crimes that lie within its power and strength. Next, let the Jesuits teach
from their doctrine by what action GOD could close the corrupt will of Shimei,
who wished every kind of evil against the LORD's anointed, to this and that
crime, yet open it to the single and sole act of reviling and throwing dust.
Certainly this is impossible unless they acknowledge either a violent
constraint of the free will or GOD's antecedent predetermination-*

[Chapter XX; printed page 271; IA PDF page 331; BSB canvas 323]

*. For every other motion of the will is always fallible and frustratable,
whereas a punishment inflicted by the supreme judge cannot be avoided. But if

Arminius's second answer is: *“Shimei was at that time a hater of David and a
man with a most abusive tongue. The presentation by which Shimei was presented
to David while he fled, the divinely effected hardening of Shimei's mind, and
even the direction of his abusive tongue are acts pertaining to that command,
and no other action of GOD can be given.”*

[Marginal reference: Second answer of Arminius, *Anti-Perkins*, accusation 3,
p. 698.]

I answer: Who doubts that this wicked man was a hater of David? But hatred of
the prince whom he was bound to love and reverence by the religious duty of
the fifth commandment of the Decalogue was forbidden by GOD. When GOD said,
*“Curse David,”* by the same judicial command of providence he said, *“Pursue
the prince with hatred.”* And this hatred, as the internal principle of the
cursing, is the very thing now in question. (2) His being a hater of David
made him more prone to curse David; but GOD has the same ruling and directive
and judicial command of providence over sins committed by those who love
David. Yet he has it over those committed by David's haters too, unless
Arminius wants GOD to be able to be the author of crimes perpetrated by
David's haters but not those perpetrated by David's friends.

(3) In the same place, Arminius unwittingly confesses our position when he
says: *“David was worthy of that disgrace and altogether needed to be
chastised through it”—*that is, through the curse. Therefore, I say, GOD justly
chastised David through the cursing Shimei. And the sin of cursing was the
punishment which David deserved. Why then do we debate any further-*

*? (4) *“If the hardening of Shimei's mind and the direction of his abusive
tongue are acts pertaining to this command,”* they are either bare permissions
or positive actions of GOD's will. If the former, Arminius deceives us with
words. For permission leaves Shimei's will indifferent to cursing or not
cursing, and indeed to either greeting the king courteously or helping him,
as the adversaries suppose. Therefore permission is not direction of the
abusive tongue. But *if the hardening of Shimei's mind and the direction of
his abusive tongue are not* bare permissions but positive actions of the
divine will, then we have won and Arminius's cause falls.

The third passage is 1 Kings 22:20: *“Who,”* GOD says, *“will entice Ahab so
that he goes up and falls at Ramoth-gilead?”* Verse 23: *“Therefore, behold,
Jehovah has put a lying spirit in the mouth of all these prophets of yours;
therefore Jehovah has spoken evil against you.”* Hence I argue:

[Marginal summary: The passage in 1 Kings 22:20 proves that the devil's
seduction by which Ahab was enticed into war was the punishment of sin.]

Ahab's believing the lying false prophets who persuaded him to go to war at
Ramoth-gilead was the penal evil which JEHOVAH spoke and determined against
Ahab in verse 23. But Ahab's believing the lying false prophets was the evil
of fault and sin, for GOD through Micaiah forbade him to go to war, verses
27–29.

Therefore a certain evil of fault, or sin, is also the punishment of sin.

This passage is so plain that the truth escaped Vorstius against his will. He
says: *“The discourse there concerns a judicial—or, if you prefer, penal—
action of GOD as punishing. It is rare and extraordinary and always indicates
GOD's consequent will, by which he permitted an impious king who had deserved
grave punishments to be deceived and indeed in some measure took care that he
be deceived. Yet he took care of this in such a way that he procured in itself
not Satan's and the prophets' very sin as such, but solely the punishment of
the impious, although it was in some measure conjoined with sin.”* But surely
we have from Vorstius what we want: namely, that by GOD's penal action Ahab was
most justly deceived as punishment for the impious king's preceding crimes.
This deception—both active on the side of the false prophets and passive on
the side of the impious king who believed the lie—was also a grave sin. (2) If
GOD in some measure *took care that Ahab be deceived,* then he did not merely
permit it, but permissively willed this sinful and penal deception. (3) The
distinction here between-*

VERSE 10. Because no decree—the swift recompense of that evil—is carried out,
the heart of human beings therefore burns within them to do evil.

[Google Books pthrWjdL0BgC | reader image PA321 | printed pp. 321–322 | begins with complete Concessio after the p. 320 catchword Con-; ends on p. 321 immediately before repeated Vers. 11]

It is conceded that this evil which threatens those Tyrants is deferred; but
what is deferred is not removed. It is always on its journey, so that, as
Psalm 31:13 says, their judgment does not sleep but remains on course day and
night. Thus, although it proceeds with slow and tardy step, nevertheless,
making no interruption, it will overwhelm them sooner than human beings
suppose, 2 Peter 3. Then, by advancing, it gathers strength, so that it would
have been better had it assailed them earlier, unless they repent. For the
longer an axe is raised aloft, the later it strikes the wood, but it strikes
more heavily and deeply; and the longer an arrow is drawn, the more lethally
it pierces.

An effect of this delay and postponement is assigned: human beings are
thereby made bolder to sin, while through this tardiness of punishment their
minds become so filled with wickedness that they can no more contain it
within themselves than overflowing rivers of water can be confined within
their banks and kept from disgorging their waters upon the dry land. Let this
warn magistrates not to defer the just and due punishment for crimes unless
they want the land to be overwhelmed by crimes as by a Flood.

But here it may be asked why God defers vengeance when the Magistrate is
inactive or when the crime escapes the Magistrate's notice. The Apostle
teaches in Romans 2 that God is long-suffering in order to lead us to
repentance. Hence this perversity of human beings is the more detestable: they
abuse this patience of God for the hardening and obduracy of their own mind,
though he granted it for the softening and, in a certain manner, melting of
minds. But it follows in the same Apostle that, according to the hardness of
their unrepentant heart, they have stored up wrath for themselves on the day
of wrath and revelation of the just judgment of God.

*Translator's notes:*

The source divides the p. 320 catchword `Con-` from complete `Concessio` on p.
321. The paired texts place the complete normalized word after the PA321
marker.

The transcription retains the witness's printed `Psal. 31. 13.` and `2. Pet.
3.` without silently adjusting their numbering. It also retains the irregular
`effectum assignatur` and `thesaurizati sunt sibi iram`; the English supplies
their evident contextual sense.

The clause `ut praestiterit citius illos invadere` is elliptical. The close
translation takes its force to be that an earlier stroke would have been
better for the Tyrants than the strengthened judgment that delay brings.

# Samuel Rutherford, *Exercitationes apologeticæ pro divina gratia* (1651)

> **Working edition in progress:** This directory contains an AI-assisted
> diplomatic-normalized Latin transcription and an independent close English
> translation. Neither file is independently reviewed or publication-ready.

## Work identity

- **Author:** Samuel Rutherford (c. 1600–1661)
- **Title:** *Exercitationes apologeticæ pro divina gratia*
- **Edition used:** Franeker, Johannes Dhüiringh, 1651
- **Structure:** three exercises in thirteen chapters, on divine decrees,
  providence, grace, and human freedom
- **Earlier witness:** Amsterdam, Henricus Laurentius, 1636

## Controlling witness and rights

The controlling witness is the Princeton Theological Seminary Library copy
digitized by the Internet Archive as
[`exesap00ruth`](https://archive.org/details/exesap00ruth). It provides a complete
564-page PDF, page-structured OCR, plain OCR, scan metadata, and downloadable
page images. The 1651 work is public domain; Wikimedia Commons also mirrors
this exact scan and marks it public domain. Internet Archive images, not OCR,
control the transcription.

The 1636 Google Books witness (`jg5jAAAAcAAJ`) is a comparison witness only.
The 1651 volume is a fresh setting with broad textual continuity but materially
different pagination and chapter distribution. No claim of exact reprint or
systematic authorial revision is made without full collation.

## Exact source boundaries

- PDF/IA leaves 1–4: cover, binding, and ownership matter, excluded.
- PDF/IA leaf 5: title page.
- PDF/IA leaf 6: blank title verso.
- PDF/IA leaves 7–13: Rutherford's dedicatory epistle to John Gordon,
  Viscount Kenmure.
- PDF/IA leaves 14–18: Christiaan Schotanus's separately attributed preface.
- PDF/IA leaves 19–557: complete treatise, logical printed pp. 1–539, ending
  `LAUS DEO`.
- PDF/IA leaves 558–559: two-page printed chapter table/index, ending `FINIS`.
- PDF/IA leaves 560–564: blank, binding, or rear-cover matter, excluded.

There are no missing or duplicated textual leaves. Logical printed pp. 538–539
are visibly misprinted 528–529; source markers will record both intended and
printed numbers. No separate errata list appears in the 1651 witness.

## Editorial method

Latin long *s*, ligatures, line-end divisions, and typographic `u/v` are
normalized, while substantive spelling and wording are retained. Page markers
record logical printed page, IA leaf, and physical PDF page. Marginal citations,
Greek, Hebrew, headings, and documentary matter are retained. Running heads,
signatures, page numbers, catchwords, and ornaments are omitted unless they
bear substantive meaning. Doubtful readings are marked rather than invented.

The English is translated independently from the accompanying Latin and has
not used a modern English rendering as a wording source. It is complete prose,
not a summary, but remains AI-assisted and unreviewed.

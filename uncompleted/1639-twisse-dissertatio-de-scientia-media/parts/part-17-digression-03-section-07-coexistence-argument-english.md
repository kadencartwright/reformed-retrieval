Perhaps, however, you restrict this only to that instant of time, while nevertheless attributing to
it a proper entity in eternity. Let it be so: eternity extends itself to things which do not yet
exist. But what else is this than that eternity coexists with things which do not yet exist? Thus
things which do not yet exist coexist with eternity. But it is impossible for things which do not
yet exist to coexist with another existing thing. To coexist with an existing thing is for both to
exist and to exist simultaneously.

In my judgment, however, it is not very accurately said that the existence of things in eternity
proceeds from eternity. The existence of things in eternity does not proceed from that from which
their existence in time does not proceed. You say by this very claim that future things exist in
eternity, not insofar as they are elevated to that existence in eternity by some passive elevation,
but through a certain containment—that is, insofar as eternity extends itself to future things.
What else is this than that it extends itself to their existence in time? Yet the existence of
things in time does not proceed from God’s eternity, but rather from God’s power operating all
things according to the counsel of his will.

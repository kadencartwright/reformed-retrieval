# The Genevan Ministers, *Response concerning the Controversy over the Mediator* (1561)

This directory presents the complete Latin text and a new English translation
of the Genevan ministers' second extant response to the Polish Stancaro
controversy. It argues that mediation belongs to the one person of Christ in
both natures, while actions proper to either nature remain distinct.

> **Editorial status:** This is an AI-assisted draft transcription and
> translation checked line by line against high-resolution facsimile images,
> not a peer-reviewed critical edition. A qualified Neo-Latinist should
> independently collate it before formal publication or before relying on the
> English alone for a disputed historical or theological claim.

## Corporate authorship and work identity

This is not responsibly described as an unqualified solo treatise by Calvin.
The text repeatedly speaks as “we,” concludes “The ministers and pastors of
the Genevan church subscribed,” and in the Bern manuscript state names twelve
subscribers. The CO editors nevertheless identify Calvin as the author of the
two Stancaro responses, writing *nomine collegarum*, in his colleagues' name.
The body itself refers readers in the third person to “Calvin” and *Institutes*
2.14, while twice reverting to a first-person singular (*fateor*, *regero*).
Taken together, the evidence supports a corporate Genevan document drafted,
compiled, or principally composed by Calvin; it does not disclose a precise
sentence-by-sentence division of labor.

The twelve names printed from the Bern state are Ioannes Calvinus, Petrus
Viretus, Franciscus Burgoinus, Remundus Calvetus, Michael Copus, Ludovicus
Enocus, Theodorus Beza, Franciscus Morellanus, Nicolaus Colladonius, Anthonius
Cevallerius, Franciscus Beraldus, and Claudius Baduellus. The second Zürich
copy likewise preserves the names but changes the introductory formula to
*Subscripserunt hanc responsionem* (“They subscribed this response”). The copy
in Charles de Jonvillée's hand suppresses the subscription, as do the printed
editions, which replace it with *Ioannes Calvinus omnium nomine* (“John Calvin
in the name of all”). This edition retains the fullest corporate subscription
printed by CO and records rather than harmonizes the shorter states.

The document must also be distinguished from several neighbors in the Calvin
corpus. A shorter response dated 9 June 1560 is a separate work at CO IX
333–342 and has its own directory in this repository. CO infers an even
earlier, now-lost short answer from the correspondence. The present response,
dated March 1561, is the longer second extant response. It is not the following
*Impietas Valentini Gentilis detecta* (CO IX 361–420), nor should it be folded
into Calvin's 1563 warnings to the Polish churches.

CO's prolegomena also expose an unresolved identity problem. Later Calvin
complained that a response or writing needed to answer Stancaro's slanders had
been suppressed or withheld in Poland. The editors considered multiple
possibilities but explicitly declined to identify that missing item with
confidence. Nothing in this directory conjecturally equates that unknown
document with one of the surviving responses.

## Stancaro and the argument

Francesco Stancaro taught in the Polish churches that Christ is mediator only
according to his humanity. One of his arguments was that if the Son were
mediator according to deity, he would be inferior to the Father in deity and
would intercede with himself. He also accused opposing theologians, including
Philip Melanchthon, of Arianism.

The Genevan response locates mediation in Christ's whole person. The eternal
*Logos* mediated creaturely life even before the fall as head of angels and
humanity; after the fall, the office also includes sacrificial expiation and
reconciliation. Death belongs properly to Christ's humanity, but the saving
efficacy of his death cannot be isolated from his deity. Conversely, divine
works are not simply transferred to the humanity except through the
communication of properties. The Son's inferiority in the mediatorial office
is explained by the economy or dispensation of God's counsel, not by a lesser
divine essence.

The final section answers Stancaro's appeal to the church fathers. Its
Augustine, Cyril, Chrysostom, and Ambrose excerpts are evidence for how the
Genevan ministers received those writers; they are not a critical edition of
the patristic sources. Several transmitted references and readings are
unstable, and the difficult cases are made explicit below and in the
translator's notes.

## Edition, title, and textual witnesses

The transcription uses:

> Ioannis Calvini *Opera quae supersunt omnia*, ed. Guilielmus Baum, Eduardus
> Cunitz, and Eduardus Reuss, vol. IX, *Corpus Reformatorum* XXXVII
> (Brunswick: C. A. Schwetschke and Son [M. Bruhn], 1870), pp. 345–358.

CO reports three manuscript copies. The first Zürich copy, in Jonvillée's
hand at Plut. VI, vol. 116, fol. 3440ff., suppresses the ministerial
subscription. A second and less corrected copy stands in the same volume at
fol. 3603ff. The third is Bern civic library MS theol. 122, fol. 8ff. The
editors additionally collated Simler's copy in vol. 99. They report frequent
differences among manuscript and printed witnesses and print only a selection
of variants. The CO main text is therefore already an editorially constituted
text, not a diplomatic transcript of one autograph. `latin.txt` follows CO's
printed main reading and does not silently create a new eclectic text.

CO IX 345–346 are a nineteenth-century divisional title spread over a full
opening. The wording reflects the manuscript heading, but its display
typography, page footer, and placement belong to CO rather than to a facsimile
of a 1561 title page. CO IX 347–348 are blank. Above the opening sentence on
p. 349, CO prints the italic notice *(Inscriptio deest.)*—“The address is
missing.” That is an editorial report about the witness, not a sentence by the
Genevan ministers, and it is excluded. The continuous historical body begins
*Antehac rogati* and ends with the twelve-name subscription on p. 358.

Running heads, CO page numbers, gathering signatures, rules, apparatus cues,
and footnotes are also excluded. Parenthetical Scripture citations and the
patristic work references embedded in the main text are retained even when
incorrect. The title is supplied as metadata rather than inserted into the
historical body.

## Material variants and cruxes

The apparatus is too extensive to blend safely into a normalized reading.
The following are the most consequential differences; minor changes of word
order, particles, and synonyms remain inspectable in CO:

- At p. 349 other witnesses replace *in quo* with *qua*, *coegit* with
  *cogit*, and *fraenet* (“restrain”) with *formet* (“form”). CO's main
  readings are retained.
- At p. 350 the second Zürich witness reads *se tandem* for *ipsum* and
  *communionis* for *communicationis*; some witnesses expand “disciples” to
  “his disciples.” None is imported into the body.
- P. 351 contains a long recasting in the second Zürich copy as well as local
  alternatives such as *eveheret* for *reveheret* and *angelicum* for
  *ordinem angelorum*. The edition follows CO's main column.
- At p. 352 Jonvillée and Bern add *modis facile* before *expediet*. More
  importantly, the second Zürich copy recasts the statement about
  reconciliation, reads “Christ suffered in the spirit” where CO prints
  “Christ offered himself through the Spirit,” and twice has *perpetuo* where
  the main text has *perperam*. These readings cannot be conflated without
  changing the argument; the CO base is translated as printed.
- At p. 353 Bern and the second Zürich copy omit the span from *et hoc* through
  *unius essentiae*. Other variants affect the language of Hosea and the
  objection about Christ's interceding. The project does not reconstruct the
  genealogy of these states.
- At p. 354 some witnesses print *individua* rather than *indivisa* for the
  works of the Trinity and reshape the parenthetical phrase *obscurius autem*.
  The awkward CO punctuation and wording remain visible in the Latin.
- At p. 355 CO's “John 3:16” is a mistaken locator for the assertion that God
  did not spare his Son; its own note points to Romans 8:32. At p. 356 CO
  prints “chapter 3 of Colossians,” while its note identifies Colossians 1:20.
  Neither embedded locator is silently repaired.
- At p. 358 CO prints *utramque sonando naturam*. The Bern witness reads
  *sociando* (“joining”), which CO judges preferable. The Ambrose excerpt is
  also unstable: other witnesses add words after *pater* and read *vocari*
  (“be called”) for the main text's *servari* (“be preserved”). `english.md`
  renders the printed base literally and flags the resulting difficulty.
- The subscription formula and its very presence vary by witness, as detailed
  under corporate authorship above. It is historical paratext, not CO's
  editorial footnote, and is retained.

No unreadable word or unmarked conjectural supplement remains in the
transcription. “Complete” here means all historical main text printed by CO at
349–358, including the subscription, not every textual variant in the
nineteenth-century apparatus.

## Translation provenance and modern English

The English was translated afresh from `latin.txt`, with the facsimile
rechecked wherever syntax, page division, patristic quotation, or technical
vocabulary affected the sense. No published translation was copied, revised,
paraphrased, or used as a base.

A complete modern English translation exists: Joseph N. Tylenda, “The
Controversy on Christ the Mediator: Calvin's Second Reply to Stancaro,”
*Calvin Theological Journal* 8, no. 2 (November 1973), 131–157. The journal's
[official issue record](https://digitalcommons.calvin.edu/ctj/16/) identifies
the article. It is copyrighted and therefore does not satisfy this project's
criterion of a public-domain English version. No wording from Tylenda's
translation was consulted or used here; only its bibliographic existence was
recorded.

The translation preserves the response's shifts among corporate, singular,
and third-person voices. It keeps the distinction between predication of the
whole person and operation proper to one nature, renders *oeconomia* and
*dispensatio* in their ordered-salvation sense, and marks rather than smooths
the unstable patristic passages. Scripture quotations follow the Latin in
this witness and may consequently differ from familiar English Bible versions.

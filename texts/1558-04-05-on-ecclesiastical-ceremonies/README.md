# On Ecclesiastical Ceremonies (5 April 1558)

This directory presents the complete Latin text and a fresh English
translation of John Calvin's advice conventionally titled *De caeremoniis
ecclesiasticis* at CO X/1 211–213.

> **Editorial status:** This is an AI-assisted draft transcription and
> translation checked line by line against high-resolution facsimile images,
> not a peer-reviewed critical edition. A qualified Neo-Latinist should
> independently collate it before formal publication or reliance on a
> disputed reading.

The piece is a self-contained response rather than a chapter extracted from
a treatise. It opens without an address or salutation, closes with singular
*Vale*, and carries an internal Roman-calendar date but no signature.

## Edition, transmission, and facsimile

The transcription follows Wilhelm Baum, Eduard Cunitz, and Eduard Reuss,
eds., *Ioannis Calvini opera quae supersunt omnia*, vol. X, part 1, *Corpus
Reformatorum* XXXVIII (Brunswick: C. A. Schwetschke, 1871), pp. 211–213.

CO does not identify a manuscript witness for this item. Its title footnote
records the earlier printed transmission in the Geneva *Epistolae et
Responsa*, p. 352; Lausanne, p. 427; Hanover, p. 479; Chouet, p. 484; and
Amsterdam, p. 221. This edition therefore follows CO's printed main text and
does not imply access to an autograph or recover a suppressed recipient.

The public source is the University of Geneva's [complete *Calvini Opera*
collection](https://archive-ouverte.unige.ch/unige:650) and its [direct volume
X/1 PDF](https://access.archive-ouverte.unige.ch/access/metadata/f526a338-c95c-4f87-b600-04df723f502b/download).

UNIGE may regenerate its modern wrapper, so a later whole-file hash can

## Authorial and editorial matter

[`latin.txt`](latin.txt) begins with Calvin's *Quod ad caeremonias spectat* and
ends with *Vale. Nonis Aprilis 1558.* It excludes the CO heading, running
heads and foliation, footnote cues, and apparatus. The excluded apparatus
consists of:

- the title-note list of five earlier printed editions;
- CO's identification of Augustine's Januarius reference as Epistle 119; and
- the brief note *In Epistolis* attached to the citation of Cyprian.

These notices are bibliographical editorial matter, not missing sentences of
Calvin's argument. No textual variants are printed for this advice in CO.

## Normalization and completeness

The transcription follows [`TRANSLATION_POLICY.md`](../../TRANSLATION_POLICY.md):
printer wrapping is removed, edition punctuation and spelling are retained,
and each printed page is explicitly marked. This preserves such forms as
*quanquam*, *autoritas*, *coelestis*, *foetidam*, *sylvam*, *quum*, and
*sequuti*.

Two printed-page turns divide words:

- pp. 211–212: *peten-dum*, normalized as *petendum* under the p. 212 marker;
- pp. 212–213: *au-diendus*, normalized as *audiendus* under the p. 213 marker.

Nothing from the CO main text between the opening and internal date is
omitted. The preceding advice on ecclesiastical administration and the
following 1561 response on church rites are separate works and are excluded.

## Translation notes and independence

The argument distinguishes restrained ceremonies from additions that burden
Christ's sacramental institution. Calvin permits water to extend scarce wine
without theological symbolism but rejects a required mixture. His discussion
of Cyprian turns on *lymphatici*, the meaning of *miscere*, and the difference
between Cyprian's defense of wine and his incidental rationale for a mixed
cup. Those distinctions are preserved in [`english.md`](english.md) rather
than reduced to a general rejection of every circumstance involving water.

Mary D. Beaty and Benjamin Wirt Farley published a copyrighted translation in
*Calvin's Ecclesiastical Advice* (1991). It was not consulted or used for the
wording of this fresh translation, which was made from `latin.txt` and checked
against the facsimile.

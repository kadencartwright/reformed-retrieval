VERSE 9. What has been is the same as what will be, and what is done is the
same as what will be done; there is nothing New under the Sun.

From the objects of the eyes and ears, he proves that there is nothing in
which they can rest. The object of the eyes and ears, however, is something
new with which the ears and eyes delight themselves. Since there is nothing
such in the nature of things, it follows that nothing is found in which the
mind may rest. For it is plain that everyone is held by eagerness either to
see or to hear something new, and that disgust steals upon the repeated use
of one and the same thing. That person is known to abuse the ears who sings
the same tune and who wanders over the same string. For there is nothing so
pleasing to the eyes, acceptable to the ears, or sweet to the taste that it
does not become distasteful through constant and frequent use. An outstanding
example of this is Numbers 4:8. For although Manna descended originally from
heaven, although it resembled coriander in shape, bdellium in color, and the
flavor of the finest oil in taste, and although it could be prepared either
raw or cooked and in many different ways, nevertheless, through its constant
use, the Israelites were seized by nausea and disgust at it, so that they
preferred onions, foul-smelling garlic, and melons to it. When John the
Baptist first began to preach, everyone with great exertion raced one another
into the Wilderness to see and hear him. Although that desire ought indeed to
have grown daily and become stronger, since by the purity of his doctrine and
the holiness of his life he rendered himself more worthy of regard,
nevertheless, a little later their eagerness to hear and see him cooled so
greatly that they were moved by no greater eagerness to see and hear him than
if he had been a reed tossed by the wind. The same can be said of the
Galatians: although Paul was so dear to them that they would have been willing
to tear out their own eyes to gratify him, he was nevertheless afterward held
in contempt by them. Galatians 4:14–15.

He confirms by induction that there is nothing new under the Sun, after this
description of what is new: namely, because nothing has existed that will not
exist hereafter, and nothing has been done that will not happen hereafter.
This is most transparently evident, first, in

natural things, since they maintain a fixed sequence and a constant course.
In civil and voluntary matters also, if anyone considers more attentively,
amid the greatest variety and inconstancy of affairs he will notice the
recurrence of the same things, as though they were turned in a circle. This is
why knowledge of history is considered so useful and necessary: by setting
the past before our eyes, it admonishes us concerning the administration of
the present. This holds its truth in ecclesiastical matters. For just as under
the Law and the Prophets there were those who drew near with their mouth while
their heart was far removed from God, so at Christ's advent, as there were
then those who worshiped God in vain, teaching as doctrines the commandments
of men, so at Christ's advent, Matthew 15:8–9. The Apostle had his own Jannes
and Jambres. Just as there was no lack of false prophets under the Law in
former times, so there will be false teachers under the Gospel. Nor have any
heretics arisen in these our centuries who have not been roused from hell.
And just as before the Flood and the end of the old world they ate, drank,
built, bought, sold, and gave in marriage, so under the conflagration of this
world. Thus it has passed into a proverb that nothing is now said that has not
been said before, so that it can easily be recognized that the same play is
always acted with only the characters changed.

*Translator's note:* The edition prints `Num. 4. 8.` for the manna example;

Christi adventu` twice in the ecclesiastical comparison. The transcription
retains both printed readings, and the translation exposes rather than
silently repairs them.

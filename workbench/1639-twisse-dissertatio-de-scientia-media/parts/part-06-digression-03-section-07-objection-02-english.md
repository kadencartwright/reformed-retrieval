NAVARRETE.

Secondly, it is argued: this inference is valid, “Now Antichrist does not exist; therefore he cannot
now terminate God's intuitive knowledge.” The consequence is proved because it proceeds from the
denial of the superior to the denial of the inferior. In my judgment, the force of this argument
would be stated more plainly in this way: “Antichrist does not now exist; therefore he is not now
understood to exist; therefore neither is he known by God's intuitive cognition.” For to be known
intuitively is to be known as existing. This expression connotes the actual existence of the thing,
which it presupposes, and signifies that this existence is known by the kind of cognition which is
therefore called intuitive.

# CHAPTER VII (continued)

4. Let these remarks on the third mark suffice. The fourth follows: those
tablets were placed in an ark made for this purpose so that they might be
preserved more securely. Thus we read in Deuteronomy 10:1–5: *At that time
Jehovah said, Hew for yourself two stone tablets—and you shall make for
yourself a wooden ark, that I may write the same words on those tablets—then
you shall place them in that ark. Therefore I had an ark made from the choicest
cedar wood—then I turned and descended from the mountain and placed those
tablets in the ark I had made, where they remain, as Jehovah commanded me.*
First Kings 7:9 says: *There was nothing in the ark except the two stone
tablets which Moses placed there in Horeb, when Jehovah made a covenant with
the children of Israel*, and so forth. So too 1 Chronicles 5:10.

To show how much this serves our purpose, the following points must be noted.

1. This ark is often called the *ark of the covenant* (Numbers 10:33; 14:44;
Deuteronomy 10:

8; 31:9, 25; Joshua 3:3, 17; 4:7, 9, 18; 6:6, 8; 8:33; Judges 20:27; 1 Samuel
4:3–4; 2 Samuel 15:24; 1 Kings 3:15; 6:19; 8:1, 6; 1 Chronicles 15:25, 29; 2
Chronicles 5:2, 7; Jeremiah 3:16; Hebrews 9:4), and sometimes the *ark of the
Testimony* (Exodus 30:26; 31:7; 39:35; 40:3, 5).

2. This ark was placed in the Holy of Holies (1 Kings 8:6).

3. This ark was the principal sign of the divine presence (1 Samuel 4:6), and
therefore it is specifically called the *ark of God* (1 Samuel 3:3; 4:11, 18;
5:1, 11; 6:3; 14:18; 2 Samuel 6:2; 7:2; 15:24, 29; 1 Chronicles 13:3, 14;
16:1, and so forth; 15:1, and so forth; 2 Chronicles 1:4) and the *ark of God's
strength* (2 Chronicles 6:41; Psalm 132:8).

4. The wings of the cherubim were spread over this ark (1 Kings 8:6),
indicating the gracious divine presence and protection.

5. Moreover, this ark was covered by the propitiatory lid (Leviticus 16:2).

6. It follows from this that God did not wish to entrust this ark with those
tablets of the covenant to just anyone's keeping, but reserved them to be kept
in a special way by himself.

7. Since this ark of the covenant, together with the propitiatory, was a
distinguished type of Christ (Romans 3:25), and nothing except these tablets
was placed there to be kept, by this deed God signified that Christ was the end
of the law (Romans 10:3–4), and that he alone would satisfy and observe the
law.

8. The ceremonial law was written in a book and placed not in the ark but
beside it. By this deed God signified that this law would at last be abrogated
and removed through Christ, but that the moral law would be established and
confirmed. He thereby signified that this moral law was far different from the
ceremonial law, including with respect to duration.

The opponents object, 1, that things other than the Decalogue were stored in
the ark: namely, the jar containing manna and Aaron's rod (Hebrews 9:4), as
well as the book of Deuteronomy (Deuteronomy 31:26).

*Answer.* 1. Even if this were granted, nothing could be inferred from it to
weaken our argument. We are speaking about laws, and it is agreed that no other
laws were stored in that ark. As for that book of the law in Deuteronomy 31:26,
it was placed *at the side* of the ark of the covenant, not *inside* its side,
מצד. See the Dutch annotations on the passage. Those annotators think this was
the book which the high priest found in 2 Kings 22:8. See also the English
annotations, which further add that the rabbis say this book was placed in a
certain chest beside the ark of the covenant.

2. The text plainly says in 1 Kings 8:9 and 2 Chronicles 5:10 that nothing
except those tablets was placed in the ark. For the jar containing manna was
placed *before the testimony*, not in the ark (Exodus 16:35). Aaron's rod was
placed *before the testimony* (Numbers 17:10), where all the other rods had
recently been deposited for a time (verse 4).

3. As for Hebrews 9:4, the common reply is either that ἐν ᾗ means *with which*,
as in Mark 1:23; Luke 4:32; Ephesians 6:2; Romans 15:32; or that ἐν ᾗ refers not
to the nearest antecedent, κιβωτός, *ark*, but to the more remote σκηνή,
*tabernacle*, constructions similar to which occur in Holy Scripture at
Genesis 10:12; 2 Samuel 21:14; Psalm 99:7; Mark 2:3; 2 Thessalonians 2:9; John
8:44; or that ἐν ᾗ is equivalent to *beside, near, or before it*, as at Luke
9:31. See also Joshua 5:13; Judges 18:12; Jeremiah 13:5. See Piscator on the
passage. Dr. Burmann agrees in *Synopsis*, book 4, chapter 14, section 16.

*Objection 2.* The ark itself was lost in the Babylonian captivity. Therefore
nothing can be concluded from this for the perpetuity of the Decalogue.

*Answer.* Granted. Nevertheless, since God saw fit to store these precepts and
no others in the ark, it is plain that God granted these precepts a prerogative
and that this prerogative especially concerned the duration of their
obligation. This is sufficient, for we do not say that this obligation had to
endure only as long as the ark itself endured. Otherwise no precept formerly
given to the Israelites would bind us now, which even the opponents will not
say.

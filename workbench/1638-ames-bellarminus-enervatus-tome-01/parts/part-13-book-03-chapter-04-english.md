### Chapter IV

##### On Antichrist, book 3 of *On the Pontiff*

The question is: Is the Roman Pontiff that Antichrist of whom the Apostles
forewarned? The Papists deny it; we affirm it.

##### Bellarmine's arguments

##### Thesis I

Bell.: *The name of Antichrist signifies an enemy and rival of Christ. But the
Roman Pontiff confesses himself to be Christ's servant and subject in all
things; he does not say that he is Christ in any way, nor does he make himself
equal to him. Therefore he is not Antichrist.* Prot. 1. There are four terms
(as they say) in this syllogism: 1. The Roman Pontiff. 2. An enemy and rival of
Christ. 3. One who says that he is an enemy and rival. 4. Antichrist. Many may
be enemies and rivals of Christ although they do not say that they are such.
When the demons confessed Christ in words, Mark 1:24 and Luke 4:41, they were
nevertheless his enemies; and when Satan transforms himself into an angel of
light, or his ministers, pseudo-apostles, transform themselves into ministers
of righteousness and Apostles of Christ, 2 Corinthians 11:13–15, they do not
thereby cease to be their enemies. Those who wished to be rulers in the Church
so as to remove Christ from his place nevertheless made a great display of
humility, Colossians 2:18–19, 23. Antichrist was to come *in a mystery* and
with *deception*, 2 Thessalonians 2:7, 10, that is, as Medina interprets it on
Thomas, part 3, question 8, article 8, *under the appearance and a certain veil
of piety*. Those Antichrists of whom John speaks in his first Epistle, chapter
2, verse 22, are said to deny the Father and the Son, although they denied the
Father only by consequence, as appears from verse 23. 2. That what Bellarmine
says is false—*that the Pope in no way says himself to be*

[1638 Amsterdam witness | e-rara canvas 186 | Augsburg canvas 200 | printed p. 176 | ZB Zürich Rp 510]

*Christ, or equal to him*—is apparent from Bellarmine himself. For he who says
that he is the foundation of the Church, that cornerstone treated in Isaiah
28:16, says in some way that he is Christ, Romans 9:33 and 1 Peter 2:6. But the
Pope says this according to Bellarmine in the preface to this third
controversy. He also says that he is that one Shepherd treated in John 10:16,
as appears from verse 14; but the Pope says this according to Bellarmine, *On
the Pontiff*, book 1, chapter 9. Finally, he who affirms that he is the head of
the Church in the manner in which Christ was while he dwelt on earth says in
some way that he is Christ. But the Pope says this according to Bellarmine in
the same place.

Bell.: *Antichrist is only one person, but there have been and will be many
Pontiffs. Therefore the Roman Pontiff is not Antichrist.* Prot. The proposition
is false; it cannot be confirmed by any reasoning and is easily refuted by
many arguments. Bellar. 1. John 5:43: *If another comes in his own name, you
will receive him. Here a certain single person opposed to Christ is designated
by “another,” “in his own name,” and “him.”* Prot. “Another,” ἕτερον, no more
signifies a certain or singular person here than it does in John 4:37,
*one is he who sows, and another he who reaps*; or in 1 Corinthians 6:1;
10:24, 29; 12:9–10; and 14:17; Galatians 1:19; and James 4:12. But since
“another,” as appears from these passages, is taken not properly and
singularly but indeterminately and particularly, we must take “in his own
name” and “him” in the same sense. So also “that man,” ἐκεῖνος, in Matthew
24:46, 48, 50. Moreover, not only that notorious Antichrist is understood
there, but also *any other who does not seek the things of God*, as Cajetan
observes. An indefinite “other” is opposed to Christ, a definite person, just
as *some other* is opposed to Paul, a definite person, in 2 Corinthians
11:20–21, and *anyone else* in Philippians 3:4. Bell.: *Christ says here that
the Jews would receive that other as Messiah, but they were expecting one
definite and singular man.* Prot. Christ does not say this. 2. Although they
expected one singular man, they nevertheless received various men, each of
whom they thought was that one. Bell.: *All false Prophets except Antichrist
came in another's name, not in their own.* Prot. They all pretend to come in
God's name, as does Antichrist, but in reality they

[1638 Amsterdam witness | e-rara canvas 187 | Augsburg canvas 201 | printed p. 177 | ZB Zürich Rp 510]

come in their own name, as the Jesuit Toletus rightly notes on that passage.

3. Bellarm., 2 Thessalonians 2: *A certain singular person is denoted by that
Greek article, “the man,” “the son of lawlessness.”* Prot. It no more denotes
one particular singular man than “the man,” ὁ ἄνθρωπος, does in Mark 2:27,
Luke 4:4, 2 Timothy 3:17, and many other passages.

4. Bellarm., 1 John 2:18: *Antichrist with the article is a singular person.*
Prot. That he is not a singular person appears from verses 22 and 23.
Antichrist with the article is called πᾶς ὁ, “everyone who denies the Son.”

5. Bell., Daniel 7, 11, and 12: *Where either Antichrist or his type is treated,
a certain and singular person is denoted.* Prot. 1. It is false that either
Antichrist or his appointed type is treated there; only a similar beast is
treated. 2. It is also false that a singular person is denoted there
exclusively. 3. A singular person can be a type of a great multitude, Romans
9:10–11 and Galatians 4:23–24.

6. Bellarm., Revelation 13 and 17: *One definite king is mentioned, as by
Daniel.* Prot. 1. The kings and the Beast are openly distinguished, Revelation
17:12. 2. The Beast and great harlot is a great city with seven Mountains,
verses 7 and 18; but the kings are the horns of that Beast, verse 12, and so
they no more ought to be singular persons than the Beast itself.

*Such are the arguments by which Bellarmine proves Antichrist to be one
singular man. We must now see how he resolves the contrary arguments, though
he touches only a few out of many.*

7. Prot. Antichrist began to operate in the world in Paul's time, yet he will
be brought to an end at the end of the world, 2 Thessalonians 2. Therefore he
is not one singular man. Bell.: *In the time of the Apostles Antichrist began
to rage, not in his own person but in his forerunners.* Prot. 1. This is what
we intend in this argument: that a certain peculiar tyranny is denoted there,
which those forerunners gradually served, just as Beza himself, whose argument
this is, declares, so that

[1638 Amsterdam witness | e-rara canvas 188 | Augsburg canvas 202 | printed p. 178 | ZB Zürich Rp 510]

they all belong to that body whose head Antichrist is in the most proper
sense. Just as Christ, had he been a mere man, could not have wrought the
mystery of godliness from the beginning of the world, so Antichrist as one
mere man could not have worked from the time of the Apostles all the way to
the end of the world. 2. What sane person will believe that all heretics and
tyrants from the time of the Apostles were forerunners of one singular man who
would come at the end of the world and reign for the briefest time? Certainly,
if those Forerunners are considered at least collectively, they were much
greater than a three-year Antichrist could be.

8. Prot. By the Antichrist who is called the Beast in the New Testament, one
singular man is no more to be understood than one singular King by the Beasts
of Daniel. Bell. 1: *Daniel sometimes signifies one king by one Beast, as in
chapter 8, Darius by the Ram and Alexander by the He-goat.* Prot. That this is
false is expressly testified by the Angel Gabriel, Daniel 8:20–23. Bell. 2:
*Paul does not understand any of the Beasts described by Daniel.* Nor were
those Beasts proposed by Beza in that sense, but as examples of similar
speech.

9. Prot. Antichrist is the head of the Apostasy. But that Apostasy is not to
be completed in a few years under one King. Therefore Antichrist is not one
King of a few years. Bell. 1: *By the Apostasy, Antichrist himself can be
understood metonymically.* Prot. This is true for this reason: as we say,
Antichrist is the head of the Apostasy. Bell. 2: *By the Apostasy, the defection
from the Roman empire can be understood.* Prot. It cannot, because the
Apostasy belongs to that sin and destruction from which Antichrist is named,
2 Thessalonians 2:3. Bell. 3: *It can be understood of that greatest and
singular Apostasy from the faith which will occur only in that very brief time
during which Antichrist will reign.* Prot. It cannot, because in one three-year
period the greatest Apostasy of all that have ever occurred cannot be begun
and completed. Bell. 4: *We can say that this Apostasy does not require one
head, but is a preparation for the kingdom of Antichrist.* Prot. You cannot
say this unless you wish to contradict Paul, who makes this Apostasy and the
man of sin's

[1638 Amsterdam witness | e-rara canvas 189 | Augsburg canvas 203 | printed p. 179 | ZB Zürich Rp 510]

revelation plainly contemporary, 2 Thessalonians 2:3. Note it. Bell. 5: *If an
Apostasy
from the faith that has now endured for many years is Antichrist's kingdom, it
will not follow from this that the Pope is Antichrist. For the question would
still have to be examined as to who have defected from the faith and religion
of Christ, we or the Lutherans.* Prot. 1. We do not immediately conclude from
this that the Pope is Antichrist, but that such a kingdom as the Pope's is. 2.
Who have defected from the faith and religion of Christ, we or the Papists,
will not be a question among those who know both parties and compare both
professions with the doctrine of Christ expressed in the Scriptures, if, once
prejudice is removed, they apply even moderate consideration. For those *who
seek righteousness from works of the law*, Romans 9:32 and Galatians 5:4;
those who *teach doctrines of demons* concerning marriage and foods, 1 Timothy
4:1, 3; those who *do not keep themselves from idols*, 1 John 5:21; those who
do not rest in Christ as the sole Mediator, 1 Timothy 2:5; those who worship
God according to the commandments of men, Matthew 15:9; those who religiously
worship Angels and other creatures, Colossians 2:18; those who deny the cup to
those celebrating the Lord's supper and furnish them only the species of
bread, 1 Corinthians 11:26; those who keep the faithful from reading Holy
Scripture, John 5:39; finally, those who have absolutely nothing in which they
have not in some way degenerated from that simplicity which is in Christ, 2
Corinthians 12:3—they have undoubtedly defected from the faith and religion of
Christ. Whoever does not see these things in the Papists is blind.

10. Bellarm.: *Antichrist has not yet come.* Prot. This is said by the Papists
just as easily as the Jews say that Christ has not yet come, and for the same
reason. For just as the Jews invent for themselves a Christ such as God never
promised, so the Papists expect an Antichrist such as Scripture nowhere
described. The former see well enough that the marks of Christ agree with no
one but Jesus our Lord, whom they do not receive; the latter also understand
that the marks of Antichrist agree with no one but the Pope, whom they refuse
to acknowledge as Antichrist. Therefore, just as the former will expect Christ
forever, so the latter will expect Antichrist forever. Bell., c.: *The Gospel
must be preached in the whole world before the*

[1638 Amsterdam witness | e-rara canvas 190 | Augsburg canvas 204 | printed p. 180 | ZB Zürich Rp 510]

*coming of Antichrist*, Matthew 24:14. Prot. 1. Nothing is said there about the
coming of Antichrist; rather, the Gospel is said to be preached to the whole
world before the destruction of Jerusalem, as is evident from verse 16: *Then
those who were in Judaea*, etc. 2. By the whole world are understood not all
the world's individual regions but many of its parts, so that the same Gospel
had also reached the others virtually, with no kind of people excluded from
participation in this benefit, as in Romans 10:18 and Colossians 1:6, 23.
Bell.: *The Apostles indeed spoke figuratively, but the Lord properly and
simply: 1. Because he makes this preaching a sign of the consummation of the
age. 2. Because all Nations are properly promised to Christ. 3. Because that
preaching was to be a testimony to all Nations, that is, so that no Nation
could plead ignorance for its unbelief.* Prot. 1. It is made a sign of the end
of the Jews' temple, city, and commonwealth, not the end of the world. 2. By
all the nations promised to Christ is understood a great multitude from every
kind of people, with no Nation individually excluded. Yet it is not necessary
that whatever was promised to Christ among the Nations should all be
contained in those words of Matthew 24:14. 3. Men are to be judged
individually, not nations in masses. Therefore, if the preaching of the Gospel
in each and every Nation is necessary to take away excuse, it is also
necessary that it should have been preached from the beginning to each and
every mortal. 4. In all these things nothing is found that can place the
coming of Antichrist at a future time.

11. Bellarm. 2: *The complete desolation of the Roman empire must precede the
coming of Antichrist, but the succession and name of the Roman Emperors still
remain. Therefore Antichrist has not yet come.* Prot. 1. The proposition
cannot be proved from the Scriptures themselves, but only from human
conjectures. 2. If by complete desolation he understands such a desolation as
cannot coexist with the name or title of the empire, then the proposition is
false. If it is understood in another sense, then there are four terms in the
syllogism, for the assumption asserts that only the succession and name
remain. 3. The removal

[1638 Amsterdam witness | e-rara canvas 191 | Augsburg canvas 205 | printed p. 181 | ZB Zürich Rp 510]

of the Empire with a diminution rather than its complete desolation is
indicated in 2 Thessalonians 2:4. This removal was to precede not the coming
but the revelation of Antichrist, at the same place. 5. That removal of the
empire is sufficient to bring about the revelation of Antichrist at Rome,
leaving Rome vacant for Antichrist so that he can place the seat of his
kingdom there. 6. That in this manner the Roman Empire has now, and long since
has, been taken out of the way is so manifest that a certain Archbishop once
said publicly that the Emperor was an empty title and a mere shadow,
Aventinus, *Annals*, book 7. Bellarmine himself in this chapter 5 admits that
Rome has lost the Empire; and for this very reason Thomas Aquinas could not
deny in any other way that Antichrist had come than by substituting the Roman
Pontiff in place of the Empire. For thus he says on 2 Thessalonians 2, lecture
1: *How is this, since the Nations long ago withdrew from the Roman empire,
and yet Antichrist has not yet come? It must be said that it has not ceased,
but was changed from temporal to spiritual.* Here it should also be observed
that Thomas concedes that the Pope is the final head of the Roman Empire, but
without the name of Roman Emperor. Therefore, if we admit Bellarmine's
proposition in chapter 15—*Antichrist is the last King who will hold the Roman
Empire, yet not without the name of Roman Emperor*—and Thomas's assumption—
*The Pope is the last King holding the Roman Empire, without the name of Roman
Emperor*—can anyone prevent us from concluding, therefore the Pope is
Antichrist?

12. Bellar. 3: *Enoch and Elijah have not yet come; therefore neither has
Antichrist*, Malachi 4, Matthew 17, Revelation 11. Prot. 1. The Jews formerly
reasoned in exactly the same way, Matthew 17:10 and Mark 9:11: Elijah must come
before Christ, but he has not come; therefore neither has Christ. The dreams
are exactly twins. 2. In Malachi 4 and Matthew 17 no mention is made of either
the coming Enoch or Antichrist, but only of Elijah; in Revelation 11, where
Antichrist is treated, no mention is made of either Enoch or Elijah. Is this
not a splendid demonstration that Enoch and Elijah will come together with
Antichrist? Moreover, whatever is said about Elijah's coming in Malachi 4
belongs entirely to

[1638 Amsterdam witness | e-rara canvas 192 | Augsburg canvas 206 | printed p. 182 | ZB Zürich Rp 510]

John the Baptist, as witnessed by Mark, chapter 1, verses 2 and 40; by the
Angel Gabriel, Luke 1:17; and by Christ himself, Matthew 11 and 17.

13. Bellarm. 1: *Malachi speaks about the second coming of Christ, which he calls great and
terrible.* Prot. The first coming of Christ is treated there, as appears most
evidently in the parallel passage, Malachi 3:1–2, etc., where the Lord who is
to come is said to come *to his temple*, and the fruit of his coming is
declared in verse 3: there will be those who *offer a gift justly*, or
*sacrifices of righteousness*, as it is commonly read. Since the Evangelist
also makes these last words of the Old Testament in Mark 1 the first words of
the New, just as by this act he rejects all the Apocryphal books forcibly
interposed by men, so also he interprets these same words clearly enough of
Christ's first coming. But that coming is called terrible—although the word
*Norah* can suitably be translated *reverend* or *dreadful*—because in its
outcome it was terrible to unbelieving Jews, through that vengeance of the
rejected Christ as he came, which appeared especially in the destruction of
Jerusalem. For when Christ came he was set for the ruin of many, Luke 2:34; he
brought a winnowing fan in his hand to cleanse the threshing floor and
separated the chaff so that it might be burned with unquenchable fire, Luke
3:17; then the axe was laid to the root of every tree, verse 9 of the same
chapter; he was a stone that broke and scattered, Matthew 21:44; and a little
later he issued public judgment upon the rebellious nation and the very land
of the Jews, Luke 19:44, according to that last verse of Malachi 4: *Lest I
come and strike the earth with a curse.* This is the clear explanation of the
passage which Arias Motanus testifies on Malachi that he learned from the
Angel Gabriel, Luke 1. Bellar. 2: *John was the promised Elijah, not literally
but allegorically.* Prot. He was indeed called Elijah figuratively, or by a
trope, Luke 1:17, because in spirit and power he resembled Elijah; but
Bellarmine himself admits that a figurative sense of this kind is literal,
*On the Word of God*, book 3, chapter 3. Bellarm. 3: *Elijah must come and
restore all things*, Matthew 17, *which John the Baptist did not do.* Prot. He
certainly did this, as witnessed by the Angel Gabriel, Luke 1:17, when *he
prepared an instructed people for Christ*; as witnessed also by Zechariah his
father, verses 76–77 of the same chapter; and finally as witnessed by all
Christ's disciples, who

[1638 Amsterdam witness | e-rara canvas 193 | Augsburg canvas 207 | printed p. 183 | ZB Zürich Rp 510]

understood that in those very words Christ had spoken of John the Baptist,
Matthew 17:13. Bellarm. 4: *That Enoch and Elijah are understood in their
singular persons in Revelation 11 is apparent from the fact that they are to
be killed, left unburied for three days, afterward raised, and taken up into
heaven*, at the same place. Prot. 1. From these things it is in no way proved
that Enoch or Elijah is understood there, because God can accomplish all
these things in others just as easily as in Enoch and Elijah. 2. What is said
there cannot be understood of two singular persons, because two men,
especially in so brief a space as Bellarmine assigns to them, cannot preach to
all peoples, tongues, and nations, be gazed upon dead by them, and be feared
when raised. The holy city or the temple of God is not given to two men. 3.
What is said of those witnesses agrees least of all with Enoch and Elijah. For
it is not credible that those who nearly at the beginning of the world were
made immortal by a singular privilege would, at the end of the world before
all the other blessed, suffer an interruption of their felicity, being called
back to mortality and grievous sufferings. 4. What is said there cannot be
understood properly, without tropes, unless the Papists also wish the two
witnesses to be two olive trees and two lampstands and to emit burning fire
from their mouths. Bell. 5: *No other reason can be given why these two were
taken up before death and still live in mortal flesh, destined to die at some
time.* Prot. That is, a sound reason for one fable cannot be given unless
another invented and related one is admitted at the same time. For although
the translation of Enoch and the taking up of Elijah are most certain matters
of faith, their mortality awaiting death is rightly numbered among fables.

13. Bellarm. 4: *Antichrist's persecution will be most severe and most
notorious, so that all public ceremonies and Sacrifices of religion cease;
yet we still see none of these things. The Roman Pontiff especially adorns and
defends the sacrifice of the altar.* Prot. 1. Antichrist's primary persecution,
or his effectual oppression, is spiritual, not bodily, 2 Thessalonians 2:8–9,
11 and Revelation 17:2, as was also that of his forerunners, 1 John 2:18 and
4:3. 2. So well known to everyone is even that external persecution by which
the Roman Pontiff for

[1638 Amsterdam witness | e-rara canvas 194 | Augsburg canvas 208 | printed p. 184 | ZB Zürich Rp 510]

several centuries raged against the saints that Baronius was not ashamed to
profess in his exhortation that this was an office commanded by God to the
Pope, Acts 10:13: *Rise, Peter, kill and eat.* But when at other times he
denies that he does this, he thereby shows himself to be the Babylonian Harlot
no less than by the deed itself, because *she eats and afterward wipes her
mouth, saying, I have done no iniquity*, Proverbs 30:20. 3. The Papists profess
and make plain that the public profession of truth was long ago taken away by
the Pope in some manner, when they contend that our Church—we who retain the
profession handed down by the Apostles—was invisible for many centuries. 4.
That sacrifice of the altar which the Pontiffs defend is a great part of
Antichristianism, since by it another sacrifice and other Priests besides
Christ are instituted in the New Testament, and hence another Christ. There is
therefore no reason for him to fear Antichrist, but Christ when he comes.
Bellarmine, *On the Church Militant*, book 3, chapter 16, denies all this with
shameful contradiction: *Those who understand Daniel's prophecy of the time
of Antichrist are deceived, for Daniel speaks most openly of the cessation of
the Jews' Sacrifice.*

14. Bellarm.: *Antichrist must reign for precisely only three and a half
years.* Prot. *It seems difficult*, Berengandus says in Dionysius the
Carthusian on Revelation 13, *that in so very brief a time Antichrist could
subject all Nations to himself and lead the whole human race, except for a few
elect, to worship him.* He might have said that it is incredible that one
singular person could in so short a time occupy through deception all peoples,
multitudes, nations, and tongues, sit as king over them as over many waters,
and so intoxicate those same peoples with the wine of fornications and
abominations that, casting away all ancestral religion, they worship him as
the one God; while that same singular person in the same interval must build
the greatest temple and sit there as an idol, and at the same time wage many
very great wars and intoxicate himself with the blood of the saints, meanwhile
overflowing with every leisure and luxury. Certainly this invented

[1638 Amsterdam witness | e-rara canvas 195 | Augsburg canvas 209 | printed p. 185 | ZB Zürich Rp 510]

chimaera of Antichrist is undoubtedly the reason why all that is said of
Antichrist seems fabulous to many Papists, as Medina testifies on Thomas, part
3, question 8, article 8.
Daniel 7 and 12 do not treat Antichrist. This duration of three years cannot
be proved from Revelation 12:14, etc., unless it is first proved that “a time”
signifies nothing but one year and “times” precisely two years.

15. Bellar. 6: *Antichrist's coming will be a little before the end of the
world. Immediately after his death the world will end.* Prot. God knows how
long the world will endure after Antichrist's destruction. After his coming
it must necessarily endure a long time, so that that mystery may be completed
in the manner stated before and afterward destroyed, and that its destruction
may be followed by the world's great astonishment together with the Church's
joy, peace, and flourishing condition, Revelation 17, 18, 19, and 20.

16. Bell.: *If Antichrist had come and were the Roman Pontiff, his name
foretold by John in chapter 13, the final verse, would certainly be known. For
all prophecies become perfectly clear when they are fulfilled. But there is
still the greatest controversy about Antichrist's name.* Prot. 1. If all
fulfilled prophecies are perfectly clear, with what consistency does
Bellarmine contend in *On the Word of God*, book 3, chapter 1, that *a great
part of Scripture contains prophecies about future things, and prophecies
written in verse, than which nothing is more difficult, nothing more obscure*?
And so on. 2. The beginning and end of Ezekiel are most obscure and are never
explained in the whole of Scripture. These prophecies have been fulfilled, at
least for the most part. Why, therefore, do they not become perfectly clear?
2. The meaning of this name need not be clearly known by everyone, but by the
wise. *Here is wisdom: let him who is endowed with understanding calculate the
number of the Beast*, Revelation 13:18. 3. Although the things themselves are
clear, the application of a name foretold in some prophecy is nevertheless
not without difficulty, as in Genesis 49:10. This often happens because one
and the same designation can agree with one thing in various respects. 4.
There is no controversy among our people whether the name of Antichrist agrees
with the Roman Pontiff, but in what sense it most suitably agrees. The mark of
Antichrist is to be judged in the same way.

[1638 Amsterdam witness | e-rara canvas 196 | Augsburg canvas 210 | printed p. 186 | ZB Zürich Rp 510]

17. Bellarm.: *Antichrist will come chiefly for the sake of the Jews and will
be received by them as Messiah. He will be born from the nation of the Jews,
from the tribe of Dan, as is probable; will be circumcised; and will observe
the Sabbath. But these things do not agree with the Roman Pontiff.* Prot. No
one should wonder that fictitious marks of this sort do not agree with the
true Antichrist. That these things are mere inventions of vain men is plain
enough. 1. They have no foundation in Scripture. 2. They conflict with the
Scriptures, which teach that Antichrist will be the head of an Apostasy to be
expected after Paul's time, 2 Thessalonians 2. But the Jews had previously
fallen and been cast away, Romans 11. Before the end of the world the Jews
will obtain mercy again, verses 25 and 31 of the same chapter. Would they at
the same moment receive Christ and antichrist? Antichrist must be the seventh
head or King of that great City which has a kingdom over the Kings of the
earth, Revelation 17. But neither that number of Kings nor the description of
such a city agrees in any way with the Jews. 3. It conflicts with reason that
all the peoples of the nations would suddenly subject themselves to the rule
of a Jewish man after such inveterate hatred and contempt. 4. These things
also conflict with other marks of Antichrist handed down by Bellarmine: the
preaching of the Gospel throughout the whole world, the desolation of the
Roman Empire, the abolition of the sacrament of the altar, and so on, have no
relation at all to a Jew occupied chiefly among Jews.

18. Bellarm.: *Antichrist's seat will be in Jerusalem and in Solomon's temple.*
Prot. Once one fable is posited, another more foul one follows. Antichrist's
seat is that great City which has seven mountains, seven Kings, and ten horns;
which in John's time had a kingdom over the kings of the earth; whose five
Kings had then fallen, one was, and a seventh was to come, Revelation 17. Do
these things agree with Jerusalem? Bellarm. 1: *It is that city where our Lord
was crucified.* Prot. 1. Our Lord was not properly crucified in the city of
Jerusalem, but outside the city, Hebrews 13:12. 2. The Lord was crucified by
Roman, not Jerusalemite, authority; and in that action

[1638 Amsterdam witness | e-rara canvas 197 | Augsburg canvas 211 | printed p. 187 | ZB Zürich Rp 510]

Jerusalem may be called a street of Rome. 3. This crucifixion is to be
understood there *spiritually*, just as that city is *spiritually* called
Sodom and Egypt at the same place, concerning Christ in his members. Bellarm.
2: *Rome will be burned at that very time*, Revelation 17:16. Prot. This is not
at that very time when Antichrist will reign and flourish, but when he goes
into destruction. For the same men are said earlier to deliver their strength
to Antichrist and to fight under him for a long time, verses 13 and 14.
Bellarm. 3: *Antichrist will sit in the Temple of God*, 2 Thessalonians 2.
Prot. The Temple of God no more signifies the Jerusalem temple there than in
Ephesians 2:21; 1 Corinthians 3:16–17; 2 Corinthians 6:16; Revelation 3:12 and
7:15; and so on. *No definite temple is signified*, Cajetan says on that
passage. Bellarm. 4: *By the Harlot in Revelation 17 can be understood not
Rome but the entire city of the Devil.* Prot. It cannot, because neither the
seven hills nor the seven Kings, with the distinction and succession declared
there, can be applied to the world of the ungodly. Bellarm. 5: *Rome indeed is
understood, but pagan Rome, not Christian Rome.* Prot. Thus at last Antichrist
is placed by Bellarmine upon two seats, Jerusalem and Rome, which are separated

person, Antichrist. These fables are certainly badly stitched together and
exceedingly monstrous.

19. Bellarm.: *In his doctrine Antichrist 1. Will deny that Jesus is Christ
and therefore attack all the Savior's institutions. 2. Will assert that he is
the true Christ. 3. Will affirm that he is God and will wish to be worshiped
as God. 4. Will say that he alone is God and will attack all other Gods and
idols. But these things do not agree with the Roman Pontiff.* Prot. 1. Some of
these things agree with Antichrist, but not all, because he must have some
external likeness of the Lamb, Revelation 13:11. *Both Antichrist and his
company*, says Dionysius the Carthusian on that passage, *will appear like
Christ through feigned holiness.* He must have some

[1638 Amsterdam witness | e-rara canvas 198 | Augsburg canvas 212 | printed p. 188 | ZB Zürich Rp 510]

likeness to a Prophet, Revelation 16:13; 19:20; and 20:10. He must be the
mother of fornications, that is, of idolatry, Revelation 17:5. Finally, in
Bellarmine's judgment he must observe the sabbath. 2. What Antichrist is said
in the Scriptures to say or deny, he can say and deny in his heart, Psalm
14:1, and in his works, Ezekiel 28:2, while proceeding to pretend the
contrary. Bellarmine in this very chapter 14 teaches that *Antichrist will
secretly worship the Devil while publicly teaching that he alone must be
adored*. Antichrist will therefore secretly do things which are plainly
contrary to those he publicly professes. But it is not credible that the most
cunning impostor, in order to seduce men, would publicly and in open words say
things so horrendous and contrary to the light of nature that scarcely any
other mortal either could have or has attempted to persuade others of similar
things. *He will not say*, Dionysius the Carthusian says, *that he is greater
than God, but he will fear God not at all and will prefer his own will to the
divine in all things, exterminating divine worship and introducing his own.*
3. The Roman Pontiffs, in their own secret way and in very deed, say and deny
all those things which Scripture foretold that Antichrist would say and deny.

20. Bell.: *Antichrist's miracles are principally three. 1. He will pretend to
die and rise again*, Revelation 13:3. *2. He will cause the image of the Beast
to speak*, verse 15 of the same chapter. *3. He will cause fire to descend
from heaven*, verse 13 of the same chapter. *But the Roman Pontiff has done
none of these.* Prot. 1. That first claim about feigning death is invented by
the Papists; it is not contained in the text. Nor is the second described as
a sort of miracle, but as the restoration or renewed representation of a
fallen power. The sending down of fire from heaven is also presented more as
an example of the great signs Antichrist will produce than as some singular
thing to be done by him. There is therefore nothing solid in this entire
argument. 2. The miracles attributed to Antichrist ought not to be ascribed to
only one singular person—for how could one man do these things in the sight of
all the inhabitants of the earth?—but to preachers traveling about in
Antichrist's name, as Dionysius the Carthusian rightly observes on Revelation
13. 3. These very miracles were formerly familiar to Papist preachers

[1638 Amsterdam witness | e-rara canvas 199 | Augsburg canvas 213 | printed p. 189 | ZB Zürich Rp 510]

or wandering Monks. Bellarmine himself relates various examples of the dead
raised by saints in *On Purgatory*, book 2, chapter 8. That this is not rarely
done among the Indians by Jesuits is witnessed by their histories and Indian
letters. It is well known that images among the Papists have often spoken. Not
only in the engraved life of Thomas, but also in the frontispieces of their
books, together with an image of Thomas praying before the crucifix, they put
the voice of that image addressing Thomas thus: *You have written well of me,
Thomas.* Bernardus de Parentinis relates the history of this matter in *The
Lily of the Mass*. For a statue of the blessed Virgin, forgetful of its own
sex, as Bernard observed, often spoke in a Church; some monks at Bern also
rightly paid the penalty for that fraud in 1509, Peucer, *Chronicle*, book 5.
As for fire sent down from heaven, John Linturius relates in the appendix to
the *Collection of Treatises on Indulgences*, chapter 1, that this has happened
quite often. Lelius Zechius also contends that *just as the Pope gives a waxen
lamb blessed by him the power to ward off God's scourges, so he can also give
it this force, that by it he may calm storms and such things, because the
Pope's power extends as far as the Church's need requires; but the Church's
need may require it. Therefore.* Can he who can give a waxen lamb power over
the atmospheric heaven not also cause fire to descend from that same heaven?
4. Many other miracles of the same kind are either regularly performed or
invented by the Papists: in visions of souls telling tales about purgatory and
asking that masses be celebrated for them; in diseases healed because of the
veneration of images; and in many marvels granted to those who vowed something
to saints.

Bell.: *These kinds of miracles were customary in the Church before the time
when the adversaries say Antichrist appeared.* Prot. 1. They were not customary
before, although they were done once or twice. 2. Antichrist existed before he
appeared.

kingdoms of Egypt, Libya, and Ethiopia. 3. Afterward will subdue seven other
Kings and in this way become Monarch*

# Calvin, *Response to the Questions of Giorgio Biandrata* (1558)

This directory presents the complete manuscript-based Latin text and a new
English translation of John Calvin's response to questions raised by Giorgio
Biandrata concerning the Trinity, the deity of Christ, divine names, prayer,
and the relation of person, essence, and nature.

> **Editorial status:** This is an AI-assisted draft transcription and
> translation checked word by word against high-resolution facsimile images,
> not a peer-reviewed critical edition. A qualified Neo-Latinist should
> independently collate the text before it is used for formal publication or
> as the sole basis for a disputed historical or theological interpretation.

## Work identity and the missing questions

The CO editors date the work to 1558, following Colladon and Beza. Their
chronology is strengthened by evidence that Biandrata had left Geneva for
Zürich by approximately the end of May 1558 and was already in Poland by
December. CO rejects the unsupported date 1559 sometimes repeated after Paul
Henry. Since Calvin supplies no date in the response, “1558” remains an
externally established work date rather than an internal subscription.

The questions to which Calvin responded do not survive in an identifiable
form. CO discusses a sheet in Biandrata's hand at BGE Cod. 113, fol. 81, whose
reverse Calvin labeled *Servetica deliria Georgii*, but expressly concludes
that the printed response does not answer that sheet. Nor should this work be
identified with the written articles that, according to Colladon's account,
summarized Calvin's and Biandrata's discussions and were signed by Biandrata.
CO observes that the surviving response does not take the form of a
confession; it is a theologian's argumentative essay. These are distinct work
identities unless new manuscript evidence establishes otherwise.

## Witness and publication identity

The work was never issued separately in Calvin's lifetime. CO reports an
authoritative scribal copy in Charles de Jonvilliers's hand at BGE Cod. 145,
fol. 73, and states that this manuscript was the basis of its main text. An
apograph is not Calvin's autograph: the work is Calvin's, the surviving
witness is Jonvilliers's copy, and the 1870 printed text is the CO editors'
edition of that witness.

Beza later printed the response in the collected *Tractatus* of 1576,
pp. 936–939. CO also lists the 1597 *Tractatus*, pp. 787–790; the Stoer
edition, pp. 679–681; and the Amsterdam edition, pp. 585–587. The CO editors
say that Beza altered the short work fairly often, so they print Beza's
readings—which were also retained in the Amsterdam edition—as variants rather
than silently allowing the printed tradition to replace the manuscript. CO
also states that the work had never been translated into French by the time
of its edition.

`latin.txt` consequently transcribes CO's manuscript-based main text. It is
not a fresh edition of Cod. 145, an eclectic reconstruction from the later
prints, or a transcription of one of Beza's editions.

## Authorial and editorial matter

CO IX 321–332 contains several layers that must not be conflated:

- Pp. 321–322 are a nineteenth-century CO title leaf and pp. 323–324 are
  blank. The display title is recorded as bibliographical metadata, not
  counted as a sentence of Calvin's response.
- The heading repeated above the text on p. 325 is likewise recorded through
  the document title but not duplicated inside the authorial body.
- Calvin's complete continuous prose begins *Quum profitemur* on p. 325 and
  ends *rixandi et calumniandi* on p. 332.
- CO's first footnote identifies Cod. 145, fol. 73 as the source and explains
  that Beza/Amsterdam readings are reported in the margin. This source note is
  editorial, not part of Calvin's response.
- All numbered and starred apparatus notes are excluded from the Latin and
  English body. Their readings are inventoried below.
- Running heads, printed page numbers, gathering marks, rules, and other page
  furniture are excluded.
- Parenthetical Scripture locators printed in CO's main text are retained.
  Without a direct inspection of Cod. 145, this edition cannot determine
  whether every such locator originated with Calvin, Jonvilliers, or an early
  editor. They are therefore not silently deleted or reclassified as
  footnotes.

## Transcription and collation

The PDF text layer and separately generated OCR were used only as finding and
provisional transcription aids. Every word and punctuation decision in the
Latin body was then checked against the native 300-DPI images of UNIGE PDF
pages 242–245. The normalized Latin follows the repository policy:

- prose is reflowed and divisions caused only by the printer's line endings
  are joined;
- CO spelling and punctuation are retained, including *quum*, *Caeterum*,
  *Iehovah*, *loquutus*, *autor*, *loquutiones*, and *hypostaseis*;
- footnote cues are removed along with the excluded apparatus, without
  removing the main reading to which each cue refers;
- each numbered CO page receives an exact visible marker; and
- no illegible or conjecturally supplied word remains.

The page turns were checked especially closely. P. 325 ends *esse* and p. 326
begins *Nomen*; p. 326 ends *ducat* and p. 327 begins *Hoc*; p. 327 ends with
the Romans 9:5 citation and p. 328 begins *Hinc*; p. 328 ends *quam* and p. 329
begins *pater*; p. 329 ends *inter eas* and p. 330 begins *nulla*; p. 330 ends
*si quis* and p. 331 begins *sincere*. The last physical turn divides
*commu-nis*. Following the repository's normalization rule, the p. 332 marker
is placed before the complete joined word *communis*.

Two transmitted cruxes are preserved rather than silently repaired. On
p. 327 Calvin's main text cites chapter 10 of First Corinthians, while CO's
note correctly directs the reader to 1 Corinthians 8:5–6. On p. 330 CO prints
*et* in the main text and reports the manuscript corrector's *ei*. Both are
made visible in the translator's notes. Neither leaves an unidentified or
illegible reading in the transcription.

## Translation provenance and the modern English version

The English was translated afresh from `latin.txt`, with the facsimile
rechecked whenever syntax, page division, or technical vocabulary affected
the sense. No published English translation was copied, revised, paraphrased,
or used as a base.

A modern English translation does exist: Joseph N. Tylenda, “The Warning That
Went Unheeded: John Calvin on Giorgio Biandrata,” *Calvin Theological Journal*
12, no. 1 (April 1977), 24–62. The journal's
[official bibliographic record](https://digitalcommons.calvin.edu/ctj/23/)
identifies the article. That modern translation is copyrighted and therefore
does not satisfy this project's criterion of a public-domain English version.
No wording from Tylenda's translation was consulted or used in preparing this
edition; only its bibliographic existence is documented here.

The translation keeps Calvin's technical distinctions visible rather than
smoothing them into a generic Trinitarian paraphrase. In particular,
*persona*, *hypostasis*, *essentia*, *substantia*, *substantivum*, and
*hypostaticum* remain terminologically related in English. The phrase
*indefinite* is rendered “without personal qualification” where Calvin means
that the name *God* is not being set relationally over against another divine
person. Scripture quotations follow Calvin's Latin and may therefore differ
from familiar English Bible versions.

## Files and completeness

- [`latin.txt`](latin.txt) contains the complete normalized CO main text at
  pp. 325–332, with all eight individual page markers.
- [`english.md`](english.md) contains a complete new English translation with
  the identical locator sequence and translator's notes for the principal
  textual and terminological cruxes.

The complete authorial inventory is one continuous response beginning *Quum
profitemur* and ending *rixandi et calumniandi*. There is no surviving prompt
attached to it, no authorial salutation or title, and no closing signature or
date. The CO title leaf, blank leaf, source note, and twenty-five variant or
corrective notices are all accounted for above. Nothing in the CO main body
at pp. 325–332 has been silently omitted.

[Book I, printed p. 66; BSB canvas 00092]

By the same reasoning, before Navarrete published his books against the Jesuits, if someone had
said, “Navarrete’s books against the Jesuits have not been published,” then in order to know that
he was speaking truly he would have needed all these many qualifications: namely, Navarrete has not
yet published them so far as depends on himself, according to his proper mode of existing, and
according to his proper measure. Yet this would not prevent him from having published those books
even then on the side of eternity, according to its mode of existing and its measure. Thus his
books were fit to terminate God’s intuitive knowledge, not in relation to their proper measure, but
in relation to the measure of eternity.

Moreover, when Antichrist exists, his existence can be affirmed only with the same qualifications:
namely, on his own part, according to his proper mode of existing, and according to his proper
measure. Yet even then he will not be fit to terminate God’s intuitive knowledge insofar as he is
on his own part and according to his proper mode of existing and proper measure. For just as
nonexistent things do not terminate God’s intuitive knowledge unless they are drawn by a higher
power to a certain more excellent mode of being, it appears that not even existing things,
according to their proper mode and measure, are sufficiently fit to terminate God’s intuitive
knowledge unless they too are drawn by a higher power to a certain more excellent mode of being.
That more excellent mode of being seems to be spoken of by comparison with a thing’s proper mode
of existing, for the grammatical force of the expression plainly makes it comparative. Nothing is
available for comparison except either the things’ proper mode of existing or their mode of not
existing.

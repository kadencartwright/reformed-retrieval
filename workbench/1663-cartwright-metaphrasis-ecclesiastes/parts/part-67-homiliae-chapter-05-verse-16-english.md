VERSE 16. Therefore this too is an evil that brings grief: entirely as he came,
so he goes away. What profit is there to him because he labored for the wind?

Solomon further intensifies the grief and anguish of mind arising from this
calamity by its efficient cause: although the man sweated, suffered cold,
wearied himself with hard labor, rose early, lay down late, and passed sleepless
nights, he made no progress at all. For love and pursuit of money drives a
person to expose himself to every labor and to lower himself to all the vilest
tasks. The emptiness and frustration of this labor is illustrated by a
comparison with equal things. The goods heaped and amassed by the miser are
compared with feathers, atoms, or things of the lightest weight, which in God's
judgment against the miser are carried off as if by a

violent wind and tempest. Reflection upon his labor, weariness, and uneasiness
of both mind and body, with which he exhausted himself, combined with thought
of the emptiness and uselessness that followed it, pierces his soul and
wretchedly wounds it. Let this rouse us to refuse no care or anxiety, no labor,
no vigils or late-night studies, in order to acquire those goods which, once
obtained, can never be lost and whose acquisition, whatever the labor or
danger, can never cause us regret.

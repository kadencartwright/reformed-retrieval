## The book and its composite authorship

The title page attributes the inception of the four books to Hugh Sanford
(d. 1607) and says that Robert Parker brought them “to the finishing point”
(*ad umbilicum perducti*). Parker's own address *Candido lectori* (Google Books
PP7–PP10) makes the relationship more exact and rules out a simple page-level
join. Sanford entrusted his papers to Parker and repeatedly asked him to finish
the work. Parker says that he gathered Sanford's scattered notes, put confused
material in order, and expanded terse annotations. He then identifies most
material drawn from Scripture and the Fathers as his own, while assigning to
Sanford the material drawn from languages and the external disciplines,
including history, poetry, and philosophy.

Authorship is therefore interwoven by kind of material, with Parker also acting
as arranger, completer, and editor throughout. No numbered page or section can
honestly be labeled an exclusive Sanford/Parker boundary on the evidence of
this edition. The paired files credit both men and preserve Parker's disclosed
editorial boundary instead of inventing a sequential one.

## Controlling public-domain witness and audit

The controlling witness is the 1611 Amsterdam edition printed in the house of
Aegidius Thorpius, digitized in full view by Google Books as
[`zcxjAAAAcAAJ`](https://books.google.com/books?id=zcxjAAAAcAAJ&printsec=frontcover).
The book is public domain by date. Image files, not Google's automated OCR,
control every reading.

The reader exposes 697 ordered images. Its sequence agrees with the physical
formula in the Folger catalogue—four separately paginated books of 51, 165,
249, and 213 pages, with preliminary and terminal matter—with these platform
peculiarities:

- PP1–PP2 are Google/cover matter; PP3 is the title page; PP4–PP10 contain the
  remaining preliminaries, including Parker's address on PP7–PP10.
- Book I printed pp. 1–49 are PA1–PA49; printed p. 50 is PT1; printed p. 51 and
  its errata are RA1-PP1; RA1-PP2 is blank.
- Book II is RA1-PA1–RA1-PA165, followed by RA1-PT1.
- Book III uses RA2-PP1–RA2-PP2, RA2-PA1–RA2-PA249, and RA2-PT1.
- Book IV is RA3-PA1–RA3-PA213, followed by RA3-PT1–RA3-PT3.

The apparent jump after Book I p. 49 is thus a Google page-ID transition, not
a missing leaf: PT1 and RA1-PP1 preserve pp. 50–51 and the end of the book.
No missing or duplicated textual leaf was found in the 697-image reader
sequence. The title page visibly prints 1611 (automated OCR sometimes reports
1614).

## Part 01 boundary and method

Part 01 contains Book I §§1–12, a coherent opening inquiry into the names
*Sheol* and *Hades* and the proposed derivation of Greek divine names from
Hebrew. It begins with the Book I title and opening paragraph on printed p. 1
(PA1) and ends on printed p. 19 (PA19) with §12's complete sentence
`erit credo operæ pretium, si, in reliquis brevitati consulentes, hic saltem
minutius singula persequamur.` Section 13, beginning `Evoi igitur...` lower on
the same page, is excluded. The unit therefore spans nineteen printed pages
without breaking a numbered section.

The Latin is diplomatic-normalized: long *s*, ligatures, line-end divisions,
and typographic `u/v` are normalized; spelling, wording, numbering,
transliterated Greek, Hebrew letters, and substantive marginal references are
retained. Catchwords, running heads, signatures, ornaments, and bare page
numbers are omitted. The English is a fresh close translation from this
transcription. Parker and Sanford's speculative etymologies are translated as
arguments made by the text, not silently corrected into modern linguistics.

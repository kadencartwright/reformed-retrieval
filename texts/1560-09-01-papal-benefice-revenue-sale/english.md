# Response concerning the Revenue and Sale Price of a Papal Benefice

This is a new English translation made directly from the normalized Latin in
[`latin.txt`](latin.txt). The printed-page markers correspond to the 1871
*Calvini Opera* witness.

**[CO X/1, p. 252]**

If advice had to be given while the matter was still untouched, I would judge
it unlawful,

**[CO X/1, p. 253]**

under any pretext however plausible, to possess any priestly benefice in the
papacy, since not a single penny can
lawfully be collected from it that is not stained by sacrilege. But if someone
through ignorance and error has consumed the revenues collected from it, I do
not see why he should labor anxiously over restitution. I distinguish
maintenance from treasures laid up. For if anyone has enriched himself from
that money, he is not clean from the accursed thing until he restores what he
unjustly retains. Thus, just as Moses left Pharaoh's court and its luxuries
with a tranquil conscience because he had not sought that kind of life of his
own accord, so today anyone who renounces those accursed riches by which he
supported himself for a time can thereafter obtain his livelihood in a lawful
way with a quiet mind.

As for the sale price, the bargain was undoubtedly wicked. But since the buyer
is dead, nothing remains except that the person who unjustly received the
money

**[CO X/1, p. 254]**

endeavor, so far as means suffice and opportunity is given, to transfer to
another the gain by which he contaminated himself. Therefore, according to
his judgment, let him distribute whatever he still retains to such people and
at such times as seem appropriate, lest he be made richer from another's
property.

The young man who took a wife without his parents' knowledge should recognize,
if his wife is disobedient, that he is paying the just penalty for his
fickleness. Let him not be surprised that, because he did not render God and
his parents the obedience he owed, a just recompense is paid back to him in
his wife's obstinacy. Since disagreement in religion does not dissolve a
marriage, let him perform the duty of a husband for as long as he can live
with his wife without danger. But if greater force and necessity compel him
to depart from her, let him remain celibate until either his wife comes to her
senses or she supplies a cause for divorce. 1 September 1560.

## Translation notes

1. *Sacerdotia* denotes papal ecclesiastical offices or benefices with their
   revenues, not simply the abstract state of priesthood.
2. *Fructus* is the income or proceeds of the benefice. Calvin distinguishes
   money already consumed for maintenance from capital still stored or used
   for enrichment.
3. *Anathema* evokes something devoted to destruction or under a curse. “The
   accursed thing” retains that biblical and moral force without identifying a
   specific legal category.
4. The body unexpectedly adds a separate matrimonial answer after the response
   matching CO's title. Both stand before the single closing date and are
   retained as one transmitted document rather than silently separated.

VERSE 4. For it comes in vanity and walks in darkness, and its name is covered
in darkness.

VERSE 5. This one has neither seen the Sun nor experienced it; its condition
is more tolerable than the other's.

First Solomon shows the respects in which the miser agrees with the stillborn
child: his birth was vain, for he attained no more of the good things for
whose sake people are brought into the light than the stillborn child attained
the end of its conception, namely, that it should be brought into the light.
Then each departs without the honor of burial and is covered in darkness, so
that henceforth neither one is mentioned with praise.

Solomon next teaches how the stillborn child surpasses this miser: greater
rest belongs to the stillborn child, since it begins and ends its misery all
at once, while the miser wastes away for a long time in labors and
afflictions. The stillborn child never experienced good, but neither did it
ever desire those things, because it does not know them. The other, pursuing
them as a good with great labor and pain, fails to attain it.

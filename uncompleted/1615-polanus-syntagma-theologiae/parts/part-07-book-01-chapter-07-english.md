CHAPTER SEVEN

In Which Christ’s Theology According to His Humanity Is Treated

ECTYPAL THEOLOGY, considered as it exists in rational creatures, is either
Christ’s, as head of God’s Church according to his humanity, or that of
Christ’s members.

Christ’s theology as head according to his humanity is the entire wisdom of
divine things communicated through the personal union to Christ’s humanity,
which is united to the God-man (*θεανθρώπῳ*), and through the Holy Spirit
poured out upon it without measure. It serves both for his everlasting
blessedness and for the illumination of all who are joined to Christ as their
head.

This is also called the theology of union, because Christ’s humanity has it
from the hypostatic union with the divine nature. Of this we read in John
1:16: “And from his fullness we have all received, and grace for grace.” And
chapter 3:11–13: “Amen, amen, I tell you: what we know we speak, and what we
have seen we testify, but you do not receive our testimony. If I have told you
earthly things and you do not believe, how will you believe if I tell you
heavenly things? No one ascends into heaven except him who descended from
heaven, namely, the Son of Man who is in heaven.” And verse 32 and those that
follow: “What he has seen and heard, this he testifies, but no one receives
his testimony. He who receives his testimony has sealed that God is truthful.
For the one whom God has sent speaks God’s words, since God does not measure
out the Spirit to him. The Father loves the Son and has entrusted all things
into his hand. He who believes in the Son has eternal life, but he who does
not assent to the Son will not see life; rather, God’s wrath remains upon
him.” Colossians 2:3: “In whom all the treasures of wisdom and knowledge are
hidden.”

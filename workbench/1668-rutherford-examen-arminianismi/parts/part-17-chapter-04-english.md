CHAPTER FOUR

ON REPROBATION

It is asked whether reprobation is temporal and not an eternal act in God. The
Remonstrants affirm it in the *Apology*, because Paul says that the Jews will
again be shown mercy if they do not remain in unbelief. We deny it.

1. For the text does not say that those who were reprobated were numerically
the same human beings from that nation who were to be saved and again shown
mercy.

2. For those who were hardened and did not obtain the promises are opposed to
the election—that is, to those elected from eternity who obtained the
promises.

3. For Esau was reprobated before he was born and had done anything good or
evil, Romans 9:11.

4. For the ungodly are rejected from efficacious grace and from glory in time.
Therefore, they were reprobated from eternity.

5. For there is no new volition in God in time which was not in him from
eternity; for in that way he would be changed.

It is asked whether God reprobated human beings absolutely from eternity—that
is, without any regard to original sin, actual sin, or final impenitence

[1668 KBNL/UvA witness | logical printed p. 277 | PDF p. 328]

as a prior cause or condition—and thus from his free and independent choice
alone. The Remonstrants, article 1, page 230, affirm it, saying that sin is the
meritorious cause of reprobation. We deny it.

1. For Scripture says in John 10:26, “You do not believe because you are not
of my sheep”; 1 John 2:19, “If they had been of us, they would certainly have
remained with us.” According to the Remonstrants, the contrary ought to be
said.

2. For Esau was reprobated and hated by God before he was born and had done
anything good or evil, Romans 9:11.

3. A potter has power to make from the same clay a vessel for dishonor and a
vessel for honor. Therefore, according to Paul's testimony, the same right
belongs to God over human beings by a superior right.

4. For God hardens whom he wills; he raises up Pharaoh so that the glory of
justice and power might be displayed in him. Therefore, he has mercy upon whom
he wills and hardens whom he wills, Romans 9:17–18.

5. For God denies Judas the efficacious grace of believing which he gives to
Peter, not from the merit of free choice but because he absolutely wills it.
Therefore, he decreed from eternity to deny it from his good pleasure alone.

6. For God can convert and save all if indeed he wills. Therefore, it is from
God's absolute good pleasure that some are not converted but perish.

7. If a created will freely determining itself

[1668 KBNL/UvA witness | logical printed p. 278 | PDF p. 329]

were the cause and maker of human reprobation, the elect would have something
in which to boast in their free choice, not in God's grace, because this one
would be reprobated from eternity because he wills, while that one would be
elect because he wills.

8. It would be in the power of free choice for human beings to pass from the
number of the reprobate to the number of the elect. The determinate number of
the elect and reprobate would not be within God's power but within human
beings' power.

9. For it would be within created free choice whether the glory of punitive
justice was displayed or not. And if it had seemed good to human beings, it
could have happened that this glory remained hidden forever.

10. For a human being would distinguish and separate himself by free choice
from others who freely make themselves objects of eternal reprobation.

11. For here there would be nothing of mystery, as Paul says in Romans 11:33,
nor any place for this Pauline response to the objection in Romans 9:20: “Who
are you, O human being, who answers God in opposition?” For there is no place
for the objections of carnal human beings in Romans 9:19 if God reprobates
human beings because human beings first reprobated God through their own final
impenitence.

It is asked whether that distinction between negative and privative
non-election—or preterition—and reprobation is empty. The Arminians affirm it,
article 1, pages 235–236. But we acknowledge that preterition, or non-election,
is distinguished from reprobation.

[1668 KBNL/UvA witness | logical printed p. 279 | PDF p. 330]

By the former, God can deny a benefit to a human being who has merited no evil,
while preserving his own justice. By the latter, God decreed from his absolute
good pleasure to create some persons and deny them
efficacious grace in order to display the glory of justice. The prior act is
called “non-election” logically and “preterition” theologically, because by the
very fact that God absolutely elects some persons to eternal glory from his
free will, he necessarily passes over and does not elect all others to glory.
For if election is of all, it is not election.

Objection: Reprobation necessarily follows from non-election. Therefore, God
does the same here as if he were to command someone to live for a thousand
years but meanwhile continue in him the life-preserving power for only ten
days. Response: This same argument is proposed by Paul in the person of carnal
human beings in Romans 9:14. For God can rightly be angry with someone who
preserves the life-preserving power for only ten days and then casts it away
of his own accord, even though God himself withdraws that power from his own
free will.

It is asked whether there is no reprobation of those who have never heard the
gospel, or whether there is another reprobation more hidden and
inexpressible than that of those who are in the visible church. The
Remonstrants affirm it, article 1, page 23. We deny it.

1. For God absolutely reprobated the Edomites, Ammonites, Moabites, and other
nations from the covenant and the means of grace only because he willed, as
may be gathered from Malachi 1:2–4.

[1668 KBNL/UvA witness | logical printed p. 280 | PDF p. 331]

2. For without any regard for worthiness or holiness, God elected the Jews as
his own people and called them to the communion of glory and grace, Ezekiel
16:3, 8; Deuteronomy 7:6–7; 9:4–5; Psalm 147:14, 20. Therefore, he rejected all
other nations from such communion because he absolutely willed.

It is asked whether absolute reprobation is the first cause of the destruction
of by far the greatest part of humanity. The Remonstrants affirm it. We deny
it.

1. For God is not at all the cause of destruction unless he is considered as
Judge beholding sin actually existing. But in reprobation he is not Judge but
absolute Lord. Therefore, God is not the cause at all.

2. For in reprobation God determined only to deny grace which he owes to no
mortal. Therefore, he cannot be either the cause of sin or of destruction
because he reprobates human beings.

It is asked whether God creates and fashions some human beings for destruction.
The Remonstrants deny it. We say that God created the ungodly for the day of
evil—not simply, but for his own glory. And although it appears unjust to
insane reason that God should create a human being for destruction, the sounder
scholastics nevertheless rightly teach that although it appears cruelty to
will to punish someone solely from an intention to punish, it is not cruelty
to do so from an intention of perfecting the good of the universe and
displaying punitive justice.

1. For in Proverbs 16:4, “God made all things for

[1668 KBNL/UvA witness | logical printed p. 281 | PDF p. 332]

himself, even the ungodly for the day of evil.” Romans 11:32: “For God
enclosed all in unbelief so that he might have mercy upon all.” Romans 9:17:
“For this very purpose I raised you up, that I might display my power in you
and that my name might be proclaimed in all the earth.” In Romans 9:22, Paul
calls those predestined to death “vessels of wrath fitted for destruction.” In
1 Peter 2:8, “Certain persons stumble at the word, to which they also were
appointed.” Jude 4: “Certain persons slipped in who long ago were
προγεγραμμένοι εἰς τῦτο τὸ κρίμα, ‘designated beforehand for this
condemnation.’” In 1 Samuel 2:15, “The sons of Eli did not listen to their
father's voice because Jehovah willed to destroy them.”

2. Many are born for afflictions, God having so determined from eternity. Job
5:7; 1 Corinthians 4:9: “God set forth us, the last apostles, as a spectacle,
as those appointed to death.” 1 Thessalonians 3:3: “For you yourselves know
that we are appointed for afflictions.” For if God fashioned some persons for
punishments of 4, neither does it conflict with him to fashion them for
punishments of 10, 20, 100, 1,000, and eternal punishments.

3. For many infants, having merited nothing more grievous than other infants,
were snatched from the breast by the sword, burning alive, and a flood of
waters and perished miserably. Therefore, God fashioned them from eternity for
these calamities. For nothing happens in time which was not decreed by God
from eternity.

It is asked whether creation therefore ceases to be a benefit of God toward
creatures because the reprobate are created for this end: that they may be
vessels of wrath for the display of the glory of punitive justice. Arminius
affirms it in the *Declaration*, page 107. We deny it.

[1668 KBNL/UvA witness | logical printed p. 282 | PDF p. 333]

1. For a benefit does not cease to be a benefit, even if it turns to the evil
of the one to whom it is given, by the fault of the recipient alone, not by the
fault of the giver.

2. For existence is by far better and more excellent than nonexistence,
although incidentally and because of the misery joined to it, it could be
worse.

3. Nor will the adversaries extricate themselves here if they do not cease to
measure the infinite God by human cubits. For no earthly father would confer
upon his child a benefit which he foreknows will turn to his eternal
destruction, when, if he had lacked it, he would have avoided destruction—
especially if, with his paternal right preserved, he could give the grace of
using that benefit in such a way that it would turn to the child's eternal
happiness. Yet such a father here is that Creator of all the reprobate.

It is asked whether God fashions human beings, and especially the reprobate,
for sinning. The Remonstrants deny it simply. We distinguish: by an approving
intention, God predestines no one to sin.

1. For he hates sin.

2. For he forbids and punishes it.

But he predestined human beings to the material acts of sin.

1. For he absolutely decreed all contingent acts, as has been proved.

2. For no acts happen by chance or apart from God's intention.

3. For we devoutly pray that God would efficaciously prevent such acts in us
and in others. But

[1668 KBNL/UvA witness | logical printed p. 283 | PDF p. 334]

efficacious prevention supposes an efficacious decree concerning efficaciously procuring
or preventing such acts.

But by a permissive intention, God creates human beings for sinning—not simply,
but for displaying the glory of punitive justice.

1. For one who intends the end also intends the means. The permission of sin
is such a means here; when that permission is posited, sin is posited.

2. For God freely decreed from eternity to deny the special grace by which,
if it were given, human beings would avoid sin.

3. For God cannot intend glory confusedly and generally, or under a
disjunction—namely, either the glory of justice or the glory of mercy, or a
glory to be manifested to angels and human beings either by bare legislation
and speculatively or by experimental practice. Rather, he must intend this
determinate glory: namely, that of justice in the reprobate and that of mercy
in the elect. Therefore, he necessarily intends to permit sin, which is the
intrinsic foundation of punishment and forgiveness.

It is asked whether God's absolute will is the cause of all things, such that
by the event itself the will of God is known to be his will. The Remonstrants
deny it, article 1, page 250. We assert that God's will is the first and supreme
necessity and the cause of all things, either by effecting or permitting them.

[1668 KBNL/UvA witness | logical printed p. 284 | PDF p. 335]

1. For nothing good or evil is apart from God's intention, because then it
would happen by chance.

2. For all good and all evil that has the character of a useful good for
displaying God's glory, such as sin, is either effectively or permissively
from the first efficient cause and for the ultimate end, Ephesians 1:11;
Revelation 4:11; Romans 11:36; Proverbs 16:4.

3. For there is no being, either positive or privative, which is not in some
way through the will of the first being. For beings come from God not by a
necessity of nature but by God's free will, Revelation 4:11.

4. For neither would thanks have to be given to God, nor prayers poured out to
him, nor would we have to acquiesce patiently in his holy will, if even all
evils of fault, which are often punishments to the saints, did not flow in
some way from God's will.

It is asked whether God needs the service and ministry of sin according to our
doctrine. The Remonstrants affirm it, article 1, page 258. We deny it.

1. For God does not need the display of punitive justice and mercy, since he
is infinitely blessed in himself and needs nothing outside himself.

2. For if he had never created the world, angels, or human beings who sin,
nothing would have been lacking from his αὐταρκεία, “self-sufficiency.”

3. Nevertheless, with such a decree posited concerning the display of the
glory of punitive justice and sparing mercy, it was necessary by hypothetical
necessity that sin should exist.

[1668 KBNL/UvA witness | logical printed p. 285 | PDF p. 336]

It is asked whether, because God entered into the covenant of grace with us,
consisting of precepts, promises, and threats by which God deals with human
beings according to truthfulness, mercy, and justice, God therefore divested
himself of the absolute dominion of disposing of rational creatures as he
wills. The Remonstrants affirm it in the place cited. We deny it.

1. For disparate things do not conflict with each other, although they are
mutually exclusive and one cannot be the other.

2. For Scripture manifestly asserts God's absolute dominion and right over
rational creatures, Romans 9:20–22, and it also says that “the ways of Jehovah
are mercy and truthfulness toward his own,” Psalm 25:10.

3. For the adversaries do not dare to deny inscrutable dominion, because God
could save more than he saves, and yet does not save them, and could increase
the number of the elect and diminish that of the reprobate, and yet does not
increase or diminish it.

It is asked whether according to our doctrine God absolutely willed that human
beings do no more good than they do and omit more evil than they omit. The
Remonstrants affirm it, article 1, page 250.

We say that if the discussion concerns the approving will, it is false; if it
concerns the will of good pleasure and the decretive will, it is true.

1. For the measure of good and evil actions, and also the number of all beings,
is absolutely determined with God.

[1668 KBNL/UvA witness | logical printed p. 286 | PDF p. 337]

2. For there would be no use of prayers or thanksgiving if this many good
actions and this many evil actions—neither more nor fewer—were not within
God's will.

3. For there would be no use of faith unless it had to be believed that,
according to his gracious will, God precisely determines the number of our
enemies and injuries, and also the number of supernatural acts.

It is asked whether God absolutely does not will more persons to be converted
and saved than are converted and saved. The Remonstrants teach that God wills
all to be converted. We deny it.

1. For if God gives efficacious grace to whom he wills without human merit, as
they teach, then by equal reasoning he denies grace to whom he wills without
their demerit. For just as he has mercy upon whom he wills, so with equal
freedom he hardens whom he wills, Romans 9:18.

2. For God is able to save all, John 10:29; Romans 11:23; Jude 24, if indeed
he willed. But he does not save all. Therefore, he absolutely does not will to
save them.

3. For the number of the elect and reprobate, which can neither be increased
nor diminished, was determined within the power of God electing and
reprobating from eternity.

4. For Christ died, prays, intercedes, and reveals himself for this many and
not for more—and indeed

[1668 KBNL/UvA witness | logical printed p. 287 | PDF p. 338]

because he wills, Matthew 11:27; Luke 1:18; Titus 3:6.

5. For according to the adversaries' doctrine—which denies that God determines
the will or can unfailingly produce more free acts than human beings freely
produce—God cannot convert more persons than he converts, nor leave more
unconverted than he leaves unconverted. Therefore, God does not will to
convert more than he converts, because God cannot absolutely will what he
cannot do through omnipotence.

6. For if God willed to convert more than he converts, provided that more
willed to be converted, then God's will would be indeterminate, suspended, and
dependent upon the creature's free will. He would not have absolutely decreed
this many free acts of the creature, neither more nor fewer. Neither would
God's election and love concerning the salvation of the elect be fixed and
immutable, but uncertain and mutable.

It is asked whether the threats and promises of the gospel conflict with the
absolute reprobation of the reprobate. The Remonstrants affirm it. We deny it.

1. For absolute reprobation is entirely consistent with the exercise of free
choice, as is evident in Esau and Pharaoh, Romans 9.

2. For God's absolutely not willing to give salvation does not conflict with
the approving offer of salvation, because God says that he approves the
obedience

[1668 KBNL/UvA witness | logical printed p. 288 | PDF p. 339]

of those who never obey, to this extent: that such obedience would be rewarded if it
existed.

3. Absolute reprobation and the conditional offer of Christ conflict with
each other no more than the peremptory reprobation of those whom God foreknows
will finally resist him calling and the conditional offer of Christ. But the
Arminians teach that these latter two by no means conflict. The reason for the
major premise is that it appears just as foreign to human reason for God to
propose conditional promises and threats to those whom he knows will never
obey, and whom he irrevocably rejects from eternity on the basis of that
foreknowledge, as to propose the same things to those whom he absolutely
rejects.

4. For God determined the non-gift of glory, according to the order of
justice and because Christ was repudiated in the gospel, for those whom he
absolutely reprobated for the display of the glory of justice. But the
absolute intention of the glory of justice does not conflict with the actual
exercise of justice.

5. For the adversaries' reason on account of which they contend that these two
conflict with each other is that, with the absolute decree standing, the will
of the reprobate would not be absolutely indifferent—when everything required
for acting has been posited—to sin finally or not to sin finally. But this
reason is worthless. 1. For it conflicts with God's dominion over all free
acts, which cannot be taken away from God without sacrilege. 2. For it places
God's

[1668 KBNL/UvA witness | logical printed p. 289 | PDF p. 340]

will concerning the display of the glory of justice in the reprobate at a
crossroads, fluctuating between the display of the glory of justice and the
non-display of the glory of justice, according as it pleased the free
creature, not the most free Creator. 3. For it supposes that free conditions
are indifferent in creatures to being or nonbeing, as the jurists teach, and
are not determined from eternity through God's will. This conflicts with
particular providence.

6. For this doctrine supposes that the promises and threats indicate to us
that God's will is conditioned in itself and not on the part of the object, as
if God intended the eternal salvation of the reprobate under a condition that
suspends God's intention in uncertainty and at a crossroads. But we by no
means deny that such a conditioned intention of God conflicts with God's
absolute reprobation. Rather, we reject such an intention as unworthy of God,
because in that way God would intend the salvation of all equally and thus
would intend the display of the glory of justice in no one. This is openly
false, Romans 9:17, 23.

7. The prolongation of Christ's life until his hour came did not conflict with
Christ's flight and his careful effort to avoid his enemies' fury. So too, the
display of the glory of justice, absolutely intended, does not conflict with
the promises and threats proposed by God.

[1668 KBNL/UvA witness | logical printed p. 290 | PDF p. 341]

It is asked whether God decreed the display of punitive justice absolutely or
conditionally from eternity. The Arminians dream here of a conditioned decree
of God. We deny it.

1. For an intention either of the glory of mercy or, if God fell short of
this intention, of the glory of punitive justice is a doubtful and confused
intention under a disjunction, and it conflicts with the infinite perfection
of the divine will. Similarly, it is the greatest imperfection for a shipmaster
first to intend this harbor, or that one if his first wishes fail, according as
it seems good to the impulse and quasi-fortune of the winds.

2. For in this way the glory of punitive justice could have remained hidden
forever if it had so pleased free creatures.

3. For God raised up Pharaoh εἰς αὐτὸ τῶτο, “for this very purpose”—namely,
for this determinate end: “that God's name might be proclaimed in all the
earth,” Romans 9:17.

4. For God would have created the noblest creatures, human beings and angels,
for an ambiguous and uncertain end, although he fashioned all other things
for their own certain and determinate ends, Proverbs 16:4; Revelation 4:11;
Romans 11:36.

Meanwhile, we by no means deny that God's will concerning the reprobate is
conditioned with respect to the object, although it is not conditioned in
itself, since God destined the reprobate from eternity to eternal destruction,
which they nevertheless were not going to incur except through voluntary

[1668 KBNL/UvA witness | logical printed p. 291 | PDF p. 342]

sins and disobedience toward God calling them.

It is asked whether absolute reprobation conflicts with the ministry of the
word and with the command to believe, repent, and pray. The Remonstrants say
that it conflicts. We deny it.

1. For reprobation does not violate the will of the reprobate or necessitate
it by a fatal force.

2. For the adversaries maintain that commands, prayers, and the free use of
such means by which such a danger is avoided have no place unless the will is
absolutely indifferent to acting or not acting. This is plainly false in
Christ praying for the glory which he had with the Father from eternity, John
17, although Christ could by no means lack that glory.

3. For God's absolute intention of displaying the glory of justice does not
conflict with the permission of sin by which the glory of justice is made
manifest. But, as the adversaries admit, the commands to repent and pray are
harmoniously subordinated to the permission of sin.

4. For the commands aim at this: that the reprobate may be rendered
inexcusable. Yet it is not from the eternal intention of God commanding that
they should obey and be saved, although this is an end of the command.

5. For it appears just as foreign to natural reason that God should command
those whom he foreknows will finally reject grace and glory to repent,
believe, and pray for grace and glory, and that he should

[1668 KBNL/UvA witness | logical printed p. 292 | PDF p. 343]

call them in that ineffectual manner in which he knows they will never obey,
as that he should command those to whom God denied grace and glory from
eternity to repent, believe, and so forth.

It is asked whether it conflicts with God's blessedness for him absolutely to
propose an end which he cannot attain without something that is displeasing
and hateful to him. The Remonstrants affirm it, article 1, page 257. We deny
it.

1. For nothing detracts from God's blessedness because he intends an end which
he does not attain without sin, which does not impair his blessedness, and
which he permits to happen while he himself remains holy and immune from every
stain.

2. For sin is indeed opposed to God's free approval. But it is not properly
opposed to God's nature either privatively or contrarily, except
conditionally, insofar as it conflicts with God's immutability and holiness
for him to will as equitable and just such an act as he himself prohibited as
unjust—although before that volition he could have absolutely commanded it as
equitable and just.

It is asked whether, because the power and benefits of Christ's merits were
denied from eternity to the reprobate by absolute reprobation, the reprobate
therefore cannot be charged with ingratitude toward mercy and Christ's merits.
The Remonstrants affirm it, article 1, page 261. We deny it.

1. For ingratitude toward Christ's merits consists in contempt for God's
approval and in the rejection of the offered

[1668 KBNL/UvA witness | logical printed p. 293 | PDF p. 344]

Christ and his benefits, but especially in not believing in the Son of God,
John 15:22: “If I had not come and spoken, they would not have had sin.” But
ingratitude does not consist in human beings' refusing to believe that God
either elected or reprobated them from eternity. Nor do the reprobate sin
because God reprobated them from eternity and did not destine Christ's
benefits for them. For God's eternal volitions fall neither under commands nor
under our free choice.

2. For evangelical ingratitude consists in this: that human beings refuse to
rest trustingly upon Christ but regard his death and merits as worthless, as
the adversaries admit. And yet according to the adversaries' position, these
same human beings who so despise Christ were also irrevocably, peremptorily,
and completely reprobated by God from eternity; and in the same decree that
efficacious calling was denied to them which, if they had possessed it, would
have caused them to believe in Christ and embrace his death and merits with
open arms. For this is a mystery of sound theology: that very many in the
visible church are condemned because they refuse to embrace and receive with
saving faith Jesus Christ, who nevertheless, by God's eternal intention and
decree, was never predestined as an efficacious Mediator and Savior for them.
Thus, therefore, let insane reason be silent.

3. For the adversaries can prove this connection neither from Scripture

[1668 KBNL/UvA witness | logical printed p. 294 | PDF p. 345]

nor by sound reason: “If God absolutely decreed from eternity that Christ and
his death should by no means pertain to the reprobate, then the reprobate are
not ungrateful toward God because they refuse to believe in Christ who died so
that they might obtain eternal life.”

It is asked whether absolute reprobation inverts the gospel, which says, “The
one who believes is to be saved; the one who does not believe is to be
damned,” because this decree says, “The one whom God absolutely wills to be
saved is to be saved; and the one whom God absolutely appointed to death from
his mere good pleasure, without any fault of that person, is to be damned.”
The Remonstrants affirm that the gospel is thus inverted. We deny it.

1. For subordinate things do not conflict, nor does a part of God's Word—
namely, the doctrine of reprobation—conflict with the gospel. For God so
absolutely appointed some persons to death that nevertheless, in second act,
no one incurs death absolutely, but only conditionally, if he does not believe
in the Son of God.

2. For there is no inversion when the order of intention and execution, and
of the end and means, is varied.

3. For a decree by which God appointed some to death whether or not they were
unbelieving or impenitent would indeed invert the gospel. But the decree of
reprobation is not so absolute that it is wholly detached from all the means
which culpably and by demerit procure eternal death.

[1668 KBNL/UvA witness | logical printed p. 295 | PDF p. 346]

It is asked whether, with reprobation posited, it would have been far better
for the reprobate never to have heard the gospel. The Remonstrants affirm it,
article 1, *Synodical Writings*, page 261.

We say that incidentally and because of the abuse of the gospel, it would have
been better for them never to have heard the gospel.

1. For it would have been better for Judas if he had never been born, Matthew
26:24.

2. For the condition of the Sodomites and Tyrians will be more tolerable in
the last judgment than that of impenitent hearers of the gospel, Matthew
10:14–15; 11:24.

3. For it is better to lack the sin of evangelical unbelief than to be guilty
of it, John 15:22.

But in itself and in first act, the proclamation of the gospel is by far the
most excellent benefit of God. It does not cease to be a benefit because it
turns to a far more grievous and sorrowful evil for human beings who abuse
such a benefit. Yet if we wish here to measure God's wisdom by our insane
reason, it may appear not to be a benefit to confer upon a human being that
good which we foreknow will turn to a far more grievous evil for him.

It is asked whether the doctrine of absolute reprobation is an abyss and
precipice of despair. The Remonstrants affirm it. We deny it.

1. For conditioned reprobation cannot be the foundation of solid hope and
consolation, but the support of perpetual fluctuation, by which someone is as

[1668 KBNL/UvA witness | logical printed p. 296 | PDF p. 347 | stop before CAPUT QUINTUM]

certain of his salvation as he is persuaded of the changeableness of his
indifferent choice. Therefore, absolute reprobation cannot be the foundation
of despair.

2. For no one is bound to believe that he was reprobated from eternity except
insofar as he himself finally reprobates and rejects God and Christ in time.

3. For abundant salvation in Christ Jesus is offered to all in the visible
church, notwithstanding this decree.

4. For God does not deny internal grace even to the absolutely reprobate
except when they reject it.

5. For the whole foundation of solid hope and consolation properly and
proximately pertains only to those who believe in Christ. And we profess that
we are unwilling to profess that doctrine of election and reprobation which
offers all mortals the proximate material of solid hope, internal peace, and
living consolation. What have hope, peace, and consolation to do with those
who give themselves over to their own desires and do not believe in Christ?
But the Arminians command all mortals to be of good courage and enjoy peace,
consolation, and Christ, since God willed eternal glory for them from eternity
and Christ died for them all. In this way internal peace and Christian
consolations pertain no less to Indians, Turks, Jews, and even all pagans than
to those who believe in the Son of God.

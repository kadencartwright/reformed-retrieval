## In the Third Colloquy, Which Treated Papist Churches and Images, We Agreed on Both Sides upon the Following

1. First: *Churches are needed in the churches, that is, definite places appointed for sacred assemblies.*

2. Second: *It is sufficient to cleanse Papist churches from idolatry and other things of this kind by which they have been profaned.*

3. Third: *The use of painting and sculpture, even for depicting sacred histories, is indifferent.*

4. Fourth: *All images painted or sculpted in such an appearance and form that they cannot be viewed by honorable eyes must be abolished; many such things are displayed in Papist churches.*

5. Fifth: *Above all, those whose very appearance incites viewers to idolatry must be abolished.*

6. Sixth: *Private persons may not abolish images and those idolatrous things at their own judgment; this must be done by the authority of godly magistrates.*

7. Seventh: *Above all, care must be taken that idolatry be torn from human hearts by preaching the Word.*

But there was not complete agreement on this matter.

*The reverend Württemberg colloquists judge that decent pictures have some use in sacred places, and that after the things mentioned above have been overturned, some allowance should be made for the weakness of the infirm while they are more fully instructed, provided that meanwhile they are prohibited from idolatrous worship of those pictures.*

*The colloquists of the other party judge it safer, as well as more consistent with God’s Word and the custom of the purer ancient church, to remove from sacred places all images which have either been worshiped or can be drawn into worship; now to imitate Hezekiah’s example when he broke the bronze serpent; and to hold that the danger that superstitious weak persons will persist in idolatry if the images are retained is far greater than the hope of gradually recalling them from their superstition while those images are still preserved.*

## In the Fourth Colloquy, Concerning Baptism, Both Sides Agreed

1. *By Christ’s ordinance, baptism succeeded circumcision in the church.*

2. *Baptism must be conferred upon the infants of Christians according to Christ’s institution.*

But they did not agree on the following matters.

1. *The reverend Württemberg colloquists judge that some hidden power is annexed to baptism’s outward action itself, by which the Holy Spirit confers upon the baptized the grace of washing from sins and regeneration.*

*The colloquists of the other party teach that the outward washing is certainly not empty, since it is the sacrament of the inward washing; but those inward things are the work of divine power alone. The water of baptism has no other use than to represent to the eyes of faith what God’s Spirit alone effects.*

2. *The former teach that all baptized infants become participants in Christ’s grace and are regenerated at the very moment when they are baptized. The latter teach that adoption, or inclusion in the covenant in which they were born, is probably sealed by the Holy Spirit’s power, leaving to God his private judgments, which afterward appear in due time in the baptized when they have grown up, according as the fruits of baptism exhibited to them in infancy manifest themselves or do not manifest themselves. For baptism’s use must not be restricted to the time at which it is administered.*

3. *The former judge that all Christian infants are endowed with faith in actuality through baptism.*

*The latter teach that infants lack at least actual faith, since it comes from hearing. Nevertheless, as persons included in the covenant which Christian parents have embraced by faith for themselves and their posterity, they are pleasing to God; and this adoption, together with the imputation of Christ’s benefits, is sealed in them with the church’s prayers added.*

4. *The former judge that salvation is absolutely annexed to baptism from the words, “Unless one is born again,” etc., and consequently that in a case of the greatest necessity even midwives themselves may baptize infants.*

*The latter teach that not every deprivation of baptism is fatal, but only that which proceeds from contempt. Therefore deprivation of baptism cannot be imputed to infants, but to those through whose fault they departed this life unbaptized. God’s express Word restrains private persons, and much more women, from the office of baptizing just as it restrains them from the public ministry of teaching.*

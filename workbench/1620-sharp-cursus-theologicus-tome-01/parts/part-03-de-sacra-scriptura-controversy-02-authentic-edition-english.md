Second Controversy: The Authentic Edition of Scripture

We and the Roman Catholics agree that the Hebrew edition of the Old Testament
and the Greek edition of the New Testament have never perished. We differ from
the Roman Catholics, however, because they maintain that the Latin Vulgate
edition of the Old and New Testaments alone is authentic, but that the Hebrew
edition of the Old Testament and the Greek edition of the New are corrupt; we
maintain the contrary. Yet the Roman Catholics also disagree among themselves.
For some, such as Bellarmine in book 2, chapter 7 of *On the Word of God*, say
that the Hebrew text of the Old Testament and the Greek text of the New are not
indeed corrupt generally, but that this does not make them so pure a fountain
that whatever differs from them must necessarily be corrected.

[1620 Tome I | col. 26 | BSB frame 00027]

Others, such as Canus in book 2, chapter 13 of *Theological Commonplaces*, say
that the Jews, out of hatred for the Christian faith, depraved and maliciously
corrupted the Hebrew text of the Old Testament. Bellarmine, however, rejects
this opinion as absurd, and it can be refuted by these arguments.

1. Argument. If the Jews corrupted the Hebrew text of the Old Testament, they
did this either before Christ’s advent or after it. If before, Christ would
undoubtedly have rebuked them for so remarkable a crime, since he did not keep
silent about lesser ones. Add that if the text had been corrupted by them, he
would not have commanded the people indiscriminately to search the Scriptures
in John 5:39. But if after Christ’s advent, then they would have erased the
testimonies cited by Christ and the apostles, as well as the chief prophecies
about Christ. But they all remain. Therefore.

2. Argument. The Jews, so widely separated and dispersed, could not have
agreed together to deprave the Hebrew text. Moreover, Augustine testifies in
book 18, chapter 48 of *The City of God*: “The Jews were dispersed for this
reason: so that, by carrying the books about, they might bear testimony to the
Christian faith.” Therefore, and so forth.

3. Argument. The Jews’ extraordinary reverence for the sacred books is
established. The ancients went so far as to count every letter, and those of
more recent times guard the books so superstitiously that if one falls to the
ground, they proclaim a fast.

4. Argument. God always willed to provide for his church in such a way that he
did not allow the words of Moses and the prophets either to perish altogether
or at least to be corrupted in some places, even in the most severe and
especially protracted persecutions. Therefore, it is not likely that the
Hebrew text was corrupted by the Jews.

What the common opinion of the Roman Catholics is, however, is clear from
session 4, canon 2 of the Council of Trent: “Let the old Latin Vulgate edition,”
it says, “be regarded as authentic in public readings, disputations, sermons,
and expositions, so that no one

[1620 Tome I | col. 27 | BSB frame 00028]

may dare or presume to reject it under any pretext.” Hence Melchior Canus, in
book 2, chapter 13 of *Theological Commonplaces*, draws the following four
aphorisms from this decree. The first is that the Vulgate edition is to be
retained by the faithful in all matters pertaining to faith and morals. 2. All
questions of faith and morals are to be decided by it. 3. No appeal is to be
made to the Hebrew and Greek text in disputations. 4. The Latin Vulgate edition
is not to be corrected from Hebrew and Greek codices.

The state of the controversy, therefore, will be this: whether the Latin
Vulgate edition is the authentic Scripture, or rather the Hebrew text of the
Old Testament and the Greek text of the New. We assert by the following
arguments that the Hebrew text of the Old Testament and the Greek text of the
New are authentic, but that the Latin Vulgate translation is by no means so.

1. Argument. If the Vulgate version is authentic, this is either because of
its translator—or at least its corrector, Jerome, as Bellarmine maintains that
Jerome only corrected it in book 2, chapter 9 of *On the Word of God*—or
because of the church’s approval, or because it agrees with the Hebrew and
Greek text. But none of these can be said. Therefore. Not the first, because
not even Jerome himself judged it to be authentic. Instead, he left readers
free to interpret many things in this way or that, concerning which he himself
doubted whether they had been correctly translated. Indeed, in his
commentaries he observes that he translated some things differently from what
the Hebrew truth required and that they should be translated differently
still. Next, if it is authentic because of Jerome, why will not all his
commentaries also be authentic? Finally, even supposing that Jerome were the
author of this vision,[^1] it still should not on that account be received as
authentic Scripture, because divine inspi—

[1620 Tome I | col. 28 | BSB frame 00028]

—ration is the cause of authentic Scripture, and the instrument for
communicating it is a prophet, apostle, or evangelist. But Jerome was not such
a person. Indeed, he himself distinguishes between a prophet and an
interpreter in his preface to the Pentateuch. Not the second, because the
church did not decree this; for the contrary judgment of the impious
conventicle of Trent is not to be received as a decree of the whole church.
And even if the church had decreed it, this version would still not be
authentic on that account, since, as Bellarmine testifies in book 1, chapter
10 of *On the Word of God*, neither the church nor the pope can make a
noncanonical book canonical. Not the third, because if this version is
authentic because it agrees with the Hebrew and Greek text, then the Hebrew
and Greek text would be even more authentic. But we shall soon demonstrate
how splendidly it agrees with them. Therefore.

2. Argument. If, as they say, Jerome translated the Old Testament and
corrected the already-translated New Testament when Pope Damasus asked him to
do so in order that there might be an authentic version, then it would have
been received as authentic in the Latin churches by the command of Damasus.
But it was not received. For in the time of Gregory, who lived in the Latin
church two hundred years after Jerome, it was not regarded as authentic, as
is clear from chapter 5 of Gregory’s preface to Job. Therefore, if it was not
received as authentic then, when it was less corrupt than it is now, it ought
much less to be received now.

3. Argument. Before Jerome’s time either the Hebrew and Greek text was
authentic, or no text was, because that old edition which had become corrupt
before Jerome’s time, as the Roman Catholics concede, was finally corrected
by him. But by the testimony even of the Roman Catholics themselves, the
latter alternative is absurd; it necessarily follows, therefore, that the
former is true. For what they say—that the Hebrew and Greek fountains were
corrupted through the ravages of time—is without any foundation. They were
corrupted either before Jerome’s time, during Jero—

[1620 Tome I | col. 29 | BSB frame 00029]

—me’s time, or after it. But not before, because then there would have been no
authentic text, since even the Latin edition, which itself did indeed exist
before his time, was finally corrected by him, and that according to the
purity of the Hebrew and Greek text. Nor is it likely that they were corrupt
in his own time, since both he and the other Fathers who lived at that time
were sufficiently zealous to preserve the truth. Nor did they finally become
authentic after his time, as though they had previously been corrupt. For it
is not likely that God’s authentic word could become unauthentic in those
languages in which it was originally written, so that it might afterward
become authentic in another language, as if it could have been preserved
better in a foreign language than in its own original language. Next, if God
had permitted the authentic Scripture to perish in the Hebrew and Greek
fountains, he would not have made sufficient provision for the church or for
our faith. But the latter is absurd. Therefore, and so forth.

4. Argument. The Synod of Trent either made this edition authentic or merely
declared it to be so. But they will not say that the synod made it authentic,
because then it would follow that it was not authentic before, and that the
Fathers of that council could in the same way make a noncanonical book
canonical. But they will not say this. If they say that it was only declared
there to be authentic, let them teach when it began to be authentic and from
whom it received that status. But they cannot do this. Therefore.

5. Argument. Bellarmine concedes that one must sometimes have recourse to the
Hebrew and Greek fountains themselves: 1. when scribal errors perhaps occur in
the Latin edition; 2. when there are variant readings; 3. when something in
the words or in the sense is ambiguous; and 4. to understand the force of a
word, because everything is more emphatic in the fountains. Hence we ar—

[1620 Tome I | col. 30 | BSB frame 00029]

—gue as follows. An edition that must be examined according to these
conditions against the Hebrew and Greek edition is not authentic; instead,
the other edition, according to which it is examined, is authentic. But the
Latin Vulgate is such an edition, as the Roman Catholics testify. Therefore.

6. Argument. An edition of the Old Testament that was always authentic before
Christ, and of the New Testament that was authentic without controversy for
six hundred years and more after Christ, is to be acknowledged as such by us.
But such are the Hebrew edition of the Old Testament and the Greek edition of
the New. Therefore.

7. Argument. The Vulgate edition is corrupt in innumerable places, such as
Genesis 1:30; 3:15; 4:13; 6:5; 8:4; 24:22; 27:5; 36:24; and 37:2; Psalms 2:12;
4:3; and 31:4; Esdras 9:8; Ecclesiastes 9:2; Joel 2:13; Matthew 9:13; Romans
1:4; 4:2; and 11:6; Ephesians 5:32; Hebrews 13:16; and so forth. Therefore.

Testimonies

Jerome says in his book *Against Helvidius*: “The water of the fountain must
be believed to remain much purer than that of the streams.” The same author
says in his epistle to Sunnia and Fretela: “Just as in the New Testament we
have recourse to the fountain of the Greek language in which the New
Testament was written, so in the Old Testament we have recourse to the Hebrew
truth.” The same author says in his epistle to Marcella, at the end of tome 2:
“I wished to bring the corruption of the Latin codices back to the Greek
original.” The same author says in his commentary on Zechariah, chapter 8:
“We are compelled to have recourse to the Hebrews and to seek the truth of
knowledge from the fountain rather than from the little streams.”

Augustine says in book 15, chapter 13 of *The City of God*: “One should not
trust a translation so much as the language from which the translation was
made into another language by interpreters.”

Gratian says in distinction 9, chapter *Ut veterum*: “Just as the reliability
of the books of the Old Testament must be examined from the Hebrew volumes,
so the truth of those of the New re—

[1620 Tome I | col. 31 | BSB frame 00030]

—quires the norm of the Greek language.” See more in Augustine, book 2,
chapter 7 of *On Christian Doctrine*;
Ambrose, book 2, chapter 6 of *On the Holy Spirit*; and Jerome’s epistle to
Vitalis.

Objections

Objection 1. An edition that the church used for a thousand years is
authentic. But it used the Latin Vulgate for a thousand years. Therefore.

The proposition is proved: it is not likely that for so many years—at least
from the time of Gregory the Great—the church lacked a genuine translation
and revered some translator’s errors as the word of God. Therefore.

The response is, first, to deny the proposition. For if use for a thousand
years makes an edition authentic, then the Hebrew edition will be much more
authentic, because the church used that text for three thousand years and
more. Next, the Greek, Syriac, and Chaldean versions will likewise be
authentic, for the church similarly used these for a thousand years and more.
3. The antiquity of the church’s error does not remove the error but rather
increases it; so far is it from changing error into authentic truth. 4. The
assumption is denied, because the Greek churches did not use the Latin
edition; and although the Latin church erred in this, the whole church of
Christ did not therefore err. 5. If it had been necessary for the Latin church
to regard the Latin version as authentic, then it would have been
acknowledged as such in the Latin church. But neither Gregory himself nor
anyone after him before the Council of Trent provided by any decree that this
version should be authentic. Therefore. 6. The church can err in the
translation and exposition of certain passages and yet remain the true
church. Meanwhile, that Latin church of which Bellarmine speaks was
antichristian from the time of Gregory; and although it did not retain the
fountains themselves, they nevertheless remained in the true church and still
remain. Therefore.

[1620 Tome I | col. 32 | BSB frame 00030]

Objection 2.

Certain Fathers, such as Augustine, Anselm, and Bernard, praise the Latin
Vulgate edition. Therefore, it is authentic.

Response.

The consequence is denied, because not all writings praised by the Fathers
are authentic. And although this edition is praiseworthy, it is nevertheless
not to be preferred to the fountains themselves, just as the Fathers
themselves did not prefer it, as was proved above.

Objection 3.

The Hebrews had authentic Scripture in the Hebrew language, and the Greeks in
the Greek language—the version of the Seventy Interpreters in the Old
Testament, and the fountains themselves in the New. Therefore, it was
equitable that the Latin church also have it in its own language. But for a
thousand years it had no edition other than the Latin Vulgate. Therefore, it
is authentic.

Response. 1. The consequence is denied, for the Hebrew church had many things
that the Latin church need not have. 2. It is false that the Greek church had
the Old Testament authentically in the Greek language. For if that version of
the Seventy Interpreters was authentic, let them show who made it authentic.
3. If the Latin church ought to have authentic Scripture in its own language,
why not also the French, German, and Italian churches, and so forth? But if so
many editions were authentic and differed from one another, could they all be
authentic, since divine truth is one and simple? Finally, today there is no
Latin church, because the Latin language flourishes nowhere except in the
schools.

Objection 4.

It is possible that in general councils few, or sometimes none, are skilled
in the Greek and Hebrew languages. Therefore, the church would be poorly
provided for if they could not trust the Latin edition. The antecedent is
proved: at the Council of Ariminum there we—

[1620 Tome I | col. 33 | BSB frame 00031]

—re six hundred bishops, and there was not one of them who knew what the word
*homoousion* meant.

Response. 1. No argument from the bishops’ ignorance is valid for taking away
the authority of the fountains of Scripture. Next, the true church ought to
have—and still does have—men who understand these languages, although the
Roman church perhaps has none or few. 3. There were many Greek bishops at that
council who knew the Greek language very well, but many, not rightly
considering the force of the word and suspecting that some evil lay hidden in
it, imprudently rejected it.

Objection 5.

If only the Hebrew edition of the Old Testament and the Greek edition of the
New are authentic, then those unskilled in these languages will be uncertain,
when reading the Scriptures, whether or not they are reading the true
Scripture. But this is absurd. Therefore.

Response. 1.

The church ought to take care that Scripture is translated sincerely and
faithfully; if this has been done, they can be certain. 2. Although those
unskilled in the languages cannot examine whether everything has been
correctly translated, this nevertheless does no harm to their salvation,
provided they are faithful, acknowledge the doctrine, and, taught by the Holy
Spirit, approve it. 3. This argument weighs as much against the Vulgate edition
as against the Hebrew and Greek.

Objection 6.

An edition that is corrupt is not to be regarded as authentic. But the Hebrew
edition of the Old Testament is such. Therefore.

The assumption is proved. In Isaiah 9:6 the Hebrew text has a word meaning “he
will call,” but Calvin contends that it should be read “he will be called.” In
Jeremiah 23:6 there is a verb meaning “he will call,” and the same Calvin
contends that it should be read “they will call.”

[1620 Tome I | col. 34 | BSB frame 00031]

Response. 1.

Whether we read “he will call”—namely, God the Father—or “they will call” or
“he will be called,” the sense is the same. 2. Nor does it follow: Calvin
thinks it should be read differently; therefore, the fountains are corrupt.
For no interpretation can render Scripture unauthentic. 3. In the passage in
Isaiah, the letters admit both readings, although according to the vowel
points they ought to be rendered “he will call.” But the Hebrew doctors teach
that third-person verbs are also used impersonally, so that “they will call”
stands for “he will be called.” 4. In the passage in Jeremiah, Jerome left it
free to read either way, “he will call” or “they will call.” Hence the Roman
Catholics Pagninus and Arias Montanus translated it “they will call.”

Objection 7.

When all the Greek and Latin texts differ from the Hebrew, then the Hebrew is
corrupt. But this occurs in Psalm 22:7. Therefore.

The assumption is proved. In the Hebrew text there is a word meaning “like a
lion,” but it is translated “they dug.” Therefore.

Response. 1.

The proposition is denied, because the agreement of those texts does not take
away the truth of the Hebrew; instead, they are false precisely because they
differ from the Hebrew. 2. All the other texts often differ from the Hebrew,
although even the adversaries themselves confess that the truth is in the
Hebrew text alone. For example, in Genesis 8:4 all the books have “on the
twenty-seventh day of the seventh month,” but the Hebrew has “on the
seventeenth day.” 3. Roman Catholic doctors, as well as the Masoretes, have
testified that “they dug” is read in many copies; Jerome also interpreted it
“they dug.” Finally, the Chaldean paraphrast combined both readings, as though
you were to say, “They dug, or pierced, my hands and feet, as a lion is
accustomed to pierce with its teeth.”

Objection 8.

A passage of the Old Testament that differs from

[1620 Tome I | col. 35 | BSB frame 00032]

Paul’s explanation is corrupt. But the passage in Psalm 19:5 is of this kind,
because in the Psalm the word is “line,” but in Romans 10:18 the apostle said
“sound.” Therefore.

Response. 1. The sense is the same. The apostle, following the Seventy
Interpreters, translated it in this way for the sake of the same sense, since
he himself was also speaking Greek. For whether you say that the line or
writing of the heavens, or their sound, serves magnificently to proclaim the
glory of God, the matter comes to the same thing. Next, the proposition is
true only of the sense—that is, when the sense is distorted—not of the words.
For although the apostles do not always cite passages of the Old Testament
word for word, they nevertheless retain the same sense and explain it in
their citations.

9. Books that do not contain everything found in the Latin and Greek books
are corrupt. But the Hebrew books are such, for in Exodus 2 an entire sentence
is missing, and likewise elsewhere.

Response. 1.

The proposition is denied, because those Greek and Latin editions are not the
authentic rule of truth. Therefore, the argument should instead be reversed:
editions that have either more or less than is contained in the Hebrew text
are not authentic. Thus it does not follow: the Hebrew edition does not have
everything contained in the Greek and Latin editions; therefore, it is
mutilated. For it is just as easy to add as to remove.

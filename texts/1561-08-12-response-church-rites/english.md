# Response concerning Certain Church Rites (12 August 1561)

*A fresh translation from the Latin advice at CO X/1 213–214. The title and
page notices are editorial, not words silently attributed to Calvin.*

[CO X/1, p. 213]

There is none among us who does not recognize that it is most useful to add
to the public confession some notable promise which raises sinners to the
hope of pardon and reconciliation. From the beginning I wished to introduce
this practice. But because some feared that its novelty would give offense, I
was too ready to yield, and so the matter was dropped. Now, however, it would
not be opportune to change anything here, because a large part of the
congregation begins to rise before the end of the confession has been
reached. We therefore wish all the more that, while the choice is still open
to you, your people may become accustomed to both elements.

In administering the Supper I sometimes used Paul's words. But since they
could not be repeated to each person without introducing a longer delay—and
if several people passed by during the recitation itself, scarcely one in ten
grasped what I meant, while no one perceived the complete sentence—I
preferred to discontinue the practice.

We would indeed be especially pleased for the Supper to be celebrated every
month, provided that more frequent use did not produce negligence. For when
the greater part abstains from communion, the church is in some measure
dispersed. Nevertheless, we would rather have the church invited every month
than only four times each year, as is customary among us. When I first came
here, it was distributed only three times a year, in such a way that seven
whole months elapsed between the Supper at Pentecost and the one at Christ's
Nativity. I favored every month; but since I could not persuade them, it
seemed better to pardon the people's weakness than to contend more
stubbornly. I nevertheless arranged for it to be entered in the public acts
that our custom was defective, so that correction might be easier and freer
for posterity.

Many weighty reasons impel me not to think that the Supper should be denied
to the sick. At the same time, I see how easy the descent is into many abuses,
which would have to be met prudently and diligently. For unless there is a
communion, there is an improper departure from Christ's sacred institution.
Some gathering should therefore assemble

[CO X/1, p. 214]

from relatives, household friends, and neighbors, so that distribution may
take place according to Christ's command. The action should then be joined
with an explanation of the mystery, and nothing should differ from the
church's common practice. It is also very dangerous to carry it
indiscriminately here and there. And here it is exceedingly difficult to
prevent some from being driven to request it by superstition, others by
ambition and empty display. Judgment and selection would therefore be needed,
so that it should be given only to those in great danger of death. To bring
bread out of the church as though it were sacred is perverse; to carry it in
procession is in no way tolerable.

Farewell, excellent men and brothers sincerely worthy of honor. May the Lord
protect, govern, and bless you forever. Geneva, 12 August 1561.

## Translator's notes

1. The opening *confessio publica* is the public confession of sins. The
   proposed *promissio* is an assuring promise of pardon and reconciliation;
   the Latin does not reproduce its wording.
2. “Paul's words” are not quoted or identified in this response. The problem
   described is practical: repeating them individually delayed distribution,
   while reciting them once as several communicants passed prevented attentive
   hearing.
3. Calvin's first-person recollection distinguishes his preferred monthly
   celebration from Geneva's actual practice. He reports three annual
   celebrations when he first arrived and four annually at the time of this
   response.
4. *Nisi enim sit communicatio* requires an actual communal action. Calvin's
   qualified permission for communion of the sick therefore requires a small
   gathering, explanation of the sacrament, conformity with common church
   practice, grave danger, and careful selection.
5. The English was translated afresh from `latin.txt` and checked against the
   facsimile. The Beaty–Farley translation was not consulted or used for
   wording.

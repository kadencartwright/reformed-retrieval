ROMANS 6:23.

“The wages of sin is death.”

Since Paul asserts that every sin, taken without qualification, deserves
eternal death—which in this passage is opposed to eternal life—therefore

[1668 KBNL/UvA witness | logical printed p. 516 | PDF p. 567 | split word joined: Quæritur]

it is asked: Should the distinction between mortal and venial sin be admitted?
The Remonstrants affirm it when they say in *Apology*, chapter 11, folio 130,
that to love God with all the heart is to worship the only true God, not idols;
or to be diligent and zealous in observing those commandments which properly
concern God. Nor, they say, is such a perfection of righteousness expressed
here as God's most rigorous judgment, considered mathematically or
arithmetically, requires: an absolute perfection in all numbers and degrees
which precisely excludes blemishes and imperfections.

We say: 1. Every sin in the regenerate is venial accidentally, even though it
should be most grievous, because the regenerate person, truly grieving over it
and repenting, obtains pardon for it in Christ from God's mercy. First John
1:7. Thus Paul's blasphemies and persecutions are venial. First Timothy 1:13.

2. A venial sin is called a sin of infirmity because it is not perpetrated
from malice; and it is venial accidentally because in the same subject it is
joined with more grievous crimes, for which a person obtains pardon in Christ
Jesus. Hence we readily acknowledge degrees of sins. Augustine, *Enchiridion*,
chapter 64, and Epistle 89: “Some sins are called crimes; some are those
without which one does not live here.”

Among the Hebrews there is a certain sin called זָדוֹן, pride or presumption,
from the root זוד, “he acted proudly”; he acted proudly and

[1668 KBNL/UvA witness | logical printed p. 517 | PDF p. 568 | split word joined: arrogantèr]

arrogantly. Distinguished from it is שְׁגָגָה, an inadvertent error or a sin
from ignorance; it is also called מֶרֶד, rebellion, and רֶשַׁע, impiety, when a
person sins with a high hand and an outstretched neck. Numbers 15:22. Hence
Romans 6:13
speaks of ἀδικία, unrighteousness; Ephesians 5:6 of ἀπείθεια, disobedience;
First Timothy 1:9 of ἀσέβεια, ungodliness; and Hebrews 12:3 of ἀποστασία,
apostasy.

As Thomas rightly says in *Summa Theologiae* I–II, question 88, article 1, a
sin is called “mortal” by analogy with a disease, which is called mortal
because it introduces an irreparable defect. But a twofold principle of life,
corresponding to a twofold death, must be considered. One is legal, and in
this respect every sin is deadly in itself, as Gerson says in *On the
Spiritual Life*, lecture 1, at letter A. And this is life through
justification. The other is the principle of life through sanctification; and
in this respect a certain sin is so light that it does not destroy and expel
the seed of God and the new life. In this sense Augustine acknowledges venial
sin, and we do so with him.

But the question is: Under the New Covenant, is there any sin which is venial
by its own nature and in relation to God's Law—from which the formal nature of
sin must be derived—so that, because of the slightness of its cause, manner,
or matter, it is venial and worthy of pardon, and by the rigor of the Law does
not, through its demerit, exclude one from the Kingdom of Heaven or of itself
deserve eternal damnation? The Arminians and Papists affirm it. We deny it.

1. Galatians 3:10: “Cursed is everyone who does not remain in all the things
written in the book of the Law.” But the person who

[1668 KBNL/UvA witness | logical printed p. 518 | PDF p. 569 | split word joined: minimum]

admits the least sin does not remain in all the things written in the book of
the Law, does he? And therefore he lies under the curse of the Law, that is,
eternal death. Ezekiel 18:4: “The soul which sins, it will die.” Romans 6:23:
“The wages of sin is death.” James 2:10: “The one who offends in one thing has
become guilty of all.” For James argues not from the magnitude of the crimes,
as the Jesuits wish, but from the authority of the Lawgiver in verse 11: “The
one who said, ‘You shall not commit adultery,’ also said, ‘You shall not
murder.’” Hence God's infinite authority is equal whether he forbids this sin
or that one, and whether he prohibits the theft of a penny or of a thousand
florins. Matthew 5:19: “Whoever breaks one of these commandments and so
teaches human beings will be called least in the Kingdom of Heaven.” Matthew
12:36–37: “For every idle word a person speaks he will render an account on
the day of judgment.” But when Purgatory is then extinguished and no place of
temporal punishment remains on the day of judgment, human beings must pay
eternal penalties for idle words, which the Papists and Arminians nevertheless
count among venial sins because of the slightness of their matter.

2. Every sin against the love of God is a violation of the Law and deserves
eternal punishment, because it conflicts with the greatest commandment of the
Law, which is violated whenever we do not observe God's commandments. John
14:14; Deuteronomy 10:12. But venial sins are against the love of God.

[1668 KBNL/UvA witness | logical printed p. 519 | PDF p. 570]

3. Every sin against the infinite Majesty deserves eternal punishment. But a
sin which they call venial is such a sin.

2. For under the Law the same victim, representing Christ's one sacrifice,
was offered for sins of ignorance—which they call venial—and for more
grievous crimes. Leviticus 4.5; Hebrews 10:4–5.

It is asked: Is venial sin not against the Law, but only beside the Law? The
Adversaries say yes. We say no.

1. For venial sin is forbidden by God's Law. What prohibits a genus also
prohibits a species. Thus theft in general is against the Eighth Commandment;
therefore the theft of a penny is also against it.

2. Venial sin is against right reason, namely, the Law inscribed on the
heart. Romans 2:15. What conflicts with whiteness insofar as it is a color
conflicts with every color. Now the dictate of right reason is the divine Law,
of which the law of nature is predicated as a genus of a species. But venial
sins conflict with right reason—for example, stealing from one's neighbor
what belongs to him or idly pouring forth speech—as Suárez, Azor, and Durandus
concede, although Bellarmine shamelessly denies it.

3. For every sin is privatively opposed to a morally right act, as a jocular
lie is opposed to the truth which we ought to speak. A word or deed beside
God's Law but not against God's Law is the same as a certain blindness in

[1668 KBNL/UvA witness | logical printed p. 520 | PDF p. 571 | split word joined: animali]

an animal which is beside sight but not against sight, and a certain death
which is beside life but not against life—like half-blindness and half-death,
which are intermediates by participation in each extreme. But the sins they
call venial are not half-virtues and half-vices; nor is there in moral actions
an intermediate by participation in each extreme. Thus the theft of a penny
and an idle word are not some intermediate thing between an act which is
morally good and conformed to God's Law and an evil act which conflicts with
God's Law.

GALATIANS 5:17.

“For the flesh desires against the Spirit, and the Spirit against the flesh;
and these are opposed to one another, so that you do not do whatever things
you wish.”

Hence it necessarily follows that the will and reason in all the regenerate
are such principles of their most excellent actions that, since those
principles are stained with natural concupiscence, all our works, even the
most perfect, must be sprinkled with some stain of sin. Hence

it is asked: Are the most excellent works of the regenerate stained by sins?
The Jesuits and Arminians deny it. They teach that the blemishes and little
stains adhering to the works of the regenerate are not properly sins, or at
least are not such as by the rigor of the divine Law ban human beings from
heaven or render them liable to eternal condemnation. But to fix the state of
the question, we teach as follows.

[1668 KBNL/UvA witness | logical printed p. 521 | PDF p. 572 | split word joined: Merita]

1. Christ's merits have such worth and power that they could raise the good
works of the regenerate to complete purity, conformed to God's Law in every
respect; but we altogether deny that Christ's merits in fact accomplish this.
For Christ's merits do remove all legal guilt from our good works, but not
every physical stain. For although our justification and remission are
consummate and perfect in this life with respect to the removal of guilt, our
sanctification is not perfect with respect to the removal of physical stain.

2. What the Adversaries Becanus, Gregory of Valencia, Bellarmine, and Pererius
say is utterly false: that we teach, 1. that good works are mortal sins; and
2. that the good works of the regenerate are good κατὰ τί—that is, in a
certain respect—but are simply mortal sins. For we teach that the good works
of the regenerate are good: 1. with respect to the substance of the act; 2.
with respect to its formal object; and 3. with respect to all four causes—the
efficient, material, formal, and final causes. Psalms 119:1 and 167; Galatians
5:22; Philippians 2:13; 1 Corinthians 10:31.

3. We teach that the culpable stain adhering to our good works is indeed
removed legally through Christ, so that our works are pleasing to God through
and on account of Christ the Mediator; nevertheless, that stain remains
physically and really and culpably contaminates our works.

[1668 KBNL/UvA witness | logical printed p. 522 | PDF p. 573]

4. Thus the good works of the regenerate are certainly not formally and in
the abstract mortal sins. God forbid! For the Adversaries maliciously impute
this to us. Nevertheless, in the concrete and materially they are stained and
sprinkled with the stain of sin. Or rather, with respect to the substance of
the act and in their causes they are good, and to omit them is a sin; but in
their manner and degree and in certain circumstances—or rather, in the
degrees of circumstances—they are blemished by a culpable defect. Therefore,
these works are blemished, not vices; sinful, not sins; culpable, not faults.
Therefore, the Lord earnestly exhorts us to such works; and we declare that
those who do not perform such works must be cut down like trees bearing no
fruit from the Lord's vineyard. Indeed, we declare these works to be pleasing
to God, acceptable, praiseworthy, and useful. Matthew 5:16; 1 Timothy 6:18;
Matthew 26:10; 1 Peter 2:5; Philippians 4:18; Hebrews 13:6, 16.

Yet we teach that they are not so perfect that they are not contaminated by
culpable blemishes and little stains, so that they need remission in Christ.

1. Ecclesiastes 7:20: “There is no righteous person on earth who does good and
does not sin.” Job 9:3: “If he wished to contend with him, he could not answer
him once in a thousand.” Verse 28: “I recognize that you will not cleanse me.”
Verse 30: “If I wash myself with snowy waters and cleanse my hands with lye,”
verse 31, “then you will plunge me into a pit, and my garments will abhor me.”
Psalm 143:2: “Do not enter into judgment with your servant, because no one
living

[1668 KBNL/UvA witness | logical printed p. 523 | PDF p. 574]

will be righteous before you.” Psalm 130:3: “If you, Lord, should mark
iniquities, who will stand?” But surely, if the works of the regenerate were
free of every sin and truly perfect, why would a human being not be righteous
before God in God's fearsome judgment?

2. It would not be necessary for even the holiest persons to pray, “Forgive us
our sins,” if they could bring forth works so perfect that they lacked every
stain of sin.

3. The same thing is evident from the confession of the saints. Isaiah 64:6:
“But we are all as an unclean person, and all our righteous deeds are like a
most rejected little cloth; and we all flow away like a leaf, and our
iniquities carry us off like the wind.” And this is the confession of the
Church and the godly from verse 15 of chapter 63 through chapter 64, verse 8:
“But now, Jehovah, you are our Father.” It does not speak of ceremonial
righteousness, as the Adversaries wish. 1. For it says, “All our righteous
deeds are like a menstruous cloth.” 2. For it adds in verse 7, “There is no one
who calls upon your name, stirring himself to lay hold of you.”

4. For the flesh always struggles against the spirit in the holiest persons.
Romans 7:18; Galatians 5:17. Concupiscence dwells in everyone. Hebrews 12:1;
John 1:8. Hence, just as a person whose leg is dislocated cannot walk upright
but limps, so it is impossible for the regenerate, in whom this principle of
moral dislocation inheres, to perform all their works from

[1668 KBNL/UvA witness | logical printed p. 524 | PDF p. 575 | split word joined: dilectione]

the most intense love of God, with sincerity in the end of their actions that
is in every respect the greatest, and with the fervor required by God's Law in
every manner of acting.

5. For human beings would be perfect in this life and conformed to the Law in
every respect if they could perform good works stained by no blemish of sin;
but the Apostle denies this of himself. Philippians 3:18.

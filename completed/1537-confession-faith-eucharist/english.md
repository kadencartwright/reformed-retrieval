# Confession of Faith concerning the Eucharist

1537

This is a fresh English translation of the complete joint confession and
endorsement printed in *Ioannis Calvini opera quae supersunt omnia*, vol. IX
(Corpus Reformatorum XXXVII), pp. 711–712. The confession is associated with
Guillaume Farel, John Calvin, and Pierre Viret; Martin Bucer wrote the
endorsement, which Wolfgang Capito subscribed. The CO heading is editorial and
is translated here only to identify the document.

## Translation

### [CO IX, p. 711]

We confess that the spiritual life Christ bestows upon us consists not only
in this, that he gives us life by his Spirit, but also in this, that by the
power of his Spirit he makes us participants in his life-giving flesh, so
that by this participation we may be nourished unto eternal life. Therefore,
when we speak of the communion believers have with Christ, we understand that
they share no less in his flesh and blood than in his Spirit, so that in this
way they may possess the whole Christ.

For since Scripture testifies plainly that Christ's flesh is truly food for
us and his blood truly drink, it is certain that we must be nourished by them
if we seek life in Christ. Nor does the apostle teach anything small or
ordinary when he declares that we are flesh of Christ's flesh and bones from
his bones. Rather, he thereby signifies the extraordinary mystery of our
communion with Christ's body—a mystery that no words can explain adequately
according to its worth.

Yet none of these things is contradicted by the fact that our Lord, having
been raised into heaven, has withdrawn from us the local presence of his body,
which is by no means required here. For although, while sojourning in this
mortal life, we are not enclosed or contained with him in the same place, the
efficacy of his Spirit is nevertheless limited by no boundaries. It can truly
join and gather into one the things that are separated by distances of place.

### [CO IX, p. 712]

We therefore acknowledge that his Spirit is the bond of our participation in
him, yet in such a way that by the substance of the Lord's flesh and blood the
Spirit truly nourishes us unto immortality and gives us life through
participation in them. Christ offers and presents this communion in his flesh
and blood under the symbols of bread and wine in his most holy Supper to all
who celebrate it rightly according to his lawful institution.

We embrace as orthodox this judgment of our excellent brothers and fellow
ministers Guillaume Farel, John Calvin, and Pierre Viret. I have never thought
that the Lord Christ, present in the holy Supper, is locally present or
diffused everywhere. Rather, he has a finite body and remains in heavenly
glory. Nevertheless, he is present in the Supper through his word and symbols.
Here he presents himself to us when we have already been lifted by faith with
him into the heavenly places, so that the bread we break and the cup through
which we proclaim Christ are truly a communion in his body and blood.

Moreover, we acknowledge as an error not to be tolerated in the church the
belief that Christ establishes bare and empty symbols in his holy Supper, and
the refusal to believe that the Lord's own body and blood are received here;
that is, the Lord himself, true God and man.

Martin Bucer wrote this in his own hand.

Wolfgang Capito subscribed it.

## Translator's notes

1. *Vivifica caro* is “life-giving flesh.” The confession attributes the bond
   of participation to the Holy Spirit while insisting that believers truly
   participate in Christ's flesh and blood.
2. “Flesh of Christ's flesh and bones from his bones” echoes Ephesians 5:30.
   The confession uses it to name a communion that it also says exceeds
   adequate verbal explanation.
3. “Local presence” translates *localis praesentia*. The text denies that
   Christ's body must occupy the same place as believers, not that Christ is
   present in the Supper; the next page describes that presence positively.
4. *Symmystae* can mean companions in sacred rites or fellow ministers. “Fellow
   ministers” fits the ecclesiastical signatories while retaining the shared
   ministry implied by the term.
5. The endorsement begins with plural *amplectimur* (“we embrace”) but shifts
   to singular *sensi* (“I have thought”). Its closing identifies Martin Bucer
   as the writer and Wolfgang Capito as subscriber; the English preserves that
   change in number rather than smoothing it away.
6. “Diffused everywhere” renders *ubique diffusum*. The wording rejects an
   omnipresence of Christ's finite body while locating communion through the
   Spirit, word, symbols, and faith.
7. The CO apparatus records variants from printed and manuscript witnesses.
   This edition follows the CO main text, including *pascamur*, *disiuncta*,
   *hic sistit se nobis*, and *vere ... communicatio*; the variants are
   evidence about the document's transmission, not additional authorial
   sentences.

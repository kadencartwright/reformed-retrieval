# Calvin to Socinus, letter 1212

## Work identity

- **Author:** John Calvin (Ioannes Calvinus)
- **Recipient:** Laelius Socinus
- **Work:** Latin letter 1212
- **Date:** assigned by the 1875 editors to 26 June 1549
- **Language:** Latin

The printed letter itself has no dateline. The editors infer the date from Calvin’s return to Geneva, his statement that Socinus’s letter arrived a few days later, and a letter Calvin sent to Bullinger on 26 June. The date should therefore be cited as editorially assigned, not as an internally attested date.

## Edition and witness identity

The transcription follows *Ioannis Calvini opera quae supersunt omnia*, vol. XIII (*Corpus Reformatorum* XLI; *Thesauri epistolici Calviniani* IV), edited by Wilhelm Baum, Eduard Cunitz, and Eduard Reuss (Brunswick: C. A. Schwetschke, 1875), letter 1212, printed pp. 307–311.

The editors state that the manuscript was not extant and list earlier printed editions beginning with Beza. This file is therefore a transcription of the 1875 edited witness, not an autograph or manuscript transcription.

## Contents and completeness

The transcription includes the complete letter text printed by the edition, from *Literas tuas paucis post reditum meum diebus accepi* through *teque spiritu suo gubernet*. It answers questions about marriage to a woman outwardly participating in papal religion, papal and playful baptism, outward participation in idolatrous worship, and the resurrection of the same body.

The edition prints no original address, opening salutation, signature, or dateline for this letter. No internal passage is omitted here, but the missing manuscript prevents confirmation that the printed beginning and ending reproduce every feature of Calvin’s original. The nineteenth-century letter number and Latin file heading are repository metadata, not words attributed to Calvin.

The following nineteenth-century editorial matter is excluded from `latin.txt`:

- the running header, letter number, editorial title, and summary;
- the witness note listing earlier editions;
- the editors’ footnote on p. 309 about the provenance of the resurrection question; and
- the dating and transmission note attached at the end on p. 311.

The final bracketed paragraph in `latin.txt` records these omissions and is not part of the historical letter.

## Transcription policy

`latin.txt` was collated against rendered images of every relevant printed page, not accepted from OCR alone. Printer line-end hyphenation and prose line wrapping are removed; unambiguous OCR artifacts, ligatures, and long-*s* forms are normalized. The 1875 edition’s spelling and punctuation—including forms such as *quum*, *utcunque*, *persequutiones*, *coelum*, and *prosequutus*—are otherwise retained.

`[CO XIII, p. N]` marks each printed page. Where a page boundary divides a word, the marker precedes the whole joined word beginning on the new page, in accordance with the repository policy. This affects *alienior* (p. 308), *affici* (p. 309), and *Tertullianus* (p. 310).

## Translation policy and status

`english.md` is a fresh translation made directly from this Latin transcription. Biblical language is translated from Calvin’s Latin rather than copied from a particular English Bible. No wording was taken from Mary D. Beaty and Benjamin Wirt Farley’s copyrighted 1991 translation or from any other modern translation.

Both transcription and translation are AI-assisted drafts checked once against the page images. They have not received independent review by a specialist in early modern Latin. The Latin edition and page locator should control any argument that depends on exact wording; the difficult *Frigidae sunt voces istae* clause is explicitly annotated in the translation.

# Calvin, *Response to Several Questions of Laelius Socinus of Siena* (1555)

This directory presents the complete Latin text and a new English translation
of John Calvin's *Responsio ad aliquot Laelii Socini Senensis quaestiones*.
The printed work consists of four answers. The questions themselves are not
reproduced in this witness.

> **Editorial status:** This is an AI-assisted draft transcription and
> translation checked against the facsimile, not a peer-reviewed critical
> edition. A qualified Latinist should review it before it is used for formal
> publication or as the sole basis for a disputed interpretation.

## Work and witness identity

- **Author:** John Calvin (Ioannes Calvinus)
- **Addressee/questioner:** Laelius Socinus of Siena (Laelius Socinus
  Senensis)
- **Latin title:** *Responsio ad aliquot Laelii Socini Senensis quaestiones*
- **English title used here:** *Response to Several Questions of Laelius
  Socinus of Siena*
- **Date:** 5 June 1555
- **Extent in the edition:** CO X/1, printed pp. 160–165

The date requires qualification. It does not occur in the response's body.
The *Calvini Opera* editors describe their source as a very neat contemporary
copy in Bibliothèque de Genève, Codex 145, fol. 58 and following, and report
that the response is assigned there to *Non. Iun. 1555*—the Nones of June, or
5 June 1555. Their note also records the work's inclusion by Beza in editions
of Calvin's letters. That editorial apparatus is documented here but is not
mixed into Calvin's Latin text.

This 1555 response is a distinct work. It is not either of Calvin's 1549
letters to Socinus concerning baptism, and it should not be used as the
English translation of those letters.

## Edition and facsimile

The transcription uses:

> Ioannis Calvini *Opera quae supersunt omnia*, ed. Guilielmus Baum, Eduardus
> Cunitz, and Eduardus Reuss, vol. X, part 1, *Corpus Reformatorum* XXXVIII,
> part 1 (Brunswick: C. A. Schwetschke and Son [M. Bruhn], 1871), pp. 160–165.

The public facsimile is supplied by the University of Geneva's *Archive
ouverte*:

- [Calvin collection record](https://archive-ouverte.unige.ch/unige:650)
- [Direct PDF for CO X/1](https://access.archive-ouverte.unige.ch/access/metadata/f526a338-c95c-4f87-b600-04df723f502b/download)

In that PDF, the work begins on PDF page 101 (printed p. 160, the right-hand
page of the scan) and ends on PDF page 104 (printed p. 165, the left-hand
page), immediately before *Contra Osiandrum*. The locally cached UNIGE
surrogate used for verification is 41,526,290 bytes with SHA-256
`a9816caa563330f83b2256297708948a9acf0ef19257bf3d8a1642b89b9d05a0`.
UNIGE regenerates the downloadable PDF wrapper, so a later download may have
a different whole-file hash even when the relevant page images are unchanged.

The collection-level UNIGE catalog gives 1863 as the starting publication
date of the multi-volume series. The title page of this particular volume
prints **1871**, which is the edition date used here.

## Transcription policy

The Latin was extracted as a working aid and then checked line by line against
rendered images of all six printed pages. It is normalized as follows:

- running heads, page furniture, and the CO editors' footnote are excluded;
- prose is reflowed and words divided only by line-end hyphenation are joined;
- OCR confusions are corrected from the page images, and any long *s* is
  represented as `s`;
- the edition's spelling and punctuation are retained, including forms such as
  *quum*, *Quanquam*, *Caeterum*, *tanquam*, and *coelestium*;
- the edition's Greek words are represented with Unicode Greek characters;
- biblical references are transcribed as printed and are not silently changed
  to match modern versification.

The page markers occur at the exact printed-page transitions, including where
a sentence continues across a page boundary.

## Translation policy

The English is a fresh translation directly from this Latin transcription,
with the facsimile consulted when needed. The copyrighted English translation
in John H. Leith, ed., *Calvin's Ecclesiastical Advice*, trans. Mary Beaty and
Benjamin W. Farley (1991), was not copied, revised, or used as a base text.

The translation favors close, readable English while preserving Calvin's
causal and theological distinctions—especially *meritum*, *placatio*,
*expiatio*, *satisfactio*, temporary faith, and the Spirit's seal of adoption.
Calvin's biblical quotations and paraphrases are translated from his Latin,
not replaced with wording from a modern Bible. Difficult semantic choices are
identified in the translator's notes. No illegible facsimile reading remains
silently conjectural, but the files have not yet received independent human
collation.

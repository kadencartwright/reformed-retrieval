### Chapter IV

#### On the Certainty of the Perseverance of the Saints

The question is whether true believers can be certain of their perseverance.
The Pontificians deny it; we affirm it.

#### Bellarmine's Arguments

##### Thesis I

Bellarmine. Ezekiel 18. *When a righteous man turns away from his
righteousness, and so forth. What could be clearer?*

Protestant reply. It would be much clearer and more effective for the point at
issue if the passage dealt with the righteousness of God through faith in
Christ, and if it asserted absolutely that a believer sometimes turns away
from it. But since it deals with the righteousness of deeds in a legal manner,
and absolutely asserts nothing about turning away from it, nothing can clearly
be concluded from those words against the certainty of perseverance.

2. Bellarmine. Luke 8:13. *Those who had received the word with joy afterward
fall away.*

Protestant reply. In this they are distinguished from true believers, who
receive the word *in an honest and good heart,* verse 15. The former were
lightly affected by the word, but they did not have the roots of true faith,
verse 13.

3. Bellarmine. John 5:2. *Every branch in me that does not bear fruit he
takes away. It cannot be a branch unless it has at some time lived in the vine,
for branches are not grafted in from elsewhere but spring alive from the
vine.*

Protestant reply. The question is about a believer who has true and living
faith, and thus about a branch which bears fruit. It is therefore irrelevant
and sophistical to confront us with a branch which does not bear fruit. II.
Bellarmine's assertion that *branches are not grafted in from elsewhere* is
exceedingly shameless, since the apostle expressly affirms in Romans 11:17, 24
that branches cut from a wild olive tree by nature are grafted contrary to
nature into the true olive tree. III. As for the matter itself, Bellarmine
answers his own objection. For so he writes in *On the Church Militant,* book
3, chapter 9: *I say that the wicked are not living members of Christ's body;
the wicked are not members except in a qualified and equivocal sense,*

[Printed p. 360 | Google Books PA360 | GB 1AfNlTSMfpEC]

*Turrecremata teaches this from Alexander of Hales, Hugh, and Thomas; Peter de
Soto teaches the same. Melchior Cano and others say nevertheless that they are
in the body as humors, hairs, and certain things which are not members; they
are called believers because of their profession of faith.*

4. Bellarmine. Matthew 24:12–13. *The love of many will grow cold, but he who
perseveres to the end will be saved.*

Protestant reply. These many possess a profession of Christian love, but not
the love and faith of God's elect mentioned in Titus 1:1, as is evident from
verse 24: *so as to lead astray, if it were possible, even the elect.*

5. Bellarmine. 1 Corinthians 9:27. *Lest perhaps I myself should become a
reprobate.*

Protestant reply. The apostle took every precaution lest his life refute his
preaching. Therefore Bellarmine does not say what follows or how it follows.

6. Bellarmine. Galatians 5:4. *You have fallen from grace; you have been made
of no effect by Christ.*

Protestant reply. That is, for those who seek righteousness in the law, the
profession of Christ cannot profit; Christ will profit them nothing, verse 2.

7. Bellarmine. 1 Timothy 1:19. *Some have suffered shipwreck concerning the
faith;* and 4:1, *they depart from the faith;* and 6:10, *they have wandered
from the faith.*

Protestant reply. All these things are said of those who did not have *a good
conscience,* and therefore did not have true and living faith. The faith from
which they departed was the doctrine of faith, to which *the doctrine of
demons* is opposed in chapter 4, verse 1.

8. Bellarmine. Hebrews 6:4. *Those who had been enlightened, had tasted the
heavenly gift, and had become partakers of the Holy Spirit had been truly
justified, and yet they fell away.*

Protestant reply. I. The apostle himself shows clearly enough that those words
are not a description of the truly justified, since he declares not those
things but others to be *things that accompany salvation,* ἐχόμενα σωτηρίας,
verse 9, among which he names true *faith* in verse 12. II. The apostle does not
affirm that those of whom he speaks had fallen away; on the contrary, he was
persuaded otherwise, verses 9–10.

9. Bellarmine. 2 Peter 2:21–22. *The dog returns to its vomit, and the washed
sow to her wallowing place.*

Protestant reply. True believers are not dogs and swine. These words therefore
must be understood of people who have not been regenerated, but have only been
lightly washed and cleansed through a pure profession.

10. Bellarmine. *Many angels in the beginning fell away from righteousness.
So also did our first parents.*

Protestant reply. These examples have nothing to do with the matter, because
neither the angels nor our first parents were under the grace of the New
Testament and justified in Christ through faith. Indeed, they were not
justified, because they had not fulfilled the condition which the law required
for them to be justified before God.

11. Bellarmine. *Saul was righteous,* 1 Samuel 9:2, *and yet he not only*

[Printed p. 361 | Google Books PA361 | GB 1AfNlTSMfpEC]

*sinned, but is also believed to have been reprobate and condemned.*

Protestant reply. It is not established that Saul was ever righteous before
God. Bellarmine is ridiculous when he proves Saul's righteousness from his
being called *handsome* and *taller from his shoulders upward than all the
people,* 1 Samuel 9:2. Righteousness does not consist in a tall and handsome
stature.

12. Bellarmine. *By everyone's agreement David was righteous, and yet who can
deny that he fell from grace and righteousness? For the greatness of his
repentance testifies to the greatness of his sin.* Psalm 6:7.

Protestant reply. We do not doubt that David's sin was very great. Yet it was
not greater than the superabounding grace of God. And since the desert of his
sin did not hinder God's grace from granting him repentance, no reason compels
us to affirm that he fell entirely from God's grace, or that he was deprived
of that principle from which his effectual humiliation and entreaty for
forgiveness flowed.

13. Bellarmine. *Solomon fell from grace.*

Protestant reply. In the same way that someone who suffers a severe fainting
fit falls away from life.

14. Bellarmine. *Peter sinned mortally by denying Christ.*

Protestant reply. This indeed we do not deny. But Bellarmine did not dare to
say—as he nevertheless needed to say in order to strike us—that Peter fell
entirely from grace. For in *On the Roman Pontiff,* book 4, chapter 8, he taught
that *Peter lost the confession of faith, not faith itself; that he denied
Christ with his mouth, not his heart.* And in chapter 3 of the same book he
says that *Peter possessed something more than the gift of perseverance;* and,
from Augustine, Chrysostom, and Theophylact, that *he did not lose the faith by
which one believes unto righteousness.*

15. Bellarmine. *Simon Magus and Judas the traitor were righteous.*

Protestant reply. Judas was righteous in the same way that a *thief,* John
12:6, an *unclean man,* John 13:10, and a *devil,* John 6:70, can be righteous.
Simon Magus remained in the gall of utmost bitterness and the bond of iniquity
at the very time when he is said to have believed, Acts 8:33. It is more
certain than certainty itself that believers and righteous men of that sort,
and all who are not rooted in faith, can readily be stripped of the faith and
righteousness with which they are whitewashed. But Bellarmine's remarkable,
twisting sophistry appears here. Previously he was unwilling to recognize any
signs of attained righteousness even in the apostles Paul, John, and others,
unless they were common and fallible. Now, however, to serve the scene, he
seizes upon every most meager profession of Judas the traitor and Simon Magus
in order to prove their righteousness with the utmost certainty.

16. Bellarmine. *A person who has the habit of faith will be able to perform
an act of unbelief. But according to the Scriptures, he who performs an act of
unbelief also loses the habit, for he becomes simply an unbeliever, an enemy of
God, and liable to eternal death. And according to the adversaries' doctrine,
through every sin justifying faith is shaken out and the Holy Spirit is poured
out from the heart.*

[Printed p. 362 | Google Books PA362 | GB 1AfNlTSMfpEC]

Protestant reply. I. This last thing related about our doctrine is a pure and
utter falsehood, which Bellarmine would never have blurted out had he not been
deprived of every defense of truth in this quibble. II. Bellarmine could not,
without the greatest ignorance or shamelessness, affirm that *every act of
unbelief shakes out the habit of faith and makes a man simply an unbeliever
and an enemy of God, according to the Scriptures.* Sarah's laughter in Genesis
18 was an act of unbelief. The sin of Moses and Aaron at the water of
contention was an act of unbelief, Numbers 20:12. Zechariah, Luke 1:20, and the
apostle Thomas, John 20:25—to say nothing of Peter and his threefold denial of
Christ—committed acts of unbelief. Yet according to the Scriptures they did
not lose faith or simply become unbelievers and enemies of God. Concerning a
more grievous act of unbelief committed under a powerful temptation, Cyprian
speaks much more correctly and in greater agreement with the Scriptures,
letter 52: *Nor should we think those whom we see wounded by deadly persecution
to be dead, but rather to lie half-alive.*

17. Bellarmine. *All infants are truly justified through baptism, and yet many
of them afterward sin most grievously when they have grown up.*

Protestant reply. Concerning individual baptized infants, it must be judged in
the judgment of charity that they are truly justified; but concerning all of
them collectively, this cannot be affirmed with certainty. However that may
be, our defense concerns adult believers.

18. Bellarmine. *All heretics formerly possessed the faith which they lost.*

Protestant reply. This is a childish game: in place of justifying faith, the
ignorant are presented with a profession of the doctrine of faith, or the
assent which is given to it.

19. Bellarmine. *If the righteous cannot sin, what is the purpose of warnings,
and so forth?*

Protestant reply. Let Bellarmine ask someone who affirms this, and ask him,
*What is the purpose of warnings?* and so forth.

20. Bellarmine. *If present grace and justification cannot be true unless a
person is certain of his perseverance, so that he knows with certainty either
that he will never fall or that he will return to repentance, how, and so
forth?*

Protestant reply. This is another invention of Bellarmine, not our position.
We do not say that present grace cannot be true without certain knowledge of
either perseverance or even the very presence of grace. We readily admit
concerning justifying faith and true grace what Bañez says about supernatural
faith in 2.2, question 6, article 2: *that a Catholic, without losing faith, can
be ignorant that he has infused faith; indeed, can doubt it; indeed, can judge
falsely about himself that he is a heretic. There are scrupulous and ignorant
Catholics who sometimes judge themselves in this way and are mistaken.* Our
argu-*

[Printed p. 363 | Google Books PA363 | GB 1AfNlTSMfpEC]

*ments.

Bellarmine knowingly passed over and concealed by far the greatest part of our
arguments. Even the few which he handles he presents so meagerly and answers
so wretchedly that he has scarcely left any material for disputation here.

21. Protestant argument. John 4:14. Whoever drinks from the water which I will
give him will not thirst forever, but it will become in him a fountain of water
springing up into eternal life.

Bellarmine. *That fountain can be dried up and obstructed.*

Protestant reply. As this is stated absolutely, it sounds like blasphemy
against Christ himself, who is the fountain of life, or against the Holy
Spirit, John 7:39. Besides, it destroys the dissimilarity which Christ makes
between the fountain of grace and a fountain of bodily water. For bodily water
is not dried up, but remains and is drawn, while thirst is taken away. Nor can
it truly happen in any way that a fountain springing up into eternal life is
dried up for even a moment.

22. Protestant argument. Romans 8:35, 38, and following. Who will separate us?
I am certain.

Bellarmine. 1. *The sense of that whole passage is that no terror can cause a
man in whom love burns to offend his God; nevertheless, love can be lost.*

Protestant reply. I. The causes of separation are sophistically restricted to
terrors. For when verse 38 says, *I am persuaded that neither death nor life,*
and so forth, death can indeed be referred to terrors, but *life* by no means.
II. If no terrors can cause him in whom love burns to offend God, then neither
can they cause love to be lost, for by that loss God is most grievously
offended. III. If terrors can in no case bring this about, neither can
enticements or other temptations; for terrors are as effective in some
dispositions as allurements are in others.

23. Protestant argument. 1 John 3:9. He who is born of God does not commit
sin, because his seed remains in him; and he cannot sin, because he is born of
God.

Bellarmine. *The apostle's meaning is this: he who is born of God does not sin
and cannot sin mortally while he perseveres as a son of God.*

Protestant reply. According to this interpretation, regeneration would no more
oppose sin than natural sleep does. For the Pontificians deny that anyone sins
mortally while asleep, because, among other causes, they determine that a sin
is venial from the imperfection of the act when we are borne toward something
unlawful, but not with the full use of reason. Filincius,

[Printed p. 364 | Google Books PA364 | GB 1AfNlTSMfpEC]

treatise 21, number 242. Far more is indicated by 1 John 2:19: *If they had
been of us, they would surely have remained with us;* and Jeremiah 32:40: *I
will place reverence for me in their mind, so that they do not depart from me.*
But Bellarmine considered it safest to pass over testimonies of this kind in
silence.

# Responses to Questions concerning Church Discipline

This is a new English translation made directly from the normalized Latin in
[`latin.txt`](latin.txt). The printed-page markers correspond to the 1871
*Calvini Opera* witness.

**[CO X/1, p. 224]**

By this device Satan everywhere attempts to ruin Christ's church: when he
cannot utterly extinguish pure doctrine, which is like its soul, he seeks, by
breaking and dissolving discipline, to

**[CO X/1, p. 225]**

cut the sinews of the body, so that once its members have been dislocated, a
grievous scattering may soon follow. Would that all who cast off the yoke and
wish unbridled license to be
allowed to everyone would duly consider that in this way they are seeking the
church's ruin and devastation. Surely they would not only pursue moderation,
but with a gentle and meek spirit would themselves also submit to the common
discipline. But since it is not our purpose to discuss this matter at greater
length, we shall reply briefly to your letter, most excellent and upright
brothers.

First you ask us whether those who request admission into the church should be
required to testify publicly to their faith. Next you ask our judgment about
the very form of confession received among you. Finally you ask whether those
who overturn order either by their obstinacy or by the disgracefulness of
their lives should be excommunicated from the company of the faithful. We do
not see why it should be burdensome for a man who wishes to be counted among
the church's household to give his name wholly to Christ its head. He cannot
do this unless he expressly subscribes to sincere piety and frankly condemns
the errors by which the purity of religion is corrupted.

The errors that must be renounced often depend on the circumstances of the
times. As Satan devises new methods of causing disorder, it is necessary to
meet them prudently. We know how strongly Paul commends to us “the unity of
the Spirit in the bond of peace” (Ephesians 4:3). Moreover, for fostering and
preserving agreement among the godly, that solemn profession of faith is more
than necessary. In short, all who desire the church of God to remain unharmed
will not resent its being supported by this aid. We do not suppose that anyone
would dispute a general profession of this kind; but it would be ineffectual
unless each person distinctly renounced both heretical and perverse doctrines.

In the form of your confession we observe nothing that does not agree with
God's sacred oracles and the orthodox faith. We therefore gladly approve the
substance of the doctrine contained there. We would only wish the Epistle to
the Hebrews not to be ascribed to Paul, since firm arguments persuade us that
someone else is its author. Yet we do not wish consciences to be bound by our
vote, or the church to be tied as though by a prejudgment, so as to prevent
individuals from freely bringing forward whatever may hinder them from
subscribing. Although we dislike the peevishness of those who depart from
public agreement over slight scruples, if someone cannot embrace a particular
word or a rather harsh expression, his weakness can perhaps be tolerated,
provided there is agreement about the matter itself, lest the church be split
over every smallest thing. Meanwhile we exhort all the godly to this modesty
and sobriety: let each person move toward all the others rather than, through
his own obstinacy, give the appearance of putting religion itself to the test.

**[CO X/1, p. 226]**

For the rest, the church's well-being will be preserved in no other way than
if excommunication remains vigorous for purifying it, restraining lusts,
removing disgraceful conduct, and correcting corrupt morals. Whoever refuses
its moderate use, especially after being admonished, reveals that he is not
one of Christ's sheep.

Farewell, excellent men and brothers whom we respect from the heart. May the
Lord always be present with you, govern you by his Spirit, and strengthen you
by his power to complete the course of your holy calling. Geneva, the third
day before the Nones of March [5 March].

John Calvin, in the name of the brothers.

## Translation notes

1. *Nervos corporis incidat* uses a bodily metaphor: discipline is treated as
   the body's sinews or connective strength, while pure doctrine is its soul.
2. *In solidum nomen dare* is literally to give or enroll one's name “for the
   whole”; “wholly” conveys an unreserved allegiance to Christ.
3. *Frigeret* literally means “would be cold.” In context it says that a merely
   general confession would lack force or practical effect.
4. *Speciem praebeat tentandae religionis* is compact and can suggest testing,
   assailing, or making an experiment of religion. The translation retains
   “putting … to the test” and does not assign a more specific controversy.
5. Because the Nones fall on 7 March, *3 Nonas Martii* is 5 March by inclusive
   Roman reckoning. The text gives no year.

CHAPTER FOUR

[1615 printed locator B]

On ectypal theology.

ECTYPAL THEOLOGY is wisdom concerning divine things, expressed and formed by
God from his own archetype through gracious communication, to his glory.

“Δηλωτικὰ τῶν πραγμάτων ἐστὶ τὰ ὀνόματα”—“Names are indicative of things”—says
John of Damascus in book 1, chapter 13 of *The Orthodox Faith*. It must
therefore also be explained in this place what things are called *ectypal*.
Clement of Alexandria, in book 8 of the *Stromata*, p. 331 of the first
Commelin edition, speaks as follows: “Τρία ἐστὶ περὶ τὴν φωνήν· τά τε ὀνόματα,
σύμβολα ὄντα τῶν νοημάτων κατὰ τὸ προηγούμενον, κατ' ἐπακολούθημα δὲ καὶ

[1615 printed locator C]

τῶν ὑποκειμένων· Δεύτερον δὲ, τὰ νοήματα, ὁμοιώματα καὶ ἐκτυπώματα τῶν
ὑποκειμένων ὄντα. Ὅθεν ἅπασι καὶ τὰ νοήματα τὰ αὐτά ἐστι, διὰ τὸ τὴν αὐτὴν ἀπὸ
τῶν ὑποκειμένων ἅπασιν ἐγγίνεσθαι τύπωσιν, οὐκέτι δὲ καὶ τὰ ὀνόματα, διὰ τὰς
διαλέκτους τὰς διαφόρους. Τρίτον δὲ, τὰ ὑποκείμενα πράγματα, ἀφ' ὧν ἡμῖν τὰ
νοήματα ἐντυποῦνται.” That is: “Three things are involved in speech: first,
names, which are principally signs of the things conceived in the mind and,
by consequence, also of the underlying things; second, conceptions, which
represent and express the underlying things. Hence everyone has the same
conceptions, because the same form and type is engendered in everyone from the
underlying things. Names, however, are not likewise the same, because of
different

[1615 printed locator D]

languages. Third are the underlying things, from which conceptions are
impressed upon us.”

Thus works formed from an archetype or prototype are called *ectypal*. Pliny
also uses this word in book 36, chapter 12 of the *Natural History*. And in
book 37, chapter 10 he says: “These are the gems suited to relief carvings.”

Archetypal theology is the first idea of theology, from which ectypal theology
receives its idea—to use this word—and is expressed. In the same way,
essential truth and goodness in God are archetypal and are the first idea of
the true and the good.

[1615 printed locator E]

From this, everything created that is true and good receives its idea.
Archetypal theology is the exemplar; ectypal theology is the copy, which ought
to agree with, correspond to, and be like the exemplar. Thus ectypal theology
in a rational creature is part of the image and likeness of God according to
which that creature was created.

It is considered either in itself or as it is in rational creatures.

Ectypal theology in itself is all the wisdom concerning divine things that can
be communicated to rational creatures in this life and in the future life,
according to the measure in which God communicates it.

Therefore, by the name *ectypal theology considered in itself* is understood
not only whatever wisdom concerning divine things has already been
communicated by God to rational creatures, but also whatever

[1615 printed locator F; the witness divides unquam across the line turn.]

ever will be or can be communicated to them in this age and in the age to
come, according to that measure which pleases God in accordance with his
gracious will, from his own inexhaustible fullness. Thus this theology is like
a most abundant spring of all spiritual wisdom welling up with God, from which
treasures to be communicated by God to rational creatures through grace are
drawn. Psalm 36:10: “For with you is the fountain of life; in your light may we
enjoy light.”

Ectypal theology, considered as it is in rational creatures, is wisdom
concerning divine things communicated to rational creatures according to
their measure or capacity, so that, rightly acknowledging God,

[1615 printed locator G]

and loving him from the heart, they may live blessedly with him forever, and
this for the purpose of glorifying him. It is also called *general ectypal
theology in the subject*.

For it is such knowledge as the created intellect is by nature able to receive
and possess concerning that theological object, since each thing is received
according to the measure and capacity of the recipient. Now *measure* in this
place is not the form of theology, nor the way of comprehending and perceiving
it, but the quantity of its comprehension and perception—that is, the measure
falling to each individual rational creature. John 3:34; 2 Corinthians 10:13;
Romans 12:3, 6; 1 Corinthians 12:7 and following; Ephesians 4:7.

Finally, the theology communicated to rational creatures has two ends. The
primary and highest is the glorification of God as the highest good; the
secondary and subordinate is the blessedness of rational creatures. True
theology leads rational creatures to these ends.

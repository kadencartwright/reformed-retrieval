VERSE 17. Moreover, throughout all his days he eats in darkness, that is, with
great indignation, with his grief, and with burning anger.

Solomon identifies another cause of the sorrow with which the miser is struck
when his goods are lost: through the pursuit and love of riches, he defrauded
his own natural appetites by a too sparing use of the goods he possessed. Thus
he took neither as much food and drink as was sufficient, nor such a kind of
food as would serve either health or honorable enjoyment. To this is added the
fact that even then this meagerness of diet was not free from cares and griefs,
anger, and vexation of mind. When he took food to restore the strength of his
body, he was pierced and bitten especially by the worm or scorpion of an evil
conscience. This appears to be what is meant by saying that he eats in
darkness. See Proverbs 23:4.

By God's command labor, and indeed troublesome labor, lies upon us. If we add
anything to it, we make life scarcely worth living. We should be ashamed not
to maintain moderation in food and drink in order to obtain life and
immortality, when these people are more sparing than is right in order to
acquire perishable riches. Here, therefore, drunkards and those addicted to
gluttony are charged as guilty of grave sin.

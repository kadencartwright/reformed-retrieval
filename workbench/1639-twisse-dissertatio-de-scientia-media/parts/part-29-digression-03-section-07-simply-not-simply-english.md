Again, it follows either that God acquires intuitive knowledge of things anew when they exist
simply, or that God’s intuitive cognition of things is terminated only at their existence in
eternity. Consequently, things are never known by God as existing simply, although he always knows
that they exist simply in eternity—that is, “simply not simply,” to touch in passing the
more-than-subtle subtleties of this Doctor. For to exist in eternity is to exist not simply.
Therefore, when he says that future things exist simply in eternity, it is just as if he said that
they exist “simply not simply,” that is, simply in a qualified respect; and I judge this to have
been stated with the greatest precision. For being in eternity is either being before God in willed
and known being, or it is an altogether empty fiction.

Again, it follows that by intuitive knowledge God indeed knows things as present in eternity—that
is, not simply, but “simply not simply,” or in a qualified respect—but that he knows neither future
nor past things, any more than things present in time, either simply or “simply not simply.”

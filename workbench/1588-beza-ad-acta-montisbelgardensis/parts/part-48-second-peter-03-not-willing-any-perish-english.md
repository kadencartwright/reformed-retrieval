### Theodore Beza’s Antithesis

2 Peter 3:9: From the preceding words, indeed from the whole discussion, it is clear that by these words, “The Lord does not will any to perish,” Peter addresses only the faithful, among whom he numbers himself. Finally, we approve what is added, that predestination of the elect must be judged neither from the law nor human reason but from the gospel alone, provided it is understood from these words in 1 John 3:24: “By this we know we dwell in him and he in us: because he gave us from his Spirit.”

And 1 John 3:10: “By this the children of God and the children of the devil are manifest: whoever does not practice righteousness is not from God, and whoever does not love his brother.”

### Andreae’s Observations

1. This statement of Peter too is so open that it can by no means admit your false interpretation. For not only is the universal term “ALL” used, but the explanation is placed before it: “NOT WILLING ANY to perish but ALL to return to repentance.” Beza’s doctrine, which impiously asserts the contrary, is therefore horrendous and blasphemous.

2. In temptations the sense by which they perceive themselves to be led by the Spirit is often so weak that the heart experiences the exact contrary. If election must therefore be judged from this sense alone, the divine promises, baptism, the Lord’s Supper, absolution, and consequently the entire ecclesiastical ministry will have no use, since according to Beza’s opinion NOTHING certain concerning our election to eternal life can be established from them all.

3. Sometimes even the elect children of God fall into manifest crimes like children of the devil, as David did into adultery, murder, and so forth. Where then is their consolation and certainty of election? From where will it be sought or obtained? According to the opinion of Beza and his associates it is not certain in the gospel’s universal promises, baptism, the LORD’s Supper, or absolution. Who therefore does not see what the devil intends by this doctrine? Namely, despair in the godly and Epicureanism in dissolute people, which will certainly follow.

QUESTION III.

*Is Christ the mediator of angels? We deny.*

[Marginal heading: State of the question.]

I. Theologians’ opinions vary here. Some think that Christ can and ought to be
called the mediator of angels, but only of their preservation and
confirmation, not of redemption. Through this mediation, when some angels
fell, the others are supposed to have stood and been so confirmed in the
righteousness received at creation that they could not fall afterward. Thus
these theologians distinguish two kinds of misery, one actual and the other
potential. The former pertains to evil angels and fallen human beings, but the
latter to angels and human beings who were liable to fall in their state of
integrity. They maintain that all who remained in integrity had to be
confirmed by Christ, and could have been confirmed by no other, so that this
potential misery would not become actual.

II. Although this opinion, explained in this way, involves nothing absurd and
seems to amplify the glory of Christ the mediator, the contrary opinion, which
denies that Christ was the mediator of angels, seems truer to us and more
consonant with sacred Scripture. If the angels were confirmed—as they had to
be, so that they would be placed beyond the danger of falling—they obtained
this through a new grace of God added to the always mutable state of nature,
and through the Son of God not as mediator but as creator and preserver,
through whom and in whom all things are said to subsist, Colossians 1:17.

[Marginal heading: Christ is not the mediator of angels.]

III. The reasons why we maintain this are: 1. Scripture calls Christ mediator
between God and human beings, but never between God and angels. 2. There is no
need for a mediator where there is no dissension. For a mediator, as Paul says,
is not of one, Galatians 3:20, but there was no dissension between God and the
angels. 3. Christ is joined not to angels but to human beings, because he did
not assume angels but the seed of Abraham. There the apostle gives the reason
for Christ’s incarnation, namely that he had to save human beings, not angels.
4. Those whose mediator he is have him as their propitiation (ἱλασμός), 1 John
2:2, which does not pertain to angels. 5. If he had been given to the angels as
mediator, he would also have had to be given to man in the state of integrity.
This cannot be said, since mediation pertains to the new covenant and is
directed to the end that sins may be taken away.

[Marginal heading: Sources of solutions.]

IV. Christ is called mediator of the whole church of human beings, gathered
from all nations, both Jews and Gentiles, but not of angels, who are
distinguished from the church.

[Marginal heading: Ephesians 1:21, 22.]

Therefore, although they can belong to the festal assembly of the glorified,

[Marginal heading: Hebrews 12:22, 23.]

and are fellow servants of believers who equally serve God, they cannot
properly be called members of the church, which constitute Christ’s mystical
body redeemed by him.

V. Although in the mediator’s economic kingdom Christ has been made head and
king of the angels, above whom he was exalted so that he might employ them as
ministers

[Printed p. 340 | Internet Archive PDF 368 | IA institutiotheolo02turr]

for the salvation of believers, Hebrews 1:14, and so that they might be subject
to him as Lord, whom they are bound to worship, Hebrews 1:6 and Philippians
2:10, he still could not properly be called their life-giving head by reason
of a vital influence. That influence is diffused by Christ’s regenerating
Spirit only to human beings redeemed by Christ’s blood and efficaciously
called.

VI. Although, strictly speaking, the angels could not merit glory because it
is beyond the merit of any creature, it was not therefore necessary for Christ
to merit it for them. For by covenant God willed to reward them beyond what
was deserved.

VII. Every healing and liberating grace comes from the mediator in the order
of redemption. But the rationale is not the same for preserving and
confirming grace, which pertains to the order of creation. For it belongs to
him who created to preserve.

VIII. The passages of Scripture adduced from Job 4:18 and 15:15, Ephesians
1:10, and Colossians 1:20 were answered in Part I, Locus 4, Question 8, from
sections 14 through 16.

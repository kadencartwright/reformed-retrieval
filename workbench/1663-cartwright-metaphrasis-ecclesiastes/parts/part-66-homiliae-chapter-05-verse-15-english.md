VERSE 15. Just as he came out of his mother's womb, he goes away naked again
as he came; and he carries away nothing from his labor which he might take in
his hand.

The effect of this loss of goods is nakedness and deprivation of everything.
It is amplified by a comparison with an equal circumstance: just as he was
brought naked into the world from his mother's womb. This is further
intensified by another accompanying circumstance, namely, the irrecoverable
nature of his condition, because he is also carried naked to the grave.

Someone may object here that this is the condition of all the best people, not
only of the miser. Yet we ought to profit from this condition and lot common to
everyone. Our abject condition both at birth and at death should admonish us to
preserve moderation in these matters. If someone born in a humble station like
a mushroom or ivy burns with ambition and sets no limit to his pursuit of
honors, he is hateful to everyone. This commonly happens with a maidservant or
manservant who reaches after dominion. Proverbs 30. Since, then, we were born
so naked and lowly, it is fitting that we should be content with little,
especially because we can carry nothing away from here.

Yet there is also something peculiar to the miser who has been ruined of his
goods. You will scarcely see a rich miser who, once ruined of all his goods,
ever afterward rises out of poverty. His melting spirit fails, his hands are
slack, and all hope of recovering his former condition has been taken away.

This nakedness admonishes us to pursue those things of which we cannot be
stripped and deprived, and which will so cover us when we have been stripped
of all these things that, although naked before people, we may remain covered
and clothed before God. There is a certain dreadful nakedness, discussed in 2
Corinthians 5 and Revelation 3. Unless a remedy is sought for it, we are
finished, for we cannot bear God's flaming eyes.

Next, the distinction between the godly and the ungodly should be observed.
When the godly have been plundered and stripped of all their goods, restoration
is promised to them, indeed one more abundant than their former condition. Job
8:5–7. An eminent example appears in Job at the end of the book and also in
David throughout almost the whole of 1 Samuel 30. By contrast, as has been
said, the miser's care and diligence is broken and extinguished by calamity and
adversity because of his despairing mind.

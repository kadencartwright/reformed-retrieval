PENOTTUS.

Thirdly, when Thomas reconciled the certainty of divine foreknowledge with the contingency of
things from their presence, he always said that creatures are present to God because the divine
gaze is borne from eternity over all differences of time and attains

[Book I, printed p. 88; BSB canvas 00114]

everything which occurs in each difference of time. This is because God's knowledge is measured by
eternity, which, existing wholly at once in a single present, represents to the divine intellect
whatever occurs successively in time. He never said that God sees from eternity the being of actual
existence of things because whatever occurs in time coexists with God from eternity, or because
things exist from eternity in eternity as in an inadequate measure. These two claims differ as
widely as heaven and earth, as I said above: God sees from eternity the present being of the actual
existence of contingent things because the divine gaze or knowledge is measured by eternity and,
anticipating the times, sees things before they occur; and God sees from eternity the present being
of the actual existence of things because they coexist with God from eternity according to their
existences and because their actual existence is measured by eternity. Although many confuse these
and take them as identical, their error arises from this confusion. In the first account the ground
of the certainty of knowledge of contingent things is drawn from God; in the second it is drawn from
the creatures themselves.

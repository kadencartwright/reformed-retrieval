It is asked whether God has sufficient dominion over free acts because he
created and preserves the will, gives it power, and cooperates with it; and
whether he therefore has dominion over the will because he gave the will
absolute dominion and a free power of ruling over free acts, independent of
God's predetermination. The Jesuits affirm it: Fasolus, on Thomas, question 14,
article 13, doubt 11, figure 156; Ruiz, on part 1 of Thomas, disputation 76,
section 4, figure 3. For “one is not the perfect lord of an estate if he cannot
grant its dominion and use to another person,” says Ruiz, book 1, *On the
Knowledge of God*, disputation 49, section 2. So also Raymond, *Moral
Discipline*, disputation 2, question 2; Aegidius Coninck, *On Supernatural
Acts*, book 1, disputation 1, doubt 3, figure 42; Grevinchovius; and the other
Remonstrants.

We, however, assert that God's dominion over the will and over free acts cannot
stand unless it is from God not only that the will exists, but also that it
acts or does not act; unless it rests with God's dominion that this free act
exists rather than does not exist; and unless there are precisely this many
free acts, no more and no fewer.

1. For to rule over the will itself

[1668 KBNL/UvA witness | logical printed p. 201 | PDF p. 252]

by creating and preserving it, giving it the power to act, and not cooperating
is not sufficient dominion. For God has
all these things with respect to the devils, yet he would not have sufficient
dominion over them if they could do whatever pleased them. And yet he created
and preserves the devils, grants them the power to act, and cooperates with
them. Nor is God said sufficiently to rule over seas, winds, storms, lions, and
fires because he creates all these things, preserves them in being, grants
them the power to act, and concurs with them by a universal concurrence. For
if, notwithstanding this dominion and against God's will, devils, winds,
storms, fires, lions, and wild beasts could assail and rage against human beings
and the church, that would not be sufficient dominion.

2. The lord of an estate is more perfect if he alienates the estate in such a
way that he retains in his own hand the use of the estate and his vassal's act
of using it, so that the vassal could do nothing concerning the estate unless
the lord willed, directed, and approved it, than if he granted another absolute
dominion to determine anything whatever concerning the estate, even when the
lord was unwilling, prohibited it, grieved over it, and wished the contrary,
as the adversaries teach.

3. For God rules over being insofar as it is being and over this being insofar
as it is this being. Therefore, he has dominion over free acts insofar as they
are such.

4. For given this dominion of God and the creature's will hardened against God,

[1668 KBNL/UvA witness | logical printed p. 202 | PDF p. 253]

the following consequence is valid: “God wills that this free act should exist;
therefore, this free act will not exist.” And this one is valid: “The free
creature wills that this free act should exist; therefore, this free act will
exist.” For according to the adversaries, the created will possesses greater
dominion over free acts than God does.

5. For given this dominion, it does not rest with God as ruler that there are
not more malicious acts of devils and human beings against God's church.
Rather, it rests with the devils and freely acting human beings. Nor does it
rest with God as ruler that there are not more good and supernatural acts than
there are. Rather, it rests with the creatures' free choice, because if God by
his dominion does not determine free causes to act, neither can he determine
the number of free acts, good and evil.

6. For it would not rest with God as ruler to prevent an evil act insofar as it
is free, or to prevent sins insofar as they are sins, except by annihilating
the will or overturning freedom. But this would be ruling over the will, not
ruling over the free act.

7. For the adversaries say that the will does not retain dominion over its own
acts unless, when everything required for acting has been posited, it can act
or not act. Therefore, God does not have dominion over free acts, because he
cannot do as much as the will can.

1 THESSALONIANS 4:3

For this is the will of God, your sanctification.

This is the principal question between us and the Arminians concerning God's
volition about sin. Let the state of the question therefore be this.

Does God will sin to occur while he permits it? The state of the question must
be fixed carefully.

1. It is agreed among us that God foreknows sins before they occur—among us, I
say, and the Jesuits—although the Arminians blasphemously deny that God
foreknows future sins.

2. It is agreed that God concurs toward the material acts of sin, although
there is no slight controversy about the mode of concurrence.

3. It is agreed that God draws good from sin.

4. It is agreed that God neither effects nor causes sin.

5. It is agreed that he forbids, prohibits, and hates it.

6. It is agreed that God knowingly and willingly permits sins.

7. It is agreed that God is not the author of sin, whatever the adversaries may
say in calumny.

8. It is agreed that although God's decree intervenes concerning the existence
of sin, the human beings who sin are not excused.

9. It is agreed that God in no way wills sin by the will of sign, or the
approving will.

The question, therefore, is not, 1. whether God is the cause of sin, as Gregory
of Valencia says.

[1668 KBNL/UvA witness | logical printed p. 204 | PDF p. 255]

Nor, 2. whether God wills sin in the same manner in which he wills punishment,
for God positively wills and effects punishment.

3. Nor is the question, as the Valentinians say, whether God impels human
beings to sin under the aspect of good, for a human being is impelled toward
the act, with freedom preserved, but in no way toward the malice.

Nor, 4. as Arminius says in *Against Perkins*, page 698, “Whether human beings
are moved by no act of God to will or do evil.” But none of our theologians
asserts that God moves human beings to will or do evil.

5. Bellarmine, *On the Loss of Grace*, book 2, chapter 3, perversely says that
the question is whether God is the author of sin.

Nor, 6. is the question, as Vorstius says in the appendix to *The Twofold
Friend*, pages 36–37, “Whether God truly and properly wills that we should
generally do the contrary of what God ordinarily commands in his Word under
penalty of death, and wills it by an antecedent and absolute will.” We do not
acknowledge such a question. For we do not teach that God wills by the will of
good pleasure that something should occur which by the will of good pleasure
he does not will to occur. Nor are there contrary wills in God here. Rather,
he approves and commands as good certain things which he has decreed to
effect, and disapproves and prohibits as evil certain things which he has
decreed to permit to occur and to bring about for his own glory.

Nor, 7. is the question, as the Tridentine Fathers dream, “Whether the work of
sin is as much God's work as the work of obedience.”

[1668 KBNL/UvA witness | logical printed p. 205 | PDF p. 256]

But the adversaries teach the following here. 1. God intends and has decreed
that everything should occur and exist which he commands and enjoins us by the
will of sign. We deny it.

2. They teach that God in no way wills sins to occur, but that, when sin
occurs, God is unwilling, inactive, and idly permitting it. We teach that God
is willing here.

3. They teach that God wills the thing which he draws from sin. Namely, God
does not will sin to exist, not even as a means of displaying the divine glory.
But on the hypothesis that sin exists although God is unwilling, God wills the
good of justice or mercy which is drawn from sin. We teach that God wills
permissively that sin should occur, not simply insofar as it is sin, but insofar
as it is a means of displaying the glory of justice and mercy.

4. They teach that permission is the bare and idle observation of God doing
nothing, by which God himself does nothing but entrusts it to the creature's
free choice to sin or not to sin. We reject the fiction of idle permission as
contrary to providence.

It is therefore asked whether God, not by a bare and idle permission but by
some active and positive act of the permitting divine will, actually wills sin
to exist insofar as it is a means which displays the divine glory;

[1668 KBNL/UvA witness | logical printed p. 206 | PDF p. 257]

whether, while God meanwhile disapproves and detests sin as dishonorable evil,
he nevertheless wills sin, useful in the genus of good, insofar as it conduces
to displaying God's glory; or whether God decreed that sin should occur while
he permitted it and the creature failed, insofar as sin is a means of God's
glory. The adversaries deny it. We prove it.

1. Genesis 45:7: “It was not Joseph's brothers but God who sent him into
Egypt.” Acts 2:23 and 4:27: Christ's crucifiers “did nothing other than what God
decreed should be done.” Acts 13:29—the edition prints `Et c.`—“They fulfilled
all things which are written concerning Christ.” 1 Kings 22:20, 23: “The lying
spirit in the mouth of the prophets, sent by God, enticed Ahab to the battle
prohibited by God so that he should be killed there.” 2 Samuel 24:1: “God
incited David to number God's people.” 2 Samuel 12:11: God ascribes to himself
that Absalom should impiously defile his father's bed: “I will do this before
the sun.” 1 Chronicles 5:26: “The God of Israel stirred up the spirit of Pul,”
and so forth, “that he should carry the people away captive.” 2 Chronicles
21:16: “Jehovah therefore stirred up against Jehoram the spirit of the
Philistines and Arabians.” Verse 17: “Ascending against Judah, they rushed into
his territory.” Isaiah 45:7: “I am Jehovah, creating light and forming
darkness, making peace and creating evil.” Job 1:21: “The Lord gave and the
Lord took away.” Ezekiel 14:9: “I, Jehovah, enticed the prophet.” 1 Corinthians
1:19–20: “He confounds the wisdom of the wise.” 2 Thessalonians 2:11: “God will
send the efficacy of error, that they should believe a lie.” 1 Peter 3:17: “It
is better, if the will of God is so,

[1668 KBNL/UvA witness | logical printed p. 207 | PDF p. 258]

that you should suffer for good than for evil.” Revelation 17:17: “God placed
it in the hearts of human beings that they should give the kingdom to the
beast.”

2. By his free ordination alone, God imposes upon all Adam's descendants the
necessity of contracting original sin. Therefore, he wills that sin should
occur while he permits it.

3. For he permits sin toward a determinate end which he does not attain
without sin.

4. For God knowingly and willingly moves human wills in that manner in which
they sin. He can move them in such a way that they do not sin, and he does not
will to move them otherwise.

5. For he uses devils and human beings in the act of sinning as his instruments
for executing his judgments. “Assyria is the rod of God's fury,” Isaiah 10:5;
“a hired razor,” Isaiah 7:20; “Babylon, a golden cup in God's hand,” Jeremiah
51:7; “God's hammer,” Jeremiah 50:23; Nebuchadnezzar, “God's servant,” Jeremiah
25:9 and 27:6.

6. For we pray in vain that God would avert the hatred of the impious. God
could answer, “I am unable to help; it is not my will that the wicked person
should hate thee.”

7. For there is no reason for the saints patiently to submit themselves to
God's will in the persecutions which they suffer from the wicked, because it is
not God's will that those people should afflict the saints. The object of their
patience would instead be the will of their oppressors, and acquiescence in
that will is idolatry.

[1668 KBNL/UvA witness | logical printed p. 208 | PDF p. 259]

8. For one must not rest upon God in gratitude, hope, faith, fear, or joy if
God in no way willed or decreed that the impious should injure God's saints.

9. For I seek consolations in vain in God if he did not offer me such a cup,
did not will me to drink it, and did not appoint me for the injuries inflicted
upon me by wicked human beings.

10. For gratitude, faith, hope, fear, joys, and consolations would ultimately
recur to the will of impious human beings and devils as the infallibilizing
cause. For God's permission here denies that it is God's will that we should
suffer from impious human beings, and God entrusts it to the choice of the
human beings afflicting us that we should suffer or not suffer these things.

11. For according to the adversaries, contrary to Isaiah 10:15, the impious
person can boast against God if God is not the principal cause in persecutions,
just as an axe would boast against the one cutting with it.

12. For if God is a bare permitter here and is wholly unwilling that the
impious should test us, then, 1. he desires the saints to be tested by evils as
gold is, yet does not will the flame to seize them. 2. If God here is unwilling
and a bare permitter, he is also unconcerned.

13. For if God is unwilling here, he does not love to chasten his own through
the most wicked instruments, contrary to Scripture, Revelation 3:19; Proverbs
3:12: “Those whom I love, I chasten.”

[1668 KBNL/UvA witness | logical printed p. 209 | PDF p. 260]

14. For it is foreign to the decree of a wise God that he should mortify the
saints through injuries inflicted upon them by the world if in such injuries
God is inactive and unwilling.

15. For it conflicts with God's fatherly care that he should not will the
persecutions inflicted upon his servants to be three rather than four, and
that the temptation should be proportioned to the strength of the person
tempted, 1 Corinthians 10:13. It likewise conflicts with it that he should not
will the church to be oppressed by the Chaldeans for seventy years rather than
only twenty or only ten. It is therefore necessary that God should will
persecutions simply. For if God's fatherly care tempers and moderates the
afflictions of his own, he cannot fail to will them. Indeed, God's just will
cannot fail to determine the number, measure, weight, kind, and mode of
temptations: that Sennacherib should sacrifice his own son rather than a sheep
to his idol; that Christ should be crucified rather than taken away by a
gentler death; and that the Sabeans should trouble Job with such grave injuries
rather than lighter ones.

16. God could not threaten the reprobate with hardening as a punishment unless
he willed it in some manner.

17. For ministers could not console those oppressed by the world in this way:
that they ought to be of good courage while suffering evils from wicked human
beings, because this is the will of the most merciful Father.

[1668 KBNL/UvA witness | logical printed p. 210 | PDF p. 261]

18. For it conflicts with the wise order of the universe that the pious should
be afflicted by evil people while God is unwilling and unconcerned; that
something which is the matter of intrinsic primitive justice should occur
against God's will; that the falls of the noblest creatures, human beings and
angels, should occur while God neither wills nor intends them; and that a human
being should have been created by God for an uncertain end.

It is asked whether God wills sin insofar as it is the punishment of sin. The
Jesuits and Arminians deny it. We prove it.

1. For Romans 1:26 says, “On account of idolatry God handed them over to vile
affections, for their women exchanged the natural use for that which is against
nature.” Verse 28: “Just as it did not seem good to them to retain God in their
knowledge, so God handed them over to a reprobate mind to do the things which
are least fitting.” 2 Thessalonians 2:11: “Therefore God will send them the
efficacy of error that they should believe a lie.” Verse 12: “That all who did
not believe the truth should be condemned.”

2. For God threatens hardening and sins as a punishment, Isaiah 6:9; Matthew
13:13–14; Acts 28:26–27.

3. For God threatens his people that the Assyrians, Chaldeans, and others will
devastate them and lead them away captive. But this devastation was a grave
sin.

4. For everything just by participation is from the first one who is just in
himself. But it was just that David's bed should be defiled by incest, because
he

[1668 KBNL/UvA witness | logical printed p. 211 | PDF p. 262]

had defiled his neighbor's bed. It was just that Nebuchadnezzar should punish
the people's idolatry by
slaughter, the sword, and captivity. And it is just that “the person who is an
abomination to Jehovah should fall into a harlot,” Proverbs 22:14. Therefore,
this is from the first one who is just in himself.

2 THESSALONIANS 2:11

Therefore God will send them the efficacy of error.

It is asked whether sin is the punishment of sin. The adversaries deny that
sin is formally the punishment of sin. They teach, 1. that the entitative act
is the punishment; 2. that permission is the punishment; and 3. that sin is
causatively and demeritoriously the punishment. But they deny that sin is
formally and properly the punishment. We prove it.

1. The Scriptures cited above prove it. Add to them 2 Samuel 12:11; 2 Samuel
16:11; 1 Kings 22:20–21; 2 Chronicles 32:31; Isaiah 19:14; 29:10; Romans 11:8;
Proverbs 22:14.

2. For that which deprives a human being of the highest good—namely God—and of
the good of reason, brands the soul with stain and guilt, deprives one of the
due good of legal rectitude, turns human beings toward creatures, and renders
them most vile is a most grave punishment. But sin as such does this.

3. For God threatens sin as a punishment, such as the devastation and
captivity of his people by the Assyrians and Chaldeans.

4. For all persecutions inflicted upon the saints by impious men are grave
sins and at the same time

[1668 KBNL/UvA witness | logical printed p. 212 | PDF p. 263]

chastisements and punishments inflicted upon God's servants.

5. For it is just that the impious should “be hardened and filled with all
unrighteousness,” as Paul says, Romans 1:29–30. Therefore, this is a punishment
justly inflicted by God.

It is asked whether, because punishment is involuntary and a passion while sin
is voluntary and an action, sin therefore cannot be a punishment. The Jesuits
and Arminians affirm it. We deny it.

1. For the stain, vileness, guilt, and aversion from the highest good, which
are the penal features in sin, are involuntary. For the sinner would wish to
be free of these things and enjoy the unlawful good.

2. For not every punishment is involuntary. If a physician gives wine as a
punishment to an unruly sick person, drinking it is voluntary for the sick
person and yet is a punishment. Thus the punishment of loss, as distinguished
from the punishment of sense, is often inflicted upon someone unaware. In such
a person it has the character neither of the voluntary nor the involuntary, as
is evident in the impious, who, unaware and blind, are fattened for the day of
destruction, Psalm 73:2–4; Job 21:2–6; Jeremiah 12:1–3; Hosea 4:13–14. For a
drunk person is punished when he is carried to prison by the officers, although
he is not carried there unwillingly.

3. For a depraved habit, original sin, and vicious inclinations in a human
being which are forbidden by God's law would not be sins because they are not
actions.

It is asked whether God is a bare permitter and the cause

[1668 KBNL/UvA witness | logical printed p. 213 | PDF p. 264]

*per accidens* of hardening, entrusting it to a human being's choice that he
may be hardened or not hardened according to his own choice. The Remonstrants
affirm it; we deny it.

1. For God very often threatens hardening as a punishment. But punishment is
not subjected to the choice of the person punished. The judge who entrusts to
a guilty person's choice whether he may be punished or not punished is idle
and negligent.

2. Punishment is a good, and punishing is a good work of justice. But it is
absurd that something just should be from the first just cause *per accidens*.

3. Everything just which is from a cause *per accidens* is from another cause
in itself. Therefore, the sinner's being punished by sin is either a work of
justice established by God, or it is from Fortune, which must be abominated, or
it is from the sinner himself, which is absurd. For as such he acts badly and
sins unjustly against God.

4. A punishment which God threatens against a creature is inescapable by that
creature. Therefore, it does not rest with a human being's choice whether he
should be hardened or not hardened.

5. For if God hardens *per accidens*, only by idle permission through good
occasions and the preaching of the gospel which human beings misuse to harden
themselves, then God has no different action in Pharaoh's hardening than in
David's and the saints' hardening. For he presents them with such occasions
which they can misuse to harden themselves.

[1668 KBNL/UvA witness | logical printed p. 214 | PDF p. 265]

6. For there is no reason for anyone to pray that God would avert hardening and
to give God thanks for a softened heart if God here is a bare permitter who
entrusts the punishment to be inflicted to the human being's free choice, so
that he can harden or not harden himself. For we do not pray to God for that
which he does not possess within his own will. But if God is in no way the
agent and cause in itself here, he does not possess culpable hardening within
his power.

1 THESSALONIANS 2:16

Forbidding us to speak to the nations that they might be saved, that they may
always fill up their sins; for the wrath of God has overtaken them to the very
end.

It is asked whether there are some acts of hardened human beings, such as
Pharaoh and others, which are evil in themselves and through themselves, and
which, considered in themselves, are contrary to the divine law and therefore
also worthy of punishment, but nevertheless, when performed by such persons as
a result of some penal judgment of God, do not enter the reckoning of sins so
as to increase guilt and punishment, any more than the acts of Satan, which do
not increase damnation. Thus the Remonstrants, in the synodical writings,
article 1, page 202. We, however, deny this.

1. For the devils sin even now, and hardening in evil does not release either
devils or human beings from obligation to the law.

2. For human beings sinning κατὰ τὴν σκληρότητα καὶ ἀμετανόητον καρδίαν,
“according to hardness and a heart that does not know how to repent,” treasure
up for themselves God's wrath for the day of wrath, Romans 2:5.

[1668 KBNL/UvA witness | logical printed p. 215 | PDF p. 266]

3. For Pharaoh, already hardened, is both reproved and more severely punished
by God, Exodus 7:14–15; 9:1–2. And when he has been hardened repeatedly, he is
hardened further so that he should pursue God's people into the Red Sea,
Exodus 14:3–4; Acts 28:24–25; Jude 8–12; 2 Peter 2:13–17.

4. For in that case sins against the Holy Spirit, which are committed only by
the notably hardened, would not be sins, which is absurd. To what purpose would
the threats against this sin be? Matthew 12:32–33; Hebrews 6:2–5; 10:26–28.

5. For murders, murders of parents, poisonings, betrayals, and blasphemies
committed by the hardened would not be sins, and they ought neither to be
reproved nor punished by God or by the magistrate.

6. The hardened could securely mock God and heap sins upon sins, since they
could no longer increase either sins or punishments.

7. For God takes care that promises are preached to the notably hardened when
they are mixed with the elect. He holds out hope of pardon, offers the kingdom
of heaven under the condition of repentance, rebukes and reproves them,
threatens them, presses them with commands, invites, entreats, and wishes that
they would repent. But all these things would here be deceitful hypocrisy and
a disguised expression of a twofold will. Indeed, they would accuse God of
blasphemy, falsehood, and evil deceit.

8. For these acts conflict with God's law and yet are not sins, which is
absurd, 1 John 3:4.

[1668 KBNL/UvA witness | logical printed p. 216 | PDF p. 267]

9. This or that particular person would remove the essential character of sin.
What in other wayfarers is sin worthy of eternal punishment and deserving to
be punished would not be sin in this or that person, and preceding sins would
remove the guilt, stain, and punishment of the sins which follow in the
wayfarer's state.

10. For universal grace would not always be annexed to the gospel, as they
rave that it is.

11. For God would often reprove those who are constrained to sin by physical,
not merely moral, necessity, which they condemn in our theologians.

It is asked whether God impels toward sins which he prohibits. The Remonstrants
deny it, article 1, page 250, in the synodical writing. We regard it as
blasphemous that God should impel toward sins by a moral impulse. But he impels
toward sins by a physical and judicial impulse.

1. For he concurs toward the entitative act of sin.

2. For he punishes sin with sin.

3. For he impels all second causes, and thus free wills, to act, Proverbs 21:1.

4. For he permissively intends sins for the display of his glory.

It is asked whether God absolutely preordained the passions in Christ's death
but not the unjust actions. The Remonstrants affirm it. We deny it.

1. For Herod and Pilate were gathered together to do actively the things which
God decreed that they should do, Acts 4:27–28.

[1668 KBNL/UvA witness | logical printed p. 217 | PDF p. 268]

2. Action and passion are the same motion in reality. It is therefore
impossible that God absolutely preordained that Christ should be killed by the
Jews unless he also preordained that the Jews should kill Christ.

3. It is insipid to say that God first decreed Christ's passive killing by I
know not what agents, and then by another new permissive decree God decreed
Christ's active killing, as though Christ's death had been decreed twice by
God.

It is asked whether Christ's death was decreed absolutely by God but whether
the free agents by whom he should be killed were decreed by God only
conditionally and avoidably. Thus Corvinus, *Against Molina*, chapter 4,
section 3. We deny it.

1. For such a decree, by which God decreed that Christ should be killed but
was ignorant by whom, is confused and unworthy of God. Was it by good or evil
instruments? By Christ himself or by others? By God or by human beings? God
then, by a second care, proceeded to decree that Christ's death should be
procured by wicked human beings.

2. In this way Christ's death was decreed twice by God, once absolutely and
once conditionally.

3. If Christ's death was first decreed by God generically and confusedly,
inevitably and absolutely, it could have occurred that he was killed by others
than the Jews. But Scripture says that this very death which Christ suffered
was preordained by God according to all its

[1668 KBNL/UvA witness | logical printed p. 218 | PDF p. 269 | stop before next question]

circumstances. As to the mode and kind of death, see Deuteronomy 21:23;
Galatians 3:10–12; Isaiah 53:12. As to the persons, attendants, place, and all
the circumstances, it is evident from the Old Testament prophecies, Christ's
predictions, and their fulfillment: Psalm 41:10, compared with Acts 1:16;
Psalm 118:22, compared with Acts 4:11; Psalm 2:1, compared with Acts 4:25–28;
Psalm 22:13, 17, 19; Luke 18:31–33.

4. For in the governance of the universe God absolutely decrees other sinful
actions of lesser moment than the killing of Christ, upon which the salvation
of all the elect depended, with respect to the agents and all the
circumstances, and infallibly executes them according to his decree. Thus he
decreed the devastation of many lands and cities, including in Judea, through
Sennacherib king of Assyria, Isaiah 37:26–27. Likewise, he decreed that these
particular kings should exalt Antichrist by these and those modes and wicked
and impious acts, Revelation 17:17: “But God placed it in their hearts to
execute what seemed good to him, to agree, and to give the kingdom to the beast
until the words of God should be fulfilled.”

# Calvin to Spytek Jordan, Letter 2373

Geneva, late December 1555 (editorial placement)

This is a fresh translation of the complete Latin letter at CO XV 912–913.
Calvin encourages the Polish noble Spytek Jordan in his public support of
reform and commends Francis Lismanino on his return to Poland.

## Translation

### [CO XV, p. 912]

To the illustrious Master Spytek Jordan, most distinguished for his many
virtues, Palatine of Sandomierz, captain of Przemyśl and Kamionka, heir of
Zakliczyn and Melsztyn.

Greetings. Since I understand, most illustrious man, that my letters were
received so courteously by Your Excellency, I seem to myself to have obtained
a worthwhile fruit from my labor, because from it I conceive a sure hope of
still richer fruit. Therefore, when the faithful servant of Christ Francis
Lismanino was setting out on his journey there, although one such man would
be worth more than ten letters, I thought I should repeat the same duty in a
few words.

It is not that you need to be greatly incited by my spurs or anyone else's,
for I see you running eagerly. Rather, the Lord by his heavenly voice
continually encourages even the most ready people to unwearied constancy, and
by his example prescribes the same rule for us. Even if I remained silent,
with your singular prudence you understand what a splendid work you
undertook when you openly and frankly gave your name to Christ and his
gospel. It would be superfluous and absurd to remind a man endowed with such
outstanding godliness and virtue how shameful it would be to turn back his
course in this race.

Only take it in good part if some confirmation from me is added to what
occurred to you of your own accord: a man placed in a lofty rank of dignity
does not properly perform his part unless he runs ahead of others and, at the
same time, overcomes with unconquered courage every difficulty that arises,
so that others may follow without fear. Furthermore, among the aids suitable
and timely for supporting the efforts of the godly there, I do not doubt that
the arrival of our Lismanino will be pleasing to Your Excellency.

### [CO XV, p. 913]

You know the man's integrity. He thinks soundly and governs himself
prudently. In short, for many reasons he was suited to discharge this office.
Since these things are better known to you, most excellent man, from the
situation itself, you need neither prayers nor exhortation to embrace a man
for whom the cause itself wins favor enough and more than enough, even if
until now he had not personally been so dear to you.

It is therefore well that, if the pope's satellites make trouble, the
faithful who care for the pure and complete restoration of the church have
obtained an upright and stouthearted helper, one who does not refuse to set
himself, to the best of his power, against even the most hostile enemies.

And now, most noble man, lest my length cause you any weariness, I shall end
my writing and turn to prayer: may the Lord devote your mind more fully to
himself day by day, kindle it to godliness by his Spirit, enrich it with new
gifts, and guard Your Excellency from heaven. Geneva.

## Translator's notes

1. The CO title and address use the Latinized *Spitheti Iordan.* for Spytek
   Wawrzyniec Jordan. Several place-names and offices in the surviving address
   are textually corrupt. The English gives the historically attested
   Przemyśl and Kamionka identifications without claiming that every
   corrupted Latin form is certain.
2. CO prints the bracketed words *Nicolao Radzwil.* at the end of the address,
   but its apparatus says a careless copyist wrongly appended that different
   nobleman's name to Jordan's titles. The normalized authorial text omits the
   acknowledged scribal intrusion and records it here.
3. Francis Lismanino was returning to Poland as a Reformed advocate and
   intermediary. Calvin's statement that “one man would be worth more than
   ten letters” explains both the carrier's value and the brevity of this
   written appeal.
4. “Gave your name” renders *nomen dedisti*, an idiom for openly joining or
   enlisting in a cause. Calvin couples personal confession with the public
   obligations of Jordan's political rank.
5. *Suo exemplo* is compact: the Lord both summons by his heavenly voice and
   sets the governing pattern. The translation retains “by his example”
   without specifying a single biblical incident.
6. The body closes only with “Geneva.” CO's placement among the letters
   associated with Lismanino's departure supports a date late in December
   1555, but the day and month are not authorial.

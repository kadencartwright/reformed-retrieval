It is asked whether, in the case of true believers who commit the gravest
sins, not only are the Holy Spirit and faith not cast off, but the habit of
faith remains safe beneath those sins and is so pleasing to God that on its
account God wills to give eternal

[1668 KBNL/UvA witness | logical printed p. 618 | PDF p. 667 | split word joined: æternam]

life. The Remonstrants say in article 5, pp. 195–196 and p. 201, that we teach
this. But here they heap many slanders upon us. We therefore answer with the
following assertions.

Assertion 1. The habitual grace of the Holy Spirit is diminished in degree by
the atrocious sins of the regenerate, Psalm 51:10. The Spirit of God is deeply
grieved, Ephesians 4:30, so that his acts are weaker and more languid and are
not as ready, vigorous, and easy as before. Yet the habit of faith and habitual
grace cannot be utterly uprooted, 1 John 3:9; John 14:23; 1 John 2:27.

Assertion 2. The acts of faith, repentance, and spiritual watchfulness can be
interrupted for a time when miserable security creeps in. This is plain in
David, who was idle and inactive when he committed adultery and defended the
killing of Uriah with exceedingly frivolous arguments; and in the church,
which slept securely and refused the Bridegroom entrance, Song of Songs 5:2.
Experience among the saints declares that this is all too true.

Assertion 3. Three kinds of God's grace, favor, and love must be recognized
here. The first is the love of good pleasure in gracious election. The
regenerate cannot fall from this grace or love through even the gravest sins.
First, its firmness and immutability depend on no works, acts, or condition in
the creature, but on the mere grace of the one who calls, Romans 9:11. Second,
this love and God's decrees are wholly immutable, John 13:1; Romans 11:29.

[1668 KBNL/UvA witness | logical printed p. 619 | PDF p. 668]

2. Another is the love of beneficence, which is in God by way of execution, or
with respect to its effects. The regenerate can fall in part from this love
through atrocious sins. Thus God deprived Solomon and others of many common
gifts of the Spirit. After his dreadful defection Solomon oppressed the people
and lost the gift of government in very great part.

3. Another is covenantal love, by which God embraces his people according to
the tenor of the covenant of grace. This love must be considered here in two
ways. First, insofar as it is measured, as it were, according to God's gracious
election; they never fall from this covenantal grace, as was proved above.
Second, this love is also considered according to certain effects of
covenantal grace. These effects can be interrupted and clouded by the gravest
sins of the regenerate. Hence, first, boldness and full assurance in prayer
are diminished; second, spiritual joy ceases for a time; third, consolations
become more tepid; fourth, confidence in forgiveness lies hidden and concealed
like fire covered in ashes; and fifth, under those sins the regenerate feel
certain effects and terrors of God's punishing wrath, Psalm 89:39; Psalm
78:5–9.

Assertion 4. Under these more atrocious sins a regenerate person retains
within himself the inward habit of grace, or the seed of God. Thus he remains
a true believer in habit and capacity, though he is not at that time fit to
enter the kingdom of heaven until he has repented,

[1668 KBNL/UvA witness | logical printed p. 620 | PDF p. 669]

Revelation 21:27, because he is defiled. Nevertheless he remains truly elect
in God's decree and truly redeemed.

Assertion 5. The supernatural habit which remains in the regenerate beneath
those atrocious sins is undoubtedly pleasing to God through Christ. The new
creation in the saints cannot be hateful to God, and his own image cannot fail
to be pleasing to him. Yet it is altogether false that our theologians teach
that this habit is so pleasing to God that on its account he wills to give
repentance and even eternal life if they do not repent. First, both eternal
life and repentance are given to the regenerate on account of Christ's merits
alone. Second, unless the regenerate repented, they would have to perish
eternally according to the evangelical threats. Third, from that first spring
of the gratuitous love of election God graciously raises the fallen
regenerate. Just as he gives repentance and perseverance from this spring, so
from the same spring he restores interrupted perseverance, without any
meritorious regard for the habit of grace. When the righteous person falls,
the Lord lifts him with his hand extended beneath him.

It is asked whether the Remonstrants rightly acknowledge no intrinsic and
living principle from which the very duration of faith flows by necessity of
the consequent, and no extrinsic principle by the force of some absolute
decree by which the elect must persevere. They affirm this in *Scriptum
Synodale*, article 5, p. 140. We believe this

[1668 KBNL/UvA witness | logical printed p. 621 | PDF p. 670]

to be blasphemous, and we set forth our opinion in the following assertions.

Assertion 1. In all the regenerate there is in fact a living principle of
eternal duration, though it does not have an intrinsic capacity and power of
eternal duration. For in them there is the seed of God, 1 John 3:9; God's law
inscribed upon the heart, Jeremiah 31:33; a heart of flesh, Ezekiel 36:26; a
circumcised heart, Deuteronomy 30:6; one heart and a new spirit, Ezekiel 11:14;
the Spirit poured out, Isaiah 44:3 and Zechariah 12:10; the anointing remaining
in them, 1 John 2:27; and Christ dwelling in the heart through faith,
Ephesians 3:17.

Assertion 2. Although this principle is never in fact cast off, it is not
intrinsically immortal or of eternal duration. First, it does not prevent the
saints from falling into the most atrocious sins, concerning which, unless
they seriously repented, they would perish eternally. Second, such a principle
does not determine the regenerate to persevere and not to sin. Third, unless
Christ interceded, habitual faith would fail, Luke 22:31. Therefore it is not
something immortal in itself. Yet insofar as it lies under the intention of
the predestining God and in fact remains, it is rightly called an immortal
seed.

Assertion 3. The extrinsic principles of our perseverance are God's keeping
us unto salvation through faith, 1 Peter 1:5; Christ's intercession, Luke
22:31; Hebrews 7:25 and 9:24; and God's actual grace, Philippians 2:13;
1 Corinthians 1:4; Jude 24. From these principles the regenerate persevere,
not by necessity of the consequent, as they falsely

[1668 KBNL/UvA witness | logical printed p. 622 | PDF p. 671]

ascribe to us, but by necessity of the consequence. The Arminians, however,
contend that the saints persevere by no necessity at all—neither of the
consequent nor of the consequence—but that all the regenerate stand or fall
from the pure and indifferent influence of free will.

It is asked whether an apostate who once truly believed can rise again and
repent through the knowledge by which he knows that what he formerly believed
is true, even though this knowledge is devoid of all assent, so that there is
no need of total regeneration, much less baptism. The Remonstrants affirm this
in article 5, p. 176. We deny it.

1. It is Pelagian to say that a person can repent by the bare knowledge which
is found in demons and in the most abandoned slaves of the devil and sin,
since divine grace is necessary for repentance.

2. David prays in Psalm 51:10 that God would create in him a new heart and a
right spirit. This is more than bare knowledge.

3. On this account the fallen person could rise from his sins not merely by
the pastor's bare proclamation, but by himself and through bare literal
knowledge.

4. If apostates have cast off all saving faith so that they are members of the
devil, wholly excluded from favor and from the kingdom of heaven, and for that
time not elect, as the Remonstrants maintain, then there will be need of a new
regeneration and of baptism as the necessary means of regeneration by God's
command.

5. Yet, as they themselves say, the knowledge which is in the intellect
cannot be extinguished by acts of the

[1668 KBNL/UvA witness | logical printed p. 623 | PDF p. 672 | split word joined: voluntatis]

will. Therefore the regenerate never fall totally, but an inward principle
remains in them, namely literal knowledge, by which they can place themselves
anew in the state of election, regeneration, grace, and adoption.

6. If this knowledge cannot be extinguished and is a sufficient principle for
rising again, what Scripture teaches will be false: that it is impossible for
those who have fallen into the sin against the Holy Spirit to be renewed, and
that they cannot obtain forgiveness of sins either in this life or in the life
to come, Hebrews 6:4–6; Hebrews 10:26–27; Matthew 12:31–32.

7. This second regeneration will be accomplished anew by moral persuasion,
without the infusion of a new living principle. And why, by the same reasoning,
is the first regeneration not accomplished in the same way? What, then, will
God's grace be except a sounding letter?

It is asked whether the Remonstrants rightly ascribe to us the teaching that
God abandons us before we abandon him. Reply: they do so slanderously. If we
speak of abandonment by way of merit, we abandon God first. First, the merit
of sin precedes punishment. Second, when God punishes earlier sins by later
ones, he punishes sinners by abandoning them. But if we speak of a purely
negative and nonculpable abandonment, in which God withholds predetermining
grace which he owes to no one, then God abandons us before we abandon him.
First,

[1668 KBNL/UvA witness | logical printed p. 624 | PDF p. 673]

God withdrew predetermining grace from Adam—which Calvin, in his *Response to
the Slanders of a Scoundrel*, p. 1016, calls “strength and constancy”—and Adam
would have stood if it had been given. Second, grace would not be grace if it
depended on the decision of free will.

It is asked whether those who are truly regenerate, deservedly excommunicated
by the church and delivered into the hands of Satan by a key which does not
err, truly remain believers and children of God, not excluded from the kingdom
of heaven. The Remonstrants deny this. We affirm it.

1. This inference is not valid: “Believers are rightly and legitimately
excluded from the kingdom of heaven in the ecclesiastical forum; therefore
they are totally excluded from the kingdom of heaven in God's forum.” The end
of excommunication in itself is eternal salvation; its incidental end is
condemnation, 1 Corinthians 5:5.

2. Excommunication excludes from the kingdom of heaven only declaratively.
Therefore, one excommunicated by a key which does not err was already excluded
from God's kingdom, and he is excluded only conditionally, for as long as he
does not repent. Thus this will be nothing other than an argument from divine
threats, to which an answer was given above.

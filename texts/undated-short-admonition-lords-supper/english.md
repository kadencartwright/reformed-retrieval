# A Short Admonition on the Lord's Supper

*An undated consilium; a fresh translation from CO X/1 157–158.*

## Translation

### [CO X/1, p. 157]

It is sufficiently agreed that in the sacred Supper believers are presented
with the communion they have with Christ. Nor is that matter in dispute.

The present question is whether Christ there only signifies and figures by
an external sign that we are participants in his body and blood, or whether
he also actually fulfills and performs what he promises: namely, that we are
made participants in his body and blood in such a way that he becomes wholly
ours, so that all his benefits belong to us by right of this communion.

We, however, believe and teach that the representation is true, and therefore
that what is promised by the visible sign is effectively exhibited there.
This cannot happen otherwise than that, while believers receive the sign,
they communicate in the Lord's body and blood—which is to possess the reality
of the sign.

So that what we say may not be ambiguous, it must be defined here what it is
to communicate in the Lord's body and blood. We do not take it to mean simply
believing, but receiving Christ by faith, so that he not only dwells and
remains in us, but also that we grow together with him into one body. Thus
Paul teaches that we are flesh of his flesh and bone of his bones.

When we wish to explain this, we say that it happens spiritually. We
interpret the word “spiritually” so that it comprehends two things. First,
this is a marvelous work of the Holy Spirit which exceeds the grasp of our
mind, just as Paul, seized with wonder, exclaims that this is a great mystery.
Second, this eating does not happen in a carnal manner, so that the body is
ground by the teeth, swallowed by the mouth, or descends into the stomach.

In this way we exclude all those absurd

### [CO X/1, p. 158]

imaginations by which the world is held concerning a local presence or a
diffusion of the glorified body. For we do not conceive of such a presence of
Christ in the Supper as would affix him to the element of bread, enclose him
in the bread, or circumscribe him in some other way—all of which plainly
detract from his heavenly glory. Just as little do we conceive one that would
take away his proper measure, distribute him among many places at once, or
ascribe to him an immense magnitude, things that not obscurely conflict with
the truth of his human nature.

These qualifications must be explained carefully to the people. For we see
how people generally, little concerned about true faith—by which alone we
both enter into fellowship with Christ and cohere with him—think that they
have him sufficiently present provided they have a carnal presence of Christ,
which beyond the Word they dream up from sheer superstition. We also see into
what idolatry the world has fallen.

## Translator's notes

1. *Repraesentari* and *repraesentatio* mean more here than a private mental
   recollection. Calvin immediately defines the representation as “true” and
   says that the promised reality is effectively exhibited. “Presented with”
   in the opening makes that claim audible while the next paragraph retains
   the technical noun “representation.”
2. *Communicare* is rendered “communicate in,” and *communio* as “communion.”
   The slightly technical English preserves Calvin's insistence that the
   faithful truly participate in Christ's body and blood.
3. “The reality of the sign” translates *veritas signi*: the true thing or
   substance corresponding to the visible sign, not merely the sign's factual
   accuracy.
4. Calvin combines the body imagery of Ephesians 5:30–32 with language that
   echoes Genesis 2:23. The biblical phrases are translated from his Latin,
   not copied from a modern English Bible.
5. “Spiritually” has two coordinated functions in the argument. It attributes
   communion to the incomprehensible work of the Holy Spirit and denies a
   bodily mode of chewing, swallowing, or local containment; it does not make
   the communion unreal.
6. *Mensuram illi suam auferat* denies removing from Christ's human body its
   proper measure or boundedness. The following clauses specify the rejected
   alternatives: multilocation and an immensity incompatible with genuine
   human nature.

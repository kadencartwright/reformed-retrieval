## Part XXIII: God’s Governance of Evil Wills

[Part II | PDF page 177 | logical source scan 176 | printed page 175 | begins after preceding unit on shared page]

That the evil wills of evil people also are always holily and rightly not only restrained and governed by God, but even moved and inclined to attempt or even accomplish whatever he has pleased; and that he is not on that account the author of any evil work

I ask the Christian reader to pardon me for unwillingly repeating the same things to one who chants the same refrain so often. I said that those who are condemned are condemned by their own fault, not God’s. Did I lie in that? I nevertheless added that this just judgment does not occur apart from God’s eternal and immutable decree. Of these three claims, I ask, what can anyone fault who knows what it means for God to be God, either from that knowledge which Paul calls the truth of God or from God’s own Word? Certainly, if anything should be done which God does not will, or anything should not be done which he does will, even Epicurus would have confessed—had he dared—that no God remained. But separating God’s will from his decree, or his decree from his will, belongs to a man who is plainly insane. For how could anyone will something to be which he has not decreed to be, or unwillingly decree what he does not will? But if such a decree exists, either absolutely nothing must be excepted from it, or whatever you subtract from his providence you subtract equally from his deity. It therefore concerns both those to be saved and those to be condemned. From this one sees what ignorance it is to confess it in the former and deny it in the latter. But if this decree is God’s, it is therefore from eternity and therefore immutable. If anyone denies this, nothing in the created order would fail to spit upon such a monstrosity. But now I seem to hear—

—Doctors Andreae and Osiander, their auxiliaries, and those gaping after provostships and abbacies, shouting that these things are “GLOSSES, GLOSSES, GLOSSES!” Let us therefore hear God’s own Word. “The Lord,” says Solomon, “has made all things for himself, even the wicked for the evil day.” Was this, I ask, without deliberate and settled counsel? “This one,” says Simeon in Luke 2:34, “is appointed for the fall and resurrection of many.” Come now: was he appointed at random, and not rather by the decree of the one who, as Paul interprets it in Romans 9:33, says, “Behold, I place in Zion a stone of stumbling”? Paul too says in 1 Thessalonians 5:9, “God did not appoint us to wrath” [οὐκ ἔθετο ἡμᾶς εἰς ὀργήν]. For the verb “to appoint” [τίθησιν], just as when John 15:16 speaks of the elect and Isaiah 49:6 of Christ himself in whom they are elect, declares altogether that first foundation to which the other, inferior causes are subordinated; by the mutual relation of immediate contraries [ἀμέσων], it must therefore be understood in the same way of the other member. Hence “I raised you up” [ἐξήγειρά σε] concerning Pharaoh in Romans 9:17, and “those long ago designated for this judgment” [προγεγραμμένοι εἰς τοῦτο κρίμα] in Jude 4, necessarily includes a decree entered from eternity in the book of secret providence. Thus when the world was created, not only is that kingdom said to have been prepared for the elect (Matthew 25:34), but the elect themselves also (Ephesians 1:4), and indeed Christ himself insofar as we are chosen in him (1 Peter 1:20). Conversely, the reprobate are said in 1 Peter 2:8 to have stumbled, being disobedient to this very Word, “to which also they were appointed” [εἰς ὃ καὶ ἐτέθησαν]. Who, then, will exclude God’s will from this decree—even if the matter did not itself protest—when the apostle says not only, “He has mercy on whom he wills,” but also, “He hardens whom he wills,” and that by a will which cannot be resisted?

What more? If Herod and Pilate assembled—as the most holy Jerusalem church testifies—to do whatever God’s hand and counsel had predestined, although nothing more wicked, so far as they were concerned, was ever conceived or done, what sort of shamelessness would deny what the Holy Spirit so expressly affirms? Thus what we teach from both the true natural knowledge of God and his own Word so expressly revealed to us is not “SETTING OUR MOUTH IN HEAVEN,” as this adversary shamelessly slanders us, but hearing God speaking from heaven. His further audacity in claiming that this overturns all consolation of the godly surpasses all shamelessness. On the contrary, what consolation can the godly have in every adversity except this conviction: neither Satan nor his ministers can so much as undertake the smallest thing against them, much less accomplish it, unless God our Savior wills and decrees it just as seemed good to him from eternity—most wisely, justly, and kindly—for the glory of his name, the salvation of his people, and finally the overthrow of his enemies; and unless in his unsearchable wisdom and goodness he governs not only the beginnings but also the course and limits both of chastisements and of trials? If I wished to prove this by citing—

—Scripture testimonies, I would have to transcribe entire books of the prophets and apostles. Let these few suffice. “Those whom God foreknew,” says the apostle in Romans 8:29, “he also predestined to be conformed to the image of his Son.” Explaining the same thing in 2 Timothy 2:12, he says, “If we endure with him, we shall also reign with him.” And in 1 Thessalonians 3:3: “Let no one be shaken in these afflictions, for you know that we have been appointed for this.” And in 1 Peter 3:17: “It is better that you suffer for doing good, IF THE WILL OF GOD SHOULD SO WILL IT, than for doing evil.” If, on the contrary, we maintain that it lies within the power of Satan and the wicked to devise anything against us—although the opposite is clear especially from Job’s history—and that God merely restrains their malice, then whatever power you have granted them you have taken from God and subtracted from the fullness of our consolation.

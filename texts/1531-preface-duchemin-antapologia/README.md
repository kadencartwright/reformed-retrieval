# Preface to Nicolas Duchemin's *Antapologia* (1531)

This directory presents the complete Latin text and a new English translation
of the young John Calvin's dedicatory preface to Nicolas Duchemin's legal
*Antapologia*, addressed to François Connan.

The CO editors describe this as an unusually early and long-forgotten Calvin
text, first recovered for modern scholarship by A. L. Herminjard. Duchemin had
written the underlying defense in 1529 and kept it unpublished for nearly two
years. Calvin supervised its 1531 printing while in Paris and prefaced it with
this letter to his academic friend Connan. It predates even the planning of
Calvin's Seneca commentary and belongs to his legal-humanist formation, not to
the corpus of his later theological tracts.

The volume is available from the University of Geneva's [complete *Calvini
Opera* collection](https://archive-ouverte.unige.ch/unige:650). The facsimile
used here was the [direct UNIGE volume IX
download](https://access.archive-ouverte.unige.ch/access/metadata/52ed043c-7f8d-4da6-ac39-1fe7db8dcd86/download),
captured with SHA-256
`76318ac388e2a6dc38fd1bdc0d960ef115a1fe479ab245298f72a3f25f61b6c2`.

## Files and editorial scope

- [`latin.txt`](latin.txt) is a complete diplomatic-normalized transcription
  of Calvin's letter. It preserves the CO main-text spelling and punctuation,
  joins printer line-end divisions, and marks the exact column/page turn.
- [`english.md`](english.md) is a fresh English translation from that Latin,
  with notes identifying people, idioms, and the one material printed crux.

The transcription excludes the CO editorial title, apparatus note, running
furniture, and page numbers. It retains Calvin's authorial address and closing.
The CO main text's *vitiligatores* is preserved; the apparatus's proposed
*vitilitigatores* is documented rather than silently adopted.

## Verification and use

The Latin was extracted for orientation and then checked word by word against
a 600-DPI rendering of the complete facsimile spread. The p. 786 marker is
placed before *illi*, exactly where the right-hand printed column begins in the
middle of Calvin's sentence. No reading remains conjectural or illegible.

Ford Lewis Battles and André Malan Hugo published a complete modern English
translation as an appendix to *Calvin's Commentary on Seneca's De Clementia*
(1969). That translation remains copyrighted and did not supply wording for
this new translation.

The English in this directory is an **AI-assisted scholarly draft**. It has
received a complete source-image check but not an independent review by a
human Latinist. Until that review occurs, quote the Latin and printed-page
locator for exact-wording claims and identify the English as this project's
draft translation.

Suggested citation:

> John Calvin, preface to Nicolas Duchemin, *Antapologia*, Paris, 6 March
> 1531, in *Calvini Opera* IX (1870), 785–786, normalized Latin and draft
> English translation, *Reformed Retrieval*.

# On Sin and Redemption

This directory presents the complete Latin-form text and a new English translation of the short, undated consilium that the *Calvini Opera* editors call *De peccato et redemptione*. Its attribution to Calvin is expressly doubtful.

| Subject | Adam's fall, death, redemption through Christ, and the first and second resurrections |

The displayed title is the nineteenth-century editors' supplied heading, not a title found in their manuscript. Although the transmitted text ends *Ioannes Calvinus*, neither that name nor the collection setting overcomes the editors' explicit reservation about authorship. This edition must therefore be cited as a work attributed to Calvin, not as secure sole-authored Calvin.

## Edition and transmission

The transcription follows Wilhelm Baum, Eduard Cunitz, and Eduard Reuss, eds., *Ioannis Calvini opera quae supersunt omnia*, vol. X/1 (*Corpus Reformatorum* XXXVIII) (Brunswick: C. A. Schwetschke, 1871), pp. 156–157.

CO reports a single witness: Bremen Civic Library, Cod. MS. 8, fol. 97, in a volume containing various letters sent to Vadianus. The one-page text is not in Calvin's hand. Its superscription, added in another hand, is a brief and partly uncertain French instruction rather than the Latin title used by CO. The editors say that they could not identify the occasion or intended recipient and preferred to await another scholar's judgment about the work's authenticity rather than propose their own.

The public facsimile is supplied by the University of Geneva's [complete *Calvini Opera* collection](https://archive-ouverte.unige.ch/unige:650) and its [direct CO X/1 PDF](https://access.archive-ouverte.unige.ch/access/metadata/f526a338-c95c-4f87-b600-04df723f502b/download). The text appears on PDF pp. 99–100 of the captured surrogate. That file was 33,386,580 bytes with SHA-256 `025973d6e4533769d0c2b870dd324497943bc14050eb2bcbcc4c964d4f2a1777`.

## Scope and collation

`latin.txt` includes every word from *Primus omnium hominum* on p. 156 through the transmitted subscription *Ioannes Calvinus* on p. 157. It excludes the CO heading, source note, running heads, page numbers, and the adjacent consilia sharing the two facsimile pages.

The Latin was checked line by line against 300-DPI renderings of both pages. Printer line-end divisions are joined, and CO spelling and punctuation are otherwise retained. The p. 157 marker follows the complete preposition *in* at the foot of p. 156 and precedes *crucem*, the first word actually printed on p. 157. No text is illegible or conjecturally supplied.

## Translation status

The item was translated in Mary D. Beaty and Benjamin Wirt Farley's copyrighted *Calvin's Ecclesiastical Advice* (1991); that translation supplied no wording here. No complete public-domain English version was located in the audit. The English is an **AI-assisted scholarly draft**, translated afresh from the normalized Latin and checked against the full facsimile. It has not received independent review by a human Latinist; cite the Latin and CO page for exact-wording claims.

# For the Bishops and Priests of the Papacy

*A fresh translation of the complete French consilium in CO X/1 184–187.*

## Translation

### [CO X/1, p. 184]

Because I have been asked what procedure ought to be followed in receiving as
pastors any bishops and priests of the Papacy whom God might graciously bring
to reform, I have been willing to set down briefly in writing what seems good
to me. I ask those who read this summary to excuse its brevity, since with
great difficulty I have had any

### [CO X/1, p. 185]

time to dictate it, already battered by a catarrh that makes me heavier and
slower.

If, then, someone who has had the care of souls, whether a bishop or a parish
priest, is touched by God so as to range himself under the pure doctrine of the
Gospel, but is incapable of exercising the office because he lacks the
knowledge and skill to teach, it would be exceedingly rash for him to wish to
involve himself in it. The fruit of his conversion will therefore be to
withdraw, recognizing that it was an exceedingly gross abuse to bear a title
without its reality. He will consequently have to yield and relinquish the
place to a successor who is found suitable and duly ordained, contenting
himself with being received into the flock as a sheep.

If some bishop or parish priest possessed the grace and learning to exercise
the office and wished to apply himself to it, he would be required before all
else to make a confession of his faith and solemnly affirm that he adheres to
the pure and simple religion. Second, he must confess that his calling was
formerly abusive and so ask to be approved anew. In particular, he must
disavow his institution by papal authority and renounce all other illicit
means contrary to the order that our Lord Jesus Christ established in his
church.

Once that has been done, I see no reason to make difficulty about receiving
him, on condition that he keep his promise and faithfully discharge his duty.
In particular, he must join the company of ministers who preach the Word of
God purely and submit to the discipline and polity that they have and observe.
Meanwhile, let the whole of the past be buried and all his faults forgiven,
except that he should be exhorted to conduct himself as he ought in time to
come.

If the rule of Saint Paul is alleged, that a bishop who is chosen must be above
reproach, I answer that in my judgment this is not a question of a simple
election but of an approval, in order to restore to wholeness what had been
corrupted. For just as such people possessed the title and rank of pastor to
their condemnation, so when they offer to discharge their duty, the church
may indeed grant them for a time what they had wrongly usurped. There are two
defects in their condition: first, they were not introduced by a legitimate
way; second, they degraded themselves by failing to execute their commission.
But when they are ready to reunite with the true church, this does not prevent
their being regarded as regular ministers and thus confirmed as if anew, so as
to correct the defect that had existed.

### [CO X/1, p. 186]

One must carefully guard here against ambition, on the one side as much as on
the other. Just as the order of the church must not be broken to gratify
people, neither should excessive rigor give occasion to think that someone is
seeking his own advantage. Indeed, the word Saint Paul uses when he forbids
doing anything from ambition carries the sense of a desire for victory. Thus
the true course to take amid such troubles is not to seek who will win.

If those who profess a desire to return to the right path feel burdened by
this, they are very much in the wrong. It is certain that they cannot be
regarded as Christian pastors unless they have renounced the papal priesthood,
in which they were installed to sacrifice Jesus Christ—a blasphemy worthy of
detestation. They must also solemnly promise to abstain henceforth from all
superstitions and filth that conflict with the simplicity of the Gospel. How
could they administer the Holy Supper unless they were separated from the
abomination of the Mass? Likewise, they cannot be ministers of Baptism without
rejecting the admixtures by which it has been corrupted. In short, the church
cannot accept them as pastors unless they bind themselves to do their duty.

That being presupposed, a bishop will have to endeavor as much as he can to
purge the churches under his charge and superintendence of every idolatry and
error. He must show all the parish priests of his diocese the way and induce
them to obey the reformation to which the Word of God summons us and which
accords with the state and practice of the primitive church. At the same time,
he must declare that he disowns those who will not acquiesce in the Word of
God, and must show, by contrast, that he agrees with God's true servants and
wishes to observe brotherly union with them.

As for temporal property, whether jurisdiction or revenue, although it is a
corruption incompatible with the pure simplicity of the church's spiritual
government, while matters remain confused they may by toleration be left in
possession of it. They should be exhorted to distribute the property in their
hands as something dedicated to God, both so that they do not profane sacred
things and so that they keep within the modesty fitting true bishops.

For the rest, if such people wished to retain their lordships and earthly
jurisdictions, it would be best for them to content themselves with being
regarded as protectors of the church. They would see to it that the Word of God
preached by the ministers was

### [CO X/1, p. 187]

received and that the service of God was maintained in its integrity, without
placing themselves in the rank of ministers, but endeavoring to assist them as
those having superintendence for this purpose by royal authority. In that
case, although there would be a weakness that was not altogether excusable,
nevertheless they could be borne with and recognized as honorable members of
the church they would serve.

*Jeh. Calvin.*

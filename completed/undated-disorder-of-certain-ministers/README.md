# On the `ΑΤΑΞΙΑΙ` of Certain Ministers

This directory presents the complete surviving Latin presentation and a fresh
English translation of the ecclesiastical advice printed under the editorial
heading *De certorum ministrorum ΑΤΑΞΙΑΙ* at CO X/1 215–217.

> **Editorial status:** This is an AI-assisted draft transcription and
> translation checked line by line against high-resolution facsimile images,
> not a peer-reviewed critical edition. A qualified Neo-Latinist should
> independently collate it before formal publication or reliance on a
> disputed reading.

| Language state | Surviving Latin presentation translated from French, according to the earlier editor cited by CO |
| Genre | Corporate ecclesiastical advice (*consilium*) |
| Subjects | Ministers bearing arms, attempted violence, church discipline, removal and suspension from ministry, restoration after censure |
| Printed locator | CO X/1, pp. 215–217 |

The body closes *Vester Io. Calvinus omnium nomine* (“Your John Calvin, in the
name of all”). It is therefore not merely an unsigned dossier item: Calvin is
the named writer, but the first-person plural response is expressly issued on
behalf of the gathered Genevan colleagues.

## Title and language state

The exact CO heading is the hybrid Latin-Greek *DE CERTORUM MINISTRORUM
ΑΤΑΞΙΑΙ.* CO prints the Greek as unaccented uppercase `ΑΤΑΞΙΑΙ`; it is not
silently regularized here to accented Greek or inflected after Latin *de*.
The nominative plural headword means “disorders,” “irregularities,” or acts of
insubordination. The heading is nineteenth-century editorial matter, whereas
the italic opening benediction belongs to the transmitted text.

CO's title note lists the Geneva *Thesaurus* of Beza, p. 353; Lausanne,
p. 662; Hanover, p. 744; Chouet, p. 485; and Amsterdam, p. 221, and adds
*teste editore ex gallico versa*: on the editor's testimony, the piece was
translated from French. CO neither prints nor identifies a manuscript of the
French source. Consequently, [`latin.txt`](latin.txt) is an edition of the
surviving Latin presentation, not a claim that Calvin composed this form in
Latin and not a reconstruction of the French archetype.

## Authorial and editorial matter

[`latin.txt`](latin.txt) begins with *Dilectio Dei patris nostri* and ends
with *Vester Io. Calvinus omnium nomine.* It includes the benediction, the
whole counsel, the farewell, the Geneva assembly notice, and the corporate
signature. It excludes the CO heading, running heads, foliation, footnote cue,
and bibliographical title note. No variant apparatus is printed for this
item.

The first column of p. 215 above the heading belongs to the preceding advice
on baptism and is excluded. The right column of p. 218 begins the following
advice concerning a veteran minister and is likewise excluded. Nothing in
the intervening CO main text is omitted.

## Normalization and textual cruxes

The transcription follows [`TRANSLATION_POLICY.md`](../../TRANSLATION_POLICY.md):
printer line wrapping is removed, page markers are explicit, and edition
spelling and punctuation are retained. Neither page turn divides a word:
p. 215 ends *Satis enim* and p. 216 begins *constat*; p. 216 ends *solent* and
p. 217 begins *multorum calumniis*.

Points requiring special notice are:

- CO repeatedly prints `N.` for suppressed names. Context sometimes suggests
  a person and sometimes a place, but the translation does not invent the
  missing identities.
- The unusual *abreptitii* describes men carried away or swept along by
  impulse. *Mores stratioticos* is a Greek-derived expression for soldiers'
  or military ways.
- CO p. 217 genuinely prints *exhibuturi*, not the expected *exhibituri*.
  The diplomatic-normalized Latin preserves the printed form; the English
  gives its evident contextual sense, “show themselves.”
- The compressed parenthesis *displosa in N. bombardula ex qua secunda vice
  ignis emicarit* describes a small firearm discharged at `N.`, with fire
  flashing from it on the second occasion. The syntax establishes two alleged
  attempts at homicide but does not permit recovery of fuller circumstances.
- *Repetere et recipere in ministrum* means to call the suspended man back and
  receive him again as a minister, not to repeat his punishment.

## Translation independence

Mary D. Beaty and Benjamin Wirt Farley published a copyrighted translation in
*Calvin's Ecclesiastical Advice* (1991). It was not consulted or used for the
wording of this translation, which was made afresh from `latin.txt` and then
checked against the facsimile.

Suggested citation:

> John Calvin, on behalf of the Genevan assembly, *De certorum ministrorum
> ΑΤΑΞΙΑΙ*, undated, surviving Latin presentation translated from French,
> *Calvini Opera* X/1 (1871), 215–217, normalized Latin and draft English
> translation, *Reformed Retrieval*.

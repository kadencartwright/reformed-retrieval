CHAPTER TWO.

From verse 1 to 10 he promises those zealous for true wisdom both a knowledge
of God and a knowledge of righteous conduct among human beings.

[Analytical diagram]

- The parts are:
  - an exhortation to the study of wisdom;
  - promises concerning the bestowal of good things upon those who pursue it.
- In the former are two things:
  - what those zealous for wisdom must do;
  - how we may attain what is commanded to be done.
- Among the things commanded are two:
  - first, that we receive and embrace wisdom;
  - second, that we keep and store up the wisdom received.

After Solomon, by a concise but dreadful sentence of God, condemned the
young man of reckless audacity and abandoned hope to utter destruction, he
returns to the instruction of the godly, upon which he spends the entire
remainder of the book, as far as I recall. Accordingly, he requires from all
who make progress in godliness the two things I set down above.

[1632 witness | PDF p. 25 | printed columns 24–25]

First, unless someone receives the word, he cannot be born again; nor is he
of the begetting (γεννήσεως) of God unless he allows himself to be cultivated
so as to receive the seed. Next, one who hears and does not understand is no
better. For the godly are precisely those who hide God’s words within them,
Psalm 119, section 2, and Luke 2:19, 51. Here there is an elegant simile drawn
from jewels deposited in the lowest part of a chest, or seed admitted into the
inner parts of a ridge or furrow.

The other part follows: how we may attain this. Here, by a certain gradation,
the means by which it is reached are indicated. The first is that we apply the
mind zealously to the word; hence we are commanded to desire the milk of the
word, 1 Peter 2. The cause of apostasy is noted in 2 Thessalonians 2: they had
not been affected with zeal and delight for the truth. Here let the mind’s
inborn aversion from truth be noted. Nor is this impeded by the statement that
a person is to incline his own ear, since it speaks of one who is regenerate,
not of a person who remains in the corruption of his nature. The mind must
therefore be purged of those sins whose love cannot coexist with love of the
word. Hence this statement: “Incline my heart to your statutes, not to
covetousness,” Psalm 119, section 5; and this: “No one can serve two masters,”
Matthew 6:24—which, although spoken about one vice, can be transferred to
others. In the second place, he demands the ear’s diligent attention, which
the affection of the heart and mind will readily produce. Hence it is clear
that the heart’s inclination, though subsequent in the order of the
discourse, is prior by nature. Nor does that slowness and deafness of the ear
(Isaiah 6) seem to arise from anything other than the heart’s thickness and
insensibility (ἀναισθησίᾳ). Here, therefore, let the difference between
hearing and attending be observed. See Acts 2:14. Another way of seeking
wisdom is to entreat it from God by prayers, and with such persistence that
you might seem, as it were, to have assigned or consecrated your voice to
accomplishing that purpose: “Ask, and you will receive,” Matthew 6. This is
taught more particularly in James 1 and Kings 1:3 and illustrated by Solomon’s
example. Those are rebuked here who not only fail to prepare themselves for
hearing the word by prayer, but even withdraw from the church’s prayers the
moment the sermon is finished, without waiting for prayer as the sermon’s
crowning conclusion. For how absurd it is, when this supreme Wisdom deigns to
call out to us and summon us to come to her—when she first invites us by
calling—that we should not answer her! The final method of investigating her
is contained in verse 4: namely, if we seek her as the covetous seek money;
indeed, if we search her out as those who seek a vein of gold or silver are
accustomed to do, Matthew 13. Therefore, just as we see that they spare
neither labor nor expense to discover a gold mine, so let us strive with equal
zeal to secure a lawful ministry and resort to a place where God’s word is set
forth and explained; let us read the Scriptures (Acts 8:30) and join
meditation with reading, Psalm 1; let us build one another up through mutual
conversation; indeed, let us inscribe the Scriptures upon gates and private
houses, so that the word of God may dwell richly in us. From this we see that
the continual prayers we must pour out for wisdom do not exclude our own
industry. A deficiency of industry in this matter is all the more
condemnable, because, although the search for a gold mine is exceedingly
difficult and uncertain, the investigation of wisdom in the Scriptures is
easy for one who earnestly seeks it, and of undoubted success.

Verse 5. Then you will understand the reverence of Jehovah, and attain the
knowledge of God.

v. 6. For Jehovah gives wisdom: from his mouth proceed knowledge and
understanding.

v. 7. He has stored up for the upright whatever

[1632 witness | PDF p. 26 | printed columns 27–28 | printed numeral 26 is skipped]

is truly a shield for those who walk with integrity.

v. 8. By guarding the paths of justice, he preserves the way of those whom he
has graciously received.

v. 9. Then you will understand righteousness and justice, and whatever is
upright—every good path.

The other part follows in the promises concerning the bestowal of good
things. These, in turn, are of two kinds: the former contains godliness and
the worship of God in four verses; the latter, righteousness toward people,
both oneself and others, even enemies. For since whatever we ask from God in
Christ’s name will be given, this is especially true of those things which
directly concern God’s glory. He teaches, moreover, that this skill concerning
God and his worship is given to people from God’s mouth through the ministry
of those who serve the church in the word. Consequently, in this passage
people must be earnestly exhorted to embrace the ministry: for they cannot
obtain it by any work of their own apart from the ministry of the word,
although their work must be applied, since otherwise invoking God would be to
mock him.

Other promises are also attached to the attainment of this wisdom: the former
is general, concerning all things that are truly good, 1 Timothy 4:8; the
latter is particular, concerning the defense of God’s true worshipers and of
all the undertakings they assume. This is added by way of a reply to a tacit
objection. For someone can readily object that the true worshipers of God are
exposed to many dangers in this life; but the answer is that God provides them
with a shield and bulwark.

The other promise contains duties toward neighbors, of which something was
said earlier. But in this place מעגל טוב, “good path,” is set down as the
genus of the remaining things enumerated.

From this it appears that Scripture teaches every good path. Since it teaches
both the worship of God and every duty toward one’s neighbor, the result is
that the man of God is complete, perfectly instructed for every good work.

[Analytical diagram]

- From verse 10 through the end, the parts concerning the study of sacred
  doctrine are:
  - effects;
  - adjuncts.
- In the former are considered:
  - what it is to be zealous for sacred doctrine;
  - what its effects are.

Verse 10. When wisdom has entered your mind, and knowledge will be pleasant
to your soul:

What it is to be zealous for wisdom was stated more fully above; here it is
repeated more briefly, yet not without an addition: namely, when wisdom
penetrates a person’s mind and is sweeter to him than honey and the honeycomb.
Accordingly, just as he earlier wished wisdom to be investigated with the same
zeal and diligence with which gold is investigated by the covetous, so in
this passage he wishes the desire to receive it to be as inflamed as is the
desire of the hungry and thirsty to be refreshed with wholesome food and
drink.

Verse 11. Prudence will watch over you; understanding will guard you.

[Analytical diagram: There follows a twofold effect of doctrine: watchfulness
and guardianship; concerning its threats.]

Here, therefore, by a certain metaphor he explains the fruit of wisdom once
acquired, teaching that wisdom and prudence themselves will be like a shield
or a wakeful guard, and thereafter will discharge the duty of a guardian. For
it causes a person to walk safely and also always draws God’s protection
along with it.

[1632 witness | PDF p. 27 | printed columns 29–30]

Thus in John 8:32 truth is said to set her worshipers free; and Solomon says
in Ecclesiastes 8:3 that strength is found wherever there is the decree of a
prince. If this must be true of a human decree, whose author is very often far
away, it is much more necessarily true of this decree of God, who is present
everywhere and almighty.

Verse 12. Delivering you from the way of evil, from men who speak perverse
things.

Wisdom’s watchfulness and guardianship are declared by two ends: the first is
to preserve the young man from evil; the other, to keep him in duty, verse 20.
From this the perfection of Scripture is established, because it both guards
from every evil and directs in every good, especially since it so cultivates
a young man, whose ways are customarily the most corrupt, Psalm 119, section
2. It therefore does not merely open the eyes, as before, but also releases
the hands and feet from chains, a thing that belongs to no other doctrine.

[Analytical diagram]

- The first end is enlarged from a distribution of the evils from which
  wisdom delivers, namely:
  - the evil man, the transgressor discussed in chapter 1, verse 1;
  - the adulterous woman.

He describes the evil man from his effects, which are set forth by gradation.
The first effect is that he speaks perverse things, that is, things contrary
to what he ought to speak, such as those found in the first chapter. If any,
therefore, become accustomed to ungodly speech, let us dare to call them
wicked. “For from your words you will be justified, and from your words you
will be condemned.” From this it is clear that the case is far otherwise than
most people suppose. When they are rebuked for corrupt speech of any kind,
they immediately reply that, however they may speak, they nevertheless think
very well and do not intend to carry out in deeds the evil which they seem to
approve in words. If someone objects that it seems strange that Solomon does
not extend wickedness to thoughts, the answer is at hand: he passed over
thoughts precisely because people in their common life cannot pass judgment
on them, nor are we anywhere in Scripture commanded to avoid the company of
those whose wickedness is disclosed by no word or deed.

Verse 13. Who forsake the paths of uprightness, to walk in the ways of
darkness.

The second degree is the abandonment of the known and well-trodden ways of
uprightness, which supply light, and their delight in walking in devious ways.
For he seems to indicate here the same thing stated above, chapter 1, verse
21: namely, when people turn away from acknowledged godliness and justice in
order to commit crimes for which they seek night and darkness. Such are those
who pursue robbery, drunkenness, and so forth, which are forbidden even by
civil laws; gamblers are also of this kind.

Verse 14. Who rejoice in doing evil, and exult in the most wicked perversities.

The third degree is that they rejoice in evils. See chapter 4, verse 16;
Micah 2:1. They seem to be tormented like a woman in labor until they have
brought forth, by word or deed, the evil conceived within. Ahab supplies a
notable example of this, for he became sick because he did not obtain his
wicked desire. The opposite of this ungodly joy is seen in the Savior, whose
food was to carry out the will of him who sent him and to finish his work,
John 4:31.

The fourth degree is to celebrate triumphs over a

[1632 witness | PDF p. 28 | printed columns 30–31 | printed numeral 30 is repeated]

crime committed. These are those whose glory is to their own shame, who boast
among their companions about adulteries, gluttony, and how many gallons of
wine or beer they have drained, and so forth.

Verse 15. Whose ways are perverse, and who are obstinate in their paths:

Their final degree is that they are so obstinate that they even flee and
abhor what is good, though they had formerly embraced it. Indeed, they wrestle
with good and praiseworthy works no less than with an enemy. See Psalm 18 and
Proverbs 8:8.

Verse 16. Delivering you from the strange woman, from the foreign woman who
flatters with her words.

Thus far he has taught the first part of the former effect, which is
deliverance from the ruined man. The other part follows, concerning the
rescue of the godly man from the prostitute—a benefit no less than the former.

[Analytical diagram]

- The greatness of this evil is taught from:
  - the description of the prostitute;
  - the attached punishment of those taken captive by her.
- The prostitute is described from her:
  - characteristics;
  - effects.

The first characteristic is that she is called “strange.” By this designation
the Holy Spirit intended to instill in us even the impulse to flee from her.
For in Scripture everything with which we are prohibited from having dealings
is called “foreign” or “strange.” Thus, fire forbidden in sacrifices is
called strange; likewise, everyone who was not a priest, even an ordinary
Levite, was called a stranger in the offering of sacrifices. Therefore,
whenever a desire to have relations with her enters the mind, let us remember
that she is a stranger—and not a stranger defined as such by civil laws,
toward whom it is not merely permitted but fitting to show humanity, but one
constituted as such by God’s law, whom it is not lawful to grant citizenship
in the society of the faithful.

Her other characteristic is that she is called foreign-born; that is, she is
separated from the godly young man not so much by an interval of countries as
by the whole breadth of heaven. For in former times women who were of another
nation than the Jews were regarded as excluded from the worship of God.
Accordingly, it was not lawful for the Israelites to give their sons in
marriage to the daughters of the nations, because they were idolaters, unless
they joined themselves to the true church and renounced both their ancestral
homes and customs. Neither, therefore, is it lawful for us—especially since
one who joins himself to a prostitute takes a member of Christ and makes that
member a member of a prostitute.

Her effects follow. The first is that she is coaxing and drips honeyed words,
professing in her words that she loves uniquely the man she wishes to deceive;
that she wishes him every good; that there is no one whom she loves and
desires as she does him; that no price or favor from anyone could induce her
to meet with anyone but him; that he is the delight and pleasure of her eyes;
and that she is prepared even to suffer death for love of him. Although she
may speak in this or a similar manner, nevertheless her words are separated
from her mind and judgment, or certainly from the truth, as will soon be
evident from her other effects.

Verse 17. Who forsakes the guide of her youth and forgets the covenant of her
God.

The first argument by which it is shown that she speaks otherwise than she
thinks—or at least otherwise than the truth—is that she forsakes the guide of
her youth, that is, the husband whom she loved as a young woman.

[1632 witness | PDF p. 29 | printed columns 32–33]

First, a young woman loves more fervently than women advanced in age. Second,
a woman’s first love is much more intense than her second, much less her third
or fourth. Third, she also deserted the man who governed her when she was
entrusted to his care, bestowed many benefits upon her, adorned her with
clothing and jewels, and, in short, provided for her from every side. From
this you may readily discern that she does this either for her own pleasure or
for gain, and that she loves herself, not you.

The second argument is taken from another of her effects: not only did she
forsake her husband and betray the pledge given to him, but she also dealt
treacherously with God, the author of the contract, whose name was invoked to
confirm the marriage. Therefore, how will she who deserts God for petty gain
or trivial pleasure not desert you much more readily if some greater pleasure
or profit should shine upon her? From this it is evident how important it is
for the preservation of chastity that those to be joined in marriage be
imbued with true fear of God and refrain from wandering lusts not merely from
fear of hell, as some do, but also from love of God.

Verse 18. [For her house inclines toward death, and her paths toward those
devoid of life:

v. 19. Whoever go in to her do not return, nor do they attain the paths of
life].

Up to this point was the description of the prostitute; there follows the
punishment of those captured by her, namely an accelerated death. Her house—
that is, metonymically, the things done within her house—is said to incline
toward death. This is also illustrated by the metaphor of her wagon tracks.
The former indicates that even the prostitute’s house itself must be fled in
order that the prostitute herself may be avoided; the latter intimates that,
by sexual love for prostitutes as though by a broad road, very many hasten to
destruction.

The death she threatens is often brought about through the dueling of her
lovers, in which she alone is delighted; sometimes by diseases contracted
from that filthiness; or, finally, by the hand of the magistrate, who ought by
the natural and moral law to punish this crime with death. And although among
human beings death is very often not inflicted upon adulterers, they gain
nothing from this. For the more the hands of magistrates are slackened, the
greater is the punishment from God that hangs over them—not only because of
the injury done to the husband, but also because his most holy name is
violated. From this passage some of the more ancient fathers concluded that
adultery is unpardonable.

v. 20. That you may walk in the way of the good and keep the paths of the
righteous.

We heard above that wisdom keeps watch for her worshipers for a twofold end:
first, to preserve the young man from evil; second, to keep him in duty. The
former having now been explained, the other remains. Scripture not only
restrains from evil but also teaches what the way of the good is; indeed, by
the power of the Spirit it brings about perseverance in that way. For just as
a minister is made complete by the study of the Scriptures, so disciples also
become complete.

Verse 21. For the upright will inhabit the land, and the blameless will remain
in it:

v. 22. But the wicked will be cut off from the land, and those who act
treacherously will be torn away from it.

[Analytical diagram]

- There follows the attached reward of the godly and punishment of the wicked
  in this life.
- The reward of the godly is:
  - to inhabit the land;
  - after a long life to leave it to posterity.
- The punishment of the wicked—and especially of covenant-breakers—is:
  - to be carried off by a premature death;
  - to have their posterity utterly rooted out. This is shown most clearly in
    Solomon himself, whose posterity, although he himself repented of his
    frequent adulteries, was extinguished in Coniah, Jeremiah 22.

# Calvin to Francis Lismanino, Letter 2373 bis (1555)

This directory presents the complete surviving Latin and a new English
translation of Calvin's travel instructions and greetings for Francis
Lismanino as he prepared to return to Poland.

## Work identity

| Field | Value |
|---|---|
| Author | John Calvin (1509–1564) |
| Recipient | Francis Lismanino (c. 1504–1566), Polish reformer |
| CO number | Letter 2373 bis |
| Date and place | Late December 1555, editorially assigned; neither survives in the body |
| Original language | Latin |
| Genre | Private correspondence; travel memorandum and commendation |
| Subject | Lismanino's route, reform contacts, greetings to Poland, postponed circular letter |

The letter is distinct from Calvin's public-facing appeals to Andrzej
Trzecieski and Spytek Jordan. Its own opening says it records matters already
discussed face to face, and its final paragraphs explain why Calvin had not
yet prepared a wider letter to the Polish churches.

## Edition and damaged witness

The transcription follows Wilhelm Baum, Eduard Cunitz, and Eduard Reuss,
eds., *Ioannis Calvini opera quae supersunt omnia*, vol. XV (*Corpus
Reformatorum* XLIII; *Thesauri epistolici Calviniani* VI) (Brunswick: C. A.
Schwetschke, 1876), letter 2373 bis, printed pp. 913–914.

CO reports that candidate of theology P. Kummer transcribed the letter for
the editors from the Herrnhut Brethren's archives, manuscript volume X, p.
72v. The editors expressly state that they had to emend its text conjecturally
in a number of places. Their three printed proposals on p. 914 are:

| CO main text | Apparatus proposal | Effect |
|---|---|---|
| *adversa* | *adverso?* | repairs agreement with *tempore* |
| *maturem* | *mutarem?* | makes Calvin say he changed his plan after Lismanino's departure |
| *comparata* | *comperta?* | changes “better established” to “better ascertained” |

`latin.txt` preserves the CO main text rather than silently promoting any of
these conjectures. `english.md` brackets the one intervention needed for a
coherent sentence and explains all three. This is a normalized transcription
of an already mediated and damaged edition state, not a reconstruction of
Calvin's lost autograph.

The public facsimile is supplied by the University of Geneva's [complete
*Calvini Opera* collection](https://archive-ouverte.unige.ch/unige:650) and
its [direct CO XV PDF](https://access.archive-ouverte.unige.ch/access/metadata/b873ef04-63a3-4f30-8d07-f55388ab850e/download).
The relevant spread is PDF p. 470 in the captured surrogate, whose whole-file
SHA-256 was
`7cd272773162dd4b9a1b8f43e224647f42a6a0bb3045df2f79781fe9fa88ae77`.

## Scope and collation

The normalized Latin contains every word of the surviving letter from its
address on p. 913 through *Vale* on p. 914. It excludes the CO letter number,
editorial title and synopsis, witness paragraph, running furniture, and four
apparatus notes. Line wrapping and printer divisions are removed while the
CO main-text spelling and punctuation, including its defective forms, remain.

The entire spread was checked at 600 DPI. The p. 914 marker precedes *Nec
vero*, exactly where the right-hand printed page begins. Nothing is illegible
in the CO print; the uncertainties arise from the deficient manuscript copy
behind it and are therefore philological rather than image-resolution
problems.

CO prints no dateline. Its editors place the item at the end of December 1555
because it occupies the middle of a Herrnhut dossier of letters prepared for
successive stages of Lismanino's journey. That reconstruction is plausible
but must not be confused with an authorial date.

## Translation status

No complete public-domain English version was located in the correspondence
audit. The English here is an **AI-assisted scholarly draft**, translated
afresh from the normalized Latin and checked against the facsimile. Because
of the damaged witness, the translation is lower-confidence at the three
phrases catalogued above even though the CO typography is clear. It has not
received independent review by a human Latinist.

Suggested citation:

> John Calvin to Francis Lismanino, late December 1555, letter 2373 bis,
> *Calvini Opera* XV (1876), 913–914, normalized Latin and draft English
> translation, *Reformed Retrieval*.

VERSE 6. For as the crackling of thorns under a pot, so is the laughter of a
fool; this too is vanity.

This is a prolepsis whose tacit objection is: pleasure and delight perceived
in laughter or joy refresh and exhilarate the mind and even bring health to
the body. The reply is that this laughter and delight of the flesh are of
brief duration. See Job 20:5, where it declares that the song of the wicked is
near its end and the gladness of the hypocrite lasts only a moment. Verse 4
cites two witnesses to this fact: Job's conscience and the experience of all
ages from the very beginning of the world.

Solomon illustrates this by a comparison between like things, namely thorns
placed under a pot. Although they make an exceedingly bright fire, they are
nevertheless extinguished very quickly, as is also evident from Psalm 118:12.
In Psalm 37 the wicked man is compared to a flourishing tree spreading its
branches in every direction, which nevertheless soon vanished so completely
that not even the place where it had stood could be recognized. To the same
point belongs the Prophet's statement in Amos 8 that the Sun will set upon the
wicked at midday itself.

Someone might object on the other side that sorrow arising from reproof is
hostile and injurious to bodily health. But the corresponding reply is readily
made: sorrow, like the pains of a woman in labor, is not long-lasting and
brings a gladness that will never perish. Job 16.

It must therefore be the height of folly to prefer so fleeting a gladness of
the flesh to sorrow, which is the parent of joy so substantial and ever
enduring.

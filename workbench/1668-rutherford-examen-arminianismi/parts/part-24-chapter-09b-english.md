EPHESIANS 1:7.

In whom we have redemption through his blood, the remission of lapses, from
his rich grace.

From Paul, therefore, we have that the redemption procured by Christ's death
is entirely the same as the remission of sins in his blood. Hence these
questions arise.

It is asked whether through his death Christ procured reconciliation for each
and every person, but does not actually confer it upon each and every person.
The Remonstrants say so. We deny it.

1. Christ does not impute sins to those for whom he procured reconciliation, 2
Corinthians 5:19; Colossians 1:10–11, and consequently he actually reconciles
them. But he does not actually reconcile each and every person. Therefore, he
did not procure reconciliation for each and every person.

2. Procured remission apart from application is only potential remission and
actual non-remission. But what Christ acquired by his blood is not merely
potential remission; it is the

[1668 KBNL/UvA witness | logical printed p. 388 | PDF p. 439]

Church acquired by blood, Acts 20:28, and the Church redeemed from all
iniquity, Galatians 1:3; Titus 2:14.

3. If Christ procured reconciliation for all only in the amplitude and
sufficiency of his merit, they say nothing other than that, once Christ's
death had been accomplished, there remained in Christ a sufficient λύτρον,
“ransom,” useful for redeeming all mortals. But this is not contrary to us. If,
however, he procured reconciliation for all with respect to efficacy, then he
did so either conditionally, if all believe—and thus reconciliation has not
been procured unless all first believe, and because not all believe,
reconciliation has therefore not been procured for all, since a conditional
procurement, if the condition is never posited, never posits anything—or it
has been procured for all absolutely with respect to efficacy, whether they
believe or do not believe. Therefore, in second act and real efficacy, all
must actually be reconciled even if they never believe. The adversaries will
not say that. Therefore, reconciliation has been procured for believers alone.

It is asked whether those for whom Christ died can perish eternally. The
Remonstrants say so. We deny it.

1. Paul argues in Romans 8:34: “Christ died; therefore, who will condemn those
for whom he died?” It is futile for them to reply that Paul argues there not
from Christ's death simply, but from his death and resurrection. For if the
apostasy of the saints stands, Paul's

[1668 KBNL/UvA witness | logical printed p. 389 | PDF p. 440]

consequence is not valid: “Christ died for us and rose again; therefore, none
of us will be condemned,” because all can fall away from faith and thus be
condemned.

2. Christ's death would be null and vain, and Christ would lay down his life
yet not see his seed, as the Father promises him, Isaiah 53:10, if all those
for whom he laid down his life could perish.

3. It is the Semi-Pelagianism of the Massilians to say that Christ died for
those who pass through this whole life with a mind utterly alienated from
Christ.

It is asked whether potential reconciliation, not actual restoration into
God's favor, is an effect of Christ's death. That is, whether through Christ's
death only this has been effected: that we are restored to such a state in
which God, avenging justice no longer standing in the way, has the will to lay
aside his wrath toward us and to open the door of salvation to all, by which
all could be saved if only they willed and believed in the Son of God; yet in
such a way that it could happen that the saving fruit of Christ's death,
saving faith, or true sanctification would reach no mortal, and indeed that,
Christ having died for each and every person, all could nevertheless have
perished eternally. The Remonstrants affirm this: *Synodical Writings*, article
2, pages 281–282; Arminius, *Against Perkins*, page 673; Grevinchovius,
*Against Ames*, page 8. We teach the contrary.

1. The effect of Christ's death efficaciously intended by God is that from
enemies we should actually become

[1668 KBNL/UvA witness | logical printed p. 390 | PDF p. 441]

friends reconciled to God. Romans 5:10: “For if, when we were enemies, we were
reconciled to God through the death of his Son, much more, having been
reconciled, we shall be saved through his life.”

2. The effect of Christ's death efficaciously intended by God is not merely
this appeasability of God, but the actual application of Christ's death.
Galatians 1:4: “Who gave himself for our sins, that he might redeem us from the
present evil age, according to the will of our God and Father.” Titus 2:14:
“Who gave himself for us, that he might redeem us from all iniquity and purify
for himself a peculiar people, kindled with zeal for good works.” Ephesians
5:25–26: “Christ delivered himself up for the Church, that he might sanctify
her, having purified her by the washing of water through the word.” Verse 27:
“That he might present her to himself glorious, not having spot or wrinkle or
anything of that kind, but that she might be holy and blameless.” Luke 18:11:
“The Son of Man came to save what had perished”—namely, to save lost sinners
in the way in which he saved Zacchaeus and other tax collectors, Luke
19:10–11, by applying his death to them. 1 Timothy 1:15: “This saying is sure
and worthy of all reception: Christ Jesus came into the world to save sinners,
of whom I am first.” But Paul was actually and efficaciously saved. Therefore,
through Christ's death God intended such efficacious salvation for Paul and
other sinners. 1 Peter 1:18: “Knowing that you were redeemed, not with
corruptible things, gold and silver, from that vain conduct handed down by your

[1668 KBNL/UvA witness | logical printed p. 391 | PDF p. 442]

fathers.” Verse 19: “But with the precious blood of Christ, an unblemished
lamb.” Therefore, regeneration and purchase out of the vanity of life are
what Christ merited by his blood. 1 Peter 2:24: “Who himself bore our sins
aloft in his body upon the tree, that, being dead to sins, we might live to
righteousness; by whose stripes you were healed.” Colossians 1:22: “He
reconciled us in that body of his flesh through death, that he might present
you holy, blameless, and irreproachable in his sight.” 2 Corinthians 5:15:
“Since you have judged this—that (he) died for all—so that those who live
should no longer live for themselves, but for him who died for them and was
raised.” Verse 21: “He made him who knew no sin to be sin for us, that we might
become the righteousness of God in him.” Therefore, our sanctification,
actual redemption from slavery to sin and from all iniquity, our
justification, and actual salvation are the end intended by God in Christ's
death and the fruit procured for us by his merit. Therefore, God's
appeasability and our reconciliability and salvability—which can coexist with
the eternal destruction of all for whom Christ died—are not the fruit and end
intended by God in Christ's death.

3. This appeasability and potential reconciliation, by which God can save
human beings if he wills, is compatible with the dead Christ's being defrauded
of his reward and fruit. For if Christ were to lay down his life for his own,
yet God the Father did not at all stand by his promises and did not give his
Son a seed, Isaiah 53:10,

[1668 KBNL/UvA witness | logical printed p. 392 | PDF p. 443]

nor children, Isaiah 8:18; Hebrews 2:13; nor the nations as an inheritance and
the ends of the earth as a possession, Psalm 2:8; nor all nations to serve
him, Psalm 72:11, Christ would be defrauded of his reward. But it is absurd
that the Father should in no way fulfill his promises to the Son when the Son
has faithfully performed all the conditions of the covenant.

4. We do not see what good is intended for the elect in Christ's death if God
immediately intended nothing other than a bare appeasability by which he could
save all. For this is God's good, not the good of human beings; and thus by
Christ's death God would render himself reconcilable but would in no way
render us reconciled.

5. Christ would have poured out his blood for human beings with an ambiguous
and uncertain end if, once his death had been accomplished, it could happen
either that no one believed, no one was actually redeemed from his vain
conduct, and no one was saved, or that all believed and were saved.

6. Once the λύτρῳ, “ransom-price,” had been paid and accepted by the Father,
it could have happened that not one of the captives was released from the
bonds of sin and death.

7. God the Father would miserably fall short of the end intended through
Christ's death—the declaration of Mercy, Wisdom, Power, Justice, and
Truth—since, though he had employed by far the most excellent means, namely,
the death of Christ his Son, he would have attained no end.

8. Through his death and

[1668 KBNL/UvA witness | logical printed p. 393 | PDF p. 444]

resurrection Christ would in no way have triumphed over death, sin, and the
devils if, once his death had been accomplished, miserable sinners enslaved to
sin, death, the devils, and hell could have remained so eternally.

9. Christ would have been a King and Head without subjects and members, a
Bridegroom without a Bride, and a Redeemer without the redeemed.

10. If through Christ's death God had not actually reconciled us but had only
acquired the power to reconcile us notwithstanding justice and to make any
covenant, whether legal or evangelical, then Christ, having been made a curse
for us, would in no way have freed us from the Law and the curse of the Law,
contrary to Paul's words in Galatians 3:10–13.

11. If salvation is uncertain once Christ's death has been accomplished, then
the new covenant would not surpass the covenant of works, contrary to Hebrews
8:6, because the promises of actual salvation in either covenant would depend
upon uncertain and contingent choice.

12. Salvation under the condition of faith in the dead Christ would not be
promised in the New Covenant if, Christ having died, God remained free either
to make or never to make such a covenant, and even to substitute for the New
Covenant the prohibition against eating the fruit. Consequently, the New
Covenant would not at all be sanctioned and confirmed by the blood of God's
Son.

13. If through Christ's death only this had been effected, that God laid aside
his wrath toward all mortals,

[1668 KBNL/UvA witness | logical printed p. 394 | PDF p. 445]

then no one would now be born by nature a child of wrath, nor would faith and
regeneration be needed if we are already in favor with God.

JOHN 3:19.

This is condemnation: that the Light has come into the world, yet human beings
love darkness rather than Light, and so forth.

By these words Christ affirms that the sin on account of which all who are
born in the Visible Church and hear the Word of the Gospel are condemned is
unbelief and rejection of the Savior. Hence:

It is asked whether the Remonstrants rightly number these three things among
the fruits and effects of Christ's death. 1. That solely because of Adam's sin
God wills no one to perish eternally. 2. That he rejects no one because of
actual sins that precede the gospel call, but has mercy upon all unto
repentance. 3. That God rejects no one from the communion of heavenly life or
punishes anyone with eternal punishment because of the sins they call sins of
weakness. We deny that these are fruits and effects of Christ's death.

Regarding the first, whatever the Remonstrants bring to the contrary in the
*Synodical Writings*, article 2, page 283, we deny to be a fruit of Christ's
death.

1. Notwithstanding this death, we are all children of wrath by nature,
Ephesians 2:3.

2. Scripture says nothing about remission of original sin alone apart from
the remission of

[1668 KBNL/UvA witness | logical printed p. 395 | PDF p. 446]

all sins, since the blood of Jesus Christ cleanses us from every sin, 1 John
1:7.

3. All barbarians and Indians would have in Christ's blood, poured out for
them, a half-remission and half-reconciliation apart from any faith in Christ
or any knowledge of the Savior—a fiction hidden from the Scriptures.

4. God retains his absolute freedom of Election and Reprobation with respect
to infants not yet born, Romans 9:11. Therefore, because of Adam's first sin,
the lack of the divine image, and the natural inclination to evil, some who
die in infancy are adjudged to hell.

Regarding the second Question, whatever the Remonstrants assert to the
contrary in the same place, page 285, we deny that it is an effect of Christ's
death that God rejects no one because of actual sins preceding the call to
faith and repentance, but has mercy upon all if they allow themselves to be
called back to repentance and faith and actually believe.

1. If this speaks of a call common equally to all nations, then children
cannot sin before they grow to more mature age and the use of reason, because
those sins have already been expiated by Christ's death and are, as it were,
not sins. But if this speaks of the gospel call of adults, then God cannot
reject Indians and Americans because of murders, parricides, poisonings,
blasphemies, and the adoration of the devil, nor can they

[1668 KBNL/UvA witness | logical printed p. 396 | PDF p. 447]

be condemned because of any crimes—the Remonstrants everywhere teach the
contrary—for those sins precede the gospel call, since they are never called
by the gospel.

2. If God has mercy upon all who allow themselves to be called back to faith,
as this position says, then either the action by which they allow themselves
to be drawn to faith begins from pure free choice, and thus salvation and
conversion will not be from grace but from nature; or it begins from God's
grace determining the will, which they will not say; or, if it begins from a
grace that does not determine the will, but from such a grace whose influence
depends upon the influence of free choice, then salvation and faith will still
begin from a purely natural act. They say nothing other than that God has
mercy upon all if he has mercy upon all and draws all to faith if he draws all,
because a human being's allowing himself to be called back to faith is the
human being's performing a free act of responding to the God who calls.

3. For some sins, but not all, to be expiated by Christ's death is for human
beings to be half-redeemed by Christ's blood. But Holy Scripture says nothing
about a half-redeemer, for it everywhere hands down the full and perfect
redemption of all who are redeemed in Christ.

Regarding the third Question: Is it an effect of Christ's death that God,
while a person lives, rejects no one from the communion of heavenly life,
deprives no one of the fruit of procured grace, or punishes no one with eternal
punishment because of weaknesses more or less common to all,

[1668 KBNL/UvA witness | logical printed p. 397 | PDF p. 448]

except one who rejects grace and the Redeemer with a new and vincible
rebellion? The Remonstrants say so in the same place, article 2, page 285. We
deny it.

1. Since the wages of every sin, even the least, is death, through Christ's
death no sins have been made venial and such that God does not punish them
with eternal punishment unless the sinner truly grieves over them and repents.

2. In that case every regenerate person could perfectly fulfill the Law if
all his sins of weakness—printed `imfirmitatis`—are venial, that is, are made
such that they in no way merit eternal death. For such sins must neither be
offensive to God nor contrary to the divine Law.

3. According to this position, Indians and Americans must be saved, because
they do not reject grace and the Redeemer with a new and vincible rebellion:
they are not bound to believe in the Redeemer because they have never heard
anything about Christ, and consequently labor under an invincible ignorance
of Christ. In Romans 10:14, “How will they believe in him of whom they have not
heard?” Paul frees all who have heard nothing about Christ from the guilt of
culpable unbelief and consequently from the guilt of rejecting Christ.

4. Unless a person is born again, even granting that he does not reject the
Redeemer with a new and vincible obstinacy, he nevertheless will not enter the
kingdom of God, John 3:3.

5. Through this doctrine there are no

[1668 KBNL/UvA witness | logical printed p. 398 | PDF p. 449 | ends before Hisce etiam aliæ quæstiunculæ tres affines sunt]

sins at all except unbelief and rebellion consisting in final impenitence and
obstinacy against Christ the Redeemer. Therefore, the parricides, adulteries,
and so forth of those who are entirely ignorant of Christ because they lack
instruction and are dull of understanding are not sins that bar human beings
from heaven. Yet this is sheer libertinism.

6. This is to turn Christ's grace into licentiousness, if through Christ's
death it has been effected that human beings can freely commit every crime
without fear of hell and exclusion from the kingdom of heaven, provided they
guard themselves against rebellion against Christ and unbelief.

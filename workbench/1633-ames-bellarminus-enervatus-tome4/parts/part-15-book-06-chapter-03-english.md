### Chapter III. On the Certainty of Election

This question is so closely joined with the preceding one concerning grace
and with the other concerning perseverance that it cannot conveniently be
treated separately. Yet we must briefly see what Bellarmine adduces. The
question is whether a believer, without a special revelation, can and ought to
determine that he is among the number of the elect. The Pontificians deny it;
we affirm it.

#### Bellarmine's Arguments

##### Thesis I

Bellarmine. *From this very fact, that we do not know what moves God to choose
this man rather than that one, and that we are compelled to refer the whole
matter to his will, Romans 11, we understand that it cannot be known in any
other way whom he has willed to choose and whom not to choose, except through a
special revelation.*

Protestant reply. The sum of this reasoning is: if we do not know the cause,
then neither do we know the effect; or if we do not know a thing through its
cause, then we cannot have it known through its effect. We know that Bellarmine
has proposed this inept sophism as his principal argument, but what impelled
him to do so, we wonder rather than know.

[Printed p. 358 | Google Books PA358 | GB 1AfNlTSMfpEC]

2. Bellarmine. *Scripture exhorts all believers to fear.*

Protestant reply. It exhorts them to reverence for the divine majesty, to
solicitous caution against sin, and to trembling at the divine wrath against
sins; but Scripture does not exhort true believers to fear rejection. Although
it does exhort some who are believers by profession, *who seem to stand,* 1
Corinthians 10:12, not to trust too much in their outward profession.

3. Bellarmine. *Scripture expressly teaches that salvation depends upon the
condition of works. But no one can determine certainly that he will perform
all those good works which Christ commands to be done.*

Protestant reply. The form of this reasoning is plainly monstrous. As to its
matter: I. Bellarmine himself proves in *On Grace,* book 2, chapters 9, 10, and
so forth, that predestination to salvation does not depend upon the condition
of works. II. Salvation does not depend upon works as upon a condition
uncertain to believers. III. Without all those works which Christ commands, a
believer can be saved by God's mercy.

4. Bellarmine. *Nothing ought to be affirmed about those things which depend
solely upon God's will, except from his own manifest revelation.*

Protestant reply. He has revealed in the Scriptures that all who believe will
have eternal life.

Bellarmine. *He has nowhere revealed that all those who say they believe,
believe as they ought.*

Protestant reply. Nor do we say that all such persons either are certain, or
can or ought immediately to be certain, of their election and salvation.

5. Bellarmine. *It is most useful for preserving humility that it not be known
in this life who certainly belongs to the number of the elect.*

Protestant reply. This is to adapt the religion of God to men's political
calculations.

6. Bellarmine. *If it were known who are predestined, all the rest, despairing
of salvation, would care to accomplish nothing good at all.*

Protestant reply. If this little objection does anything to the point, it has
this sense: if believers could, by doing well, become certain of their
salvation, then other men would care nothing about doing well, which is
ridiculous.

7. Protestant argument. Our one foundation proper to this cause is that
statement of the apostle, Romans 1: *those whom he called, he justified and
glorified.*

Bellarmine. *No one can know who are called and justified according to
purpose.*

Protestant reply. No single person certainly can know all such people, but
Bellarmine did not dare deny that those effectually called can know that they
have been called according to God's purpose. And the apostle teaches it as
clearly as if he had written it with a sunbeam, when he proposes this for the
consolation of the faithful and afterward applies it

to himself with the other faithful, out of a certain and invincible
persuasion, *verses 31, 32, 34, 37, and 39.* It is also sufficiently evident
from *verse 28* that all *who love God* are also *called according to purpose,*
and from 2 Timothy 1:9 that all who are called *by grace* are also called *by
purpose.*

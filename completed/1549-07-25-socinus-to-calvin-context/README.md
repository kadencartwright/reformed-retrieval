# Socinus to Calvin, letter 1231

## Work identity

- **Author:** Laelius Socinus (signed “Laelius Sozinus” in the edition)
- **Recipient:** John Calvin
- **Work:** Latin letter 1231
- **Date and place:** Basel, in the house of Master Münster, 25 July 1549
- **Language:** Latin, with one Greek term

This is Socinus’s letter, not a work by Calvin. It is included because it preserves the questions to which Calvin’s letters 1212 and 1323 respond, especially the questions about private and papal baptism.

## Edition and witness identity

The transcription follows *Ioannis Calvini opera quae supersunt omnia*, vol. XIII (*Corpus Reformatorum* XLI; *Thesauri epistolici Calviniani* IV), edited by Wilhelm Baum, Eduard Cunitz, and Eduard Reuss (Brunswick: C. A. Schwetschke, 1875), letter 1231, printed pp. 337–340.

The editors identify their source as Socinus’s autograph in Geneva, Codex 112, fol. 238. They also state that the autograph contains no punctuation, that its handwriting is highly involved, and that its Latin is that of a beginner. The punctuation reproduced in `latin.txt` is consequently the 1875 editors’ intervention, not autograph punctuation. Work, autograph, printed edition, normalized transcription, and English translation should not be conflated.

## Contents and completeness

The transcription includes everything the edition prints as letter 1231: the address to Calvin; the main letter about the resurrection of the body; Socinus’s place, date, and subscription; and the copy of his earlier questions written on the other side of the folio. Those appended questions concern marriage, presentation of a child for papal baptism, baptism performed in play or by a private person, the baptism of a convert’s household staff, and attendance at the Mass.

The main letter’s subscription appears before the copied questions because of the folio arrangement described by Socinus and preserved by the edition. The question section ends with *ne spem meam frustreris supplico* on p. 340. No beginning or ending is known to be missing from the edition’s text.

The following nineteenth-century editorial matter is excluded from `latin.txt`:

- the running header, letter number, editorial title, and summary;
- the witness and handwriting note;
- the footnote explaining that Calvin’s 26 June answer had not yet reached Socinus; and
- the footnote identifying “Master Münster” as Sebastian Münster.

The final bracketed paragraph in `latin.txt` records these omissions and is not part of Socinus’s letter.

## Transcription policy

`latin.txt` was collated against rendered images of every relevant printed page, not accepted from OCR alone. Printer line-end hyphenation and prose line wrapping are removed; unambiguous OCR artifacts and typographical ligatures are normalized. The 1875 edition’s spelling, capitalization, and supplied punctuation—including *Melanchton*, *inprimis*, *adstare*, and the signature *Sozinus*—are otherwise retained. The Greek word is transcribed as *μετεμψυχώσεως*.

`[CO XIII, p. N]` marks each printed page. Where a page boundary divides a word, the marker precedes the whole joined word beginning on the new page. This affects *expetendum* (p. 338) and *vivere* (p. 339).

## Translation policy and status

`english.md` is a fresh translation made directly from this Latin transcription. No wording was taken from a copyrighted modern translation. Because the autograph lacks punctuation and Socinus’s syntax is often irregular, the translation does not silently regularize every ambiguity. Footnotes identify the genuinely uncertain constructions, particularly the bodies-and-food comparison, the shared-matter or “substrate” problem, the scope of *credunt* in the baptism question, and the unusual *indemnem* in the Mass question.

Both transcription and translation are AI-assisted drafts checked once against the page images. They have not received independent review by a specialist in early modern Latin or comparison with the autograph itself. The 1875 Latin and exact printed-page locator should accompany any quotation used for close interpretation.

Before the rise of Luther, the Albigenses and others acted rightly—*

[Printed p. 57 | Google/KBNL-UvA PDF 108 | GB 9IhiAAAAcAAJ]

*by making a negative separation—for they did not share in the Roman
Catholics' idolatry—although before their condemnation, persecution, and
expulsion they did not
make a positive separation by establishing another new visible Church. Where
there are some fundamental errors in a Church, it is not permissible at once
to make a positive separation. Christ commanded people to hear those who sat
in Moses' chair, although they taught some things that overthrew the foundation
and rejected Christ. Nor does separation from an orthodox Church because of
some errors in it separate one from Christ, because only separation from the
orthodox faith separates one from Christ, being opposed to inward communion
with Christ and the Church. But merely outward separation is not inward
separation. Indeed, one who separates in this way retains inward communion
with the Church in the orthodox faith, and therefore with Christ.

We regard as sickly and nerveless the Remonstrants' argument by which they are
unwilling to separate from Arians, Socinians, Antitrinitarians, and
Anabaptists, on the ground that they do not dare consign all such people to
hell and banish them from heaven—as though the rule of our communion with the
Church were not God's Word, but our judgment concerning people's salvation and
damnation!

But if in outward communion we are compelled to participate in errors, even
nonfundamental ones, in a Church that nevertheless does not err in the-*

[Printed p. 58 | Google/KBNL-UvA PDF 109 | GB 9IhiAAAAcAAJ]

*foundation, then to that extent it is permissible to make a partial, not a
total, separation; a negative, not a positive, one. The reason is that it is
never permissible to have fellowship with the unfruitful works of darkness,
Ephesians 5:11.

What Laud of Canterbury says in his sermon to the Star Chamber, p. 36, is
false and blasphemous: *The Christian religion is one and the same in the
Roman Church and in ours, and they are not different religions.* He adduces a
worthless reason: *Because*—he says—*the learned acknowledge only three
religions: paganism, Judaism, and Christianity, to which they now add a fourth,
Turkism.*

Potter, *On the Lack of Charity*, chapter 6, section 3, p. 63: *The most
necessary and fundamental truths which constitute the Church are called into
question by neither side—that is, neither by the Roman Catholics nor by the
Protestants.* Page 68: *We Protestants still retain a true and real union in
faith and charity with the Roman Church.*

But on the contrary, the Roman Catholics do not possess the true faith; they
sin against fundamental truths. They do not share with us the Holy Scripture
as the sufficient canon of faith and morals, but append their traditions to it
and wish Scripture to be understood according to them. They have not Christ,
but the Roman pope—that is, Antichrist—as their head and Lord, as the
infallible master of faith, interpreter of Scripture, and judge of religious
controversies.

[Printed p. 59 | Google/KBNL-UvA PDF 110 | GB 9IhiAAAAcAAJ]

They utterly deny that God alone is to be religiously worshiped and invoked,
and therefore worship creatures, angels, human beings, bread, images, and
idols. They abolish Christ's mediatorial office by adding other mediators and
saviors to him. They deny Christ's satisfaction rendered by a bloody death
and his obedience rendered for us, since they wish to make satisfaction to God
for sins by themselves and by others. To Christ's bloody sacrifice they join
an unbloody one of the Antichristian Mass, fabricated by themselves, as useful
and necessary for appeasing God, making satisfaction for sins, and taking away
sins and punishments. And they contend that in the divine judgment a person is
justified and saved not freely, by the sheer grace of God, on account of
Christ's obedience rendered for us and applied by faith, but by their inherent
righteousness and the merits of their works. We have no union in faith with
those who overturn the faith in this manner.

2. Laud pointlessly enumerates four religions differing in genus, as though
there were not more differing in the lowest species, and as though the
Christian religion were one species—true, sound, and fundamental—among our
people, Roman Catholics, Socinians, Arians, Anabaptists, Libertines,
Antinomians, and all impure and diabolical sects that peddle the Christian
religion.

3. Perhaps he is satisfied with Medina's statement in *On Right Faith in God*,
book 4, chapter 6, that the faith of the Decalogue is preserved in these-*

[Printed p. 60 | Google/KBNL-UvA PDF 111 | GB 9IhiAAAAcAAJ]

*implicitly known precepts: *“Do not do to another what you do not wish to be
done to you,”* and *“Whatever you wish people to do to you, do also to them.”*
And he may be satisfied with Sotus on book 4, distinction 5, sole question, and
Vega, book 6 on the Council of Trent, chapter 20: *No more fundamental truths
are required for salvation than believing that God wishes to come to the
world's aid and to reconcile it by such means as please his providence.* And
with this from chapter 2 of the Turkish Koran: *A person can be saved in any
religion, provided that he does nothing against his own conscience.* Yet in
chapters 28 and 47 they teach that *human blessedness consists in food and
drink.*

4. These innovators, who measure all faith by royal courts and regard profit
as piety, think that a catholic catechism can be written embracing a summary
of fundamentals to which everyone can subscribe. For in this way Jews and
Christians can subscribe to the summary of the Old Testament—which certainly
implicitly contains the New Testament and all fundamentals—and constitute one
true Church. For if we swear to the fundamental articles confusedly and
implicitly, understanding them according to that vague and indefinite sense
which the Holy Spirit intends even though it is unknown to us in particular,
then surely Jews and we can in this way rightly and duly subscribe to the Old
Testament. But if each sect has its own contrary and mutually opposed senses
of those articles, then there is no one fundamental catechism in which we may
all rest satisfied. Rather, there are and will be as-*

[Printed p. 61 | Google/KBNL-UvA PDF 112 | GB 9IhiAAAAcAAJ]

*many catechisms with respect to their meanings as there are different
opinions among the different sects. For teaching must be accepted according to
its meaning, not according to
letters and syllables. Otherwise it would be permissible to subscribe to any
general teaching. Thus the Arians would swear that Christ is God, but they
would understand him to be God only in name, not essentially.

I do not deny that there is a broad distinction between one who hates and
detests a fundamental doctrine of the faith and one who overthrows such a
doctrine by a sufficiently plain consequence. Thus an Arian denies and detests
this: *Christ is God, consubstantial with the Father.* Likewise the Marcionites
not only deny but also hate and detest this: *Christ is a true human being,
like us in all things except sin.* By an evident consequence the Roman
Catholics and Ubiquitarians deny the proposition *Christ is a true human
being*, but in words they confess, love, and embrace it. If there were not a
perverse disposition toward evident consequences, they would by no means err
in fundamentals. Therefore theirs is an error by consequence, not a direct
error.

Before the giving of the law there were fewer fundamentals. Adam was bound to
believe in his own misery, the remission of sins and salvation through the
Savior who was to be incarnate, the last judgment, and the resurrection of the
dead, concerning which Enoch prophesied—*

[Printed p. 62 | Google/KBNL-UvA PDF 113 | GB 9IhiAAAAcAAJ]

*Jude verse 14—were things he was bound to believe. Nevertheless, there was no
time in which Adam was bound to believe only the
fundamentals of the law of nature, unless perhaps during that interval between
the first fall and the revealed Gospel. William of Paris in his treatise *On
Faith*, and William of Auxerre in book 3, treatise 3, chapter 5—as Bañez says
on 2.2, question 24, article 8—taught that in the time of grace it is not
necessary to believe any article of faith explicitly, but that it is enough to
believe everything that mother Church holds and believes. But Domingo Bañez,
Gregory of Valencia, and others are ashamed of this opinion. Aquinas, however,
does not speak badly at 2.2, question 1, article 7, reply: *Over the succession
of times, the articles of faith increased—not with respect to faith, but with
respect to explicit and express profession. For all the things believed
explicitly and in greater number by later people were implicitly believed in
smaller number by the earlier fathers.*

Rules can perhaps be given for distinguishing fundamentals from
nonfundamentals.

1. A fundamental article is one concerning Christ the Mediator and concerning
faith and repentance, and one which has such a necessary connection with these
that, if it is overthrown, the doctrine concerning Christ, faith, and
repentance collapses. Hence one errs in the foundation: 1. When the deity is
denied. 2. When we think heterodoxly about God and the Trinity. 3. When we
invent three gods. 4. When Christ is denied expressly or by-*

[Printed p. 63 | Google/KBNL-UvA PDF 114 | GB 9IhiAAAAcAAJ]

*evident consequence. 5. When an idol is put in Christ's place, as when the
idol of bread is
worshiped as Christ. 6. When hope and trust are placed in creatures, with
providence ruined. 7. When something is taught that is directly opposed to a
fundamental head of faith and Christian practice.

2. Rule. Knowledge of those things without which one can believe in Christ is
not a fundamental article. Hence knowledge of Christ's miracles is not a
fundamental article. They were instituted only for this end: that we might
believe, John 20:30–31. Since, therefore, many savingly believe in Christ who
have known little or nothing about his miracles, certain Roman Catholics are
wrong to deny that *the Apostles' Creed embraces everything to be believed*,
on the ground that it says nothing about Christ's miracles, circumcision, and
baptism.

3. Rule. The Apostles' Creed, the Decalogue, and the Lord's Prayer, with
respect to their letter and what they say expressly, are not an exact rule for
identifying fundamentals. For Roman Catholics, Arians, Socinians, and all
heretics willingly subscribe to them. 2. Nor are these three a safe rule with
respect to a fuller explanation of all the doubts and questions arising from
the Apostles' Creed, Decalogue, and Lord's Prayer. They are a rule only with
respect to the explanation supplied by other creeds, such as the Athanasian
and Chalcedonian, and with respect to the easier and plainer heads explained
in these.

[Printed p. 64 | Google/KBNL-UvA PDF 115 | GB 9IhiAAAAcAAJ]

4. Rule. It is one thing to be some part of a fundamental doctrine and another
to be the fundamental doctrine itself. Whatever is expressly in God's Word is
some part of a fundamental article, because it is God's Word and is resolved
into a fundamental principle, namely, that Holy Scripture is the canon of
faith and morals. Therefore every error concerning what is in God's Word in
some manner and remotely conflicts with the fundamental articles. A
fundamental doctrine itself is either expressly in Scripture or follows from
Scripture by the clearest consequence, and is such that ignorance of it
excludes from salvation.

### Is the Roman Church truly a Church?

For resolving the question, the following distinctions must be considered.

1. The Roman curia is one thing, the seduced common people another, and the
crowd of the weak another.

2. A materially true Church is one thing; a formally true Church another.

3. A Church *in concreto* is one thing; a Church *in abstracto* another.

4. A physically or metaphysically true Church is one thing; an ethically and
morally true Church another.

5. A calling assembly is one thing; a called assembly another.

6. The teaching Church is one thing; the Church as recipient and user another.

7. The Church *in becoming*, or according to pastoral or ecclesiastical being,
is one thing; the Church *as constituted*, or according to Christian being,
another.

8. It is one thing for the Roman Church to be saved; it is an-*

[Printed p. 65 | Google/KBNL-UvA PDF 116 | GB 9IhiAAAAcAAJ]

*other thing for salvation to exist in the Roman Church.

9. The Church considered speculatively is one thing; considered practically,
another.

10. A Church *κατὰ τί*—in some respect—is one thing; a Church simply speaking,
another.

11. A pastoral act ratified by formal consent is one thing; one ratified
ecclesiastically and finally, another.

12. A mark of the Church *in becoming* is one thing; a mark of the same Church
*as constituted*, another.

13. A Church professing rejection of the foundation, such as the Jewish, is
one thing; a Church professing the foundation, such as the Roman, another.

Assertion 1. The theologians do not assert that the Roman Church is wholly and
altogether nonexistent. Junius, volume 2, his separate book *On the Church*,
chapter 27: *In part it is still called Ammi—my people.* See the other passages
cited above, and also Calvin, Tilenus while he was still orthodox, and Rivet.
We do not number among our own those innovators who strive to reconcile us
with the Roman Church. They teach that the Roman Church should be called a
Church simply and without qualification, and that it truly is one with
metaphysical truth, although it is a languid and sick Church, just as a sick
human being is called a human being simply. Potter, *On Charity Badly
Understood*, chapter 6, section 3, p. 63: *The most necessary and fundamental
truths which constitute the Church are called into doubt by neither
side—neither the Roman Catholics nor the Protestants.* Page 68: *We Protestants
still retain a true and real union in faith and charity.* So too Laud against-*

[Printed p. 66 | Google/KBNL-UvA PDF 117 | GB 9IhiAAAAcAAJ]

*Fisher, section 20, pp. 30–31: *The Roman Church is not right—that is, not an
integral Church—yet it is true with the truth of essence, receiving Scripture
as the rule of faith and both sacraments as causes and seals of grace.* But in
this manner the Roman Church is not integral but liable to errors, although it
possesses the true essence of the Church. And thus, according to Laud, the
English and Scottish Churches—indeed, no Reformed Church—would be both true
and free from errors, because on this account perhaps only the Church
triumphant in heaven is a true Church. Yet this does not prevent the Roman
Church from being as truly a Church with metaphysical truth as the Protestant
Church. In section 35, p. 281, no. 1, Laud cites some of our writers asserting
that the Roman Church is truly a Church. He cites Junius, whose meaning we
shall consider; he cites Luther against the Anabaptists from Bellarmine, *On
the Marks of the Church*, book 4, chapter 47: *We confess*—he says—*that under
the papacy there is very much good, indeed every Christian good.* He cites
Richard Field, *On the Church*, book 3, chapter 47, saying: *There are some in
the Roman Church at such a degree of orthodoxy that we can hope well for their
salvation.* But these men say nothing other than that a Church exists under the
papacy, which is not in question. Joseph Hall, Shelford, William
Chillingworth, and other theologians in England hankering after episcopacy
teach the same with them. Others say that their errors are fundamentally
destructive by reduction, not fundamental in themselves. John Jewel, bishop
of Salisbury, however, speaks otherwise in the *Apol-*

[Marginal: `A`.]

[Printed p. 67 | Google/KBNL-UvA PDF 118 | GB 9IhiAAAAcAAJ]

*ogy for the Church of England*: *They are accustomed to boast that they are
the Church of God. Aristotle says that bastards cannot constitute a state.
Whether a Church can be constituted
from them, let them see; certainly they are neither legitimate abbots nor
genuine bishops.* Page 147: *What if the law has perished from the priest and
counsel from the elders? What if, as Micah says, night is to them instead of
vision?* And so forth.

Assertion 2. The Roman curia is one thing, the blind and miserably seduced
common people another, and the assembly of the weak another. Likewise, an
assembly professing and teaching papism and imposing it on others' consciences
is one thing; a papal assembly following blind leaders is another; and the
crowd of the weak groaning under the papacy is yet another.

Assertion 3. If the word *Church* is taken for the assembly of believers for
whom Christ delivered himself, Ephesians 5:25–26, then the Roman Church and
papal curia as such do not retain even the essence of the Church. 1. Because
that assembly obstinately errs in fundamentals, transforms God's Word into the
word of human beings, overthrows Christ's natures and offices, and professes
and teaches open idolatry. 2. Grant that this assembly professed all the
fundamentals verbally and acknowledged God's Word as the rule of faith. It
would still not thereby be proved that the Roman curia is metaphysically the
Church and bride of Christ, which cleaves to Christ the bridegroom by true
faith. For there is no impure Christian sect that would not subscribe to the
Apostles' Creed, the Decalogue, and the Lord's Prayer. Would-*

[Printed p. 68 | Google/KBNL-UvA PDF 119 | GB 9IhiAAAAcAAJ]

*that subscription overthrow formally fundamental errors? And grant that a
most impure sect believed enough of God's Word to be capable
of saving a person—as the Jews today profess that they believe the Old
Testament—it does not thereby become truly and physically a Church. For it is
one thing for salvation to be in the Word which some assembly acknowledges as
God's Word, and another for that assembly to be an assembly of true believers.
For in true faith attention must be paid not only to faith's material object,
but also to its formal object.

Assertion 4. The blind and miserably seduced common people who follow blind
leaders are a Church, insofar as they follow those leaders, no more than the
unconverted, deaf, and senseless among us are considered to be materially in
the Church—that is, in the assembly of believers—but are not formal believers
or formal members of the invisible Church. I say *insofar as they follow those
leaders*, because a different judgment must be made about what may happen to
the seduced through God's Word, which exists, sounds in some manner, and is
read among them, even if they live and die in the papal assembly. Indeed, a
different judgment must be made about what may happen to the impure members of
the curia themselves through the Word of God which it professes, as will be
seen later.

The members of the curia are materially the visible Church, just as scandalous
people in a true visible Church are in it materially and can be called. You
will say that the Jewish assembly is thus a Church, since Jews can-*

[Printed p. 69 | Google/KBNL-UvA PDF 120 | GB 9IhiAAAAcAAJ]

*be called. Reply. It does not follow, because the Jews have not been called by proximate
potency—not even outwardly—but are yet to be called. The curia itself,
however, is an outwardly called assembly and thus materially the proximate
matter of the Church. Nor do we deny that certain people from the curia, just
as formerly certain Pharisees, have been inwardly called to Christ.

Assertion 5. Beneath the papacy there exists a true Church, with both
metaphysical and ethical truth. 1. Because under papal tyranny there groans
that crowd which calls us blessed who have gone out from Babylon and heartily
detests their idolatry and errors. Nor does this assembly cease to be a true
Church because it lacks the Church's essential mark, the pure profession of
God's Word, for profession is either potential or actual. That crowd is in the
first act ready to profess Christ and die for him, while actual profession is
a mark of the true visible Church. Christ himself did not profess his noble
testimony at every moment, but only in the due place and time, before Pontius
Pilate. I add that there is a negative profession, when godly people in hiding
do not profess idolatry or bend the knee to Baal. This profession is a mark of
the invisible Church in hiding. Another is a positive profession, when someone
openly resists idolatry and openly testifies that corrupt teaching is hated by
him from the heart. This is nothing other than a mark of the visible Church.
Negative profession arises from weakness and some-*

[Printed p. 70 | Google/KBNL-UvA PDF 121 | GB 9IhiAAAAcAAJ]

*times from godly prudence; positive profession arises from zeal for the faith.

Assertion 6. Hence it is not surprising that the Roman Church *in concreto* is
sometimes called a true Church, even physically, since denomination is often
made from the more excellent part. Papality *in abstracto* can no more be
called the Church or the Church's essential form than gangrene, fever, or gout
is called a human being or an essential part of one. Hence, the Roman Church
considered materially, consisting of persons to some of whom the essence of
the Church applies and to some of whom its name but not its essence and
definition applies—since there are various believing people and destroyers of
the faith in that assembly—is one thing; the Roman Church considered formally
is another.

Assertion 7. *Church* is taken in two ways. 1. As *in becoming*, according to
pastoral or ecclesiastical being—that is, the Church calling or teaching by the
Word and sacraments, for all these are the same; or, according to Christian or
inward being, by reason of faith—that is, the Church *as constituted* and
established in its essence. The reason for this distinction is: 1. Because the
outward form of the Church through calling pastors and a called people is one
thing; that is, outward κλῆσις, or calling, is one thing, while the Church's
inward form and inward calling is another. 2. Its foundation is in the
Scriptures, because Zion and Jerusalem are sometimes named from the pastoral
act which belongs to the Church by reason of its outward form—*

[Printed p. 71 | Google/KBNL-UvA PDF 122 | GB 9IhiAAAAcAAJ]

*by reason of the rulers of the Church, whose task it is to announce salvation.
Isaiah 40:9: *Climb a high mountain, O you who bring good news to Zion; lift
your voice powerfully, you who bring good news to Jerusalem; lift it up, do
not fear; say to the cities of Judah, “Behold your God!”* Isaiah 54:1: *Sing,
O barren woman who does not bear; break forth in singing, raise your voice, you
who do not labor in childbirth, for the children of the desolate woman will be
more numerous than those of the married woman, says Jehovah.* Galatians
4:26–28. Isaiah 49:21: *So that you say in your heart, “Who has borne these for
me, when I was bereaved and solitary?”* Isaiah 66:11–12; John 20:21–23. The
power of loosing and binding is given to the rulers, and the Church is named
from what is proper to the rulers: *a woman bearing children to God*, Isaiah
66:11–12. Revelation 12:1: *A woman clothed with the sun, on whose head is a
crown of twelve stars.* And it is named from what chiefly concerns the rulers,
1 Timothy 3:15: *The Church of the living God, the pillar and support of the
truth.* Therefore, according to the language of Scripture, the assembly which
offers the breasts of ministry to its children, Song 8:1, and mother Jerusalem,
Galatians 4:26, is called the Church. At least the Church is denominated the
Church from that act.

If *Church* is taken for the teaching or outwardly calling Church, or for the
Church according to pastoral being and insofar as it exercises certain
pastoral acts by the outward ministry—the acts of teaching the Word,
admonishing those who err, correcting scandalous people, converting some to
God, and administering the sacraments, especially baptism—

[Printed p. 72 | Google/KBNL-UvA PDF 123 | GB 9IhiAAAAcAAJ]

which is regarded as valid and ratified even when administered by the Roman
Church—then in this manner the Roman Church also, as distinguished from the
invisible crowd of weak believers groaning under the papacy, is called a
Church by Junius and some of our writers, not reduplicatively as papal, but *in
concreto*. Nor in this sense would I altogether deny that the Roman Church is a
Church. *Everything divine which it possesses*—Junius says in the cited
place—*is from God; everything corrupt which it possesses is from itself.
Insofar as it possesses everything divine, it is the Church; insofar as it
possesses all those same things corrupted, it is a corrupt Church.*

Whitaker, controversy 2, question 3, chapter 2: *For the Roman Catholics
possessed certain things which belong to the true Church, namely baptism—even
if not rightly administered—Scripture also, and a certain ministry.*

And Calvin, *Institutes*, book 4, chapter 4, section 11: *I call them Churches
insofar as the Lord wonderfully preserves there the remnants of his people,
however miserably scattered and dispersed; insofar as some symbols of the
Church remain, especially those whose efficacy neither the devil's cunning nor
human depravity can destroy.*

In this manner the Roman Church is a Church with metaphysical truth. 1.
Because an assembly possessing the Word, though hidden under a bushel, as Rivet
says, the marriage documents, and baptism, the ratified and valid seal of the
covenant, is physically a Church. But the Ro-*

[Printed p. 73 | Google/KBNL-UvA PDF 124 | GB 9IhiAAAAcAAJ]

*man Church is such an assembly. Therefore, and so forth. The reason for the
major premise is that in an assembly which retains absolutely nothing of the essence of the Church, such
as an assembly of pagans, there is neither a marriage contract nor the seal of
that contract. But an assembly which possesses that by which it is essentially
distinguished from an assembly possessing absolutely nothing of the Church
possesses something of the Church's essence according to ecclesiastical and
pastoral being. It is true that our theologians—especially Whitaker,
controversy 2, question 6, chapter 3—contend by various arguments that the
Roman Church is not the true Church of Christ. But it must be known that the
Roman assembly, consisting of the hierarchy and its members, must be
considered in two ways: 1. As pastors. 2. As such pastors, namely,
Antichristian bishops distinguished in species from presbyters. 3. An assembly
in which there is, on one side, the formal instrument of active calling—the
Word and sacraments—and, on the other, passive calling—that is, people begotten
and reborn to God through that instrument—is truly a Church with metaphysical
truth according to pastoral and ecclesiastical being. But the Roman hierarchy
or curia is such. 1. Because they call actively through the Word, and through
the Word sounding among them some are begotten as children to God. Therefore
the curia itself is in this manner the Church. 2. This is confirmed by the fact
that Roman Catholic bishops and doctors are capable of the sin against the
Holy Spirit, which occurs only in those among whom there is active calling and
the Word of the Church. 3. It is con-*

[Printed p. 74 | Google/KBNL-UvA PDF 125 | GB 9IhiAAAAcAAJ]

*firmed because unbelieving people who reject the foundation can exercise truly
pastoral acts. They can preach Christ, Philippians 1:15–17; they can truly
administer the
sacraments and pastorally exhibit the seals of grace and the covenant to the
people.

3. Because God's Word, however it sounds, and God's sacraments, however
corrupted, so long as they remain God's Word and sacraments in their essence,
do not derive efficacy from human beings, even if they are most holy, nor lose
their efficacy, even if the instruments are ungodly and unbelieving. Instead,
they derive their efficacy from God and divine institution. To explain this
more fully, it must be noted: 1. Certain promises are made to the Church only
with respect to true believers, such as these: *The Holy Spirit will lead you
into all truth; I will be with you until the consummation of the age; I will
give you a new heart.* These are set before an assembly consisting of goats
and sheep, but they are by no means made to the goats. 2. Certain things belong
to an assembly gathered from goats and sheep with respect to each and every
one, such as hearing the Word and receiving the sacraments. 3. Certain things
also belong to the ungodly which are nevertheless truly ecclesiastical, as:
*Whatever you bind on earth will be bound in heaven.* As Augustine says on
Psalm 61: *It is known that citizens of an evil city administer certain acts of
a good city.* The acts of preaching, administering the sacraments, binding,
and loosing-*

50: *The reprobate can be called God's house because of outward calling and

*former manner the Roman Church is truly a Church, because it delivers and
teaches all the fundamentals from knowledge of which saving faith and the
practice of the Christian life can be drawn, even though it adds other things
which are destructive of those fundamentals. Thus a Church that is
speculatively true is true with metaphysical truth, just as a sick human being
is truly human. But it is not true with ethical truth; it is impure and
corrupt. Yet this is a partial corruption which does not take away the essence
of the Church, just as a sick person does not lose human essence because of
diseases. Thus the baptism of heretical and papal pastors is true with
metaphysical truth, just as a person baptized by an adulterous and scandalous
minister is truly baptized. For baptism derives its validity from Christ's
institution, not from the minister. Thus Augustine replied in book 2 *Against
the Letters of Petilian*, chapter 108: *Thus in heretics we approve baptism—not
the heretics' baptism, but Christ's—just as in fornicators, idolaters, and so
forth we approve not their baptism, but Christ's.* Thus Balaam the false
prophet prophesied orthodox doctrines concerning the star of Jacob, that is,
the Messiah, yet practically remained a false prophet. In the same way the
Roman Church is not practically a true Church, because it does not itself
believe savingly the fundamentals which it delivers. This is similar to the
distinction by which dialecticians distinguish logic as teaching from logic as
used. For one Church teaches and another uses. And if the Church is considered
*as constituted* and according to Christian being—

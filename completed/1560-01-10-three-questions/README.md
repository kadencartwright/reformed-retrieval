# On Three Questions (10 January 1560)

This directory presents the complete Latin-form witness and a new English
translation of the consilium headed *Ad tria quaedam capita* in the *Calvini
Opera*.

## Work and witness identity

| Field | Value |
|---|---|
| Author | John Calvin; colleagues expressly approved the response |
| CO consilium | VII.3, *Ad tria quaedam capita* |
| Recipient | Unnamed singular “dearest brother” |
| Internal date | 10 January 1560 |
| Place | Not stated |
| Authorial language | French according to the transmission note; archetype lost |
| Language presented here | Latin |
| Genre | Ecclesiastical and ethical advice answering three questions |
| Topics | Ministers and monetary returns, oaths in the consistory, civil magistrates serving in ecclesiastical oversight |
| CO locator | X/1, pp. 262–263 |

The three answers concern a minister receiving returns from invested money,
the requirement of oaths in the consistory, and the participation there of men
who also hold civil jurisdiction. The final paragraph says Calvin consulted
named-but-unidentified brothers who approved his answer so that it would not
appear to be his judgment alone. The document is therefore Calvin's response
with an explicit collegial check, not a formally subscribed corporate act.

CO states that the French archetype is not extant. Its Latin text survives in
the collected *Epistolae et responsa* tradition and is treated in the corpus
crosswalk as a translation from French. This directory presents that
public-domain Latin-form witness; it neither reconstructs the lost French nor
claims that the Latin wording is Calvin's unrevised original phrasing. The
displayed title is a collected-edition heading rather than an internal title.

## Source and transcription

The transcription follows Wilhelm Baum, Eduard Cunitz, and Eduard Reuss, eds.,
*Ioannis Calvini opera quae supersunt omnia*, vol. X, part 1 (*Corpus
Reformatorum* XXXVIII; Brunswick: C. A. Schwetschke, 1871), printed pp.
262–263. Printed p. 262 occurs on UNIGE PDF p. 152 and p. 263 on PDF p. 153.
The volume is available through the University of Geneva's [complete Calvin
collection](https://archive-ouverte.unige.ch/unige:650) and as a [direct volume
X/1 PDF](https://access.archive-ouverte.unige.ch/access/metadata/f526a338-c95c-4f87-b600-04df723f502b/download).

The local wrapper used for collation has SHA-256
`025973d6e4533769d0c2b870dd324497943bc14050eb2bcbcc4c964d4f2a1777`.
Both source openings were inspected at 300 DPI. The transcription excludes
the CO heading, running heads, page numbers, and title footnote while retaining
the complete response and date. No word is split at the p. 262/263 boundary;
the marker falls between *a me* and *requiris*, and the English deliberately
preserves the corresponding break between “you” and “ask.”

The CO note identifies earlier Geneva, Lausanne, Hanover, Chouet, and Amsterdam
printings and reports the lost French archetype; it supplies no manuscript
variant for the Latin body.

## Translation status and use

Mary D. Beaty and Benjamin Wirt Farley's copyrighted *Calvin's Ecclesiastical
Advice* (1991) translates the complete 46-item *consilia* corpus. The audit
located no complete reusable public-domain English version of this document.
No wording from that modern translation was consulted or reused;
[`english.md`](english.md) is a fresh translation from the Latin-form witness.

This is an AI-assisted working edition checked against the facsimile, not a
peer-reviewed critical edition. The lost French, unnamed correspondent, and
missing questions limit reconstruction of the immediate cases. It is a
historical source, not present-day financial or legal advice.

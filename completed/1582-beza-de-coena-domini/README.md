# Theodore Beza, *De coena Domini, plana et perspicua tractatio* (1582)

This directory contains an AI-assisted working transcription and independent
English translation of Beza's treatise against Joachim Westphal.

## Controlling witness and extent

- Controlling witness: Theodore Beza, *Tractationum theologicarum*, vol. I
  (Geneva, 1582), printed pp. 211–258.
- Local extract: `/tmp/reformed-retrieval-beza-sources/de-coena-1582.pdf`.
- Extract mapping: PDF page 1 is e-rara platform boilerplate; PDF pages 2–49
  are printed pp. 211–258, corresponding to logical scans 235–282 of the full
  volume.
- The treatise begins with its title and response “Ad caput primum” on p. 211.
  Its final authorial sentence ends on p. 258. The catchword at the foot of
  p. 258 points to the adjacent *Κρεοφαγία, sive Cyclops* and is excluded.

The OCR files `vol1-1570-ocr.txt` and `vol1-1570-ocr.xml` contain the same
work with the same printed pagination. Correspondence was established by
line-for-line checks at the opening, middle, and endpoint. They are used only
as secondary reading aids; the 1582 images control every reading.

## Editorial method

The Latin is diplomatically normalized: long *s*, ligatures, line-end
hyphenation, and purely typographical capitalization are normalized, while
Beza's spelling, syntax, headings, quotations, and substantive wording are
retained. Printed-page markers are supplied throughout. Marginal references
are incorporated in square brackets. Illegible or genuinely uncertain text is
marked `[[...]]` and explained in an editorial note.

The English is a fresh translation made directly from the Latin. David Noe's
modern translation was neither consulted nor used. This is an AI-assisted
working edition, not a critical edition; all quotations and references should
be checked against the facsimile before scholarly citation.

## Source-critical note

- Printed p. 245 (PDF 36), first sentence under “Ad Caput XXXV”: the faded
  Greek after *addidisti* was resolved by collation with the much clearer 1559
  first edition (printed p. 123; PDF p. 128 of the e-rara copy). It reads
  `ἐκ περιουσίας`, “as something beyond what was needed.”

No unresolved reading or missing leaf remains within the treatise. The isolated Greek word
at the foot of p. 258 belongs to the next work and is not part of this text.

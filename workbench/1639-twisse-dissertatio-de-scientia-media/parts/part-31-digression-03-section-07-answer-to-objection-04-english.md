[Book I, printed p. 69; BSB canvas 00095]

A bad knot requires a bad wedge. Navarrete therefore answers that if God’s production of things is
considered as it stands on God’s side—and how could the production of angels or the creation of this
universe at the beginning be considered otherwise?—it is measured by eternity. Yet it must not be
conceded absolutely that he produces things in eternity, because the actual production of things
posits only a certain change on the side of the things. Since this change occurs in time, the things
themselves are also produced in time. On eternity’s side, however, no change occurs; rather, things
have permanent and uniform being in eternity. Therefore, for God

[Book I, printed p. 70; BSB canvas 00096]

to intuit things practically in eternity, it suffices that he produce them in time, because that
knowledge, on the side of the things it intuits, denotes change and production in time. God intuits
things according as, on the thing’s side, they are future in time; consequently that knowledge is
practical and productive of things in time.

To the confirmation it is answered that it does not belong to the nature of intuitive knowledge to
presuppose the thing at which it is terminated. It suffices that it be simultaneous with the thing,
considering the thing not as produced but as the terminus of knowledge. Thus, although the thing is
not produced in eternity, it nevertheless terminates intuitive knowledge in eternity, and indeed
terminates it for the time in which it is to be produced.

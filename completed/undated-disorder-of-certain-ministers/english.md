# On the Disorders of Certain Ministers

*A fresh translation of the complete surviving Latin presentation at CO X/1
215–217. CO's exact hybrid heading is* De certorum ministrorum ΑΤΑΞΙΑΙ; *the
heading and page notices are editorial, not words silently attributed to the
historical body.*

[CO X/1, p. 215]

May the love of God our Father and the grace of our Lord Jesus Christ always
be with you through the fellowship of the Holy Spirit.

We were deeply distressed by the report brought to us, dearest brothers, that
among you the word of God has been exposed to disgrace in this way, and by the
fault of those who ought to have led others by their example. For we hear that
the very men whose duty it was to exhort anyone among the common people to
peace and brotherly concord boiled up so far under the force of contention
that they themselves raised the cry to arms. What an unworthy and monstrous
deed! And how great a scandal will be spread everywhere by the report of it.
From this not only will contempt for God's word arise, since the scandal must
be traced back to ministers of the word, but an opportunity will also be
offered to Satan and his instruments to tear you up at last and scatter you so
thoroughly that, once the harm has been inflicted, scarcely any remedy can
then be applied.

Certainly, as concerns the church of God, everyone sees that today it is
besieged everywhere by enemies. Yet your own circumstances absolutely compel
you to preserve peace and concord by every means. For it is sufficiently

[CO X/1, p. 216]

clear that it depends on God's providence alone, which keeps your adversaries
in restraint, that those who have already planned your destruction so often
do not rush headlong into it when even the slightest occasion is offered.
Therefore, we cannot judge N. and N. otherwise than as men swept away by
impulse. All at once, along with regard for their ministry,
this consideration also escaped them: their audacity would bring utter ruin
upon all the churches there.

First of all, we cannot approve ministers of the word applying themselves to
bearing arms and handling affairs of that sort. We know that urgent necessity
sometimes causes individuals not to keep themselves so strictly within the
bounds of their office. We also know that dangers have grown so strong that
everyone everywhere has had to strive against them to the best of his
ability. Yet we cannot see how it can be tolerated that ministers of the word
become commanders of troops, and accordingly levy forces themselves and then
lead the forces they have assembled. Doing this causes a man to withdraw
correspondingly from his office and to degenerate utterly from his calling;
this is precisely what has happened to those men. It has caused them to grow
hardened to soldiers' ways and, having completely put off the character of
their ministry, to break out into military license.

The matter itself compels us to speak in this way. When we heard these things,
we lay utterly confounded, although, according to your letter, the wrong
committed by one is more serious than the other's. Now, since you await our
judgment, we have assembled in the Lord and thought that we should answer you
as follows.

First of all, unless we wish everything to be turned upside down, so unworthy
and monstrous a crime cannot be buried by concealment and connivance. If N.
were asked why he left the County of Montbéliard, his answer would be ready:
all things there were confused, because no established discipline existed.
Yet if we ourselves prescribe discipline for others, by how much tighter bonds
must we bind ourselves?

As for applying a remedy to this evil, we see well enough what trouble will
follow when those at fault will not accept correction but try to shake off
the yoke. Whatever happens, however, it cannot be endured that the evil should
be fostered by our timidity. Therefore, when the purpose is both to maintain
right order and to repress scandals, whatever gales and storms threaten, we
must not permit anything to be relaxed in constancy and legitimate courage,
especially where the bridle must be drawn tight. We consequently cannot see
how what happened with those men can be passed over in silence without the
Lord's curse clinging to you on that account and continually pressing upon
you.

The passage in 1 Timothy 5 with which N. thinks to defend himself helps him in
no way. Paul wished regard to be shown for God's servants there only so far as
their innocence should be safeguarded, since they are usually exposed to the
slanders

[CO X/1, p. 217]

of many. But N. is already held sufficiently convicted of two attempts at
homicide—a small firearm was discharged at N., and fire flashed from it on the
second occasion. In addition, he had already conducted himself inadequately
in his office among you, so that it is astonishing he could have been
tolerated for so long. We can in no way approve his continuing to retain a
charge of which he has shown himself wholly unworthy. In particular, his
obstinate refusal to yield to your most godly exhortation so aggravates his
deed that it is now clearly established that he must be dismissed.

As for the manner of proceeding, we give this counsel: choose men from your
assembly who, in the name of the whole assembly, are to explain to the
brothers at N. how, after suitable reasons were considered carefully and in
due form, it was adjudged that N. must no longer be tolerated in the ministry
he has exercised badly. They should accordingly ask the church of that place
to discharge him on its side as well, and approve the election of another to
be sent in his place, whom they too may recognize and approve. If the church
asks for the grounds of the judgment so that the whole matter may be clearer
to them, it will then be appropriate to set before them, point by point, the
same things we received from you.

But if the man has so enticed the people of the place and ensnared them by his
devices that they do not yield to your counsel and exhortation, they must then
be warned earnestly to consider how they are going to permit this. For if they
obstinately persist in it, they will show themselves to be schismatics, and
you will consequently be compelled to separate them too from the unity of
your body.

As for the other man, because there is more imprudence and impulsiveness in
him than settled wickedness, it seems it will be enough if his own church is
also informed that he is suspended from the ministry. This will serve both to
humble him and to repair the scandal, and only on this understanding: whenever
it seems good to them, they may call him back and receive him again as a
minister. If the question of calling him back arises, however, they must be
specifically warned that this cannot proceed unless they themselves also bury
every memory of his fault, do not reproach him in any way for what happened,
and do not allow this temporary chastisement to prejudice him, so that he
remains free afterward to correct others in the discharge of his ministry.
Indeed, his teaching must possess the full right and authority it deserves.

We would have wished to incline to a milder judgment if it had been possible,
but we feared that by sparing them the Lord would meanwhile spare all the
less. Farewell, dearest brothers. May the Lord protect you by his defense,
strengthen you by his Spirit, and always increase his gifts in you. At Geneva,
in our assembly.

Your John Calvin, in the name of all.

## Translator's notes

1. The editorial Greek `ΑΤΑΞΙΑΙ` is an unaccented uppercase nominative plural
   used as a quoted headword in a Latin heading. “Disorders” leaves room for
   both irregular conduct and insubordination.
2. The source behind this Latin presentation was French according to the
   editor cited by CO. No French wording has been reconstructed or silently
   substituted.
3. CO's `N.` can stand for a suppressed person or place. “The brothers at N.”
   in the procedural paragraph reflects the likely local sense but does not
   purport to identify the community.
4. The firearm parenthesis is unusually compressed. It plainly connects a
   small gun, two alleged attempts, and a flash on the second occasion, but
   supplies too little information to decide whether a projectile was fired
   in either event.
5. The counsel distinguishes the two cases: removal for the man judged
   obstinate and guilty of attempted lethal violence, but reparative
   suspension with a route back to full authority for the man judged rash
   rather than settled in wrongdoing.
6. The English was translated afresh from `latin.txt` and checked against the
   facsimile. The Beaty–Farley translation was not consulted or used for
   wording.

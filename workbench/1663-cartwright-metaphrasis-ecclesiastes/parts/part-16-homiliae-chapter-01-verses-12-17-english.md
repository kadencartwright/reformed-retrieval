VERSE 12. I, Ecclesiastes, was king over Israel in Jerusalem.

VERSE 13. Applying my mind to inquire and to explore wisdom concerning
everything that is done under the heavens (God gave this occupation as an
evil to the sons of men, that they might occupy themselves in it.)

VERSE 14. I saw all the deeds that are done under the Sun, and behold, all
those things are vanity and affliction of spirit.

VERSE 15. What is crooked cannot be corrected, and what is lacking cannot be
included in the number.

VERSE 16. I spoke with my mind, saying, Behold, I increased and added wisdom
beyond all who ruled before me in Jerusalem, and my mind perceived much
wisdom and knowledge.

VERSE 17. I also applied my mind to knowing wisdom and the knowledge of every
kind of madness and foolishness; I knew that this too was affliction of
spirit.

He places before this particular teaching about his own experience nearly the
same preface with which he prefaced the whole book, so that greater authority
might accrue to it. This teaching from the testimony of his own experience
(to which this preface is affixed) extends as far as the beginning of the
third chapter. The reason why he uses this preface is that we may know that
these things were done not when he was a boy and lived as a private person in
his father's house, but after he had been admitted to the helm of the
Commonwealth and had attained that divine and incomparable wisdom, together
with riches unheard of before that time. In their support and under their
auspices lay the greatest power for accomplishing those things in which men
place man's highest good.

For men of the greatest ability are often found who, being destitute of the
means with which to buy books, seek out teachers, and obtain other aids to
study, do not produce such rich fruits of their ability as they do when the
attendance of wealth and authority is joined to keenness of intellect. It
brings a great additional aid to persuasion concerning the teaching proposed
if someone teaches those things which he himself has experienced in himself.
This is required for a special reason in a Minister of the Word when teaching
about faith, the remission of sins, repentance, patience in adversities, and
so forth. Psalm 51:17; 1 Timothy 1:16. But what use Solomon had for wealth and
authority in attaining the things described later in this and the following
chapter will readily become clear from the treatment of the passage itself.

From this it is gathered that the rich and

those endowed with authority are to be blamed very greatly if they are
ignorant and unskilled, like simpletons and illiterate people, because, though
all things for acquiring wisdom have been supplied as if by a divine wand,
they fail themselves. Proverbs 17:16: To what purpose is a price in the hand
of a fool for acquiring wisdom, when he lacks the heart? From this it is clear
that wealth provides a great support for acquiring wisdom. It therefore comes
at great harm to the Commonwealth and the Church that those who devote
themselves to the study of letters are generally poor: deprived of many
literary aids, they cannot make such successful progress in this course of
letters. They themselves are also not free from grave reproach, because, when
enriched through letters and established in eminence—at the very time when
they are permitted to make greater progress through the benefit of wealth and
authority—they either slacken their studies or even cast away concern for
them. The rich, though well armed, are unwilling to serve in this literary
warfare; the poor, unarmed, cannot reach and penetrate to letters placed, as
it were, in a citadel and lofty tower.

VERSE 15. What is crooked cannot be corrected, and what is lacking cannot come
into number.

But it is better to hear Solomon's judgment on this matter, on the strength of
which he delivers this opinion concerning this study of wisdom. The argument,
moreover, is drawn from the effect. First, by the benefit of this wisdom, that
which is crooked and perverse in us is neither recalled nor brought back to
rectitude and integrity, or incorruption. For this wisdom concerning natural
things not only fails to amend the depraved morals of men, but does not even
show the way and means by which they might be rightly formed. This is readily
apparent from the Philosophers who devoted their effort to this wisdom and
spent the whole course of life in its study. Besides the fact that Gentile
Philosophers set sail for their own honor and sought the applause of men, it
has been handed down to memory that those who stood forth as leaders among
them were excessively devoted to refined curiosity in the conduct of their
lives. Add to this what the Apostle says: Knowledge puffs up. 1 Corinthians
1:8. Another effect is that it does not supply the countless things lacking
for uprightness of life. But if this liberal learning does not render a man
blessed, what can other, less liberal branches of learning accomplish—and
least of all pseudo-wisdom? In this class are Judicial Astrology, the
Philosopher's Stone, and the Ephesian arts, or Magic.

*Translator's note:* The edition prints `1. Cor. 1. 8.` for “Knowledge puffs
up”; the sentence occurs at 1 Corinthians 8:1. The transcription retains the
printed reference, and the translation discloses rather than silently repairs
it.

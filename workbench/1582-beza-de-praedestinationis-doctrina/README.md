# Theodore Beza, *De praedestinationis doctrina et vero usu* (1582)

This workbench contains a diplomatic-normalized Latin transcription and a
complete independent English working translation of Theodore Beza,
*De praedestinationis doctrina et vero usu tractatio absolutissima*, as printed
in volume III of the 1582 *Tractationes theologicae*, pp. 402–447.

## Files

- `parts/complete-latin.txt` — diplomatic-normalized Latin transcription.
- `parts/complete-english.md` — AI-assisted English working translation.

## Source alignment and scope

The controlling witness is the isolated facsimile
`/tmp/reformed-retrieval-beza-sources/de-praedestinatione-1582.pdf`.
PDF page 1 is modern e-rara.ch cover material and is excluded. PDF pages 2–47
correspond exactly to printed pages 402–447. As OCR aids, printed pages 402–447
correspond to objects 0417–0462 in
`/tmp/reformed-retrieval-beza-sources/vol3-ocr.xml`; the plain-text OCR is also
consulted but does not preserve the two-column reading order reliably.

The edition begins with the work's title on printed p. 402. It ends on printed
p. 447 with the sentence *Haec hactenus, ex Lutheri libello, De servo Arbitrio,
adversus Erasmum, excerpta sunt.*, followed by `FINIS.` The volume-wide errata
list printed below `FINIS` is not part of the treatise and is excluded, as are
all adjacent works and modern scanner boilerplate. Beza's address to the
Christian reader and the Luther excerpts that he appended after the first
`FINIS` on p. 440 are retained through their own terminal `FINIS` on p. 447.

## Editorial status

This is an AI-assisted working edition, not a publication-ready critical text.
Long *s*, ligatures, line-end hyphenation, and obvious OCR artifacts are
normalized; original orthography and substantive wording are otherwise
retained. Printed-page/PDF-page/OCR-object markers are matched in both files.
Marginal headings and biblical references are retained in brackets where they
can be recovered. Unresolved or syntactically difficult readings are flagged
rather than silently supplied. The English was translated independently from
the normalized Latin while checking the 1582 images; it has not received
independent specialist review.

Several Greek technical terms and the Homeric phrase quoted on p. 435 have been
normalized from the facsimile rather than the OCR. The compact biblical
reference string in thesis V on p. 440 is retained as printed but its punctuation
is ambiguous and should be checked in a critical review.

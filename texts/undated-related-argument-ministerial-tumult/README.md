# On a Related Ministerial Tumult

This directory presents the complete Latin-form witness and a new English
translation of the consilium headed *Eiusdem fere cum superiore argumenti* in
the *Calvini Opera*.

## Work and witness identity

| Field | Value |
|---|---|
| Author | John Calvin (work attribution); the surviving Latin is Beza's reported translation |
| CO consilium | IV.10, *Eiusdem fere cum superiore argumenti* |
| Date | Undated |
| Authorial language | French; the archetype is not extant according to CO |
| Language presented here | Latin translated from the French |
| Genre | Admonitory ecclesiastical advice in epistolary form |
| Addressees | An unnamed congregation addressed as “most beloved brothers” |
| Subject | Tumult over ministerial selection, congregational consent, pastoral examination, rhetorical display, and church order |
| CO locator | X/1, pp. 220–223 |

The work/witness distinction is essential. CO's source note says, *Textus
gallicus quem Beza se vertisse dicit, non exstat*: “The French text which Beza
says that he translated is not extant.” The Latin in this directory is
therefore a normalized edition of Beza's public-domain translation, not
Calvin's recoverable French wording. The new English is necessarily a
translation of that mediated Latin witness.

The displayed heading means “Of nearly the same subject as the preceding
document.” It is an editorial or collected-edition label, not an authorial
French title that can be checked against the lost archetype. The document
preceding it in CO ends with the dateline 22 December 1551, but neither this
heading nor the present body supplies a date. That neighboring date has not
been transferred to this work.

## Edition and transmission

The transcription follows Wilhelm Baum, Eduard Cunitz, and Eduard Reuss,
eds., *Ioannis Calvini opera quae supersunt omnia*, vol. X/1 (*Corpus
Reformatorum* XXXVIII) (Brunswick: C. A. Schwetschke, 1871), pp. 220–223.

CO reports the item in the Geneva *Epistolae et responsa* at p. 350 and in
the Lausanne, Hanover, Chouet, and Amsterdam collections at pp. 229, 257,
481, and 219 respectively. Its note cites no French manuscript, autograph, or
other witness from which Calvin's exact wording could be restored.

The public facsimile is supplied by the University of Geneva's [complete
*Calvini Opera* collection](https://archive-ouverte.unige.ch/unige:650) and
its [direct CO X/1 PDF](https://access.archive-ouverte.unige.ch/access/metadata/f526a338-c95c-4f87-b600-04df723f502b/download).

## Scope and collation

`latin.txt` includes every word from *Utinam nunquam aliud mihi scribendi* on
p. 220 through *Amen* on p. 223. It excludes the CO heading and footnote,
running furniture, the preceding document sharing p. 220, and the following
consilium beginning later on p. 223.

The text was checked line by line against 600-DPI renderings of all three
facsimile pages. Printer line wrapping and line-end divisions are removed,
while CO's spelling, capitalization, punctuation, and biblical locators are
retained. The only printed-page turn dividing a word is pp. 221–222,
*intenda-mus*. The normalized transcription joins it as *intendamus* and
places the p. 222 marker before the complete word. The p. 220–221 and p.
222–223 markers fall between *ministrum / a* and *momentum / fortasse*. No
reading is illegible or conjecturally supplied.

## Translation cautions

- In *illum vos inhalasse quodammodo* (p. 220), *illum* most naturally refers
  to Satan in the preceding sentence; the English renders the image as “he
  had somehow breathed upon you.” The lost French prevents checking the
  direction or precise force of Beza's unusual Latin expression.
- The compressed question *sed a quo stetit?* is rendered “but on whom did
  its stopping depend?” Its context credits the Lord's grace and faithful
  ministers with calming the flame.
- The literal “worst tail” (*pessimam caudam*, p. 221) is rendered “worst
  aftermath.”
- The referents of *uti aperte ostendit, quum illum admisit* on p. 222 depend
  on a situation not named in the surviving heading. The translation retains
  “he” and “that man” rather than inventing personal names.

## Translation status

John H. Leith's edited volume *Calvin's Ecclesiastical Advice*, translated by
Mary D. Beaty and Benjamin W. Farley (1991), contains a copyrighted modern
translation of the *consilia*. It was not consulted and supplied no wording
or interpretation here. No complete public-domain English version of this
item was located in the research audit.

`english.md` is an **AI-assisted scholarly draft**, translated afresh from
the normalized Latin and checked against every printed line. It has not
received independent review by a human Latinist. Exact-wording claims must
identify Beza's Latin translation as the witness and should not be presented
as quotations recovered directly from Calvin's lost French.

Suggested citation:

> John Calvin, *Eiusdem fere cum superiore argumenti*, undated, French
> archetype lost, Latin trans. Theodore Beza, *Calvini Opera* X/1 (1871),
> 220–223, normalized Latin and draft English translation, *Reformed
> Retrieval*.

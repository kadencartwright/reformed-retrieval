VERSE 7. For as vanities belong to a multitude of dreams, so they will inhere
in many words; but fear God himself.

He applies here against one species—the lapse committed in vows—the same
remedy which he applied to speech directed to God in general, in order to free
us from error: we must not multiply vows. For sin can no more be absent from
that multiplication than a variety of dreams can be absent from a multitude
of affairs with which someone is occupied during the day. Here the papist
religion is again arraigned as guilty, since it makes no end of vowing.

By commanding us to fear God, he designates the sum of divine worship and, as
it were, the fountain of lawful worship. Proverbs 1. This same fear both
directs us in vowing, so far as is sufficient, and then compels us to pay
lawful vows. Therefore, our work and effort must be placed in fearing the Lord.
See Ecclesiastes 7:18 and 12:13. From a comparison of those passages with this
one, it appears that Solomon is concluding the question which he proposed to
treat in this book.

### QUESTION IX

#### Who was the Artaxerxes by whom Ezra and Nehemiah were sent to Jerusalem?

Answer. This question must be decided with all the more careful attention
because many place the beginning of Daniel’s most celebrated weeks in either
the seventh or the twentieth year of this Artaxerxes, whoever he finally was;
and yet very little can be added here to what has already been said. Scholars
hold three opinions concerning this Artaxerxes. Josephus and a few followers
think he was Xerxes, the son of Darius Hystaspes. Sulpicius Severus thinks he
was Artaxerxes Mnemon, son of Darius Nothus; he is followed by Scaliger and
the writers from among us cited above, who hold that Darius Nothus built the
temple. Eusebius and Jerome, however, following Julius Africanus, prefer
Longimanus; the Greek and Latin Fathers, Roman Catholic chronologists,
Capellus, Ussher, and others follow them.

I argue as follows against the first opinion, Josephus’s. According to no
author did Xerxes reign beyond his thirtieth year; but according to all the
ancient writers the Artaxerxes who sent Ezra and Nehemiah to Jerusalem ruled
for at least forty years. Nehemiah expressly mentions his thirty-second year
in chapter 5, verse 14. Moreover, Xerxes is distinguished by name from his son
Artaxerxes, and no one called Xerxes Artaxerxes—unless perhaps you confuse

Artaxerxes with Artashasta, Artashasta with Ahasuerus, and Ahasuerus with
Xerxes. We have stated above how great an error that involves.

No arguments other than those we employed in the preceding question against
their earlier position concerning Darius Nothus as builder of the temple are
needed against the second opinion, that of Sulpicius and Scaliger. For this is
the foundation of the present opinion: they suppose—as we too freely grant—
that the Artaxerxes who sent Ezra and Nehemiah to Jerusalem followed the
Darius who issued the decree for completing the temple. And they concede
that no arguments are needed to overturn their opinion and establish the
third, which is ours, other than those proving that the Darius named in the
decree was Hystaspes, not Nothus. But in the preceding question we treated
that point at length with both reasons and authorities. “Cabbage twice served
is death”; therefore, lest we repeat what has already been done, let us
proceed to other matters.

# Calvin, *Letter Confirming His Recently Published Admonition among the Poles* (1563)

This directory presents the complete authorial Latin and a new English
translation of John Calvin's published open letter supporting his earlier
*Brevis admonitio ad fratres Polonos*. The letter answers antitrinitarian
arguments current in Poland and exhorts orthodox nobles and citizens of Krakow
to continue defending the deity of Christ.

> **Editorial status:** This is an AI-assisted draft transcription and
> translation checked word by word against high-resolution facsimile images,
> not a peer-reviewed critical edition. A qualified Neo-Latinist should
> collate it independently before it is used for formal publication or as the
> sole basis for a disputed historical or theological interpretation.

The title's *fidem ... confirmat* can suggest confirming the credibility,
authority, or truth of the recently published *Admonitio*. The shorter English
identifier avoids forcing a single shade of *fidem* into the title. It should
not obscure the work's actual sequence: Calvin first issued the *Brevis
admonitio* and then wrote this separate letter after hearing both that the
earlier pamphlet had helped and that controversy continued.

## Work and witness identity

This text is a printed theological tract in epistolary form, not merely an
entry in Calvin's private correspondence. The corporate address and the 1563
Perrin title page belong to the work's publication setting. Calvin's closing
supplies the date directly.

The CO editors' prolegomena say that they consulted rare copies of both Polish
pamphlets from Zürich's civic library. They describe the first edition of this
letter as an octavo of one quaternion, with fourteen unnumbered printed pages
and the final leaf blank, and list later Latin reprints in the 1576, 1597,
1611, and Amsterdam tract collections. The Bibliothèque de Genève now makes a
1563 copy, shelf mark **Bc 71**, available through
[e-rara](https://doi.org/10.3931/e-rara-2680).

The base text here is nevertheless the CO IX witness requested for this
project, not a newly constituted critical text from the first edition. The
1563 scan was used as a collateral control to distinguish Calvin's words from
CO's supplied apparatus. No reading in `latin.txt` silently replaces CO with
the earlier witness.

## Authorial and editorial matter

CO IX 641–650 contains several layers that must not be conflated:

- Pp. 641–642 reproduce the first edition's title page. Its title, author,
  printer, place, and year are bibliographical paratext and are recorded here;
  its display typography is not treated as a second occurrence of Calvin's
  letter.
- Pp. 643–644 are blank.
- Calvin's authorial text begins with the corporate address at p. 645 and ends
  with the dated Geneva closing at p. 650.
- The bracketed tags `[fol. 3]` through `[fol. 7]` in CO align the text with
  folios of an earlier edition. They are nineteenth-century navigational
  apparatus, not words in the letter.
- CO's seven French footnotes record additions or phrasings in a French
  version: *ou environ*; *et vilains poltrons Italiens*; *tiré et séparé*; *ou
  cest Eternel*; *forgé ou fondu*; *ces rustres et forgerons*; and *pour
  cercher faveur et gens qui leur soustiennent le menton*. They are excluded
  from the Latin and English body.
- CO supplies parenthetical biblical references after 1 John 5:20, Acts
  20:28, John 1:1, Romans 9:5, and related quotations. Comparison with the
  1563 print confirms that those references are editorial. The translator's
  notes identify the passages without inserting the references into Calvin's
  Latin.
- Running heads, printed locator numbers, signature marks, and the dividing
  rule after the letter are likewise excluded.

The list above represents seven numbered French notes distributed across CO
IX 645–648. None is silently treated as a Latin variant.

## Transcription and collation

The PDF text layer and OCR of the 1563 copy were used only as provisional
aids. Every word and punctuation decision in the Latin body was then checked
against the native 300-DPI images of UNIGE PDF pages 402–404. The normalized
Latin follows the repository policy:

- prose is reflowed and divisions caused only by the printer's line endings
  are joined;
- CO spelling and punctuation are retained, including *quum*, *nuncius*,
  *pronunciavit*, *Iehova*, *oeconomia*, *tanquam*, and *Caeterum*;
- the corporate address and conventional closing abbreviation `S.` are
  preserved;
- each numbered CO column receives an exact visible page marker; and
- no illegible or conjecturally supplied word remains.

The page turns were checked especially closely. P. 645 ends with *dicitur,*
and p. 646 begins *nimium*; p. 646 divides *per-fidum*, for which the normalized
text places the p. 647 marker before the complete word *perfidum*; p. 648 ends
*ad stabiliendum* and p. 649 begins *suum errorem*; p. 649 ends *afferam* and
p. 650 begins the final exhortation. These alignments make the six individual
markers inspectable rather than treating a scan image as one undifferentiated
two-page block.

## Translation policy and difficult terms

The English was translated afresh from `latin.txt`, with the page images
rechecked whenever grammar, punctuation, or a technical term affected the
sense. No modern English translation was copied, revised, or used as a base.
The repository's bibliographical audit, current to 11 August 2026, did not
locate a complete public-domain English translation of this work.

The translation deliberately retains or explains several consequential
features:

- *deitatis principium* is rendered “principle of deity” and explained as a
  personal relation of origin, not a temporal beginning of the divine essence;
- Gentile's coined *essentiatus* is represented as “essentiated” rather than
  hidden beneath a smoother paraphrase;
- *ex Dei traduce* retains the idea of derivation or propagation from a stock;
- *alius ... sed non alius Deus* remains “another than the Father, but not
  another God,” preserving the person/essence distinction;
- *de toto complexo* is translated “the whole complex” and then glossed from
  Calvin's own apposition, “God manifested in the flesh”;
- the compressed contrast *non comparative ... indefinite* receives a close
  rendering and a note rather than a conjectural expansion; and
- biblical quotations are translated from Calvin's Latin rather than replaced
  with the wording of a modern English Bible.

Calvin's descriptions of his opponents are frequently abusive. Words such as
*nebulones*, *rabula*, *praestigiator*, *falsarii*, and *errones* have not been
sanitized into neutral academic labels. They remain Calvin's rhetoric, not the
project's characterization of the people named.

## Files and completeness

- [`latin.txt`](latin.txt) contains the complete diplomatic-normalized
  authorial Latin at CO IX 645–650, with all six individual page markers.
- [`english.md`](english.md) contains a complete new English translation with
  the identical locator sequence and explanatory notes.

The complete authorial inventory is one corporate address, the full continuous
letter, the farewell and benediction, and the Geneva date. The reproduced
title page and blank leaf are accounted for above. Nothing authorial within CO
IX 641–650 has been silently omitted.

VERSE 3. Indignation is better than laughter, because through sadness of
countenance the mind is made better.

Solomon confirms the immediately preceding comparison from the effects of
the two things, which are contrary and are compared with one another. For the
effect of going to a house of mourning is indignation and sorrow, just as the
effect of a feast is gladness, which he designates by the word “laughter.”
Since, therefore, sorrow and indignation, the effects of going to a house of
mourning, are more excellent than laughter, the effect of a feast, it is also
proved that the source from which this sorrow proceeds is more excellent.

By this very fact death must be preferred to riches, since sorrow arising
from death is to be preferred to gladness springing from an abundance of
riches. This may seem paradoxical, but it is nevertheless entirely certain,
inasmuch as it is confirmed by the authority of the Holy Spirit and daily
experience. It is also confirmed by the reason attached: the mind is improved
through sadness of countenance. Hence sorrow is also compared to water, by
which we are washed clean of filth; to fire, by which we are refined from
dross; and to the threshing of a threshing floor, by which the chaff of sin
that clings tenaciously to us is shaken out.

This fact ought to soften our sorrow; see Proverbs 3 and Hebrews 12. Christ
confirms this teaching in Matthew 5: “Blessed are those who mourn, because
they will receive consolation.” Conversely, through laughter people are often
released into vices; on this see what was said above at Chapter 2, verse 2.
See examples in Ahasuerus, Chapter 1:10; in Herod, Mark 6; and also in Nabal,
1 Samuel 25. Hence also the Apostle, Romans 13, joins drunkenness to feasts.
What Christ pronounces in Luke 6 agrees with this: “Woe to those who laugh,”
and so forth.

# An Exhortation and Apology Addressed to a Papist

This directory presents the complete Latin-form witness and a new English
translation of the consilium headed *Ad pontificium quendam exhortatoria et
apologetica* in the *Calvini Opera*.

## Work and witness identity

| Field | Value |
|---|---|
| Historical author | John Calvin (collection attribution) |
| CO consilium | II.2, *Ad pontificium quendam exhortatoria et apologetica* |
| Date | Not preserved |
| Place | Geneva, from the closing *Genevae* |
| Addressee | An unnamed adherent of the Roman church whom Calvin did not know by sight; the addressee's children prompted the letter |
| Authorial language state | French, according to Theodore Beza as reported by CO |
| Witness edited here | Beza's Latin translation, reprinted by CO |
| Genre | Exhortatory and apologetic letter |
| Principal topics | Scripture as the rule of worship, ecclesial corruption and authority, grace and merits, good works, invocation and saints, sacraments, the mass, and the Lord's Supper |
| CO locator | X/1, pp. 181–184 |

The opening supplies an unusually specific but still anonymous rhetorical
situation. Calvin tells the recipient that they do not know one another by
sight, cites the recipient's children as the *autores* of his plan to write,
and says they led him to expect a patient reading. Nothing in the body names
the recipient or children. The final *Genevae* gives the place of composition
or dispatch but is followed by no date or signature.

CO says this work follows the preceding proxy letter in all the early editions
and, like that letter, was translated by Beza from French. CO supplies no
French copy. `latin.txt` therefore presents Beza's historical Latin
translation, while [`english.md`](english.md) newly translates that witness;
neither claims to recover Calvin's unlocated French diction.

## Edition, heading, and transmission

The transcription follows Wilhelm Baum, Eduard Cunitz, and Eduard Reuss,
eds., *Ioannis Calvini opera quae supersunt omnia*, vol. X/1 (*Corpus
Reformatorum* XXXVIII) (Brunswick: C. A. Schwetschke, 1871), pp. 181–184.

The displayed title and CO's numbered source note are collection or
publication paratext and are not included in the historical letter body. The
body begins *Quum de facie tibi ignotus sim* after the preceding letter ends on
p. 181. It concludes on p. 184 with *Vale in Domino*, a blessing for the
recipient, and *Genevae*. Running heads, CO page numbers, the p. 182 gathering
mark, the terminal space, and the following French consilium are likewise
excluded.

CO's note records earlier printings at Geneva p. 144, Lausanne p. 318, Hanover
p. 356, Chouet p. 195, and Amsterdam p. 89. It cites no manuscript or body
variant. Its *itidem a Beza ex gallico versa* establishes a French-to-Latin
translation witness while leaving the underlying French text unidentified in
this edition.

2500 × 3150-pixel renderings; extracted text served only as orientation. The

divides printed *funda-mento*: the normalized Latin removes *funda-* from p.

the exact marker sequence 181, 182, 183, 184.

The transcription excludes the title, source note, running and page heads,
gathering mark, and neighboring works. No body reading is illegible,
conjectural, or silently supplied, and CO prints no textual variant for the
letter body.

## Translation status and cruxes

Mary D. Beaty and Benjamin Wirt Farley's copyrighted *Calvin's Ecclesiastical
Advice* (1991) translates the complete *consilia* corpus. The bibliographical
audit located no complete reusable public-domain English version of this
letter. Their wording was not consulted or reused; the English here was
translated afresh from the Latin and checked against the full facsimile.

Two classical images have been retained and explained rather than replaced.
The *regula Lesbia* is a flexible rule that conforms itself to what it measures
instead of furnishing a fixed standard. The appeal to “the Church” becomes an
*Aiacis clypeus*, Ajax's great shield, behind which Calvin says opponents hide.
Both figures serve his contrast between adaptable human authority and the
fixed prophetic and apostolic foundation.

The transcription also preserves CO's future *compellet* in the account of
God summoning people to himself. At p. 184 *postliminio* casts the restored
Lord's Supper as returning from exile with its former right. The English notes
these readings and translates all embedded biblical propositions directly
from Calvin's Latin presentation.

This is an **AI-assisted scholarly draft**, not an independently reviewed
critical edition. Calvin's polemical labels, including the title's
*pontificium*, “counterfeit church,” and claims about Roman rites, belong to
the historical argument and should not be mistaken for neutral descriptive
metadata. Exact-wording claims should cite the Latin and printed CO page.

Suggested citation:

> John Calvin, *Ad pontificium quendam exhortatoria et apologetica*, undated
> French work in Theodore Beza's Latin translation, Geneva, *Calvini Opera*
> X/1 (1871), 181–184, normalized Latin and draft English translation,
> *Reformed Retrieval*.

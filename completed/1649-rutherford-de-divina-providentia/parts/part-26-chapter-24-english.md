CHAPTER XXIV

The Same Is Proved by Other Arguments

THIS fiction of bare, fortuitous, and idle permission conflicts with the
patience of the Saints. Therefore it must be rejected. The antecedent is
proved. Patient submission under calamities inflicted by wicked men is owed
either to the will of GOD, to the will of the men who oppress us, or to
fortune. For there can be no fourth alternative. Not the first, because one
oppressed by wicked men cannot profess to GOD, *“I kept silence, JEHOVAH,
because you did it”* (Psalm 39:9), since GOD neither caused that oppression as
Judge, nor willed it, nor decreed it, but merely did not prevent it, never-*

[Marginal argument IX: This loose and fortuitous idle permission conflicts
with the patience of the Saints.]

[Chapter XXIV; printed page 359; IA PDF page 418; BSB canvas 411]

*willed it, but merely permitted it. He must therefore submit himself to GOD’s
will or non-volition, but not to [the human] will. If you say that we ought to
submit to GOD’s permitting will, nevertheless a permitting will does not offer
the cup to us. Indeed, a permitting will neither in itself nor accidentally
wills sin, or that wicked men afflict us; it merely permits them either to
afflict us or, absolutely according to their own choice undetermined by GOD,
not to afflict us. Therefore we must not acquiesce in GOD’s will. Nor can the
second alternative be affirmed, because a created will is not the object of
patience except on account of GOD; for otherwise the acquiescence would be
plainly idolatrous. 2. Ruiz rightly says that *“according to right reason we
can dissent from the disordered will of our enemies.”* I add that we ought to
detest our enemies’ sinful and blameworthy oppression. It remains, therefore,
*“that we must acquiesce in fortune alone.”*

[Marginal reference: Ruiz, *On Predetermination*, treatise 2, disputation 11,
section 3, figure 11.]

10. This idle permission, which includes no positive act of the divine will,
conflicts with gratitude, trust, hope, fear, joy, and solid consolation in GOD.
Therefore it must be rejected. The antecedent is proved. 1. Gratitude is not
owed to him, nor ought one to rest upon him in hope, fear, faith, and joy, when
his will is not the cause why evil comes to me from wicked men. But if the
fiction of GOD’s negative and contingent permission stands, GOD is neither the
first cause who inflicts this evil which befalls the Saints through the hatred
and ill will of criminal men, nor did he will or decree that *Angitiosi* men
should hate the Saints or afflict them with any atrocious injury; he merely
permitted it without qualification. Why, I ask, should Paul and Silas, or Peter
and the Apostles, give thanks to GOD and rejoice that for the Name of Jesus
they were beaten with rods, shut in prison, and suffered insults and reproaches
from the most wicked men, if GOD wholly did not will those Tyrants to beat
them, shut them in prison, and assail them with reproaches? One must not trust,
hope, and rejoice in that GOD as the first cause of all the afflictions we
suffer from evil men if he wholly does not will evil men to afflict us, if he
is not the cause which makes persecutions infallible and determines them, and
if by his bare permission we are just as able not to be afflicted by them as
to be afflicted. Yet such is the GOD of the Arminians and Jesuits. All the
Saints’ consolations are empty. Let someone oppressed by wicked men say: “I
rejoice in GOD, who neither offered me such a cup nor willed me to drink it,
nor appointed me to suffer such a thing.”-*

[Marginal argument X: This fortuitous and contingent permission conflicts with
gratitude, faith, hope, fear, and joy.]

[Marginal reference: Acts 5:41.]

[Chapter XXIV; printed page 360; IA PDF page 419; BSB canvas 412]

*Indeed, Peter spoke falsely when he said that it was GOD’s will that we should
suffer from evil men. And because, according to the Adversaries, the will of
devils and men is the first cause of all the calamities we suffer from them,
trust, hope, fear, and consolations will ultimately terminate in the wills of
devils and men. Indeed, one must rest with gratitude, faith, hope, fear, and joy
in that will as the sole first cause, in whose power it lies whether evil comes
to us or, according to its own free choice, does not come. Yet according to the
Adversaries such a cause is the wicked man who persecutes us. What, I ask,
could be more execrable idolatry than this? Ruiz the Jesuit, a fierce opponent
of our doctrine in this matter, acknowledged—surely with an evil conscience—
that this argument is valid.

[Marginal reference: 1 Peter 3:17.]

[Marginal reference: Ruiz, tome on providence, treatise 2, disputation, “se
11,” chapter 2.]

11. This idle permission is a road to the vain arrogance of the Assyrians who
mocked GOD’s determinative providence, Isaiah 10:15: *“Will the axe boast
against the one cutting with it? Will the saw magnify itself against the one
who moves it? As though the rod moved the one who raises it; as though a staff
which is not wood raised itself.”* Therefore this permission must be rejected.
The Assyrian who laid waste GOD’s people denied that he was a rod and staff in
GOD’s hand. *“If a staff,”* says Calvin, *“should exalt itself against the hand
that raises it, and forget that it is wood, what a monstrous spectacle that
would be!”* Our Assyrians too—I mean the Arminians and Jesuits—deny that the
persecutors of CHRIST’s Church are moved and raised by GOD, as though they were
not rods or staffs in GOD’s hand. For they say that GOD is not the first cause
judicially inflicting these evils upon the Church, but that GOD wholly does not
will the rod to raise itself against the Church. Rather, he merely idly permits
rods endowed with reason and freedom to raise themselves. Hence the Church’s
enemy is free to boast and say: “Therefore I am the first, proper, and sole
cause of every evil inflicted upon GOD’s people.”

[Marginal argument XI: This fortuitous permission truly gives creatures a
cause for boasting.]

[Marginal reference: Calvin, commentary on Isaiah 10:5.]

12. Just as this opinion conflicts with the duty of men toward GOD, so it
conflicts with GOD’s attributes. For 1. It conflicts with GOD’s wise will: he
wills the Saints to be tried, yet does not will wicked men to try us. This is
as contradictory as if a goldsmith desired gold to be assayed, yet did not
will it to be cast into the fire. 2. In this contingent permission no care of
GOD for his own people appears. For if GOD neither wills, nor decrees, nor by
any positive action causes the godly to be afflicted by evil men, then he does
not care whether they are afflicted or not. 3. According to the adversaries,
to permit sin is not to will sin to occur; and-*

[Marginal argument XII: This contingent permission conflicts with the wisdom,
care, love, and firmness of GOD’s decrees.]

[Chapter XXIV; printed page 361; IA PDF page 420; BSB canvas 413]

*what GOD does not will to occur and nevertheless occurs, occurs against GOD’s
will and despite him, which conflicts with GOD’s omnipotence. 4. It conflicts
with GOD’s love, by which he chastens those whom he loves (Revelation 3:19;
Proverbs 3:11–12; Hebrews 12:6–7). For GOD not to will the wicked to chasten his
own and to exercise them with hunger, exile, and death is diametrically opposed
to GOD’s love and fatherly care, by which he exercises his own through the
ministries of the most wicked men, who drive them into exile, hunger,
starvation, and prison. For if GOD intends the Saints to be afflicted by the
world, he must intend the world to afflict them. 5. Such a decree of GOD, by
which he does not will sin to occur at all, conflicts with GOD’s wise decree by
which he has resolved to fatten evil men—grown rich from robbery, brigandage,
and the unrighteous zeal for adding house to house and field to field—for the
day of destruction, and to mortify his children to worldly allurements and
momentary pleasures after they have been burdened by the oppressions and
injuries of the worst men. 6. If GOD wholly does not will sin to occur, then it
will not belong to GOD’s wise decree that he wills his own people to enter the
Kingdom of Heaven through persecutions. For GOD neither wills nor decrees such
persecutions to occur. 7. This decree, by which GOD does not will sin to occur
at all, also conflicts with his decree to display the glory of grace and the
necessity of grace, and to humble and cast down through the most atrocious sins
into which they fall those men who presume upon their own powers and are
ignorant of their frailty and the corruption of nature. This is evident in
David’s fall into adultery, Peter’s denial of the Savior, and Lot’s commission
of drunkenness and incest. Who does not know that Augustine truly taught from
the Scriptures that *“it is beneficial for self-confident and proud men to fall
into some sin?”* This is clearly shown by these Scriptures: Psalm 30:6–7; Job
29:18 compared with chapter 30:1–3, and so forth; 1 Kings 10 compared with
chapter 11:1–4; 2 Chronicles 32:1: *“GOD abandoned Hezekiah, testing him, that
he might discover what was in his heart.”*

13. GOD’s idle permission, by which he does not will sin to occur at all,
conflicts with GOD’s fatherly clemency and indulgence, by which he softens and
tempers the oppressions the Saints suffer from evil men. For if GOD does not
will these oppressions to occur at all, then neither does he will them to come
upon the Saints in such number, weight, and measure; and it is contrary to the
Scriptures that GOD does not moderate the Saints’ afflictions: Psalm 118:18;
1 Corinthians 10:13; Luke 22:18; Isaiah 54:7–8; Psalm 6:1–2; Jeremiah 46:28;
Job-*

[Marginal argument XIII: This permission conflicts with GOD’s fatherly clemency
in moderating and tempering the Saints’ afflictions.]

[Chapter XXIV; printed page 362; IA PDF page 421; BSB canvas 414]

*28; Job 1:12; Psalm 129:3–4; Psalm 125:3. The connection is proved: it is
impossible for GOD to will that oppression and persecution be at degree 3 and
not 5, and temptation at degree 4, proportionate to the strength of the Saint
who is tempted, and not 6, which exceeds his strength, if GOD does not in some
manner will that persecution exist simply. For the willing of a comparative
degree necessarily entails the willing of the positive degree. And who is
ignorant that it was the divine will that GOD’s people should be pressed by
Babylonian servitude for seventy years rather than one hundred or four years?
It is certain that the Chaldeans sinned by keeping them captive for one year,
sinned more gravely by keeping them for two or twenty years, and sinned most
gravely by keeping them for seventy years. If GOD wills the graver sin, and
then the gravest, it follows that GOD simply wills sin by permission—not by
approval or production.

XIV. This idle permission also conflicts with GOD’s justice, which prescribes
even the measure, weight, and number of the oppressions the reprobate suffer.
For it belongs to GOD’s just will that Sennacherib sacrifice his son to his
Idol rather than a bull or sheep; that the Medes and Persians lay waste the
Chaldeans with two or three defeats rather than one; and that the Romans
utterly conquer the Jews and reduce them to extreme desolation and inescapable
misery, such as the execrable killing of the Messiah deserved, rather than
afflict them lightly. Yet it is perfectly clear that Sennacherib, the Medes,
and the Romans sinned atrociously in such acts. This is also clear from these
Scriptures: Job 20:12–16, and so forth; 18:9–14; 21:17–18, 3; 27:19–22; Psalm
11:6. For if the wicked are wrongfully harassed by other wicked men (as happens
not infrequently), and GOD by his just will determines the number, weight, and
measure—indeed, the very kind—of calamities which the wicked suffer from wicked
men who afflict them most unjustly, GOD cannot fail to will the injuries
blamefully and unjustly inflicted upon the wicked. Since the wicked must suffer
at the hands of the wicked a repayment for unjust oppressions, GOD’s just will
must intervene here.

[Marginal argument XIV: It conflicts with GOD’s justice.]

XV. This idle permission is diametrically opposed to GOD’s providence which
determines all things, for by plainly sacrilegious idolatry it sets fortune
upon GOD’s throne. Things which occur without counsel and beyond GOD’s
intention occur by fortune. But if GOD does not will sin to exist with himself
permitting it, then the falls of all men and-*

[Marginal argument XV: This contingent permission erects fortune’s throne.]

[Chapter XXIV; printed page 363; IA PDF page 422; BSB canvas 415]

*Angels, CHRIST’s sufferings and those of all the Saints, and the crimes and
tyrannies of the Church’s persecutors would occur by fortune, in precisely the
manner that the discovery of Treasure occurs to a peasant digging the earth.
Therefore this permission must be rejected.

[Marginal reference: Stapleton, tome 2, *On the Cause of Sin*, book 11, chapter
7.]

The major premise is conceded by Stapleton. So also the Coimbra Doctors and
Franciscus Murcia de Lallana. *“Fortune,”* he says, *“is called an accidental
cause because it lies beyond intention.”* So also Antonius Ruvio, a polished
and refined Philosopher. *“With respect to GOD,”* he says, *“who foreknows and
orders by his providence that, for example, the treasure should be found by
that intermediate cause, the effect is called neither fortuitous nor from
fortune, but intended and ordered in itself by his providence.”* The Coimbra
Doctors rightly reject as absurd that anything should be fortuitous to GOD.
*“With respect to GOD,”* they say, *“nothing can occur by chance or
fortuitously.”* And Augustine: *“In that immensely broad and boundless
commonwealth of the whole creation, there is nothing which is not either
commanded or permitted from the inner and intelligible court of the supreme
Emperor.”* Indeed, as the Coimbra Doctors argue, GOD could thus be an
accidental cause; but every accidental cause has another, nobler cause to which
it is referred. So Aristotle and Bradwardine also teach. I am astonished that
Julius Sirenius surrenders to this argument as one defeated. *“For,”* he says,
*“we may confess in a Catholic manner that depraved wills and all the operations
of sinners occur rashly and fortuitously, however they may occur.”* Didacus
Ruiz approves Cicero’s philosophy when he says, *“What else is lot, fortune, or
chance, except when something happens in such a way that it could either not
happen or happen otherwise?”* But if the Jesuit receives that without any
distinction, I will be free to reason thus: all sins, the Savior’s killing, and
all the sufferings of the Saints could have happened or not happened.
Therefore, with respect to GOD, all these things will be fortuitous. I do not
know by what fate Didacus Ruiz and Gabriel Pennottus evade this argument. *“No
effect foreknown by an agent is fortuitous,”* they say, *“because what is not
wholly beyond knowledge is not wholly beyond at least an intention to permit.”*
I answer: if sin is *“according to the intention to permit sin,”* it is
according to the intention not to will sin. For the intention to permit sin is
GOD’s intention by which he does not will sin to-*

[Marginal reference: Aristotle, *Physics* 2, chapters 5 and 6.]

*occur at all and detests its existence. But if sin occurs according to the
intention not to will sin, it occurs wholly beyond the intention of willing
sin, and thus beyond GOD’s will and fortuitously. 2. If sin therefore does not
occur *“beyond GOD’s intention,”* as the Jesuits argue, *“because it is not
wholly beyond his knowledge,”* then that knowledge must include some will of
GOD by which he in some manner wills sin to occur. Yet this is what we teach.
If that knowledge includes no volition of GOD terminating upon sin, then the
Jesuits argue badly: Sin is not beyond GOD’s knowledge; therefore it is not
beyond GOD’s intention. For then I would be free to argue: Ahab knows that he
will die in battle, for this is divinely revealed to him as something that will
occur; therefore Ahab intends that he will die in battle. But the conclusion
is false. 3. According to the Jesuits and Arminians, the will to permit sin is
subsequent to the nod and inclination of the created will. Therefore the
created will’s deviation is in no manner according to GOD’s intention. 4.
Fortune is formally opposed to intention and counsel, not to knowledge;
otherwise it would have to be said that the day of judgment comes wholly
fortuitously upon the Saints, who await that day with prayers, supreme desire,
and watchfulness—which is absurd. For no mortal possesses knowledge of that
day. 5. The Jesuits’ permissive ordering determines no more concerning the
existence of sin and the Saints’ persecutions than men and Angels determine
about them, because that ordering would exercise no more causality concerning
sin’s futurition than concerning its non-futurition. Indeed, if middle
knowledge stands antecedently to GOD’s knowledge and will, we are pressed,
oppressed, and perish a thousand times by death, cross, wounds, disgrace,
exile, starvation, and hunger. Alas, what deplorable providence! Ah, what empty
consolations! *“Lots,”* says Solomon, *“are cast into the lap, but their entire
decision is from GOD.”* משפט, “judicial determination,” is from GOD. I suppose
two tyrants contend by lots as to which should torture some Saint because of
the name of CHRIST: *“the judgment of the lot is from GOD.”* But according to
the adversaries, how, I ask?

I therefore argue as follows. The drawing of this lot rather than that one is
either from the lot itself, from the hand taking up the lot, from GOD
determining this lot rather than that one, or finally from fortune. It is not
from the lot itself, because the lot is itself determinable either by GOD, as-*

[Chapter XXIV; printed page 365; IA PDF page 424; BSB canvas 417]

*Solomon says, or by fortune, as the Epicurean Atheist supposes. Nor, 2, is it
from the hand itself which takes up the lot, because bare touch can make no
discrimination between a black and a white lot. For touch needs the light of
direction, but neither eyes, nor reason or counsel, nor any sagacity, art, or
instinct is of use here or lends light to the hand’s touch, as the thing itself
testifies. For if the matter could be accomplished by such light, there would
be no need of lots. Nor is the question whether second causes possess an
inherent force and power of acting. The Philistines, who asserted that chance
rules in nature, did not doubt that the cows carrying the ark would travel by
some road. The question was whether they would travel by this less direct road,
and thus whether diseases had befallen them by chance and blind fortune ruled
in the world; but if they went straight along the road of Beth-shemesh, they
would think JEHOVAH to be GOD. The whole question, however, concerns a cause
which directs and predetermines things in their operations.

[Marginal reference: 1 Samuel 6:12.]

[Marginal reference and note: See Thomas, part 1, question 22, article 2, reply

Nor, third, can the adversaries say that the drawing of this lot rather than
that—that is, to the man who would torture the Saint more cruelly rather than
the lot for the one who would treat him more mercifully and gently—is from
JEHOVAH. 1. For then GOD would have to be said to will a graver sin rather than
a lighter one, and thus to will sin; for the casting of lots would be impious
for each man in this case. 2. The drawing of this lot rather than that one would
not be from GOD determining this lot rather than that. This is both because the
adversaries deny that any second causes are determined by GOD in their
operations (for, if the adversaries are to be believed, the nature of
contingency and the lot would thus perish), and because GOD’s will could not
determine the drawing of this lot rather than that, since each is impious. Nor
could GOD’s concurrence with the hand taking up this lot rather than that
determine the drawing, for according to Arminian doctrine GOD’s concurrence is
general, indifferent, and determinable by the second cause. Thus the hand
taking up the lot, which is a contingent cause, might perhaps determine its
drawing. But that is again the point in question, for Solomon refers the whole
disposition to GOD as the predetermining cause. According to the adversaries,
therefore, it remains that the entire disposition of lots is from fortune. The
question is whether the race belongs to the swift rather than the sluggish and
lazy, whether victory belongs to the strong rather than the unwarlike, and
whether food belongs to the wise rather than the foolish. Here the Epicureans
shout that *“chance rules.”* Certainly the race will always belong to the
swift,-*

[Marginal reference: Ruiz, tome on GOD’s will, disputation 50, section 9,

[Marginal reference: Thomas, *Against the Gentiles*, book 3, chapter 5. What

*victory to the strong, and food to the wise, unless GOD’s predetermining
providence resolves the dispute and arranges matters otherwise. For according
to the adversaries, GOD’s will and his concurrence with mutable and contingent
causes does not predetermine such causes. I therefore do not see why Christians
should not repeat this saying:

*Through uncertain paths, changeable fortune wanders.*

*And if fortune wills, from Rhetorician you will become Consul.*

Sirenius says here: *“That a sheep is snatched by a wolf is a contingent event
and beyond GOD’s intention insofar as GOD acts through the sheep, but it is
intended in itself insofar as he acts through the wolf.”* Pennottus says that
*“some effects are contingent with respect to GOD’s primary intention, by which
GOD intends something good and perfect in itself, but not with respect to GOD’s
secondary intention.”* Vorstius says that *“the event of a thing is not
intended but occasioned by chance or accidental,”* and thus says that *“CHRIST
is appointed for the ruin of many.”*

[Marginal reference: Sirenius, *On Providence*, book 4, chapter 35.]

[Marginal reference: Pennottus, *Defense of Human Liberty*, book 5, chapter 7,

I answer: 1. When GOD acts through the sheep, he intends sheep, men, and lions
to be nourished by sheep. No reason can be imagined why, *“when GOD acts
through the sheep,”* the destruction of the sheep should be *“contingent to GOD
and beyond his intention,”* unless it is because GOD does not intend evils of
loss and the destruction of living things in themselves. But in this manner,
the lion’s devouring of the prophet and GOD’s Martyrs will be contingent to
GOD. Alas, what miserable consolations for the dying servant of GOD: *“This
could not be averted; it is contingent even to GOD himself, much more to me, a
blind man.”* Thus all works of justice would be contingent and fortuitous to
GOD: that the Church’s enemies fall in war; that Pharaoh’s army is drowned in
the waves of the Red Sea; that demons are bound in chains of darkness; and
that the Reprobate are cast into hell. So also death, exile, cold, hunger,
starvation, and disgrace which come upon GOD’s servants will be *“contingent
and fortuitous”* to GOD.

[Marginal reference: 1 Kings 13:14, 25.]

2. *“If some effects are contingent to GOD with respect to GOD’s primary
intention but not with respect to his secondary intention,”* then, since
according to the Adversaries GOD intends sins neither by primary nor by
secondary intention, all the falls of Angels and men, CHRIST’s killing, the
burning of people alive, tortures, gallows, wounds, and the execrable killings
of GOD’s Martyrs will be fortuitous and contingent to GOD in every way, both by
GOD’s primary and secondary intention. Alas, what pitiable and thousandfold
lamentable provi-*

death, the sudden drowning of children in the waters, exile, and flight will
be *“contingent”* to GOD. For according to the adversaries, GOD intends evils of
punishment *“not by primary but only by secondary intention.”* Therefore GOD,
conquered by chance or fate, is shaken from the end he initially intended,
which is to bring the Saints into eternal glory through the most delightful
rose gardens and the most abundant pleasures of the world (and this end is
surely a mountain of gold or a goat-stag), and he is forced by some unknown,
inauspicious fortune and unhappy chance to bring us into the Kingdom of Heaven
through many afflictions. I refer the reader to the Scriptures in the margin,

4. Likewise, *“the discovery of Treasure by the one digging”* is contingent
with respect to the digger’s primary intention, for his primary intention was
to dig the earth. But by secondary intention, once knowledge of the Treasure’s
certain existence in that place has already been presupposed, the digger
intends to enrich himself and his children. In precisely that manner, the
Saints’ persecutions and calamities are contingent to GOD as the discovery of
Treasure is to the digger. But once GOD already foreknows the future
persecutions and calamities of the Saints, he intends to direct them to a
further good end (and this is a secondary intention blasphemously fastened upon
GOD). 5. *“If any event is,”* as Vorstius says, *“occasioned by chance or
accidental to GOD,”* chance rules in GOD just as much as in us, and in truth
fortune rules in everything—in the Heavens and in GOD. Thus, 1. GOD will be the
accidental cause of that event, and every accidental cause will be reduced to
some intrinsic cause which is prior and nobler than it. But what God is prior
and nobler than our GOD, who intrinsically executes this work of justice upon
the disobedient, *“that he may appoint CHRIST for their ruin?”* For the work of
justice does not arise intrinsically from the disobedient themselves, nor from
chance, because chance is not the just judge of the world, nor from fortune,
which is nothing. 2. The ruin and eternal destruction of all the wicked will
thus be from chance.

XVI. GOD’s idle permission, by which he does not will sin to occur, conflicts
with the ministry of the word, and especially with the promises and-*

*threatenings set forth in GOD’s word, and therefore with the nature of
religion, conscience toward GOD, hope of reward, and fear of punishment.
Therefore it must be cast out. The antecedent is proved. GOD could not promise
that he would produce a new heart absolutely and effectually in the Church of
the Elect unless he effectually and absolutely did not will the non-production
of a new heart, and thus did not permissively will blameworthy non-conversion
and that certain non-Elect persons should remain with a stony heart. For if GOD
gives a new heart because he wills, not from human merit, then he promises and
wills a new heart to those he wills because he wills, with no regard to
worthiness; and by equal reason he denies a new heart to those he wills because
he wills, with no regard to demerit and unworthiness. This argument refutes a
new opinion held by Robert Baron, the Aberdeen Doctor, a man otherwise learned.
He wills GOD to predetermine human wills to good and supernatural acts, as he
at least professes, but denies that GOD predetermines wills to the entitative
acts of sin, because in that case, he says, GOD would be established as the
Author of sin. But that Doctor most falsely charges Calvin, Beza, and the
Reformed Churches with the crime of θεομαρτησίας, “making GOD the sinner,” a
thing from which they greatly recoil. I often called this amicably to his
memory. For if GOD by the action of efficacious grace inflexibly impels the
wills of the Elect toward conversion, and is therefore the cause of conversion,
then according to this Doctor’s opinions, by the very fact that GOD bends the
wills of the reprobate toward conversion by no motion of efficacious grace, he
will be the author of non-conversion and the cause of sin—unless the Doctor
flees with the Arminians and Semi-Pelagians to the Arminians’ sufficient grace
and to GOD’s consideration of merit and worthiness, and demerit and
unworthiness, so that conversion thus ceases to be gratuitous. For thus, as I
heard the Doctor argue, GOD necessitates to sin by predetermining the
entitative act of sin and destroys freedom in sinning. By equal reasoning,
because GOD predetermines wills to good and supernatural acts, as the Doctor
himself confesses, GOD will also destroy freedom in good acts. Furthermore,
GOD could not threaten the disobedient with blindness and hardening, which he
nevertheless does. Indeed, GOD could not threaten evil to be inflicted upon
his servants and to come about wickedly by the sin of the ungodly, which he
nevertheless does repeatedly in the Scriptures. For if GOD neither wills nor

Argument XVII. If idle permission stands, Ministers of the Word cannot
encourage and strengthen the Saints’ spirits against the sufferings and
disasters inflicted upon them by persecutors. They cannot say, *“Take courage;
do not lose heart. Your GOD is Omnipotent and can avert and remove the hatred,
robberies, and slaughter of your enemies.”* For the Saint would answer: “What
GOD neither wills nor decrees to be done against me, he can neither accomplish
himself nor remove by his omnipotence. For if he does not will these
afflictions of mine, they will escape the power which operates through the
will.” 2. The Minister could not say, *“Take courage; it is your most merciful
Father’s will that you suffer”* (1 Peter 3:17). *“You are appointed by GOD for
sufferings”* (1 Thessalonians 3:3). The Saint would answer: *“But I am an
Arminian, and I believe that persecutions—which I have not deserved from evil
men—and all sins come upon me with GOD not willing them, and with GOD neither
decreeing nor appointing them, nor determining their weight and measure.”* 3.
The Minister could not say: 1. *“The Father tempers the cup.”* 2. *“Do not
despair, but fear GOD, by whose will you suffer these things.”* 3. *“Give thanks
to GOD, who willed you to suffer these things from evil men for your good, that
you might be humbled and crucified to the world.”* The Saint would answer: *“But
GOD neither increases nor diminishes the degrees of sins, as my Apostle
Arminius teaches me. He wills no sins at all; these persecutions are the
persecutors’ crimes.”* 2. *“I must fear the Devil and fear the persecutors, not
GOD.”* For the one feared is the principal cause which renders the evil
infallible, in whose power it lies either to inflict the evil or, according to
his own choice, not to inflict it. But GOD is not such a cause; only the Devil
and my persecutors are. And the one who wholly neither wills nor decrees that
this evil be inflicted upon me by evil men, and who acts not as any cause at
all, much less as its principal-*

[Chapter XXIV; printed page 370; IA PDF page 429; BSB canvas 422]

*cause in whom religious fear would ultimately terminate as in the first cause,
is feared in vain. Instead, he is a spectator who indeed knows but does not
will evil to be inflicted upon me by these scoundrels; and GOD is such a cause.
3. Nor is there any reason for me to give thanks to GOD for these afflictions.

Argument XVIII. Just as this permission is blasphemously unjust toward GOD and
his glorious attributes; 2. just as it wholly weakens and destroys religion,
threatenings and promises, and the duties of Ministers in consoling the godly
who are subject to persecutions; so it wholly conflicts with the things
themselves most beautifully ordered by GOD’s wise providence. 1. All things are
beautifully ordered by GOD’s providence, yet if this permission stands, GOD’s
Church is laid waste with GOD not willing it—that is, with fortune ruling. 2.
It is a beautiful order that the godly are afflicted by evil men, that the
godly are finally delivered, and that evil men perish eternally. Yet if this
permission stands, this order is contrary to GOD’s will and wisdom, since GOD
neither wills nor intends this chain, and he never intended or willed that the
godly be afflicted by the ungodly. 3. It is also a most beautiful order that
the infinitely glorious GOD intends the glory of his Omnipotence by delivering
men from the abyss of sins, and decrees to display the glory of grace, mercy,
justice, and truthfulness in the incarnation and death of his Son and in the
pardon and punishment of sins. But if GOD never willed sin to occur, this order
came contingently and accidentally to GOD, while the opposite order—namely,
that this glory should have been buried forever and hidden from men and
Angels—would have been initially and intrinsically intended by GOD. And this
present order happened against GOD’s will and without his intending it. Ah,
what pitiable and deplorable fates, which even JEHOVAH the Omnipotent must break
or tame! By GOD’s grace I hope never to enter into these men’s secrets.

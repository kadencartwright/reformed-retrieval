It is asked: Does the efficacy of grace depend upon grace or upon Free Choice?
For the question to be understood, it must be noted that the following are not
being asked. 1. Does grace have its intrinsic power of operating from Free
Choice? For grace is of itself the formal principle of supernatural acts, as
both sides concede. 2. Nor is it asked whether the efficient activity of
actual grace, with respect to specification, comes from Free Choice. For it is
not from Free Choice but from the nature of grace that grace acts in the manner
of grace, that is, in a supernatural and spiritual manner, suitably to its own
nature and character, in the manner of an agent determined to one thing. 3.
Nor is it asked:

[1668 KBNL/UvA witness | logical printed p. 489 | PDF p. 540]

Do refusing or willing, assenting or dissenting, and such vital acts flow from
grace as from a formal principle? For grace itself is not that which formally
wills and does not will, but only the will furnished with the habit of grace.

Thus it is asked: Does Conversion rather than non-Conversion, and assent given
to God who calls rather than dissent, come from actual grace determining the
free will, furnished with habitual grace, according to God's eternal purpose
of election; or from Free Choice, which is indeed indifferent but determines
itself to one of the opposites? The Arminians make the efficient activity and
second act of grace depend upon the outcome, that is, upon the actual influence
of Free Choice, which is of itself indifferent to acting or not acting. We,
however, hold that the actual efficient activity of inward grace depends: 1.
Upon the intention of God, who elects freely. 2. Upon habitual grace as a
formal principle. And 3. Upon actual grace.

1. For Scripture speaks thus. Deuteronomy 30:6: “Jehovah will circumcise your
heart and the heart of your offspring, that you may love the Lord your God
with all your heart.” Ezekiel 36:26–27: “I will also give you a new heart and
put a new spirit within you.” Deuteronomy 29:3: “Your eyes saw those very great
trials, those very great signs and wonders.” Verse 4: “But Jehovah had not
given you a heart to understand, eyes to see, and ears to hear, even to this
day.” Isaiah

[1668 KBNL/UvA witness | logical printed p. 490 | PDF p. 541]

44:3: “For I will pour waters upon the thirsty and streams upon the dry ground;
I will pour my Spirit upon your offspring and my blessing upon your
descendants.” Verse 4: “And they will flourish as among the grass, like willows
beside the courses of waters.” Here the flourishing and second act of grace is
ascribed to the Spirit poured out. Zechariah 12:10: “And I will pour upon the
house of David, etc., the spirit of grace and supplications; and they will look
upon me whom they pierced. They will mourn over this as in mourning for an only
son, and they will mourn bitterly over him as one mourns bitterly for a
firstborn.” Jeremiah 24:7: “I will give them a heart to know me, and they will
return to me with their whole heart.” Matthew 11:25: “I thank you, Father, Lord
of heaven and earth, because you have hidden these things from the wise and
revealed them to little children. Even so, O Father, because thus it was well
pleasing before you.” John 6:37: “All whom the Father has given me come to me.”
Verse 45: “Everyone who has heard and learned from the Father comes to me.”
Philippians 1:29: “It has been given to you, for Christ, not only to believe in
him but also to suffer for him.” Philippians 2:13: “For God effects in you both
to will and to accomplish according to his good pleasure.”

2. For all the saints' solid consolation and patience, all faith, and all
gratitude are overthrown if the cause that effectually determines one to
believe is changeable human Liberty and not God's special grace.

3. It conflicts with true humility. For if

[1668 KBNL/UvA witness | logical printed p. 491 | PDF p. 542 | split word joined: actualis]

actual Conversion is from Free Choice and not from grace, then the converted
person owes the glory of Conversion to himself, not to God and his grace.

4. If the efficacy of grace with respect to the outcome depended upon Free
Choice, then Conversion and salvation would be of the one willing and running,
not of God who shows mercy; nor would God show mercy upon whom he wills.

5. In vain do we pray for the Conversion of human beings or give thanks to God
for conversion, contrary to the Scriptures. Ephesians 1:17–18; 2 Thessalonians
1:3; Colossians 1:12–14.

6. The whole ministry of the Gospel is overthrown, for it tends to this: that a
human being, stripped of all his own boasting, should glory only in God, in
Christ his Savior, and in his grace. 1 Corinthians 1:26, 31; Matthew 11:25. All
the arguments brought forward above for the irresistible and indeflectible
operation of special grace also prove this assertion of ours powerfully.

It is asked: Is non-conversion to be ascribed to God, who denies the effectual
grace by which a human being would be converted, and not instead to the human
being who refuses to believe and struggles against God's calling, if someone
is not converted because God denied effectual grace? The Remonstrants say that
the guilt of non-conversion is cast upon God, not upon the human being, if the
efficacy of grace does not depend upon Free Choice. We deny it.

1. For God, by denying effectual grace which he is bound to give to no one,
will be the physical cause

[1668 KBNL/UvA witness | logical printed p. 492 | PDF p. 543 | split word joined: non-conversionis]

of non-conversion negatively; but he will not be the moral and culpable cause
of privative and moral non-conversion, because God may do with his own what
pleases him.

2. For God so denies effectual grace, which he is not obligated to give, that
the unconverted person himself acts against God's command and rejoices that he
lacks that effectual grace, and from the innate liberty of a depraved will
maliciously struggles against God who calls.

3. For here God is an agent above every Law, while the human being is subject
to the Law and acts without right and contrary to legal obligation. Here God,
however, is an Agent not from a debt of Justice or natural equity but from his
own purely free good pleasure.

4. For if non-conversion is imputed to God on this ground, then conversion will
come from the good use and influence of Free Choice, which is for grace to be
given according to merits and is Pelagian.

It is asked: Can it happen that, of two people furnished with equal habitual
grace, one is converted because he wills, while the other is not converted
because he does not will? The Arminians say yes. We deny it.

1. For the person converted through Free Choice would separate himself from
the unconverted person, would have something in which to glory, and would say,
“I have from myself an act of Conversion which I did not receive from God and
which the other person, furnished with equal grace, refused to have.”

[1668 KBNL/UvA witness | logical printed p. 493 | PDF p. 544]

2. For Free Choice, not grace, would be the cause of conversion.

3. For there would be no reason why the converted person should render thanks
for his conversion to God.

It is asked: Is it from Free Choice, or rather from the nature of grace as
weaker or stronger, that the supernatural acts of believing and repenting are
more intense or weaker? The Remonstrants say that it is from Free Choice. We
deny it.

1. For Paul says, 1 Corinthians 15:10: “But I labored more abundantly than they
all—yet not I, but the grace of God which is with me.”

2. For the one who believes and repents more intensely would have something
which he did not receive from God and could glory in it as though he had not
received it.

3. For in vain would we pray for increased faith, and the Apostles would
wrongly say, “Lord, increase our faith”; and increased and more intense faith
would be from Free Choice, not from God's grace.

4. Thanks for more intense faith would not have to be rendered to God.

5. Nor would one have to acquiesce humbly in the wise dispensation of God, who
distributes grace by that measure which seems good to him.

6. If more intense faith is from Free Choice and not from God's grace, then
faith simply and in a positive degree will also be from Free Choice and will
not be God's gift.

It is asked: Does God influence a supernatural

[1668 KBNL/UvA witness | logical printed p. 494 | PDF p. 545]

work by actual grace because the created will influences it; or does the will
influence it because God, prior by nature, influences it by his actual grace?
The Remonstrants say that God influences the work because our will first by
nature influences it, and not the reverse. We deny it.

1. For God's gracious influence is the cause of all supernatural acts.
Therefore, it is the cause why we obey God who calls.

2. For the bride rightly says, Song of Songs 1:4: “Draw me; after you we will
run.”

3. For our will would thus precede God's grace, while grace would not precede
the will.

4. For all the glory of Conversion would be owed to Free Choice going before,
not to grace, which would merely follow at its heels.

5. For God's eternal decrees of Election, Calling, and Adoption, inward
Calling, Conversion, Perseverance, and thus Glorification would depend upon the
one willing and running, not upon the grace, free in every way, of God who
shows mercy.

6. For this was Cassian's blasphemous opinion, book 3, chapter 13: “Sometimes
the grace of God either requires or awaits certain attempts of good will from
a human being, lest it seem to bestow its gifts upon someone utterly asleep or
dissolute in idle inactivity.” Thus Prosper, *Against the Collator*, book 13,
chapter 11: “The great question is whether, because we have furnished a
beginning of good will,

[1668 KBNL/UvA witness | logical printed p. 495 | PDF p. 546 | split word joined: misereatur]

God has mercy upon us; or whether, because God has mercy, we obtain a beginning
of good will.” And
this was Cassian's account of merit; Prosper and Hilary disputed this with the
Massilians. Augustine, *Epistle* 107, *To Vitalis*.

It is asked: Upon what does the certainty and indeflectibility of Conversion
depend—upon Free Choice or upon God's Grace? The Arminians say upon Free
Choice. We say instead upon God's Grace.

1. For Conversion and the number of the elect who will be saved would be
uncertain and contingent if the certainty of conversion depended not upon the
decree of Election and God's grace but upon a cause changeable in every way,
unstable, fallible, contingent, and indifferent to acting or not acting.

2. It would lie with Free Choice that no mortal be converted and saved.

3. The existence of the Church, and Christ the King, Bridegroom, and Head
having Subjects, a Bride, and Members, would depend upon Free Choice, not upon
God's Grace.

4. For God could not convert and save more people than those who actually are
converted and saved.

It is asked: Do the Remonstrants rightly deny that the Lord opened Lydia's
heart irresistibly because he dealt with her will through precepts, promises,
reasons, and arguments? That is, is the irresistibility of grace incompatible
with precepts, promises, and threats? We deny it.

[1668 KBNL/UvA witness | logical printed p. 496 | PDF p. 547]

1. For in the Gospel God commands faith, and yet he works it in us by that
surpassing greatness of power and efficacy of strength which he exercised in
Christ when he raised him from the dead. Ephesians 1:20.

2. For the irresistibility of grace and God's unconquered Dominion over the
will are entirely consistent with liberty of the will.

It is asked: Do the Remonstrants rightly say in the *Apology*, chapter 17, page
184, that the Lord opened Lydia's heart because the whole substance of Paul's
sermon—namely, its precepts, promises, reasons, and arguments—was from God? We
deny it.

1. For then God would also be said to justify human beings because the whole
substance of the Law—its precepts, promises, reasons, and arguments—is from
God.

2. For then that inward grace by which God opened Lydia's heart would be
nothing other than Pelagian grace, namely, the utterances of God which consist
of the precepts, promises, reasons, and arguments of the Law.

3. For then God opened Lydia's heart no differently from the hearts of Simon
Magus and Judas the betrayer, because the whole substance of the sermon
delivered to Judas and Simon Magus—the precepts, namely, promises, reasons, and
arguments of the Law—is from God.

4. Thus Plato opens Aristotle's heart, and Aristotle the hearts of his
disciples, so that they understand Physics and Metaphysics, because all their
arguments were thought out by them with the aid of more common grace.

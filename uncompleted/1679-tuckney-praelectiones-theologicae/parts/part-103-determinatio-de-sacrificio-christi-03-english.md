The third argument is drawn from passages which call Jesus Christ and his blood a “price,” 1 Corinthians 6:20 and 7:23, a “ransom,” Matthew 20:28, and a “counter-ransom,” 1 Timothy 2:6, and proclaim that we have been “bought,” 1 Corinthians 6:20, and “redeemed,” 1 Peter 1:18. Parallel to these are “the one who was going to redeem,” Luke 24:21; “he accomplished redemption,” Luke 1:68; “unto the redemption of the purchased possession,” Ephesians 1:14; and other expressions of this kind.

These passages press Socinus so hard that at last he takes refuge in saying that the word *redemption* is used improperly in this matter. Well done, good and exceedingly pious man! On your position, then, both the redemption will be improper and Christ will improperly be the Redeemer. You have erred thoroughly. But what, finally, do you understand by this so-called improper redemption? A bare and simple deliverance, of course, without the intervention of any price or satisfaction, such as the redemption of Abraham, Isaiah 29:22; of David, Psalm 31:6; of Israel from Egypt, Exodus 15:13; of believers from the devil, Hebrews 2:15; from iniquity, Titus 2:14; from vain conduct, 1 Peter 1:18; and from the curse of the Law, Galatians 3:13. Yet none of these things is satisfied, nor is any price of redemption paid to them.

I reply:

It is not paid to Satan, indeed, since he is the jailer, but nevertheless to the Lord.

It is not paid to iniquity, vain conduct, or the curse of the Law; yet, so that we might be delivered from all these things, it is paid to God the Judge.

and the rest were redeemed when they were delivered from their distresses. But when redemption is said to be effected by some price, we maintain that redemption properly so called is meant. For example, although the verb פדה, when used absolutely in the Old Testament, frequently denotes nothing but simple deliverance, whenever something of this kind is added, as in Exodus 13:13, ופטר חמר תפדה בשה, “you shall redeem the firstborn donkey with a lamb,” it always connotes some counter-ransom and exchange and signifies redemption in the proper sense of the term.

But this is indeed how the matter stands here when our redemption through Christ is discussed: “you were bought with a price,” 1 Corinthians 6:20; “you were redeemed with precious blood,” 1 Peter 1:18–19; “we have redemption through his blood,” Ephesians 1:7; and “he purchased the church through his own blood,” Acts 20:28. These and very many similar passages express not simple deliverance but redemption properly so called, for a price was paid, and that price both possessed and exercised the force of a price. Whoever delivers someone by way of a price properly redeems him. Christ did this very thing through his death, and therefore he was a properly expiatory sacrifice.

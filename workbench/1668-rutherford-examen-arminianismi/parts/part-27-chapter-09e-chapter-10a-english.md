It is asked whether the reprobate were made reconcilable to God by Christ's
death. The Arminians affirm this without qualification.

We distinguish, and we teach that they were made reconcilable through Christ's
death in the sense in which Prosper, in his *Response to the Chapters of the
Gauls*, chapter 9, says that Christ is “the Savior of the whole world because
of his true assumption of human nature and because of the common ruin in the
first man.” Hence, although Christ did not die for the reprobate, they were
nevertheless made reconcilable through Christ's death in this sense.

1. Christ assumed the nature that is common—although not insofar as it is the
common nature of all, but insofar as it is the nature of Abraham, the father
of believers—Hebrews 2:16. Hence devils were by no means made capable of
salvation through Christ, since he did not unite the angelic nature to himself
in his person.

2. Christ expiated Adam's common sin on the tree of the cross, although not
insofar as it is common, but insofar as it inheres in the elect and believers.

[1668 KBNL/UvA witness | logical printed p. 422 | PDF p. 473]

3. Through Christ's death the grace of preaching the gospel was obtained for
the reprobate born in the visible Church, and an approbative offer of Christ
and one of simple good pleasure was made to them. Hence they are called to the
feast, Matthew 22 and Luke 14:16, and they are called to Christ, Matthew 11:16;
Matthew 23:37–38; 1 Corinthians 1:18, 23; Acts 14:46; Proverbs 1:24–25; Isaiah
6:10–11; and Isaiah 65:2–3. These things cannot be said about devils.

But the reprobate were not made reconcilable to God through Christ's death:

1. As though a conditional and decretive offer of Christ were made to them,
by which God offers Christ to them in the way in which he determined actually
and effectually to apply his merits to them, as believers were made
reconcilable through Christ's death.

2. They were not made reconcilable as though Christ bore their persons on the
tree of the cross and suffered the penalties owed by them.

3. Nor were they made reconcilable as though Christ by his death merited
reconciliation, remission, and eternal salvation for them.

4. Nor as though, in the promises preached, God intended for them the things
promised in the gospel, either absolutely or conditionally.

It is asked whether each and every person, even the reprobate, is bound to
believe that Christ died for him. The Remonstrants say so, as does Corvinus in
*Against Molina*, chapter 29, section 16.

[1668 KBNL/UvA witness | logical printed p. 423 | PDF p. 474]

We deny it. 1. No one is bound to believe what is false. But it is false that
God elected each and every person to glory, or that Christ died for each and
every person, as was proved elsewhere. Therefore.

2. Believing that Christ died for each and every person is not the proper and
adequate object of saving faith. For the object of faith is an incomplex
entity, namely, Christ, the sufficient Savior, “made the author of salvation
for all who obey him” and believe.

3. Christ's dying for sinners connotes God's counsel, and thus the decree of
Election to glory. But the counsel, intention, and decree of my election is
not properly the object of my faith; instead, it is revealed to me by
experiential sense and the Holy Spirit's internal testimony.

4. Nor are all the elect bound immediately and without a definite order to
believe that Christ died for them. Therefore, much less are the reprobate
bound to do so. The antecedent is proved: 1. The elect are bound first to be
burdened by the mass of their sins, conscious of their misery, and to rest
trustingly and with a reliance of the heart on Christ as the sufficient Savior
who saves all believers, before they are bound to believe that Christ died for
them. For only those who are burdened and labor are immediately bound to come,
that is, to believe, Matthew 11:28 and Isaiah 55:1.

[1668 KBNL/UvA witness | logical printed p. 424 | PDF p. 475]

2. If those who were secure and unconscious of their misery were bound to
believe that Christ died for them, they would be bound to nourish vain
presumption.

It is asked whether, following Arminius in *Against Perkins*, Corvinus rightly
said in *Against Molina*, chapter 29, section 17: “It is not justly required of
the reprobate that they believe in the dead Christ, since he did not die for
them, because it is injustice for human beings to be condemned for not
embracing by faith that grace which by no means pertains to them and was not
obtained in their name.” We deny it.

1. The equity of the obligation to believe, by which the reprobate are bound
to believe in Christ, rests on the authority and dominion of God the
Lawgiver—not on some right of ownership by which Christ was destined from
eternal election to be their Savior. For Arminius could thus have said that
the reprobate are not obligated to believe in eternal life and the remission
of sins, because in the decree of Election God willed that eternal life should
by no means pertain to the reprobate.

2. It cannot be said that Christ in no way pertains to the reprobate. For
Christ pertains to them: 1. by right of the gospel offer; 2. by right of the
sufficiency of Christ's death; 3. by right of the common nature assumed; 4. by
right of the common first transgression expiated by Christ; 5. by right of the
New Covenant, preached to the reprobate from Christ's grace; and 6. by right
of God's command ordering the reprobate to receive Christ by faith.

[1668 KBNL/UvA witness | logical printed p. 425 | PDF p. 476]

3. Arminius ought to have opposed this Mystery of the gospel with a valid
argument—which it would be fitting for little worms of the dust to adore and
admire, rather than scrutinize, much less reject: “The reprobate are bound to
believe in Christ and to rest trustingly on him as the sufficient Savior of
all believers; nevertheless, by God's intention and decree Christ is not their
Savior, nor was he destined for them as the author of salvation and Mediator.”

It is asked whether no one can rest trustingly on Christ as the sufficient
Savior unless he first knows that Christ was given to him as Savior by a
definite intention of God. The Remonstrants say so. We deny it.

1. One who is going to believe in Christ cannot have a persuasion concerning
reconciliation covenanted for him through Christ before he believes, because
such a persuasion is generated through the act of believing.

2. No one can be certain about God's special, gratuitous love in the decree of
Election until there is something in him that specifically distinguishes the
elect person from the non-elect. For in that case it is always doubtful to him
whether God pursues him with the hatred of reprobation or the gratuitous love
of Election. But faith is the first thing that specifically distinguishes the
elect person from the non-elect.

3. Before someone believes, grant that Christ died for him in the sense in
which the Arminians will have him die for

[1668 KBNL/UvA witness | logical printed p. 426 | PDF p. 477]

all: he has as much reason to fear eternal destruction as to hope for eternal
salvation. For one who does not yet believe and rest trustingly on Christ
knows nothing about God's effectual or ineffectual intention to save or not to
save him, because a persuasion concerning God's effectual intention to save is
generated through the trusting act of believing alone.

CHAPTER TEN.

ON

THE COVENANT OF GRACE.

HEBREWS 8:8.

For finding fault with them, he says: “Behold, days are coming, says the Lord,
when I will make a new covenant with the house of Israel and with the house of
Judah. Verse 9: Not according to the covenant that I made with their fathers
on the day when I took their hand to lead them out of Egypt, for they did not
continue in my covenant, and I disregarded them, says the Lord. Verse 10:
Therefore, this is the covenant that I will make with the house of Israel
after those days, says the Lord: I will put my laws in their mind and inscribe
them on their heart, and I will be God to them, and they will be a people to
me.”

In these verses we have: 1. The foundation of the New Covenant, namely, that
it rests on God's

[1668 KBNL/UvA witness | logical printed p. 427 | PDF p. 478]

good pleasure. He intimates this when he says that those with whom God entered
this covenant are accused of sins.

2. Here we have plainly spiritual promises of the covenant, namely, that he
will give a new heart and inscribe laws on their mind.

3. We have the contracting parties in this covenant, namely, Jehovah and the
household of Israel and Judah, not each and every mortal.

4. Thus the perpetuity and stability of the New Covenant is also established,
namely, that it is eternal and far different from that former covenant which
they violated.

Hence Question 1: Did God enter the covenant of grace with humanity solely
from his free good pleasure, or from some necessity? The Remonstrants indeed
teach in words that this covenant was made freely, but in reality they deny
it.

1. They teach that all human beings were released and freed from the obedience
that the Legal Covenant requires. So Corvinus in *Against Molina*, chapter 8,
section 7. Therefore, either fallen humanity had to be lawless and subject to
no Law, or it was necessary that God enter this covenant of grace with all.
And this is the first necessity.

2. They teach that no one can sin unless sufficient grace to obey and avoid
sin is available to him. Hence either God was obligated to furnish sufficient
grace according to the tenor of this covenant; or, if he had never

[1668 KBNL/UvA witness | logical printed p. 428 | PDF p. 479]

made such a covenant, human beings would have lived in perjury, murder, and
hatred of God and neighbor, yet would have been innocent and free from sin.
And this is the second necessity.

3. They teach that God cannot justly condemn human beings because of Adam's
sin and what theologians call Original Sin, which in their judgment is not
sin: *Apology*, chapter 7, folio 68; Corvinus, *Against Molina*, chapter 9,
section 5; and *Synodical Writings*, article 4, page 145. Nevertheless, they
teach that “all Adam's descendants are born children of wrath by nature,
guilty of condemnation and of both temporal and eternal death”: *Confession*,
chapter 7, thesis 3; Arminius, public disputation 7, thesis 16. But it would
have been altogether unjust for human beings to be subject to condemnation
because of no sin. Therefore, because he could not actually inflict eternal
death, he was compelled to make the covenant of grace and send his Son in the
flesh, lest he be forced most unjustly to execute the Law of works given to
Adam upon all Adam's descendants. And this is the third necessity.

But we teach that all human beings are subject to eternal death, and that God,
from his mere grace and driven by no such necessity, made this covenant.
Ezekiel 16:8: “Passing by you, when I saw you, behold, your time was the time
of loves; and swearing to you, I entered into covenant with you, says Jehovah,
that you might marry me.” And chapter 36:32: “I will not do this for your
sakes,” etc.

[1668 KBNL/UvA witness | logical printed p. 429 | PDF p. 480]

Hence another question can easily be answered, namely: Was God's antecedent
love, by which he inclined from a natural affection toward the salvation of
all, the foundation from which it came about that God entered a covenant with
all mortals? The Remonstrants say so. We deny it.

1. There is in God an antecedent affection of justice inclining toward the
destruction of sinners, as Grevinchovius admits on page 20. Therefore, there is
just as much a foundation in God for not making this covenant with humanity as
for making the covenant. “For there can be no place for the love which tends
toward creatures—the antecedent love—except insofar as the prior love—toward
justice—permits,” Arminius, *Declaration*, article 19, page 12. If they say,
“Through Christ's death divine justice was so satisfied that from antecedent
love he can save each and every person, and thus make this Covenant of Grace
with each and every person; consequently, after satisfaction was furnished
for each and every person, God can no longer will their destruction,” I reply:
according to the adversaries' doctrine, no such actual satisfaction was
furnished unless each and every person actually persevered in faith. And
consequently God still inclines toward the destruction of each and every
person if they have not believed. Further, Christ's death is a part of the New
Covenant, by which Christ in his Testament bequeathed remission and peace to
his friends. Therefore, the Covenant of Grace is not subsequent in nature to

[1668 KBNL/UvA witness | logical printed p. 430 | PDF p. 481]

Christ's death. Indeed, because God entered the Covenant of Grace with the
elect, he therefore gave Christ over to death for them.

2. Such antecedent love is a mere dream, as was proved elsewhere.

It is asked whether the Fathers under the Old Testament had no promises other
than bodily ones and those that belong only to this life, or whether by faith
they looked to Christ and the promises of eternal life. The Remonstrants say
that the faith of the ancient people by no means looked to spiritual things.
So Arminius in his response to article 31 and Episcopius in disputation 5 on
the Old Covenant, thesis 5.

We teach the contrary. We believe that the Jews were saved through the same
Messiah, were justified by the same faith as we are, and looked to the
spiritual promises concerning the remission of sins and eternal life.

1. The blessed Seed was proclaimed to Adam in Genesis 3:14. And Genesis 22:18:
“In your seed all the nations of the earth will be blessed.” Isaiah 7:14:
“Behold, a Virgin will conceive and bear a child, and they will call his name
Immanuel,” which is cited by Matthew in chapter 1, verse 23. Isaiah 9:6: “To us
Jews a child is born; to us a Son is given.” Acts 2:39: “For the promise was
made to you and your children, and to all who will be far off, as many as the
Lord our God will call.” Acts 3:24: “But all the Prophets, from Samuel and
those who came afterward, as many as spoke, also foretold these days.” Acts
10:4: “All the Prophets also testify about him that everyone who believes in
him will

[1668 KBNL/UvA witness | logical printed p. 431 | PDF p. 482]

receive remission of sins through his name.” Acts 15:11: “Indeed, through the
grace of the Lord Jesus Christ, we believe that we will be saved, just as they
will.” 1 Corinthians 10:, 34: “I do not want you to be ignorant that all the
Fathers ate the same spiritual food and all drank the same spiritual drink.
For they drank from the spiritual rock that followed them, and that rock was
Christ.”

2. They not only believed in and knew the Savior materially—as the Arminians
and Socinians will have it, who say that those who have heard nothing about
Christ believe in Christ—but also formally and under the aspect of Mediator.
John 8:56: “Your father Abraham rejoiced to see my day; and he saw it and was
glad.” And in chapter 53 Isaiah prophetically wove together the Gospel History
of Christ's Passion no less than the Evangelists did.

3. There are clear promises that grace and eternal life would be conferred
upon them, and promises that are merely spiritual. Genesis 17:7: “I will be
your God and the God of your seed.” Deuteronomy 30:6: “Jehovah will circumcise
your heart and the heart of your seed, that you may love Jehovah your God with
your whole heart.” Isaiah 49:10: “They will not hunger or thirst, nor will heat
or sun strike them”—which is explained concerning the joys of eternal life in
Revelation 7:15, 19, 17. Verse 15: “Can a woman forget her offspring, that she
should not have mercy on the son of her womb? Even these can forget, but I will
not forget you.” Verse 16: “Behold, I have engraved you

[1668 KBNL/UvA witness | logical printed p. 432 | PDF p. 483 | ends before Quæritur, An factum sit Fœdus gratiæ]

on my hands.” Isaiah 45:7–14 and 59:21; Jeremiah 31:31–35; Jeremiah 32:40;
Ezekiel 36:26, concerning the covenant of grace; and Hosea 2:19, concerning
the eternal marriage between Christ and the Church, which is plain throughout
the Song of Solomon.

4. The faith and hope of believers under the Old Testament rested on God with
a certain expectation of eternal salvation. Thus the dying Patriarch Jacob in
Genesis 49:18: “I wait for your salvation, Jehovah.” Psalm 73:25: “Whom do I
have in heaven besides you? And besides you, I delight in no one on earth.”
Verse 26: “When my flesh and my soul fail, God is the rock of my soul and my
portion forever.” And verse 24: “You will guide me with your counsel, and
afterward you will receive me into glory.” So also Job 19:25–27; Jeremiah 148;
Jeremiah 17:7–8; Psalm 17:15; Psalm 16:10–11; 52:1–2; 26:1–3; 39:6–8; and
countless other passages.

QUESTION XVIII.

THE SEVENTH COMMANDMENT.

What is forbidden and commanded by the commandment against adultery?

I. Just as the sixth commandment commends both our neighbor’s *life* and our
own to us, so this seventh commandment commends both our own *chastity* and
that of others to us. A threefold rationale is assigned for this.

The *divine* rationale is that, since God is holy, it is right for man, who
was created in the image of God, to order his life in holiness and therefore
also in chastity, since chastity is a part of holiness. The *natural*
rationale is that, since the union of male and female belongs to the law of
nature, it must be directed according to the standard of right reason so that
nature itself does not remain unbridled. Therefore, there is no nation in the
world that has not approved marriage and condemned promiscuous sexual unions.
The *civil* rationale is that, just as civil εὐταξία, *good order*, rests
upon the distinction of families, so the disruption of those families
through promiscuous lusts overthrows commonwealths and ruins homes.

[Marginal heading: Under adultery, by synecdoche, every unlawful sexual union
is forbidden.]

II. Therefore, every kind of immodesty and lust is forbidden by this
commandment. It is contained synecdochically under the name of *adultery*,
which is one form of it—the grossest, most opposed to human society, and most
insulting to one’s neighbor. Thus every unlawful sexual union outside

[Printed p. 106 | Internet Archive PDF 134 | IA institutiotheolo02turr]

honorable marriage is condemned here. Under this is included, first,
*adultery* properly so called, which is *the violation of another’s marriage
bed*. This is so whether one person is unmarried or both are married, in
which case the adultery is doubled through the violation of two marriage
beds.

[Marginal heading: Fornication.]

2. Every kind of *fornication*, and simple fornication of an unmarried person
with an unmarried person, is also included. Although it was generally
regarded as an indifferent matter or a rather slight fault by heathens
deprived of the knowledge of God, as it also is by the Roman Catholics, it is
nevertheless expressly condemned throughout both the Old and New Testaments
as a grave sin (Deut. 22:28; 23:17–18; 1 Cor. 6:18; Heb. 13:4; Rev. 21:8).

The apostle notes its gravity in 1 Corinthians 6. The one who fornicates makes
his body one with a prostitute, sins against his own body, profanes the temple
of the Spirit, and pollutes the vessels of grace. From this it is evident how
great a crime entangles those who champion public brothels contrary to right
and divine law. This happens in the papacy, where brothels and houses of
prostitution are publicly tolerated and even taxes and tribute are exacted
from them. Yet God formerly prohibited the wages of a prostitute and the
price of a dog from being brought into the temple (Deut. 23:18).

Espensaeus complains about this in *On Continence*, book 3, chapter 4, while
others defend this infamous cause, such as Emanuele Sá, Toledo, and various
Roman Catholic casuists. Bellarmine admits that brothels are necessary evils
for avoiding greater ones, but here the apostle’s rule must prevail: evils are
not to be done so that goods may result. Nor are the prostitutes said to have
existed in the Old Testament, as in the time of the patriarchs and Solomon,
therefore approved or proposed for imitation. Solomon’s example of giving
himself to many lusts must not be pressed, because it was vicious. Rather, the
zeal of Josiah must be imitated, who reformed the worship of God and especially
destroyed the brothels (2 Kings 23:7), as must the zeal of Louis IX, called
the Saint, who expelled actors from his kingdom and destroyed brothels.

[Marginal heading: Incest.]

III. Third, *incest* is included. It is committed with persons joined by
blood or affinity, if the forbidden degrees in either kind of relationship
are defined from God’s law and not by human decrees.

[Marginal heading: Abduction.]

4. *Abduction* is included, when a girl is forcibly carried away from her
father’s house for lust, whether the girl resists the abduction or consents to
it. For violence is at least then done to her father, whose possession is
snatched from him against his will.

[Marginal heading: Concubinage.]

5. *Concubinage* is included, when a man lives habitually with a woman whom
he keeps at home, not with a husband’s affection but merely for the sake of
cohabitation. She can therefore be dismissed, and the children are regarded
as neither legitimate nor heirs. It is a species of fornication and indeed
adds something more serious. Fornication denotes only a transitory act, but
concubinage adds permanence and a settled state.

Hence the apostle in Hebrews 13:4 makes an immediate antithesis between
marriage and fornication, because every sexual union outside lawful marriage
is fornication. Although concubinage occurred among the patriarchs under the
Old Testament, it cannot therefore be made honorable or drawn into imitation,
because life is governed by rules, not examples. Nor are the faults and
blemishes of the saints that are related by the sacred writers approved by
that very fact. Nor, if concubinage is excused in canon law, can it immediately
be excused by divine law.

[Marginal heading: Sin against nature.]

IV. 6. *Sin against nature* and those unspeakable crimes customarily expressed
by the names of sodomy and bestiality are included. They are mentioned in
Leviticus 20:13 and Romans 1:27 and ought not even to be named among Christians,
although today they prevail far too much in various places, to the disgrace
of the Christian name.

[Marginal heading: Lust.]

V. 7. *Lust of every unchaste kind* is included, both that which has a place
in the heart through evil thoughts and dishonorable desire, condemned by
Christ in Matthew 5:28, and that which displays itself through obscene speech
and immodest gestures. Under this we do not doubt that the following are
contained: face paint and soft and wanton adornment of the body, since
ἀσέλγεια, *wantonness*, flows from the soul into

[Printed p. 107 | Internet Archive PDF 135 | IA institutiotheolo02turr]

clothing and from the conscience to the outward appearance;

[Marginal heading: Dances.]

dances and wanton dancing; base stage plays and theatrical shows, which are
in use today; gluttony, drunkenness, revelries, and the like, which are other
lures and incitements of unlawful sexual desire of this kind. It is certain
that all these are contained under this prohibition, just as they are
condemned more than once elsewhere in Scripture as characteristic of the
heathen and the children of this age (Rom. 13:12–13; Eph. 4:19; 1 Pet. 4:3; 1
John 2:15–16). It is sinful for Christians to devote themselves to them.
Christians must not conform themselves to this age (Rom. 12:2), but are bound
to order their whole life according to sobriety, temperance, modesty, and
holiness (Titus 2:12; Rom. 13:13).

VI. The apostle wills every impurity and wantonness to be absent from
Christians in Ephesians 5:3–4 and 1 Thessalonians 4:3. Yet it does not follow
that this was forbidden only under the New Testament or that it is a
supplement added by Christ to the moral law. It is evident that the holiness
commanded in the law excludes every kind of impurity, both the inward impurity
of the heart and the outward impurity of the body. From the apostle’s words it
is gathered only that this duty is commanded no less by the gospel than by
the law and that what had already been forbidden in the law is confirmed more
strongly in the gospel.

This could be proved further from various passages: Proverbs 6:25, 25; Job
31:1, 9; Genesis 39:7, 9; and Jeremiah 5:8–9. Nor, if the wise man in
Ecclesiastes 11:9 seems to grant a young man permission to walk according to
the way of his heart and the wantonness of his eyes, does he immediately
approve this as lawful. He speaks ironically, immediately correcting his
statement when he adds that because of these things God will bring him into
judgment.

[Marginal heading: Polygamy.]

VII. 8. *Polygamy* is included, not successive polygamy, which is lawful, but
simultaneous polygamy, which sins in the following ways.

1. It sins against *the first institution of marriage* in Genesis 2, to which
Christ recalls the Jews. In that institution God made and joined one man and
one woman, and sanctioned by decree that two, not three or more, should be one
flesh, and that the husband should cleave to his wife, not to wives. Nor are
those whom God joined to be separated (Mal. 2:15; Matt. 19:5).

2. It sins against *the nature of marital love*, which is so exclusive and
singular that it cannot admit a third person to participation in the same
thing, as experience teaches in marital jealousy.

3. It sins against *the nature of the marriage contract*, by which a spouse
no longer has authority over his or her own body (1 Cor. 7:4). Hence the one
who joins himself to another is held guilty of injustice and
faithlessness (Mal. 2:14, and so forth).

4. It sins against Paul’s command, by which he wills every wife to have τὸν
ἴδιον ἄνδρα, *her own husband*. Hence in Romans 7:2–3 a wife who
becomes another man’s wife while her husband is still living is called an
adulteress. Marital fidelity ought to be reciprocal, which could not occur if
one woman could have several husbands or one man several wives.

5. It sins against the unified care of offspring that the end of marriage
requires, which is divided in polygamy.

6. It sins against *the peace of the family*, because countless quarrels
arise from it (Lev. 18:18), as was seen in the house of Jacob (Gen. 30) and
of Elkanah (1 Sam. 1:6).

VIII. The lawfulness of polygamy is not inferred from *the example of the
fathers of the Old Testament*, who were polygamists and to whom polygamy must
have been permitted. Rules, not examples, must be followed. 2. If it was
permitted, this occurred either by an ethical *permission of right* through a
particular dispensation on account of their weakness—because they were still
infants, to whom many things are granted that are denied to adults, a
permission that now ceases under the New Testament—or, which is more likely,
by a *permission of fact*. That is, it occurred through mere toleration, not
approval, so that in this respect they were exempt from civil punishment in
the court of earth, but not likewise from divine punishment in the court of
heaven. They had to be freed from the latter by the grace of God through the
intervention of their repentance, whether explicit or at least implicit. See
above, Question II, sections 27–28.

Nor is the lawfulness of polygamy inferred from the fact that in Deuteronomy
21:15–16

[Printed p. 108 | Internet Archive PDF 136 | IA institutiotheolo02turr]

God gives laws concerning the inheritances of polygamists. Although he gives
a law to polygamists, he does not thereby approve polygamy itself. Thus he
gives a law in Deuteronomy 23:18 that a prostitute’s wages are not to be
brought into the house of God, but he does not approve the payment itself. He
gives a law concerning the choice of a king, but does not approve the people’s
desire (1 Sam. 8:6–7).

Nor is it inferred from the fact that in Deuteronomy 25:5 a man, who could
have been married, was bound to raise up offspring for his dead brother. The
passage speaks about *brothers dwelling together*, that is, those who were
not both married.

IX. Lawful sexual union found in honorable marriage is the remedy for the
promiscuous lust and unlawful sexual desire forbidden by this commandment.
Therefore, for those who are unwilling or unable to contain themselves, the
lawful use of marriage instituted by God is commanded. It is to be the lawful
and indivisible union of two in one flesh through *right consent*, not only
of those contracting marriage but also of those under whose authority they
are. Without the latter consent, the marriage is clandestine and null by the
law itself. The consent must be *full*, not extorted by force or fraud, which
would amount to abduction, and *sincere*, not deceitful, since it must aim at
that indivisible union not only of bodies, but also of souls and possessions,
which as a rule is severed only by death (Rom. 7:2–3).

X. Chastity and purity are also commanded, whether in celibacy or in
marriage, together with modesty, sobriety, and every virtue pertaining to the
true sanctification of body and soul. Temperance leads their company. Through
it, reason restrains lust and every impulse of the concupiscible appetite and
teaches them to obey the reins.

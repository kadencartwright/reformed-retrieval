# Response concerning Certain Church Rites (12 August 1561)

This directory presents the complete Latin text and a fresh English
translation of John Calvin's response conventionally titled *Ad quaestionem
de quibusdam ecclesiae ritibus* at CO X/1 213–214.

> **Editorial status:** This is an AI-assisted draft transcription and
> translation checked line by line against a high-resolution facsimile image,
> not a peer-reviewed critical edition. A qualified Neo-Latinist should
> independently collate it before formal publication or reliance on a
> disputed reading.

## Work identity

| Field | Value |
|---|---|
| Author | John Calvin (1509–1564) |
| Addressees | Unnamed plural recipients, addressed at the close as excellent men and deeply respected brothers |
| Conventional title | *Ad quaestionem de quibusdam ecclesiae ritibus*; supplied by the editorial transmission |
| Internal date and place | Geneva, 12 August 1561 |
| Original language | Latin |
| Genre | Ecclesiastical advice (*consilium*) |
| Subjects | Public confession and assurance, words used during communion, frequency of the Lord's Supper, Geneva's practice, communion of the sick, sacramental procession |
| Printed locator | CO X/1, pp. 213–214 |

The response is a separate documentary unit. Its first-person recollection of
Calvin's arrival and efforts to secure monthly communion belongs to the
answer, while its plural closing establishes unnamed external recipients. No
questioner's letter or wording of the proposed rites is reproduced.

## Edition, transmission, and facsimile

The transcription follows Wilhelm Baum, Eduard Cunitz, and Eduard Reuss,
eds., *Ioannis Calvini opera quae supersunt omnia*, vol. X, part 1, *Corpus
Reformatorum* XXXVIII (Brunswick: C. A. Schwetschke, 1871), pp. 213–214.

CO does not identify a manuscript witness for this item. Its sole footnote
records earlier printings in the Geneva *Epistolae et Responsa*, p. 329;
Lausanne, p. 625; Hanover, p. 704; Chouet, p. 452; and Amsterdam, p. 206.
The present transcription consequently follows CO's main text without
claiming autograph status.

The public source is the University of Geneva's [complete *Calvini Opera*
collection](https://archive-ouverte.unige.ch/unige:650) and its [direct volume
X/1 PDF](https://access.archive-ouverte.unige.ch/access/metadata/f526a338-c95c-4f87-b600-04df723f502b/download).
The captured 362-page PDF was 33,386,580 bytes with SHA-256
`025973d6e4533769d0c2b870dd324497943bc14050eb2bcbcc4c964d4f2a1777`.

Both printed pages occur on UNIGE PDF page 128. Printed p. 213 first contains
the end of the 1558 ceremonies advice, which is excluded; the present response
begins below its CO heading. Printed p. 214 concludes the response in the
upper portion of the right column; the following advice on irregular baptism
is excluded. The entire source spread was rendered at 600 DPI as a
5000-by-6300-pixel image and checked line by line. OCR supplied orientation
only.

## Authorial and editorial matter

[`latin.txt`](latin.txt) begins with *Confessioni publicae adiungere* and ends
with the internally written place and date *Genevae, 12. Augusti 1561.* It
includes the complete three-part answer and closing blessing. It excludes the
CO title, running head and foliation, the title footnote and its cue, and the
heading and prose of the adjacent baptism advice.

The title is a useful description of the question answered, but CO supplies
it outside the body. The footnote contains only the five-edition
bibliographical list above; CO prints no variant readings and identifies no
named recipient or manuscript.

## Normalization and completeness

The transcription follows [`TRANSLATION_POLICY.md`](../../TRANSLATION_POLICY.md):
printer line divisions are joined, paragraph divisions are retained, CO
spelling and punctuation remain controlling, and the consecutive markers
`[CO X/1, p. 213]` and `[CO X/1, p. 214]` identify the printed pages.

No word is divided by the p. 213–214 turn. The syntax continues from
*Conveniat ergo aliquis coetus* to the postponed *oportet ex cognatis,
familiaribus et vicinis*; the translation renders the unit naturally without
inventing a textual break.

Nothing from the main text is omitted. The response covers, in order:

1. adding an assuring promise to the public confession;
2. words used at communion and the desired frequency of celebration; and
3. tightly qualified communion for the sick and its possible abuses.

## Translation notes and independence

The opening *confessio publica* is understood from its promise of pardon and
reconciliation as the liturgical public confession of sins. The unquoted
“words of Paul” are left unidentified. The autobiographical distinction
between monthly celebration, three annual celebrations on Calvin's arrival,
and the then-current four is preserved. In the final section,
*communicatio* is rendered as an actual communal observance rather than a
private delivery of consecrated bread.

Mary D. Beaty and Benjamin Wirt Farley published a copyrighted translation in
*Calvin's Ecclesiastical Advice* (1991). It was not consulted or used for the
wording of this fresh translation, which was made from `latin.txt` and checked
against the facsimile.

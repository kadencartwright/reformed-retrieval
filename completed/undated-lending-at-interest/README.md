# On Lending at Interest

This directory presents the complete French text and a new English translation of the letter printed by the *Calvini Opera* editors as *De usuris*.

The transmitted address reads “John Calvin to one of his friends.” No personal name, place, date, or individual signature is supplied in the French body. The Latin title is the collected edition's organizing heading rather than an opening title in Calvin's French letter.

## Edition and transmission

The transcription follows Wilhelm Baum, Eduard Cunitz, and Eduard Reuss, eds., *Ioannis Calvini opera quae supersunt omnia*, vol. X/1 (*Corpus Reformatorum* XXXVIII) (Brunswick: C. A. Schwetschke, 1871), pp. 245–249.

CO bases the text on an old copy in Geneva Cod. 145, fol. 107 and following. It reports two Latin translations: one surviving in manuscript after the French copy and in the Dupuy collection, and another printed in the collected *Epistolae et responsa*. The manuscript Latin has a different personal subscription, but that derivative witness does not identify the addressee of the French body with certainty and is not substituted here.

The public facsimile is supplied by the University of Geneva's [complete *Calvini Opera* collection](https://archive-ouverte.unige.ch/unige:650) and its [direct CO X/1 PDF](https://access.archive-ouverte.unige.ch/access/metadata/f526a338-c95c-4f87-b600-04df723f502b/download). The body appears on PDF pp. 144–146 of the captured surrogate. That file was 33,386,580 bytes with SHA-256 `025973d6e4533769d0c2b870dd324497943bc14050eb2bcbcc4c964d4f2a1777`.

## Scope and collation

`french.txt` includes the transmitted address and every word from *Ie nay point encore experimente* on p. 245 through *Amen* on p. 249. It excludes the CO divisional title and document heading, all editorial footnotes, running heads, page numbers, and the following consilium.

The French was checked line by line against 300-DPI renderings of all three spreads. Printer line-end divisions are joined while historical spellings, elisions, accents, and punctuation are otherwise retained. At the suppressed p. 245/246 divisional spread, printed *sump-tueux* is joined and the p. 246 marker precedes complete *sumptueux*, with the English marker before “lavish.” The p. 247 and 248 markers fall between complete words. The p. 249 marker follows *vous* and precedes *voules*, matching the printed page turn. No body text is illegible or conjecturally supplied.

## Translation status

The item was translated in Mary D. Beaty and Benjamin Wirt Farley's copyrighted *Calvin's Ecclesiastical Advice* (1991); that translation supplied no wording here. No complete public-domain English version was located in the audit. The English is an **AI-assisted scholarly draft**, translated afresh from the normalized French and checked against the full facsimile. It has not received independent review by a human specialist in sixteenth-century French; cite the French and CO page for exact-wording claims.

Suggested citation:

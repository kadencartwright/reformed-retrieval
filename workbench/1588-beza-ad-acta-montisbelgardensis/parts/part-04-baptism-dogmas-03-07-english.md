## Dogma III

*Regeneration or salvation does not depend upon baptism.*

[Part II | PDF page 122 | logical source scan 121 | printed page 120]

### To Dogma III

1. *The forgiveness of sins is included in the word “salvation,” for there is no salvation where there is no forgiveness of sins.*

2. *This reply is manifestly impious, because it takes away all consolation from believers, who have it stored up in baptism as the foundation of salvation, according to Christ’s saying: “He who believes and is baptized will be saved.”*

We are surprised that one of baptism’s chief ends is omitted here: namely, the forgiveness of sins. Yet we have so far made our salvation depend upon baptism in such a way that we neither begin the foundation of salvation itself from it nor judge that people are excluded from salvation by its absence, but by contempt for it.

### Reply to the Two Preceding Observations

Salvation is the end to which both the simple preached Word and the sacraments—indeed, God himself and all intervening causes—lead us. Andreae therefore ineptly joined Regeneration with Salvation by the particle *or* in that thesis, just as though the two were one and the same, as that phrase is understood in Latin usage. Yet I did not intend to act as a grammarian but as a theologian, and I think rightly so. Since those two things are everywhere expressly attributed to baptism in Holy Scripture—namely, washing from sins and regeneration—why, and with what judgment, did he omit the former? Of course, we take away all consolation from baptized believers when we teach that they were born by a peculiar grace within the church so that this grace would afterward be sealed in them through baptism too!

## Dogma IV

*These are improper or figurative forms of speech: “Baptism is the washing of sins”; “Baptism washes away sins.” But this is the proper form of speech: “Baptism is the sign of the washing away of sins.”*

## Dogma V

*When baptism is called by the Apostles the washing of sins and of renewal, and likewise cleansing, it must not be understood according to the letter.*

### To Dogmas IV and V

Whenever what belongs to the thing signified—as in baptism Christ’s blood and the Holy Spirit’s work, which is inward cleansing through Christ’s—

[Part II | PDF page 123 | logical source scan 122 | printed page 121]

—blood—is attributed to the outward action, we affirm that there is a sacramental metonymy.

1. *We openly condemn this metonymy, as in the Lord’s Supper, because it overthrows the substance of baptism. The water of baptism was ordained by Christ not for signifying regeneration, but for conferring it. Therefore no metonymy and no interchange of names has a place here.*

### Theodore Beza’s Reply to the Preceding Observation

Andreae deals with me here just as though I had said of baptism considered as a whole what I said only of its visible essential part, namely water, and its outward form alone, namely the sprinkling of water. He therefore acts as a sophist by changing the genus. If it is true—and that it is most true the thing itself testifies even to the insane—that the sign is one thing and the thing signified another, it also necessarily follows either that it is false, or true only by figurative speech, to say that the sign is the thing signified itself or that the thing signified is the sign itself.

The same account applies to forms of speech in which either the effect of things signified is attributed to the sign, or the sign’s effect is transferred to the thing signified. Both Scripture and the Greek and Latin Fathers everywhere use such phrases to commend the most true efficacy of the sacramental conjunction, as our theologians have demonstrated for so many years by almost innumerable testimonies and reasons. Because Andreae denies this, contends that two things numerically and altogether of different kinds, joined only by relation and analogy, are one and the same, and dreams that baptism’s fruit and consolation are abolished unless we concede what Augustine ascribes to extreme madness—namely, that signs are to be taken for the things themselves—he is surely more worthy of pity than refutation.

## Dogma VI

*Baptism is a most certain pledge of the washing away of sins, regeneration, renewal, and finally adoption in the elect, in whom alone its power exercises itself.*

### To Dogma VI

We assert that the whole baptism is always offered to any adults who are baptized, but that its inward part is finally received only by those who bring true faith—whether at the very time they are baptized or when, by God’s grace, this befalls them. For we do not confine baptism’s efficacy to the moment when it is administered, but affirm that the benefits of Christ offered in it sometimes precede that moment, as in Cornelius in Acts 10 and as Abraham was justified before he was circumcised, while at other times they follow it, namely in those who do not possess the faith which they profess when baptized but will possess it in due time.

1. *They invent a twofold baptism, inward and outward, although there is only one baptism, consisting of an outward thing, namely water, and an inward thing, the power of the regenerating Spirit.*

[Part II | PDF page 124 | logical source scan 123 | printed page 122]

2. *This is a manifest and open profanation of the divine name in the action of baptism, by which baptism’s power is deferred until its effect.*

### Theodore Beza’s Reply to the Observation on the Antithesis to Dogma VI

See what we discussed most fully about a twofold baptism in the observations on our first antithesis, page 44 and following.

If what Andreae teaches here is true, then an infant’s soul does not participate in reason because it does not exercise its power until some years later. Trees which do not grow green immediately after planting would be dead. To approach more nearly to our subject: neither the thief nor Paul was circumcised with true circumcision, because the former was circumcised in heart only at his death and the latter only after renouncing Pharisaism. Finally, to think that the efficient cause is deprived of its power to act whenever its effect is impeded by a defect in the recipient is certainly an error more worthy of pity than refutation in a person who suffers from an inexpressibly impaired understanding.

## Dogma VII

*Not all infants who are baptized participate in Christ’s grace and are regenerated.*

### To Dogma VII

We think it absurd to say that infants are renewed in such a way that, either at the very time when they are baptized or even before they have reached adult age, recognized Christ, and apprehended him through faith, they become new people when the old man is extinguished. We nevertheless assert that in them are sealed the adoption of the covenant in which they were born and the grace of remission of original sin, provided that when they become adults they do not deprive themselves of these benefits.

1. *This assertion too is openly false and impious, since it denies that infants born and baptized in the church are regenerated.*

### Reply to Andreae’s Observation on Theodore Beza’s Antithesis to Dogma VII

The thesis itself indicates to what extent and in what sense I deny that infants are renewed. I nevertheless explained it more clearly above in another reply, and shall now repeat it briefly so that the mouths of slanderers may be stopped. I distinguished infants born in the church into those who are called from this life before the use of reason and those who grow up, presupposing—without prejudging God’s eternal will hidden from us—that all the former belong to the number of the elect.

I said and say that at their very beginning, from the right to heaven’s kingdom acquired for them through the faith of godly parents, the washing away of original sin and Christ’s perfect obedience are imputed to them. Thus the cause of death is destroyed and the dignity of eternal life is found freely in Christ. As for innate corruption, infants are also endowed, by a work of the Holy Spirit hidden from us, with that renewal of mind which the spirits of the saints enjoy in eternal life as they await the day when God will finally be all things in all his own.

I added, and add, that this work is created in infants in a certain measure inscrutable to us, yet most certainly, since otherwise, if they remained wholly in the flesh, they could not please God. And since I am treating those who die before reason and thus before the use of this truly human life, I wished what was said in this antithesis to mean that they are indeed renewed, but not in the same way as those who live this whole life using understanding and reason. Why would they need illumination of the mind to discern according to God’s Spirit the truth comprehended in God’s Word which they will never hear? Why an ordering of the assent of apprehension through faith? Why a composing of their affections according to the Spirit against the flesh? Finally, why the directing to a right end of all the soul’s faculties which are exercised through the senses and imagination?

Thus far, therefore, I have denied and deny that the regeneration proper to adults belongs to infants of this kind, not that regeneration without which no one will see God. And what I said is given to them at life’s very beginning I confess is also confirmed by baptism: first, because the infants of the faithful were not formerly commanded in vain to be circumcised—and baptism succeeded circumcision—and second, because the prayers of those presenting them and of the church are certainly not useless. Yet, as it seems to me, neither the faith of the presenters nor that of the church is the true foundation of infant baptism.

But in the case of infants who will reach adult age, whom I likewise presume to be elect, why is it said more absurdly that faith precedes baptism than in adults approaching baptism, in whom it is agreed that faith precedes baptism? If these things do not satisfy Andreae, I nevertheless hope they will satisfy those unlike him. If not, I do not in the least avoid the judgment of truly godly and peace-loving theologians.

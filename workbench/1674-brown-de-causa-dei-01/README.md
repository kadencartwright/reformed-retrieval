# John Brown of Wamphray, *De causa Dei contra antisabbatarios*, volume I (1674)

> **Working edition in progress:** This directory contains an AI-assisted,
> diplomatic-normalized Latin transcription and an independent close English
> translation. Both are unreviewed working drafts, not a publication-ready
> edition.

## Work and edition

- **Author:** John Brown of Wamphray (c. 1610–1679)
- **Title:** *De causa Dei contra antisabbatarios tractatus*, tomus primus
- **Edition:** Rotterdam, Henricus Goddaeus, 1674
- **Volume contents:** Book I, divine laws; Book II, worship and sacred time;
  Book III, the nature and origin of the Sabbath
- **Extent of the treatise:** visible printed pp. 1–757, followed by indexes
  and errata

## Source map

- PDF 1–4: cover, binding, and ownership matter; excluded.
- PDF 5: title page; PDF 6 blank verso.
- PDF 7–11: Brown's dedicatory epistle to the magistrates and senate of
  Rotterdam.
- PDF 12–14: Brown's preface to the reader.
- PDF 15–18: separately attributed theological judgments and approbations;
  these are not silently treated as Brown's prose.
- PDF 19–749: the three-book treatise, with visible printed pagination 1–757.
- PDF 750–771: subject and word index.
- PDF 772–773: Scripture index.
- PDF 774: errata.
- PDF 775–778: blank, binding, or rear-cover matter; excluded.

The treatise occupies 731 scanned pages although its visible terminal number is
757. The opening sequence used here is continuous, but later printed-number
irregularities have not yet been fully audited; the volume-level span must not
be read as a claim that every later number corresponds one-to-one to a leaf.

## Editorial method

Long *s*, ligatures, line-end divisions, and typographic `u/v` are normalized.
Substantive spelling, wording, headings, citations, marginalia, Greek, and
Hebrew are retained. Running heads, signatures, catchwords, ornaments, and page
numerals are omitted unless they carry substantive meaning. Printed quotations
are transcribed as Brown prints them rather than silently conformed to modern
critical texts. The terminal errata leaf is applied transparently: its sole
correction within this unit, printed p. 7 line 39, changes `sciscat` to `sciat`;
the corrected form appears in the Latin and is recorded in the Part 01 note.
The English is a full independent translation from the paired Latin, not a
summary, and no modern English translation has been used as a wording source.

CLERGY SERMON

for the doctoral degree, delivered in the Church of Blessed Mary

on May 8, 1650.

PRAYER.

Almighty and eternal God, you who alone dwell in the high and holy place and yet stoop down to see all things in heaven and on earth: we pray that you would deign to look upon us, the most unworthy of all, with the more gracious light of your countenance. Above all we are ashamed to lift these sinful hands of ours toward your supreme and equally most holy Majesty. We are indeed less than all your benefits and unworthy of even the least of them. Whatever rectitude we possessed by nature we have shamefully disfigured, and whatever we possessed by grace we have utterly lost. As for all the aids that your most gracious love for mankind had granted us for restoring anew our former blessedness, we have not so much squandered them ruinously as rejected them obstinately, to the no lesser dishonor of your most holy name than the most deadly loss of our souls, if you should reckon accounts with us more strictly. Our flesh and our heart surely fail; as far as we ourselves are concerned, the salvation of all is altogether hopeless.

But by your mercy, most gracious Father, it has been ordained that where sin abounded, grace should much more abound. We, the most wretched of all sinners, revere this grace alone and implore mercy. Fainting, we thirst for the blood of Christ alone and for the saving efficacy of your Spirit alone, that by the former the guilt of all our sins may be removed and by the latter their filth may be utterly taken away and washed clean. May the merciful

[1679 new-series fol. 35 | IA leaf 355]

Father grant repentance to offenders, so that he may grant pardon to the penitent. Grant strength and victory to faith; hope greater than all the evils that will befall us here; and finally, the blessed increase of love in this age, until in the age to come we attain together the highest summit both of your grace and of our blessedness, through Jesus Christ our only Savior.

Further, in his name we commend to your fatherly care and protection your Church, however dear she is to you, yet today wretchedly torn by schisms, not only stained but infected with heresies, besieged by every evil, and at last fearing the worst. But how long, Lord God of hosts—how long will your people invoke your name in vain? How long will enemies assail your name with insult and impunity? Arise at last, we pray, and swiftly aid your people, whose courage is all but failing. Bind up the bleeding wounds of your Church, and quickly wipe away all the tears that have so disfigured the beauty of your bride, so that her former splendor may at last return and dazzle the eyes of all the malicious, at which, though envious and unhappy, they may ever stand astonished.

Above all the rest, take special care for this British and Irish Church of ours, which also was once among your delights. As she has always been distinguished in all other respects and by far the most renowned of all for a purer profession of your truth, may she never be ill spoken of among foreigners because of our crimes, nor become a laughingstock and mockery to posterity through the punishments most justly inflicted upon us by you.

Instruct our senators, and be yourself counselor to our counselors. Make our governors peace and our exactors justice, so that at last we may call our walls Salvation and our gates Praise.

Fill the ministers of the Word with your Spirit and protect them by your right hand. The more hated they are by this age of ours, the more widely open your bosom to them and grant them closer and more tender embraces.

Show special favor to the schools of the prophets, especially the flourishing universities; to the authorities of both; to our most honorable Chancellor and most worthy Vice-Chancellor; to the most learned professors, the proctors, taxors, and the rest who discharge any public office among us; to every college, and by name Emmanuel College; and to its fellows, scholars, and all other students both of godliness and of the liberal arts.

For the rest, deign both to be present with and preside over us who have assembled here in your name,

[1679 new-series fol. 36 | IA leaf 356]

so that we who can do nothing without you may at last, under your guidance, be able both to speak what is true and do what is right, and finally attain those eternal things through Jesus Christ our Lord and Savior. Further, in his name we address you most humbly, as he himself taught us in the gospel:

Our Father, who are in heaven, hallowed be your name. Your kingdom come. Your will be done on earth as it is in heaven. Give us today our daily bread. And forgive us our debts, as we also forgive our debtors. And lead us not into temptation, but deliver us from evil. For yours is the kingdom, and the power, and the glory, forever and ever. Amen.

# John Calvin Texts Without a Located Public-Domain English Translation

Bibliographical audit current to **11 August 2026**.

## Purpose and scope

This document inventories Calvin texts for which the audit did not locate a
**complete, reusable public-domain English translation**. It includes original
Latin and French works, short and collaborative documents, correspondence,
sermons, and materially distinct states of the *Institutes*. It is intended as
a research and translation queue, not as proof that an obscure translation
never circulated.

For Calvin, this document supersedes an earlier preliminary survey that had a
narrower remit. Several false positives from that first pass are corrected
below.

The audit distinguishes:

- **work identity** from the identity of a particular edition, translation, or
  manuscript witness;
- a complete translation from excerpts, summaries, quotations, or machine
  renderings;
- free online access from a legal public-domain or CC0 dedication; and
- Calvin's sole authorship from collective, ghostwritten, attributed, or
  disputed documents.

### Public-domain criterion

The working jurisdiction is the United States. As of 2026, works published in
the United States before 1 January 1931 are in the public domain. Calvin's
sixteenth-century texts are themselves public domain, but a modern English
translation has a separate copyright. Unpublished manuscripts, later critical
editions, and non-U.S. reuse require their own legal analysis. See the [U.S.
Copyright Office overview](https://copyright.gov/what-is-copyright/).

No item is called untranslated in an absolute sense. **N** means only that the
searches described here did not locate a complete English version. Before work
begins on an item, its title, variant titles, incipit, and exact witness should
receive a fresh catalogue and full-text search.

### Status legend

- **M — modern only:** a complete English translation exists, but no
  public-domain or CC0 English version was located.
- **N — none located:** no complete English translation was located.
- **C — corpus gap:** the corpus contains many outstanding texts, but a
  document-level concordance or overlap audit is still required.
- **R — repository edition:** the audit found a gap, and this repository now
  supplies a new normalized source text and CC0 draft English translation.
- **† — qualification:** attribution, collective authorship, source language,
  recension, completeness, or witness identity needs to be retained in the
  edition metadata.

Throughout this inventory, “CC0 draft English” refers only to the
project-authored content of the linked `texts/*/english.md` file. It does not
describe the normalized source transcription, README, metadata, or research
note. See the repository's [path-based licensing statement](../LICENSING.md).

## Bibliographical controls

The principal corpus controls were:

- the University of Geneva's [complete digital collection of the 59-volume
  *Ioannis Calvini opera quae supersunt omnia*
  (CO)](https://archive-ouverte.unige.ch/unige:650), including its general
  index;
- the H. Henry Meeter Center's [title list of Calvin's original
  editions](https://calvin.edu/sites/default/files/migrated/centers-institutes-meeter-center-files-microform-collection-Works-of-John-Calvin-Titlelist.pdf)
  and its [English-language resource overview](https://calvin.edu/meeter-center/resources);
- the [Post-Reformation Digital Library Calvin
  bibliography](https://prdl.org/author_view.php?a_id=1);
- the University of Birmingham [Neo-Latin Calvin
  bibliography](https://philological.cal.bham.ac.uk/bibliography/calvin.html);
  and
- early-English catalogues, scans, modern publishers' records, dissertations,
  and journal contents cited under the relevant entries.

CO is the indispensable organizing edition, but it is not literally exhaustive
of every Calvin text or witness; T. H. L. Parker's [“Calvini Opera sed non
omnia”](https://www.cambridge.org/core/journals/scottish-journal-of-theology/article/abs/calvini-opera-sed-non-omnia/2B5E074CF7B0A97B513E3F8CFA904A35)
is an important caution. Modern *Supplementa Calviniana* (SC) and *Calvini
Opera Recognita* (COR) volumes therefore also matter, especially for sermons.

## The *Consilia* or ecclesiastical advice

CO X/1 153–266 contains a closed set of **46** advice documents. Mary D. Beaty
and Benjamin Wirt Farley translated the complete corpus in the copyrighted 1991
volume *Calvin's Ecclesiastical Advice*. The CO presentation-form division is
**32 Latin-form documents and 14 French-form documents**. Before the repository
editions linked below, no complete public-domain or CC0 English collection was
located; this repository now supplies a normalized source text and CC0 draft
English translation for every item in the corpus. See the [contemporary review
and corpus description](https://www.persee.fr/doc/ether_0014-2239_1992_num_67_1_4357_t1_0126_0000_2)
and [book record](https://ci.nii.ac.jp/ncid/BA13228923).

## Correspondence

This is the largest unresolved bibliographical problem, and subtraction alone
cannot produce an honest item-level translation queue.

CO Xb–XX numbers **4,271 editorial correspondence items**, but that sequence
contains incoming letters, outgoing letters by others, and related documents.
Modern scholarship counts approximately **1,247 surviving Calvin-authored
letters**. Jules Bonnet's public-domain English selection contains **686
numbered and appendix entries**, some of which are incoming or translated from
French. Consequently, 1,247 minus 686 equals 561 only under an impossible
maximum-coverage assumption. The actual number of Calvin-authored letters
outside Bonnet is higher, while scattered public-domain and copyrighted
translations elsewhere reduce the unknown no-PD remainder. The defensible
finding is therefore **C: hundreds of Calvin-authored letters still lack a
located public-domain English version**, not a spurious exact total. A concise
survey reporting the CO and authored-letter counts appears in [*Free
Presbyterian Magazine*, April
2020](https://media.fpchurch.org.uk/2020/04/FPM-2020-04.pdf); compare the
[Bonnet public-domain edition](https://www.gutenberg.org/ebooks/45423) and
[Folger record](https://catalog.folger.edu/record/73467).

Five high-confidence examples found without complete English during the audit
now have repository editions:

- [letter 1211, Calvin to Heinrich Bullinger](../texts/1549-06-26-calvin-to-bullinger/),
  26 June 1549, CO XIII 305–307;
- [letter 1324, Calvin to Bullinger](../texts/1549-12-07-calvin-to-bullinger/),
  7 December 1549, CO XIII 488–490;
- [letter 2372, Calvin to Andrzej Trzecieski](../texts/1555-12-calvin-to-trzecieski/),
  late December 1555, CO XV 910–912;
- [letter 2373, Calvin to Spytek Jordan](../texts/1555-12-calvin-to-spytek-jordan/),
  late December 1555, CO XV 912–913; and
- [letter 2373 bis, Calvin to Franciszek Lismanino](../texts/1555-12-calvin-to-lismanino/),
  late December 1555, CO XV 913–914.

The relevant source volumes are [CO
XIII](https://access.archive-ouverte.unige.ch/access/metadata/41532070-e825-447b-bbc7-584bb1347fa5/download)
and [CO
XV](https://access.archive-ouverte.unige.ch/access/metadata/b873ef04-63a3-4f30-8d07-f55388ab850e/download).
Letter 1090, Calvin to Johannes Brenz, 5 November 1548, CO XIII 99–100, is a
probable additional candidate but needs one more translation search.

Two further Calvin letters already had new CC0 translations here:

- [letter 1212 to Laelius Socinus](../texts/1549-06-26-calvin-to-socinus/), 26
  June 1549, CO XIII 307–311; and
- [letter 1323 to Laelius Socinus](../texts/1549-12-07-calvin-to-socinus/), 7
  December 1549, CO XIII 484–487.

Together these make **seven Calvin letters** with normalized Latin and CC0
English in the repository. The separate [Socinus letter 1231 to
Calvin](../texts/1549-07-25-socinus-to-calvin-context/) is indispensable
context but is not Calvin-authored.

An exhaustive correspondence phase requires a CO–Bonnet–modern-publication
concordance with one row per letter, recording author, language, incipit,
recipient, date, CO number and pages, Bonnet coverage, other English coverage,
and source copyright.

## Sermons and congregations

Calvin preached in French. The principal exception at the witness level is the
1 Samuel series, whose only surviving complete text is David Claude's 1604
Latin translation of the lost French. Geneva estimates that Calvin preached
roughly 4,000–5,000 sermons, about 2,300 were taken down, and only a fraction of
the original manuscripts survives. See the [University of Geneva sermon
history](https://www.unige.ch/theologie/calvin-sermons/sermons), [manuscript
inventory](https://www.unige.ch/theologie/calvin-sermons/banques-donnees/inventaire-sermons),
and [Isaiah project description](https://www.unige.ch/theologie/calvin-sermons/presentation-projet).

## Translation-production implications

This inventory is comprehensive at the **named-work and identified-corpus
level**, but it is not yet a safe instruction to translate every row in order.
Before a translation directory is created, record:

1. the work and witness identity, including language, date, incipit, edition,
   and exact page or folio span;
2. the attribution and collaborative status;
3. a renewed English-translation search and its date;
4. the copyright and reuse status of the source transcription;
5. overlap with an already completed work, sermon, letter, or earlier
   *Institutes* state; and
6. whether an outstanding target is **M**, **N**, or a still-unresolved unit
   within **C**, or has already become a repository edition marked **R**.

The immediately enumerable remainder consists of the independent and short
texts above and the qualifying institutional documents. The closed
*Consilia* corpus is no longer part of that remainder: all **46 documents (32
Latin-form + 14 French-form)** are linked **R/R†** repository editions. The
sermon and correspondence corpora still require item-level concordances before
their true translation counts can be stated, and the other **M/N/C** rows in
this broader inventory remain outstanding as described. This distinction is
essential: a broad claim that “all Calvin” has been translated would otherwise
count incoming letters as Calvin's, duplicate public-domain sermons, conflate
editions, or reproduce copyrighted critical transcriptions.

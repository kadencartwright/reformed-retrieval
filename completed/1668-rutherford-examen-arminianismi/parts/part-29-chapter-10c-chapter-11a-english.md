MATTHEW 10:5.

Do not go into the way of the Gentiles, and do not enter a city of the
Samaritans.

It is asked whether the gospel, or this word of the covenant, is preached to
nations and human beings with regard to their merits or demerits, or whether
it is preached from sheer grace and God's good pleasure. The Remonstrants,
article 2, page 326, say that the gospel is preached from grace, but that the
reason it is not preached to certain nations is their own wickedness and
depravity, since God, of himself and from his antecedent will to save everyone,
is ready to grant his gospel to all nations and human beings.

[1668 KBNL/UvA witness | logical printed p. 447 | PDF p. 498]

We, however, affirm that it depends on God's good pleasure alone, apart from
all human merits or demerits, that the gospel is preached to these Nations but
not to those.

1. God does whatever he is bound to do. But in Psalm 147:19 God “declares his
word to Jacob”; “he has not done so to every nation.” Acts 14:16 says, “In past
ages God permitted all nations to walk in their own ways.” Acts 16:6–7 says,
“And after they had passed through Phrygia and the Galatian region, they were
forbidden by the Holy Spirit to speak the word of God in Asia. When they came
to Mysia, they attempted to go toward Bithynia, but the Spirit of Jesus did
not permit them to go.”

2. Nowhere in the whole of Scripture does such a covenant exist: “Those who
have done this or that and have rightly used the gifts of nature will receive
as a reward the benefit of the gospel.” Nor is there such a threat: “Those who
are more wicked than others and use the gifts of nature less rightly will be
denied the proclamation of the gospel.”

3. In Romans 9, “God has mercy on whom he wills and hardens whom he wills.”

4. The whole reason why the Word of the Covenant was announced to the Jewish
people and not to other nations lies in God's good pleasure, Deuteronomy 7:7
and chapter 9:5. It was by no means because of their Righteousness.

5. God denied the Tyrians and Sidonians—who would have been more pliable and
teachable toward the gospel and Christ's mighty works—the aids of the gospel
and miracles, Matthew 11:21–22. But to the Jews,

[1668 KBNL/UvA witness | logical printed p. 448 | PDF p. 499]

who were far more obstinate, he granted the same aids. He sent the Prophet
Ezekiel to the rebellious and stubborn house of Israel, but he denied the
prophetic word to peoples of an unknown language who would have been far more
obedient to God's Prophets, Ezech 3:6, 7.

6. The gospel is a pure gift of God, Matthew 11:25–26, and God is permitted to
do what he wills with his own, Matthew 20:15.

7. If the matter had to be settled by Free Choice, indifferent when all the
things required for acting were in place and indifferent to meriting or
demeriting the proclamation of the gospel, then thanks for the grace of the
gospel granted would have to be given to Free Choice, not to God and his
grace.

8. For, as the Adversaries do not deny, God sometimes grants the gospel to
worse and more wicked people and denies it to better people. Thus from his
absolute freedom he gives the gospel to whom he wills and denies it to whom he
wills.

9. Otherwise calling would be from works, not from grace, contrary to 2
Timothy 1:9; Titus 3:3; and Ephesians 2:3–4.

10. From the contrary position it would follow that God wills the gospel to
be preached to each and every mortal. Because this is absurd, the Arminians
place the cause of the gospel not being granted in the free will of Preachers
who refuse to go to barbarous nations for the purpose of spreading the gospel.
But first, since

[1668 KBNL/UvA witness | logical printed p. 449 | PDF p. 500]

God now does not call Preachers outside the ordinary course, and since God
does not bestow on called Preachers, as in the time of the Apostles, the
extraordinary gift of languages—without which the preaching of the gospel
among barbarous and unknown peoples is exceedingly difficult—and since he
permits Princes, Commonwealths, and companies of merchants traveling there
for profit to fail in the duty of communicating the gospel to those peoples,
and to fail to assign sufficient revenues from goods dedicated to God, where
such goods remain, or to establish new and sufficient revenues from acquired
wealth, this is evidence that God does not will the gospel to be preached to
them. For he who does not will an indispensable means also does not will the
end. Even though God meanwhile wills Princes, Commonwealths, and societies of
Christians to be bound to communicate the gospel to them, that will of God
will not excuse those obligated parties. Second, this is to speak against God's Justice.
Indeed, it is contrary to God's Justice that an innocent nation should pay
the penalties for the sloth and disobedience of Preachers who are joined to
them by no blood relationship.

11. Prayers cannot be poured out to God in faith for the gospel to be granted
to the Jews and other nations if Free Choice is the cause of the gospel being
granted or not granted, when that choice is not even capable of being
determined by God to merit the gospel.

It is asked whether the Covenant of Works entered with Adam is rigorous and
of such a kind that God could not strictly execute it upon his

[1668 KBNL/UvA witness | logical printed p. 450 | PDF p. 501]

posterity. The Remonstrants consequentially teach this in their doctrine of
the penalty of Original Sin, which for them does not exist. So teach Corvinus
in *Against Molina*, chapter 9, section 5, and the *Apology*, chapter 7, folio
84. We deny it.

1. God would be unjust and cruel if, because of Adam's first sin, he threatened
Adam and all his posterity with both temporal and eternal death, and yet it
conflicted with God's Mercy, Clemency, and Goodness for infants to undergo the
eternal torments of hell because of that sin alone—a sin they did not commit
in their own person, but which was committed only in the person of another.
For just as it conflicts with God's Truthfulness and Faithfulness to promise
such good things as he cannot in fact provide while preserving his Justice or
Goodness, so it conflicts with God's Justice and wise Goodness to threaten a
creature with a penalty which he cannot actually inflict unless God is willing
to be called cruel and severe by inflicting such a penalty, whose infliction
conflicts with God's Justice, Goodness, Clemency, and Mercy. But God is not
unjust and cruel. Therefore, according to the threat of the Covenant of Works,
he could justly punish Adam and all his posterity, even infants, with temporal
and eternal death because of Adam's first sin; nor would this conflict with
his Mercy, Clemency, and Goodness.

[1668 KBNL/UvA witness | logical printed p. 451 | PDF p. 502]

2. Since God sent his Son into the world from sheer grace and his absolute
good pleasure, John 3:16 and Titus 3:4, he could have refrained from sending
him and would not thereby have been unjust, even if Adam and all his posterity
had suffered both temporal and eternal death.

3. The whole Law of God is just and holy. Therefore, the threats of that same
Law, when put into execution, are also just. For a Law cannot be just if its
threat is unjust.

4. The Remonstrants suppose that an administration of the Covenant on God's
part under which the entire human race would necessarily have perished
eternally conflicts with God's will to save each and every mortal. Yet no such
will is in God, and the eternal ruin of one human being conflicts with such a
will of God just as much as the eternal perdition of each and every human
being does.

5. From this doctrine they conclude that Original Sin is not properly sin and
does not merit eternal death. It was demonstrated above from the Scriptures
that this is false.

It is asked whether no mortal is now subject to the Law insofar as it is Law,
and to the Covenant entered with Adam insofar as it is such, because this
Covenant has been broken, involves everyone in its penalty, and therefore
cannot at the same time bind them to obedience. The Remonstrants say so, as
does Corvinus in *Against Molina*, chapter 8, section 7.

[1668 KBNL/UvA witness | logical printed p. 452 | PDF p. 503]

We deny it. 1. For the Law, insofar as it is Law, would no longer condemn
murderers and perjurers who have heard nothing about the written Law, although
Romans 2:12 says they “will perish without the Law.” For if the Law, insofar as
it is Law, did not bind them to active obedience, neither would it in Justice
bind them to punishment for failing to render active obedience.

2. Some are under the Law, while others are under grace and married to Christ,
Romans 7.

3. No mortal would be liable to that curse of the Law in Deuteronomy 27:26
because of sins now committed after the fall. For no one sins by acting
against a Law that does not bind him to active obedience.

4. Christ, then, would have died only for the broken first Covenant entered
with Adam, and he would not have been made a curse for our actual
transgression of the Law. Paul teaches the contrary in Galatians 3:10–11,
13–14.

CHAPTER ELEVEN

ON

THE MANNER OF CONVERSION

EPHESIANS 1:18–19.

That you may know what is that surpassing greatness of his power in us who
believe, according to the efficacy of his powerful might, which he exercised
in Christ when he raised him from the dead and set him at his right hand in
the heavens.

From this it is evident that the powers of Efficacious grace are no less than
those of God's Omnipotence, and thus that we believe in Christ through that
strong, powerful, and irresistible action by which Christ was raised from the
dead. Hence, concerning the manner of Conversion,

it is asked whether grace effects conversion irresistibly or not. To establish
the state of the Question, the following points must be noted beforehand.

1. Resistance is a reaction of an agent in a patient, as Zabarella says in
*On Reaction*, chapter 5. Or rather, Resistance is a hindrance or repulsion of
an action, or the non-admission of an action, which follows upon a form. By
that form it endeavors to preserve itself and at the same time endeavors to
hinder or retard the action of a contrary agent by being acted upon and acting
in return. Thus I do not agree with Zabarella when he denies that there is a
reaction or

[1668 KBNL/UvA witness | logical printed p. 454 | PDF p. 505]

reciprocal passion. For everything that resists, insofar as it resists, reacts
and is acted upon in return; otherwise it yields and does not resist. This
occurs either among Physical agents, as when fire resists water; among
hyperphysical agents, as when an Angel strives against an Angel; or among
moral agents, as when a human being struggles against sound counsel. Hence
Resistance is threefold: Physical, Hyperphysical, and Moral. Next, the powers
of agents and patients are either equal, whence resistance is feeble and
incomplete, or unequal, whence resistance is unto victory and complete.

2. We acknowledge, first, that with respect to persuading and inviting Grace,
which some call prevenient, exciting, and outwardly calling, grace is by no
means irresistible but is very often resisted by us. Jeremiah 7:13 says, “I
spoke, and you did not hear.” Psalm 81:14 says, “If only my people had obeyed!”
Second, we acknowledge that with respect to inward and operating grace, all
the elect have a propensity to resist, although it is not complete because of
the overpowering strength of grace. For every contrary is naturally fitted to
resist its contrary; but the habit of corruption and inward grace are
contraries, and this resistance is as it were Physical.

3. The Adversaries concede that illumination occurs irresistibly. *Synodical
Writings*, article 4, page 16: “Whether we will it or not, we cannot but know.”

4. They likewise teach that the affections are drawn and the powers of
believing are given irresistibly. Corvinus, *Against Tilenus*, chapter
12, page 467.

[1668 KBNL/UvA witness | logical printed p. 455 | PDF p. 506]

5. We say that the habit of sanctifying grace and a new heart are infused
irresistibly. The Arminians deny such an infusion because they maintain that
conversion is an act of our Free Choice.

6. Three things must be distinguished in the will. 1. The power of believing.
This is conferred irresistibly, as they say. 2. There is the perception of the
Holy Spirit's knockings and persuasions. Concerning this Corvinus says in
*Against Bogerman*, pages 270–271: “The Will cannot fail to sense the impulses
of the illuminated mind, the persuasion and enticements.” And in *Against
Molina*, chapter 33, section 31: “We acknowledge indeed that a human being is
passive in the first moment of conversion, but with respect to perception,
not with respect to assent.” The third thing in the will is assent or dissent.
Concerning this third thing they maintain as follows in the *Confession*,
chapter 17, thesis 7; Corvinus, *Against Bogerman*, part 1, page 363, and
*Against Tilenus*, chapter 5, page 223; and Episcopius, disputation 15, thesis
11: “God,” they say, “never so furnishes a human being with new powers of grace
that it does not always remain within the human will to use them or not to use
them. Indeed, when all the things required for acting have been put in place,
the will can assent to or dissent from God calling.”

Hence, it is not asked, 1. whether grace is resistible with respect to external
means, for these can be resisted; 2. nor whether grace prior to conversion is
resistible, for we concede this; 3. nor whether human beings can resist a more
common grace, for temporary believers resist such grace, Luke 8:13;

[1668 KBNL/UvA witness | logical printed p. 456 | PDF p. 507]

1 Timothy 1:19. Nor, 4. is the question about habitual grace proper to the
elect. For it is also resistible; otherwise the truly regenerate elect could
not sin. Nor, 5. is it asked whether the mind is irresistibly illuminated, and
whether the Will and affections irresistibly sense the persuasions and
invitations of the illuminated mind. The Adversaries grant us this, although
we do not see how they can deny that free Will can call the intellect away
from weighing the reasons set before the Intellect, so that the act of
illumination is thereby hindered. Nor, 6. is it asked whether God brings force
to bear upon the free Will. We also deny this. Nor, 7. is it asked whether the
corruption of the Will is naturally fitted to struggle against the action of
divine grace. But this is the Question:

Does God so affect the Will with new grace—namely, with the special grace of
Regeneration that flows from God's eternal Election—that it lies within the
Will's power to reject that grace completely and finally, and to obstruct its
most powerful action so finally that it utterly resists God calling and is
able to refuse to open the door to him knocking? The Arminians say so. Or does
God's grace, proper to the Elect and brought into act, so overcome the vicious
rebellion and hardness of the heart that it cannot resist God calling
completely and finally, but must obey God by hypothetical necessity?

We teach that the will can by no means resist God's inward grace.

XLII. *Paradox* said in § 4 and § 6: *[In the sacred writings, truths and true
meanings are everywhere joined by an indissoluble bond, so that it is certain
that whoever has drawn out the former has, in the very same act, also drawn
out the latter, because the sacred writings have God himself as their
author.]* From this he concludes: *[All expositions opposed to truth are also
opposed to its true meaning, although not every truth appears also to be a
true meaning, because it might* (he says) *have hap-*

[Page 180 | ONB canvas 234 | ABO Z176010803 image 00000234]

*pened that in the same passage of Scripture, which admits several meanings,
several truths are contained, yet the holy author of that passage had only
one of them in mind, to the exclusion of the rest, when he penned those words,
and wished to signify it.]* To these things *Wolzogen* responds on page 142
and following that it is wrong to ask whether that matter is true, so that
one can conclude whether it was stated; and he says that the *Exercitator*
falsely supposes two things: 1. That everything delivered in the Scriptures
can be classified under truths. 2. That all the truths delivered by Scripture
can be known by reason. He undertakes to refute these two things with many
arguments down to page 160. As to whether in this he has faithfully discharged
his office and refuted his adversary, let the reader judge from his own
statements above on pages 67, 68, and 69, where he said that Reason is not only
a *servant* but also a *guide*, and subjects everything, without even a single
thing excepted, of whatever kind it may be, to examination. By this assertion
he himself overturns with his own hand everything said here. For if reason can
subject everything contained in the Scriptures to examination, everything
can be classified under truths, and all these truths can be known by reason.
For Reason can subject nothing to examination which it does not apprehend or
know, and that which it subjects to examination and concerning which it
judges must necessarily possess truth in itself. He himself admits on page
152 that *Paradox*, unless he wishes to be regarded as a deserter of his own
cause, must maintain that all the truths of Scripture can be investigated by
the aid of reason, because *[He who wishes Philosophy to be the Interpreter of
the Scriptures and the arbiter of the truths contained in the Scriptures must
maintain that all sacred truths are examined by another truth enclosed in the
Interpreter’s intellect.]* Why, then, did he say that Reason is the arbiter of
everything that is in the Scriptures, and that with a view to interpreting
the Scriptures? Has he not betrayed the cause to the adversary? This is so by
his own testimony here, even though at the end of this same page 152 he utterly
denies that all the truths of Scripture can be brought before the tribunal of
Rea-*

[Page 181 | ONB canvas 235 | ABO Z176010803 image 00000235]

*son. For this is merely to contradict himself and make the adversary bolder,
not to refute him solidly. Moreover, did he not himself say above on page 70:
*[Above all other writings, the divine Writers have this singular
constitution, that they contain pure truth, concerning which reason can
judge]*; and on page 68 that *nothing is contained in the Scriptures which is
not true*? From this he had concluded that Reason is often a guide and
determines whether a meaning agrees with the words. How, then, can he now say
that countless things in the Scriptures are not truths properly so called? I
would also like to know what all these things tend toward. Does *Wolzogen*
think that *Paradox* believes all the things he enumerates—namely, histories,
prophecies, ceremonies, mysteries, promises, and things of that kind—must be
proved by Reason, as if they were universal axioms? Or does he think that
*Paradox* does not concede that many things exceed reason? Certainly the
*Socinians* readily confess this. Let your elder *Velthuysen* be heard in *On
the Use of Reason*, etc., page 197: *[Those seem to come closer to the matter*,
he says, *who say that Philosophy treats only natural things, while Theology
is occupied chiefly with matters known by revelation. But they have not
grasped the Author’s meaning. For the author does not deny that very many
things are revealed in the sacred Bible which no one could have known by
reason and principles implanted in nature; but he contends that nothing is
revealed in the words of Scripture whose meaning is not investigated by
Philosophy, or right reason, as a principle, and according to which, as a
norm, the interpretation of a word ought not to be tested.]* What will you now
answer to these things, *Wolzogen*? But there is no need for us to fight
against *Wolzogen* with *Velthusian* arms. This much is certain: *Wolzogen*
overturned all these things above and advances nothing here as an argument
which the *Socinian* adversaries will not concede and which *Paradox* himself
will not also concede. For they will never deny that many things are contained
in the Scriptures which surpass reason, and

[Page 182 | ONB canvas 236 | ABO Z176010803 image 00000236]

that many things cannot be classified under axiomatic truths, as they are
called. Yet *Wolzogen* will not deny that these things are true and possess
truth; and from this the adversary concludes that Reason can and ought to
judge them. *Wolzogen* the betrayer does not overthrow this foundation. He
goes about something else: namely, he proves what the adversary does not deny.
Yet this is *Wolzogen’s* sole argument by which he refutes the adversary. It
must also be noted that on page 159 he appears to concede to the adversaries
everything they seek when he grants that reason can detect a false meaning
with the greatest certainty. For although our men concede that truth is not
contrary to truth, nor can it be, and accordingly that God teaches us nothing
in the Scriptures which is manifestly repugnant to right reason, they do not
therefore say that Reason ought to be the Norm for interpreting the
Scriptures. The *Socinians* desire nothing else, as the very distinguished
*Hoornbeeck* shows in *Refutation of Socinianism*, book 1, chapter 6, section
3, than that reason be admitted to examine whether a meaning is false, and,
if it is detected by Reason to be false, that it be rejected. And for this
reason they reject the mysteries of the *Trinity* and the *Incarnation*, as
well as the *Satisfaction* of Christ, etc., because Reason judges these things
to be false—that is, their corrupted reason. Nor will the *Socinians*
contradict what follows: namely, that care must be taken lest we immediately
believe that whatever reason does not attain is repugnant to reason, as we
have shown elsewhere. And if he admits the *Exercitator’s* Interpreter under
these conditions (as he himself says on page 160), then assuredly, whatever
he may snarl, it is most certain that he deserts the Reformed.

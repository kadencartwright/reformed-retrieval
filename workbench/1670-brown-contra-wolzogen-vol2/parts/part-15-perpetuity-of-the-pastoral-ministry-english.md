ASSERTION XII.

This ministry—or this pastoral office or function, which is proper to pastors
lawfully called, elected, and duly or-

[Volume II; printed p. 206; ONB canvas 214]

dained—is perpetually necessary in Christ’s Church and will endure to the end.
The Enthusiasts, Anabaptists, Schwenckfeldians, Socinians, and Erastians deny
this Assertion, especially the author of *Grallæ*, a certain sect of Quakers
and Seekers, and others whom the celebrated Voetius enumerates in
*Ecclesiastical Polity*, part 2, book 2, treatise 1, chapter 1, pages 217–18,
and following. And although Velthuysen, as far as I remember, does not in this
book contradict this Assertion in express terms, nevertheless, because he has
many things which tend quite directly toward this, I considered it worth the
effort to confirm this truth also. We therefore establish the Assertion with
the following arguments.

I. It is confessed that Christ once instituted a ministry in his Church—the
Apostles and others—to found, form, build up, and confirm the Church. These
testimonies of Scripture also confirm it, Ephesians 4:10–12: “Christ, who
ascended far above all the heavens so that he might fill all things, gave some
as Apostles, others as Prophets, others as Evangelists, and others as Pastors
and Teachers, for the fitting together of the Saints,” and so forth. See also
1 Corinthians 12:28; Acts 20:28; Matthew 28:18, 19. But no evident testimony
is given, nor can any be given, which can prove with any appearance of
probability that Christ instituted this ministry or Ecclesiastical office
only for that state of the Church. If there are any, it belongs to our
adversaries to produce them—testimonies which can demonstrate either
expressly or by good consequence that these institutions of Christ have
become obsolete or have been abrogated. For the burden of proof lies upon the
one who affirms. We will see later what sort they produce.

II. By whatever reasoning it can be proved that this ecclesiastical ministry
was necessary formerly, by the same reasoning it can be proved no less
necessary now. Whatever necessity then pressed for there to be Ecclesiastical
Ministers to perform ecclesiastical duties presses

[Volume II; printed p. 207; ONB canvas 215]

the same today. Did necessity require the primitive Church to have
Ecclesiastical Officers to teach and govern it? The same necessity also
requires today that there be persons who discharge this office. For the
Church now needs Teachers and Rulers who can both teach the truth and govern
the Church well, no less than it did formerly. The present-day Church is
surrounded on every side by adversaries who corrupt true doctrine with
errors. It therefore needs men who know how to stop the mouths of such
frenzied and raging satellites of Satan. Who will deny that the Church now,
no less than formerly, needs men who, instructed and taught by Christ the
Chief Shepherd, know how to comfort the dejected, strengthen the doubtful,
stabilize the wavering, fortify the weak, lead the erroneous and wandering to
the knowledge of the truth, rebuke the vicious, and drive the obstinate from
the flock?

III. Indeed, it seems that necessity now presses and urges much more strongly
for there to be ordinary Ecclesiastical Officers appointed to discharge this
ecclesiastical office. For the primitive Church had Apostles who were endowed
with the gift of infallibility in announcing the Gospel. It also had not a
few persons adorned with the extraordinary gifts of the Holy Spirit to
confirm the truth by miracles. It had persons furnished with kinds of tongues,
their interpretation, and the gift of healings. Nevertheless, it had seemed
good to God to institute ordinary Ministers also to build it up. Therefore,
besides the extraordinary Ministers mentioned above, the Church also had
certain ordinary Pastors and Teachers, and so forth, to feed the flock, “to
reprove, rebuke, and exhort,” 2 Timothy 4:2, and “to watch for souls,” Hebrews
13:17. What? Did that Church, which had so many and such extraordinary men,
need these, while the present-day Church will need none? Who will demonstrate
that this is even

[Volume II; printed p. 208; ONB canvas 216]

probable? Who does not see that it is much more necessary now for
Ecclesiastical Ministers to be set over the Church, since there are no
extraordinary Ministers and those extraordinary gifts have now ceased?

IV. If those institutions of Christ which Ecclesiastical Ministers were
ordained to administer—such as the Word of the Gospel, Baptism, and the Lord’s
Supper—are still necessary today, then the Ministers whose responsibility it
is to administer them will also be necessary. But the former is true;
therefore, and so forth. The major premise is clear in itself, because those
necessary institutions must be administered by human beings, since Christ
does not now administer them immediately himself, nor has it seemed good to
him to use the ministry of Angels. And we proved above that it is not
permissible for everyone indiscriminately—indeed, not for anyone who is not
lawfully called and separated to perform them—to discharge these duties.
Therefore, those special stewards of God’s mysteries, 1 Corinthians 4:1, and
fellow workers who dispense these mysteries and administer the institutions
are necessary. The minor premise is certain among the Orthodox. For who will
doubt the necessity of the preaching of the Gospel if he has once called to
mind that ordinarily through this announcement of the Gospel—as we will also
prove—faith is generated, the people are converted to God, the weak are
strengthened, and all God’s children are built up until they attain to the
unity of the faith and of the knowledge of the Son of God, to a mature man,
to the measure of Christ’s full stature, Ephesians 4:12, 13? Or who will doubt
with the Socinians the necessity of Baptism, which was instituted by God,
Matthew 21:24; Luke 7:30, and administered by the Apostles and others according
to Christ’s prescription, Matthew 28:18—both with respect to the Jews,
Matthew 3:6; John 4:1, and with respect to the Gentiles, Acts 8:10; 10:48;
16:43; 1 Corinthians 1:14, 16? Its necessity is clear from Christ’s command,
Matthew 28:20, and from the fact that the ends for which Baptism was instituted
are perpetually necessary. Is it not as necessary now as formerly that we be
baptized into Jesus Christ and into

[Volume II; printed p. 209; ONB canvas 217]

his death, Romans 6:3, 4; Galatians 3:27, and that we be baptized into one
body, 1 Corinthians 12:13? Who will deny its usefulness and necessity after
reading 1 Peter 3:21: “The corresponding figure, Baptism, now also saves us—
through the resurrection of Christ”? See also Mark 16:15, 16. From this it is
perfectly clear that Baptism is necessary by the necessity of a command. The
same can be proved concerning the Lord’s Supper, instituted by Christ himself,
Matthew 26:26–28; 1 Corinthians 11:23. It was administered frequently in the
primitive Church, Acts 2:42, 46, and because of Christ’s command it must still
be administered frequently now, 1 Corinthians 11:26: “For as often as you eat
this bread and drink this cup, you announce the Lord’s death until he comes.”

V. This can be demonstrated clearly from Ephesians 4:11–14. “Christ gave
Apostles,” and so forth, for this end: “for the fitting together of the
Saints, for the building up of Christ’s body, so that we may no longer be
children who are tossed about and carried around by every wind of doctrine.”
But how long, or to what point, will this administration endure? Namely,
“until we all attain to the unity of the faith and of the knowledge of the
Son of God, to a mature man, to the measure of Christ’s full stature.” From
this passage it is evident that ordinary Ministers—namely, Pastors and
Teachers—are the fruit of Christ’s ascension no less than the extraordinary
Ministers were, and thus were given by Christ no less than the Apostles were
given. But who can take away or abolish those whom Christ once gave to the
Church? It is indeed certain that there are now no Apostles, Prophets, or
Evangelists. From this it is clear that Christ did not will them to remain
perpetually in the Church, nor did he promise this. Otherwise it would have to
be said that Christ did not fulfill his promises but deceived the Church,
that matters turned out otherwise than Christ willed, and that he was thus
frustrated in his hope and desire. All these things are absurd and
blasphemous. Therefore the perpetual Ministers here are only Pastors and
Teachers sent to teach. Therefore

[Volume II; printed p. 210; ONB canvas 218]

they must be perpetually in the Church, or the ministry must endure
perpetually, until these ends are obtained, because Christ does not deceive
his Bride but stands by his promises. They were instituted for “the fitting
together of the Saints,” by which Bodius, on the passage, understands “their
joining and that spiritual fitting together, both with Christ the head
through faith and confirmation to his image, and with one another through
that bond of charity.” And a little later he says: “This joining together is
advanced in two ways. First, when new members are from time to time admitted
to the structure of this body, are gifted with faith and grafted through
faith into Christ the head, while they adhere to the rest of the body through
the glue of charity, bound together by the same Spirit of Christ. Second,
when those already called and grafted into Christ advance more and more in
faith and charity.” Therefore, until this end is obtained the Church will not
lack the ministry: “until we all become,” and so forth. “He instituted”
(Bodius further says on the passage) “this order and these ranks and functions
of the Evangelical ministry to endure until, and to be continued in his
Church until, this admirable work attains both the integrity of its parts and
the perfection of its degrees: until all the members have not only been
called to the structure and communion of this mystical body, but have also
received the due fullness of that spiritual stature, which will finally occur
only at the consummation of the ages. Therefore the Lord wills this order to
be perpetuated in his Church until then.” See also the Belgic Annotations.

If someone says, “How will these orders of Ministers endure to the end of the
ages, when the first and principal three of them were instituted only for a
time? Is there anything here which supports the perpetual duration of Pastors
more than the perpetual continuation of Apostles?” Bodius replies in the place
cited above in three ways. “First, namely, these words must be understood not
jointly but separately, so that this measure of duration expressed in verse
13 is referred not to each of the five

[Volume II; printed p. 211; ONB canvas 219]

preceding members, but only to the last two, to which alone it can properly be
adapted. Second, Prophets, Apostles, and Evangelists still endure in the Christian Church and
will endure until the end of the world—not, indeed, as far as their persons
are concerned, but as far as their God-breathed writings and doctrine are
concerned, which are no less useful and necessary to the Church now than they
were so many ages ago. Finally, if we refer verse 13 not to that more remote
enumeration laid down in verse 11 but to the immediately preceding statement,
no place and no further occasion will remain for this doubt which we raise;
that is, by saying that the work of the ministry and that building up of
Christ’s body which was just mentioned, and to which the preceding gifts are
directed as their end, must be perpetuated until the consummation of the
ages.” In addition, we can add that those extraordinary Ministers were
necessary for laying the foundation, 1 Corinthians 3:10; Ephesians 2:20, but
that God used, and still uses, the labor of others to build upon this
foundation.

VI. This is also proved from that remarkable promise in Matthew 28:20: “And
behold, I am with you every day until the consummation of the age”—πάσας τὰς
ἡμέρας ἕως τῆς συντελείας τοῦ αἰῶνος. Therefore, if Christ will be with his
Ministers to the end of the ages, it follows that this promise was not made to
the Apostles alone, because they were not going to remain in the world until
the end of the ages. It also follows that there are certain successors of the
Apostles with whom Christ will be, after the Apostles’ departure, even to the
end of the world. Therefore it is absurd to say that this promise applied only
to the Apostles, ended with their death, and was limited to some age or some
dispensation—namely, while the Apostles remained alive—because every word
which Christ uses here demonstrates the contrary.

[Volume II; printed p. 212; ONB canvas 220]

For 1. *aiōn* is the period for which the world will endure, as will appear
immediately. 2. Further, he says *heōs tēs synteleias tou aiōnos*, “to the end
of this world, to the consummation of the age.” 3. Nor only this, but he also
adds *pasas tas hēmeras*, “every day, without interruption.” But so that these
things may appear more clearly, it must be noted that the word *aiōn* is
sometimes taken as an adjunct of time; and because it is the same as *eis aei
ōn*, it properly denotes eternity, whether without beginning, Acts 15:18, or
without end, John 4:14; 6:51, 58; 8:51, 52; Luke 18:18; Matthew 25:46. And when
it is applied to this world, it denotes its continuation until the end,
Ephesians 2:2. See also Luke 1:33, 55, compared with 1 Corinthians 15:24, 25;
or duration from the beginning, Luke 1:70; Acts 3:21; John 9:32. Thus it
sometimes denotes the duration of this world in opposition to eternity, Mark
4:19; Luke 16:8; 20:34; 2 Timothy 4:20; Titus 2:12; Matthew 12:32; Galatians
1:4, compared with Matthew 12:32; Hebrews 6:5; Mark 10:30; Luke 18:30.
Sometimes it is taken for the subject, this world, 2 Corinthians 4:4; John
12:21; 14:30; and elsewhere. But whether it is taken here subjectively or as
an adjunct, it clearly denotes continuous duration to the end of the world.
The added word *synteleia* also places this beyond every hazard of dispute,
for the phrase *hē synteleia tou aiōnos* is used elsewhere by the same
Evangelist in the same sense, as may be seen in Matthew 13:39, 40, 49; 24:3.
See Calvin and Pareus on the passage.

VII. The same is clear from other promises, such as Matthew 16:19; John 20:23:
“Whose sins you remit, they will be remitted to them; and whose you retain,
they are retained,” and similar passages. For if there is now no ministry by
divine right, these and similar promises will be empty.

VIII. Christ promises sympathy, Matthew 10:40: “He who receives you receives
me, and he who receives me receives him who sent me.” See also Luke 10:16;
John 13:20. All these promises will be of no use if there is no

[Volume II; printed p. 213; ONB canvas 221]

ministry now by divine right.

IX. This is also confirmed by those passages of Scripture in which the
members of the Church are commanded how they ought to conduct themselves
toward Pastors. For a nonexistent thing has no attributes; otherwise it must
be said that all those commands perished with that age, which is absurd. See
Hebrews 13:7, 17; 1 Timothy 5:17; 1 Thessalonians 5:12, 13; 1 Corinthians 4:1.

X. If the ministry were not going to endure to the end, to what purpose would
those passages of Scripture pertain which treat the support and other
necessities to be given to Ecclesiastical Ministers, such as 1 Corinthians
9:7–19; Galatians 6:6; 1 Timothy 5:17? Did God will to provide for ministers
in this way if they were not going to exist and remain to the end?

XI. It will be proved further from those passages in which Ecclesiastical
Ministers are prescribed how they ought to discharge that office. There are
very many; it will suffice to name a few: Acts 20:28; Colossians 3:17; 1
Timothy 6:11–16; 2 Timothy 2:24, 25; 4:2; Matthew 28:19; 1 Corinthians 4:1;
11:24; 1 Timothy 5:22; 1 Peter 5:2, 3; Titus 2:2, and so forth. Two
testimonies prove that all these did not perish with that age. (1.) 1 Timothy
6:14. In this Epistle the Apostle prescribed many things for Ecclesiastical
Officers, and now at the end he addresses Timothy solemnly in this way: “I
charge you before God—to observe this command undefiled and blameless until
that glorious coming of our Lord Jesus Christ.” From this it is evident that
all these commands applied not only to Timothy but to all Ecclesiastical
Ministers who will be in the Church until that glorious coming of Jesus
Christ. “By the word *command*” (Calvin says on the passage) “he signifies
everything which he has discussed thus far concerning Timothy’s office, the
sum of which was that he should show himself a faithful minister to Christ
and the Church.” (2.) Revelation 2:24, 25: “But I say to you and to the rest
of the Thyatirans: hold fast what you have until

[Volume II; printed p. 214; ONB canvas 222]

I come.” This applied not only to the pastors or members of that flock, for
they died long ago, but applies also to all believers and pastors to the end
of the world.

XII. This is also confirmed by all those conditions and qualifications
required in pastors to be ordained: that they be apt to teach, 1 Timothy 3:2;
that they be blameless, 2 Timothy 2:2—see also Titus 1:6–8; that they hold
fast the faithful word and be fit to exhort and convince those who contradict,
Titus 1:9; and that they know how to divide the word of truth rightly, 2
Timothy 2:15—see also Luke 12:42; 1 Corinthians 4:2. For what purpose, I ask,
would these qualifications be prescribed if the ministry was going to perish
immediately afterward and not endure beyond that age?

XIII. Indeed, if so, is it credible that Paul willed so earnestly to adjure
Timothy to proceed carefully in ordaining pastors and to observe the
prescribed rules exactly? And did he adjure not only Timothy but, in and
through him, all who possess this power of ordaining Ministers and of
approving and examining those to be ordained, 1 Timothy 5:21, 22, so that they
might observe these things “without prejudice, doing nothing by inclining to
either side,” and so forth?

XIV. It was formerly promised and predicted that there would be Ecclesiastical
Ministers in the New Testament. Isaiah 30:20: “But none of those who teach you
will any longer be confined to a corner; your eyes will see your teachers.”
And chapter 62:6, 7: “Upon your walls—I will appoint watchmen who will not
keep silent continually, all day and all night,” and so forth. Chapter 66:21:
“And from those I will also take Priests and Levites, says Jehovah.” Jeremiah
3:15: “I will give you pastors according to my mind, who will feed you with
knowledge and understanding.” And chapter 23:4: “I will appoint pastors over
them who will feed them, so that they will no longer fear or be dismayed, nor
will any be missing, says Jehovah.” Ezekiel 44:23: “And in teaching my

[Volume II; printed p. 215; ONB canvas 223]

people, let them establish for them the distinction between the holy and
profane and between the unclean and clean.” Commentators testify that all these things
must be taken of the time of the New Testament, and the circumstances confirm
that they are not restricted only to the time of the Apostles.

XV. The same is confirmed from Luke 12:42, where Christ says that he will
appoint faithful and prudent stewards over his household. Nor is this only
for that age, but until the end, for in verse 43 he says: “Blessed is that
servant whom his Lord, when he comes, finds doing so.”

XVI. The Gospel must be announced to all nations, Matthew 24:14; 26:13; Mark
16:15; Luke 24:47. And although this was done in large part in the Apostles’
time, nevertheless no one doubts that this promise will yet have a fuller
fulfillment and that the Gospel is to be propagated far and wide before the
burning of the world. Therefore there must be a ministry to perform this work.

XVII. The Holy Spirit says that the condition of those who lack the ministry
is most wretched, Proverbs 29:18: “When there is no vision, the people are
scattered.” And God threatens the removal of the ministry as a severe
judgment, Amos 8:11, 12; Psalm 74:9; Matthew 9:36–38; 21:42.

XVIII. If this Ecclesiastical office were not necessary now, it would follow
either that faith is not necessary now, or that it is ordinarily produced in
some way other than through the ministry of Ecclesiastical Ministers who have
been specially called to this office. But both are absurd. As to the first,
without faith there is no salvation, because without faith there is no
justification, Romans 3:28; 4:13; Habakkuk 2:4; Romans 5:1; no sanctification,
Acts 15:9; no salvation, Ephesians 2:8; 1 Peter 2:9; no communion with Christ,
Ephesians 3:17; and no victory, 1 John 5:4. Without it we will not be pleasing
to God, Hebrews 11:

[Volume II; printed p. 216; ONB canvas 224]

6; we cannot call upon God, Romans 10:14; and our prayers will not be heard,
James 1:6. As to the second point, we have confirmed it abundantly; see
Assertion XIV. Therefore the ministry must endure perpetually to the end of
the world, so that by this means the elect who are not yet called may be
converted to Jesus Christ.

XIX. If the Ecclesiastical ministry were not an office instituted by Christ
and destined to endure perpetually in the Church, there would be no
ministerial Church today. For it is impossible that there be a ministerial and
organic Church in which no Ministers and other Ecclesiastical Officers are
appointed.

XX. Indeed, if this Assertion were not true, it would then be all over with
the Church, both visible and invisible. For without the ministry who would be
led either to a profession of faith or to embrace the Evangelical promises
with true faith? For, as was said, this is the ordinary means instituted by
Christ to gather, establish, preserve, and strengthen his Church.

XXI. The same can be confirmed from the titles with which Ecclesiastical
Ministers are adorned. By these they are not only clearly distinguished from
the rest of the flock, but it is also demonstrated how necessary they are.
(1.) They are *Pastors*, Ephesians 4:11; Acts 20:28; 1 Peter 5:2. But who does
not know how necessary shepherds are for feeding, preserving, and guarding a
flock? (2.) *Teachers*, 1 Corinthians 12:28; Galatians 6:6. Do the ignorant
not need Teachers who teach and lead them into the truth? (3.) They are
*Stewards of the mysteries of God*. Is it not necessary now that these
mysteries be dispensed to us? Or are there other stewards? (4.) They are
called *Stars*, Revelation 1:20. But we all know how necessary stars are to
illuminate this whole world. (5.) They are also called *Watchmen*, Ezekiel
3:7; 33:2; Isaiah 52:8; 56:19; Jeremiah 6:17; Hosea 9:8. But watchmen

[Volume II; printed p. 217; ONB canvas 225]

are necessary to an army or a besieged city. Ministers are no less necessary
to the Church now militant and besieged on every side by enemies. (6.)
Likewise, *Architects*, 1 Corinthians 3:9, 10, without whom a house cannot be
built or erected. So too, without Ecclesiastical Ministers the Church will
not be erected. It is God’s spiritual temple, Zechariah 6:12; 1 Corinthians
3:16, and God’s spiritual house, 1 Timothy 3:15; Hebrews 3:2; 1 Peter 3:5, and
so forth. (7.) They are also called *Fathers*, 1 Corinthians 4:13; Galatians
4:19. Therefore they are necessary for instrumentally begetting God’s
spiritual children. (8.) They are *Governors* and those who *rule*, 1 Timothy
5:17; Hebrews 13:17. But no Commonwealth can endure long without Rulers;
therefore neither can the Church endure without Ecclesiastical Officers. (9.)
They are also called the *Salt of the earth*, Matthew 5:13, because through
them, as Jerome says on the passage, “the whole human race is seasoned.”
Therefore, just as salt preserves flesh from worms and other corruption and
is supremely necessary for this and other uses, so too Ecclesiastical
Ministers will be necessary. (10.) They are called the *Light of the world*,
Matthew 5:13, because they are burning and shining lamps, John 5:35; because
through the announcement of the Gospel they illuminate the world lying in
Cimmerian darkness and drive away the darkness of ignorance. For this same
reason they are compared to a *lampstand*, Revelation 1:13. (11.) They are
*laborers hired into the vineyard*, Matthew 20:1, and *cultivators of the
vineyard*, Matthew 21:33; Mark 12:1; Luke 20:9. Therefore they are necessary.
(12.) They are *servants inviting people to the royal wedding*, Matthew 22:3;
Luke 14:17. (13.) *Friends of the bridegroom*, John 3:29, who betroth the
bride to Christ, 2 Corinthians 11:2. (14.) They are *fishers of men*, Matthew
4:19; Mark 1:17; Luke 5:10. (15.) *Laborers in the Lord’s harvest*, Matthew
9:38; John 4:37, 38. Therefore they are necessary to reap the crops and gather
them into barns. (16.) *Heralds*, 1 Timothy 2:7; 2 Timothy 1:11, sent by God
for the public proclamation of the Gospel to be announced in God’s name.
(17.) *Husbandmen*, Mark 12:2; *Sowers*, Matthew 13:31, because they cast the
seed of the word

[Volume II; printed p. 218; ONB canvas 226]

into human hearts. See Bucanus, locus 42, question 55.

XXII. If the ecclesiastical ministry which Christ instituted was not going to
endure to the end, it is asked: how long was it going to remain? If only until
the death of the Apostles, I ask: 1. Why did the Apostles never signify this
to the Church? 2. Why did they take such diligent and careful pains to have
presbyters ordained in every Church? 3. Why did the Apostle Paul exhort the
Presbyters of the Ephesian Church in Acts 20 to watch over the Church, using
this argument: “And now behold, I know that none of you all, among whom I
passed preaching God’s Kingdom, will see my face again. And I know this: after
my departure fierce wolves who do not spare the flock will enter among you,”
verses 25, 29? 4. Why did all Ecclesiastical Ministers not desist and cease
from office immediately after the death of the Apostles? 5. Why did the
Apostles not limit all the Presbyters and Deacons whom they ordained to that
period? 6. Why did they command Timothy and others to ordain others? 7. Why,
once the ministry was abolished, did they not prescribe another manner of
gathering Churches, converting the elect, strengthening the godly, and
guarding them from seducers and wolves?

If this institution was going to endure even after the time of the Apostles,
I ask: how long? Who will prescribe for us an endpoint not designated by
Christ, or demonstrate to us a reason for the change?

XXIII. Since it is certain that throughout the time of the Old Testament and
that legal dispensation the Church had Rulers instituted by God, why will the
Church not also have such Ministers throughout the time of the new
dispensation? Does Christ pursue the Church of the New Testament with less
love and care than that of the Old Testament? Or does necessity not press as
much now as formerly? Or must we dream of an age of the Holy Spirit, as the
Montanists once dreamed, and afterward certain papists

[Volume II; printed p. 219; ONB canvas 227]

of the Dominican and Franciscan orders, who were the authors of the little
book entitled *The Gospel of the Holy Spirit*, or *The New and Eternal
Gospel*? They fabricated it so that they might respond more easily to all the
arguments of the Waldensians drawn from God’s word. More recently the
Weigelians, the Brothers of the Rosy Cross, and similar Fanatics have done the
same.

XXIV. This privilege, *to have no Temple*, belongs uniquely to the Church
triumphant, Revelation 21:22. But by the *Temple* here are understood all the
means which God now uses to build up his Church. Therefore, as long as the
Church is militant, it needs the temple and also needs the ministry. In the
Church triumphant prophecies will cease, 1 Corinthians 13:8, but not here; for
the command always applies: “Do not regard prophecies as nothing,” 1
Thessalonians 5:20.

By these arguments our Assertion is proved abundantly. Let us see what can be
objected.

They object, I, with Isaiah 54:13: “They will all be taught by God”; Jeremiah
31:34: “A man will no longer teach his neighbor and his brother, saying,
‘Know God,’ for they will all know me, from the least to the greatest.”
*Reply.* They will all be taught by God—that is, externally through the word
and internally through the Spirit, as the Belgic Annotations explain. The
reply of Gerhard, *On the Ministry*, section 46, can satisfy: “Some reply,” he
says, “that the words of that promise concerning the knowledge of God in the
New Testament must by no means be understood absolutely and exclusively, in
the sense that the external ministry of the word was to be abolished entirely
in the time of the New Testament. (For this sense is contrary: 1. To the
words of the Apostle in Romans 10:14, ‘How will they hear without a preacher?’
2. To the promise of pastors to be given also in the New Testament, which is
found in Jeremiah 3:14. 3. To Christ’s discourse in John 6:45, where in the
very act of ministry and preaching he cites this promise: ‘They will all be
*didaktoi tou Theou*—taught by God.’ 4. To experience itself, which teaches
that without someone instructing him, no one

[Volume II; printed p. 220; ONB canvas 228]

aspires to the knowledge of God, Acts 8:31, and which testifies that for some
ages of the New Testament the external ministry has been preserved by God.)
But they must be taken comparatively, namely in this sense: 1. That the
knowledge of God in the New Testament will be more illustrious and fuller
than it was in the Old. For today a young child instructed in catechesis
understands the mystery of the Gospel concerning Christ more explicitly than
one of the chief Priests formerly did in the Old Testament. The prophecy of
Isaiah 11:9 has indeed been fulfilled: ‘The earth is filled with the knowledge
of the Lord.’ 2. That the number of those imbued with the saving knowledge of
God will be greater in the New Testament than in the Old, since the solemn
and public preaching of the word is no longer restricted to one Nation but is
common to all peoples. 3. That because of the spiritual worship of the New
Testament and God’s more abundant grace, there will be no need for great labor
and trouble in teaching the godly. This interpretation is gathered from the
preceding verse: ‘I will write my law upon their hearts’; and from 1
Corinthians 2:3: ‘You are Christ’s Epistle, written not with ink but by the
Spirit of the living God, not upon tablets of stone but upon fleshly tablets
of the heart.’ Verse 6: ‘God made us fit ministers of the New Testament, not
of the letter but of the Spirit.’ 4. So that in this way a tacit opposition
is established in these words of the prophetic prediction between the Old
and New Testaments, between the doctrine of the Law and of the Gospel. The
former is the ministry of the letter, the latter of the Spirit; the former is
written upon tablets of stone, but the latter upon human hearts by the
Spirit, and has the grace of the Spirit joined with it. And these things are
indeed said rightly.”

As for the other exposition which he adduces from Augustine, we dismiss it,
because this exposition and reply which has been given is satisfactory. See
the Belgic Annotations on Jeremiah 31:34. Bucanus gives the same reply in
*Commonplaces, On the Ministries*, question 29, saying that the prophet speaks
comparatively and adding this reason: “For in the Old Testament most things
were presented by Moses and the Prophets obscurely, under shadows and types,
and only according to the letter, so that it was necessary to inquire what
those ceremonies meant and when

Christ would come. But in the New Testament, now that he has been manifested,
he is preached openly; the veil of the temple having been torn and the way of
the Holy Places opened, all those typical things have been manifested,
Hebrews 9:8. And those who have Christ’s Spirit know all things, so that now a
ten-year-old child who is permitted to hear and learn understands them more
clearly than many Priests formerly did, because the earth is full of the
knowledge of the Lord, Isaiah 11:9.” Indeed, there is no lack of other passages
which must be expounded comparatively in this way. See Hosea 6:6: “I desire
mercy, not sacrifices”—that is, mercy more than sacrifices. So also John
7:16; 16:7; Matthew 12:7; and 1 Corinthians 1:17.

Bucanus adds another reply in the same place: “Namely, by an elliptical
expression *kat’ arsin kai thesin*—by negation and affirmation—such as that in
Genesis 42:28, ‘Your name will not be called Jacob but Israel,’ it is signified
that the elect are taught by God not only externally through the ministry of
the spoken word, but also internally through the Spirit, as is said in 1 John
2:27: ‘You do not need anyone to teach you, but the anointing—that is, the
Holy Spirit—teaches you concerning all things,’ namely, through Pastors and
Teachers.” Furthermore, although believers under the New Testament do not
need teachers to teach them as if they were wholly ignorant, they nevertheless
need the ministry to increase their knowledge and to stir them up and remind
them, as may be seen in Ephesians 4:12; 2 Peter 1:12; 3:18; Philippians 1:9;
1 Thessalonians 3:2. This is also clear from the fact that as long as we are
here we know only in part, 1 Corinthians 13:9, 12: “For we know in part, and
we prophesy in part—we now see through a mirror and by a riddle, but then we
will see face to face. Now I know to some degree, but then I will know fully,
as I have been more fully taught.” Therefore that promise will not be
perfectly fulfilled as long as it has been granted us to remain here.

Finally, this must be noted: when that very promise began to be fulfilled,
the ministry was not excluded. For it began to be fulfilled in the time of
Christ and the Apostles, and nevertheless Christ both taught and sent his
Disciples to teach; and after their ascension he en-

larged the commission, Matthew 28:18, 19. They performed this work continually
and diligently. Indeed, if this passage were taken strictly, it would exclude
not only the public ministry but also private admonitions, exhortations, and
so forth, contrary to Hebrews 10:24, 25; Colossians 3:16; 1 Thessalonians
5:14; Galatians 6:1, 2.

They object, II, with 1 John 2:20, 21: “You have an anointing from the Holy
One and know all things”; and verse 27: “You do not need anyone to teach you.”
*Reply.* This passage can be answered in the same way. “These words of the
Apostle,” says Gerhard in the place cited above, number 47, “must not be taken
absolutely and universally, as if in the New Testament no person whatsoever
needed to be taught by anyone. 1. For what else is the Apostle doing in this
Epistle than instructing and strengthening in writing those whom he addresses?
2. This explanation would conflict with the grounds advanced a little earlier,
and especially with experience, which testifies that after the fall the
knowledge of God is innate in no one whatsoever. Therefore they must be
understood with qualification and comparatively. (1.) Since those whom the
Apostle addresses had been instructed by the Apostles in the necessary heads
of heavenly doctrine and divinely imbued with the anointing of the Holy
Spirit, they did not need to hear and learn something new from seducers. This
interpretation is gathered from verse 21: ‘I did not write to you as persons
ignorant of the truth, but as persons who know it’; and from verse 26,
immediately preceding: ‘I have written these things to you concerning those
who seduce you.’ Likewise from the same verse 27: ‘As the anointing has taught
you, remain in it.’ (2.) They did not need someone to labor greatly and
anxiously in instructing them and persuading them of heavenly truth, since
they are instructed by the Holy Spirit, the internal Teacher, whom they had
nevertheless received through the preaching of the Gospel, Galatians 3:2. (3.)
They are instructed by the teaching of the Holy Spirit in particular actions,
so that they may know how to conduct themselves in

[Volume II; printed p. 223; ONB canvas 231]

all things rightly. Meanwhile, however, the word of God remains the general
norm and rule of all actions, and by meditating upon it the light of their
knowledge is rendered more illustrious.” See Calvin on the passage. Gualther
has the following: “Some have twisted these words to such a point that they
would overturn the ministry of the word which Christ instituted in the
Church. But if their error cannot be proved by other arguments, it can at
least be proved by Christ’s example, for he wills people to be brought to
faith and participation in salvation through the preaching of the Gospel.
Therefore those who think that the Church’s ministry and office of teaching
are abrogated by this statement of John are mistaken. The Apostle instead
opposes new traditions, which he says the faithful do not need at all. For
because everything which contributes to our instruction and salvation has
been given to us in Christ, it is foolish to wish to sew anything onto them.
For what will human reason devise that can be compared with the divine
commands?” and so forth. See the Belgic Annotations on the passage.

Finally, III, they object with 1 Peter 2:9: “You are a Royal Priesthood.” But
we answer elsewhere this objection as proposed by Velthuysen. Here I will add
only the following. 1. Peter explains himself in verse 5 of that chapter,
where he says that they are “a holy Priesthood, to offer spiritual sacrifices
acceptable to God through Jesus Christ.” Other passages of Scripture
sufficiently teach what these spiritual sacrifices are: Mortification,
Romans 12:1; contrition, Psalm 51:17; prayers, Psalm 141:2; Hebrews 5:7;
Revelation 5:8; thanksgivings, Psalm 50:14, 23; Hebrews 13:15; and alms,
Philippians 4:18; Hebrews 13:16. Next, the same thing had formerly been said of
the Israelite people, Exodus 19:6. Nevertheless, at that time a special
ministry had been instituted. 3. The one who made Christians under the New
Testament a royal priesthood promised that the ministry would be instituted
in the New Testament, Isaiah 66:21; Ezekiel 44:15, 16, and actually instituted
it, as we have seen. There is therefore no conflict. See Calvin and other
Commentators on the passage,

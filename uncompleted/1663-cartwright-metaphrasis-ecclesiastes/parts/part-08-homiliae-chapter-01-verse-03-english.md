VERSE 3. What profit is there for a man from all his labor, at which he labors
under the Sun?

From the first category of the induction he proves the vanity of all things,
namely from the uselessness of all a man's labors and pursuits. If there is no
profit from human labor and pursuits, then all those things will be vain. But
the former, and so forth. Therefore the latter, and so forth. The proposition
is omitted. The assumption is contained in verse 3, where the Prophet,
assessing all the labors and pursuits of all human beings, pronounces them all
empty and useless for acquiring happiness. Nor does he merely pronounce this;
rather, by employing a question he confirms more forcefully that the matter is
so, such that even to doubt it is the mark of a scarcely sound mind. There are
many individual things in which the wise of this world persuade themselves
that they will be happy, provided they can attain them by their own labor or
skill. By contrast, God through the Prophet, the true appraiser of things,
plainly affirms that there is nothing in the nature of things which, when
attained by human industry, not only does not make a person happy but does not
even contribute anything to happiness. Indeed, not even all the things that
can be acquired by human skill could contribute anything to happiness, even
if with the highest accord and concert they should converge upon some one
person. I shall say more—and shall say nothing that the truth of the matter
does not confirm: so far are all these things from making a person happy or
bringing any support to happiness that they even render the one who abounds
in them more exposed to unhappiness and ruin. Matthew 19:24.

1 Corinthians 1:26. Finally, it comes about that by those things which are
diametrically opposed to the things people eagerly pursue, and which they
strive with every sinew and effort to turn away from themselves, we are
advanced toward happiness sooner than by those very things which we pursue
with so much labor. In this class are disgrace, poverty, captivity, and so
forth. For what is said of the rich and the poor can equally be applied to
honor and disgrace, to freedom and captivity.

Let this admonish us not to devote too much effort to acquiring these fleeting
and perishable goods. Habakkuk 2:13. For what is this but to labor amid fire
and weary oneself for emptiness? It is indeed grievous for a person to carry
away no fruit from his labor; but it is more painful still to receive loss
from that from which he had hoped for gain. Yet this most certainly happens to
all those who chase these fleeting things beyond measure or do not devote
themselves to the fear of God above all of them.

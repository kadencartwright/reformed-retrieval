### QUESTION III

#### On the worship of the saints

Bellarmine, book 1, *On the Blessedness of the Saints*, chapter 12, says that
adoration embraces three acts. The first is an act of the

[1620 BSB Tome II | canvas 14 | printed col. 28 | bsb10175568]

intellect, by which
we apprehend someone’s excellence. The second is an act of the will, by which
we are inwardly inclined and wish to do something by an inward or outward act
through which we testify to the excellence of the one whom we adore and to
our own subjection. The third is an outward act by which we bow the head or
bend the knee. Of these he says that the second is most properly adoration,
because the first can exist without adoration and the third can be done in
mockery. He then says that there are as many kinds of adoration or worship as
there are kinds of excellence. He says that excellence has three kinds: 1.
Divine excellence, to which *latreia* corresponds. 2. Human excellence, to
which civil honor corresponds. 3. An excellence intermediate between human
and divine, such as the grace and glory of the saints, to which *douleia*
corresponds. And because Christ’s humanity and the Virgin Mary excel among
holy creatures, he therefore subdivides this worship into *douleia* properly
so called, which belongs to all the saints, and *hyperdouleia*, which belongs
to Christ’s humanity and to the Virgin Mary. He maintains that, as far as
outward acts are concerned, these kinds of adoration are not easily
distinguished, but that all their outward acts are common except sacrifice.

We too divide worship into civil and religious. We say that the latter is to
be rendered to God alone, while civil honor is what we render to those who
surpass us either in office or in gifts. This worship or honor is commanded
by the fifth commandment. We say, however, that the saints are to be revered
or honored in three ways. 1. By holy remembrance. Thus Mary predicted in Luke
1:48 that all would call her blessed; likewise, the pious people of the Old
Testament are mentioned in the New. See also Mark 14. 2. By giving thanks to
God because he gave them so many and such great gifts for the good of the
Church, as in Galatians 1. 3. By imitation of their faith, patience,

[1620 BSB Tome II | canvas 15 | printed col. 29 | bsb10175568]

and humility, as these virtues existed in them, Hebrews 12:1. For just as the
people were once led to the land of Canaan by the cloud, so by the example of
the pious we are led to heaven.

We therefore say that civil honor is not to be rendered to the saints because
they are absent from us, and much less the religious honor that the Papists
desire. This is proved by the following arguments.

Argument 1. If it cannot be proved by a command or example of Scripture that
any honor apart from the threefold honor we have described was rendered to
saints or to the dead, then the worship the Papists invent is not to be
rendered to them, because whatever is done without faith is sin, Romans
14:23. Now all the examples Bellarmine presents of honor rendered to saints,
angels, or men concern saints who were present, not absent, as will be clear
later. Therefore.

Argument 2. Since they render *douleia* to the saints and *latreia* to God,
they render what is weightier to the saints and what is lighter to God, for
it is lighter to honor than to serve. But it is ungodly to render more to the
saints than to God in worship. Therefore.

Argument 3. If angels and saints are to be worshiped, it must be with either
civil or religious worship. But it is not with civil worship, because they
are absent from us and are not everywhere so as to see and understand us; nor
is it with religious worship, because that is to be rendered to God alone,
Isaiah 42:8. Therefore.

Argument 4. If worship is rendered to the saints, then *latreia* is rendered,
because *latreia* signifies worship. If, therefore, they render worship to
the saints but not *latreia*, they render worship without worship, which is
absurd. Therefore.

Argument 5. In Scripture *latreia* and *douleia* are taken for the same thing,
because when the worship of God is discussed Scripture uses the word
*douleia*, as in Deuteronomy 6:16; Matthew 4:14; Romans 1:9, 7:6, and 14:18;
Ephesians 6:7; and Colossians 3:24. And where a civil duty is discussed it
uses

[1620 BSB Tome II | canvas 15 | printed col. 30 | bsb10175568]

the word *latreia*, as the Septuagint translators do in Leviticus 23:35.
Bellarmine, chapter 12, teaches that both words are applied to creatures, and
Augustine, book 20, *Against Faustus*, chapter 22, defines *latreia* by
*douleia*.

Argument 6. If Scripture teaches that saints and angels, while present,
refused this religious worship, then it is much less to be rendered to them
when they are absent. But the former is true, Revelation 19:10 and 22:8; Acts
10:26 and 14:15.

Argument 7. The Papists render greater honor and worship to Mary than to God,
as is evident from their forms of prayer. Therefore.

Argument 8. Near the end of chapter 12 Bellarmine concedes that the Fathers
rarely speak of *douleia*, and he concedes that the saints ought to be honored
but not with *latreia*. He wishes the word for serving, that is, *douleia*, to
be taken in the sense we set out above from Galatians 5:13. Augustine
therefore rightly says in *On True Religion*, chapter 55: *Let the worship of
dead men not be our religion. For if they lived piously, they are not so
disposed that they seek such honors; rather, they wish us to worship him by
whose illumination they rejoice that we share in their merit.*

On Romans 1 Ambrose censures those who adore their fellow servants.

Athanasius, in sermon 3 against the Arians, teaches that neither holy men nor
angels are to be worshiped with adoration.

Epiphanius, in his treatment of the Collyridian heresy, repeatedly insists
that Mary is not to be adored, but God alone.

## OBJECTIONS

Objection 1. From Psalm 99:5: *Adore his footstool, because it is holy.* By
the footstool he

[1620 BSB Tome II | canvas 16 | printed col. 31 | bsb10175568]

means the ark. But the ark was a creature, while its worship is proved to be
religious because it is called holy. Therefore creatures are to be adored
with more than civil worship, namely, religious worship.

Response. This does not answer the question, because the question is not
about the adoration of inanimate things.

Second, the text does not have, “Adore the footstool,” but, “Adore at the
footstool.” By this he means the pavement of the temple, upon which God’s feet
are said to have been placed because of his singular presence, 1 Chronicles
28:2. It therefore signifies where one should adore, as in Psalm 132:7.

Further, that holiness is attributed to God himself, as the Seventy and
Jerome interpreted it and Bellarmine himself concedes. Nor does it follow,
“The ark or the temple is holy; therefore it is to be adored.” Augustine,
however, says on this passage, from Isaiah 66, that this footstool is the
earth, and that the earth signifies Christ’s flesh, or Christ by synecdoche.

Objection 2. Pious men adored angels: Abraham, Genesis 18:2; Lot, Genesis
19:1; Balaam, Numbers 22:31; and Joshua, Joshua 3:11. Therefore.

Response. Lot and Abraham, as Bellarmine concedes, honored them in a civil
manner because they thought them to be men. Second, Balaam bowed himself to
the ground as a sign of honor; it does not follow that he worshiped
religiously. Moreover, he was ungodly. Third, the angel who appeared to Joshua
was Christ, because he is called the prince of God’s army. Nor does this
follow: “He did not prohibit Joshua from adoration; therefore Joshua did not
act wrongly by adoring angels.” For if he had been an angel, he would have
forbidden it, as angels forbade it elsewhere, Revelation 19 and 22. Finally,
examples prove nothing against

[1620 BSB Tome II | canvas 16 | printed col. 32 | bsb10175568]

commands, because even pious men often fall.

Objection 3. The saints are said to have been adored in Scripture. In 1
Samuel 28 Saul adores the soul of Samuel appearing to him. But civil honor is
not owed to the souls of the dead, nor did Saul err here, because he is not
reproved. In 1 Kings 18 Obadiah adores Elijah; in 2 Kings 2 the sons of the
prophets adore Elisha; and in Daniel 2 Nebuchadnezzar adores Daniel.
Therefore.

Response. It is false to say that Saul adored Samuel’s soul, because the text
does not say that his soul appeared.

Furthermore, that honor was civil. Nor does it follow, “He is not reproved;
therefore the deed was lawful,” because many things are narrated historically
in Scripture about which the Holy Spirit does not pronounce a judgment in
those passages, yet they are evil. Second, the remaining examples concern
honor rendered to living men, not religiously but civilly, while the question
concerns the worship of dead saints.

Objection 4. If civil honor is due to a civil virtue such as wisdom, power,
and so forth, then more-than-civil honor is due to supernatural virtue. But
the saints are endowed with supernatural virtues. Therefore.

Response. The proposition is denied, because Scripture has commanded no
spiritual honor except that which is owed to God.

Moreover, civil honor is also given to spiritual persons on account of their
spiritual virtues. Finally, if they call the degrees of honor we named above
spiritual, we too render a spiritual honor, namely, that of love,
fellowship, imitation,

[1620 BSB Tome II | canvas 17 | printed col. 33 | bsb10175568]

thanksgiving to God for them, and holy remembrance of the saints, but not
adoration.

Objection 5. God promised honor and glory to the saints, 1 Samuel 2:30;
Romans 2:12. Therefore honor is owed to them, and it can be rendered by none
other than us. But civil honor is not suitable for the dead; therefore
religious honor is.

Response. We concede that we ought to honor the saints, but in the way we
have described. Furthermore, the cited passages concern the honor with which
God honors the saints, both in this life and in the future life. Finally, it
does not follow: “He promised glory to the saints; therefore he promised the
worship proper to himself, namely, religious adoration.”

CHAPTER XVII

All Days Are Not Now Equal with Respect to Holiness

Although a more suitable occasion for treating this subject would be given to
us later, when we came to discuss those questions which more particularly
concern *the Lord's Day,* an examination of this question must nevertheless
not be wholly omitted here, because it contributes very greatly to confirming
what has now been said about *Solemn Time.* For if what the adversaries
repeatedly insist upon is true—namely, that all days are now equal and none is
holier than another—then everything said thus far about *Principal and Solemn,
Necessary and Ordinarily Sufficient Time* will be in vain. Since, therefore,
this appears to be a most weighty objection, it must not be passed over here
but examined more carefully, so that the things said thus far may be received
all the more firmly and we may proceed more securely.

But in order that the truth of our assertion may be evident, it should be
noted:

1. *Time and a Day* are considered in two ways: either *Materially, in itself,
and in a divided sense,* and *physically;* or *Morally and Theologically, in a
composite sense,* and *with respect to divine determination.* No day,
considered in the former way, is holier or more excellent than another, or more
necessary for divine worship; rather, they are all equal. But a day taken in
the latter way can be holier than another, as one determined by God, appointed
for divine use, and separated and set apart from the others. In this way many
days under the Law were holier than others, because by divine authority they
were separated from the others and appointed for particular sacred uses. In
the same way, many other things were once separated by God's command for
sacred use in the Tabernacle and Temple. Although they were not sacred in
themselves or materially considered, they were nevertheless regarded as sacred
with respect to the sacred use for which God appointed them. The same must now
be said of the Sabbath: with respect to divine determination and separation,
it is a sacred day and is to be preferred to all others in this respect.
Further, it should be noted:

2. We do not say that this day is morally sacred as if it were an instrument
instituted by God through which God conferred spiritual and supernatural grace,
as he customarily confers it through the *sacraments.* Rather, it is only a
sacred Time sanctified by God, in which, when it has seemed good to him, he is
accustomed to manifest himself and bless his people; and in which, when it is
rightly observed, we may ordinarily expect God's blessing more than at another,
more common time or at one devised by ourselves. Finally, it should be noted:

3. The question here is whether under the New Testament there are any Times
determined as necessary to religion, and thus sacred, such that they cannot by
right be changed by men,

[1674 logical printed p. 350 | IA leaf 368 | PDF p. 368]

appointed to other uses, or neglected, but must be observed as distinguished
and separated from the others and applied to sacred uses. The Adversaries deny
this; we affirm it,
supported by these reasons.

*First reason.* Nature itself dictates that all Times are not equal, because:

1. Nature dictates that some longer span of time, and one recurring more
often, must be dedicated to God and to divine exercises. This is evident from
the fact that nature teaches that it would be a sin if in the whole course of
our life we spent no more than a quarter of an hour—and that only once—in divine
exercises. Therefore, if it would be a sin to dedicate no time to God, or only
a very short time without any recurrence, there is some Time holier than
another in this respect. That is the Time which must necessarily be passed in
divine exercises, for God's glory and our own salvation. For sin presupposes a
law, of which it is the transgression; and a law states some determination,
while determination states inequality. Therefore nature teaches that there is
inequality, and consequently that all Times are not equal.

2. Nature teaches that at least one whole day in the course of our life must
be dedicated to God; otherwise the person who did not spend even one day on
sacred things during his entire lifetime would not sin. But since this cannot
be said, it is evident that all Time is not equal; for if the law of nature
teaches that at least one day must be dedicated to God, that one day will be
determined by the law of nature and thus holier than the others with respect to
this use.

*Second,* we proved above that some Time must necessarily be determined both
with respect to *Duration* and with respect to *Frequency,* or with respect to
*How Long and How Often.* From this one may conclude that all Time is not equal.
For that Time, by whomever it is determined and whether its proportion is large
or small, is *Necessary* to Religion and cannot be changed by men and made
shorter or less frequent—nor longer or more frequent, because it is
*Sufficient.* Therefore this Time, thus determined, is distinguished from all
others and is holier than the others.

*Third,* by many reasons we proved that the power to determine *Solemn Time*—
that is, the Duration and Frequency of Time—belongs to God alone. And since we
have also demonstrated that it is necessary that it be determined, it is
evident that God has determined this Time, and that in the fourth precept of
the Decalogue. For what must necessarily be determined and can be determined
by God alone must be supposed to have been determined by God. And since in the
fourth precept of the Decalogue God determines Time both with respect to its
*Continuation* and with respect to its *Recurrence*—that is, one day in the
course of the week—it follows that all Times are not equal. Rather, there is
one day in the cycle of the week *Necessary* to Religion and *Ordinarily
Sufficient,* not to be transferred to other uses, and therefore to be preferred
to all others and regarded as holier.

*Fourth,* we demonstrated that it is necessary that the beginning of Time, or
the *When,* be determined; and from what has been said, it is clear that the
power to determine this also belongs to God alone. Hence it is evident that
this beginning of Time, or this particular day, whether it is the first or the
last of the week, is to be preferred to all the others.

*Fifth,* the Adversaries themselves concede that some Sufficient Time must be
given to God and to God's

[1674 logical printed p. 351 | IA leaf 369 | PDF p. 369]

worship. Therefore all Time is not equal, but some Time is holier than another,
because it must necessarily be spent in God's worship.

*Sixth,* if all Time is equal and none surpasses another with respect to
holiness, then God has totally divested himself of his dominion over Time; he
has reserved absolutely no portion for himself, nor has he wished it to be
rendered back to him by men. But this must not be said, as all Christians will
acknowledge. The consequence of the *major premise* is evident from this: if
God has either reserved some portion of Time for himself or wished men to
render it back to him, that portion must necessarily be placed before all other
portions of time and be holier. And from this it will further follow that all
Time is not now equal.

*Seventh,* if the fourth precept of the Decalogue commands anything about Time
to be dedicated to God—whether with respect to *Duration, Frequency,* or the
*Proper Season*—that time must be regarded as sacred and holier than every
other. This is so whether in duration it is only an hour or a whole day; whether
in Frequency it is an hour or a day in a cycle of seven, more, or fewer days;
and whether it is the first or last hour or day of its cycle, which we do not
now determine. Even those who deny that the fourth precept of the Decalogue
commands us to observe one day out of seven nevertheless do not dare deny that
by force of that precept we also are bound to dedicate some time to God and
spend it in sacred use. Therefore that Time, whatever and of whatever kind it
finally is, enjoined by a moral precept, will not be equal to every other or no
holier than another. And since even ceremonial precepts have some general moral
character, it appears reasonable that this fourth precept also has some moral
character, and by reason of this moral character commands some Time to be spent
in divine exercises, whether short or long, recurring rarely or more often.
And this Time will be holy, since it is necessary to Religion and dedicated to
God.

*Eighth,* to say that all Time is now equal and none is holier than another
with respect to the divine command is to erase the fourth precept entirely from
the roll of the moral precepts and to say that the whole of it is ceremonial.
There would thus now be only nine moral precepts, and the Decalogue would be an
Ennealogue. But until now our Adversaries have been ashamed to blurt this out
with full mouth. For to free themselves from this charge they have employed,
and still employ, many coatings and various glosses, as we shall hear in its
proper place.

*Ninth,* from the beginning God created some Time distinguished and separated
from all the others, and instituted it to endure until the end of the world.
Therefore there is some specially sacred time to be applied to divine things.
The *consequence* is certain, for no one can make God's institutions obsolete
or abolish them. Therefore if God instituted that there be some Sabbath
dedicated to him until the end of the world, there is even now under the Gospel
some sacred Time distinguished from the others. As to the *antecedent,* we
shall prove later in its proper place that the Sabbath was instituted from the
beginning. And what was instituted from the beginning is not abolished along
with the ceremonial law but remains firm, as Christ himself testifies when he
argues thus in *Matthew 19:4 and Mark 10:6.* We do not now dispute whether that
Sabbath is the first or last day of the week; we shall deal with this later.
It is enough here to say that there must be some Sabbath by reason of the first
institution. For when this has been proved, it will follow that all

[1674 logical printed p. 352 | IA leaf 370 | PDF p. 370]

Time is not equal, but that the Time which must be called the Sabbath is holier
than another.

*Tenth,* the Heathen themselves testify by their practice that all Times are
not equal. For those among whom any Religion flourishes have their own Feast
Days and Sacred Days, which they prefer to all the others. And the Adversaries
maintain that what nature teaches the Heathen is moral because it is natural.
Therefore it conflicts with nature to say that all Time is now equal under the
Gospel. And no one will say that the Gospel destroys nature instead of
perfecting it.

*Eleventh,* that prophecy in *Isaiah 56* says that there will be a Sabbath
under the New Testament, because there Jehovah promises many Gospel blessings
to those who will observe Sabbaths, *verses 2, 4, and 6.* That this prophecy
also regards the Times of the *Messiah* is evident from the fact that it speaks
of the Time when the foreigner, who under the Old Testament was distinguished
and separated from the people of God, will be received and made a participant
in the promises of grace no less than the Jew, *verse 7.* See *Acts 10:34–35;
Romans 1:10–11 and 3:29–30 and 10:12; 1 Corinthians 12:13; Galatians 3:15, 28;
and Colossians 3:11.* But here the foreigner is called בן הנכר, the son of a
foreigner, because—as the author of the *English Annotations* supposes—by force
of the Mosaic law in *Deuteronomy 23:3, 7, and 8,* there were some whose
descendants were not to be received for several generations. Thus it points to
the time—under the *Messiah,* namely—when that law would become obsolete, the
partition wall of the enclosure having been dissolved, *Ephesians 2:13–15.* And
when he also speaks of eunuchs and promises them a place in God's house, he
indicates clearly enough that he is speaking of the times of the *Messiah,*
when, namely, no such defect can be an impediment preventing them from becoming
participants in the spiritual Priesthood and the privilege of members of the
Church, contrary to the way things stood under the pedagogy of *Moses in
Leviticus 21:20, 23 and Deuteronomy 23:1.* Further, those times are understood
here when God's House will be a House of prayer *for all peoples,* that is, the
Gentiles no less than the Jews, *Romans 10:1, 15.* The following words also
confirm this: *I will gather to him yet more besides those gathered to him;*
that is, I will gather to him many others from the peoples and bring them to
the Church. Moreover, all those promises are spiritual and Gospel promises,
although presented in certain legal terms. For that *everlasting name, better
than sons or daughters,* agrees very well with the Gospel state; let the reader
consult the *Belgic Annotations* on verses 3 and 7.

But perhaps someone will say that the Prophet here speaks only of the Sabbath
of the Old Testament, because he mentions burnt offerings and sacrifices in
*verse 7.* *Answer.* It is customary for the Prophets to speak of Gospel
worship in this manner and to use the terms of legal worship in explaining it,
so that they might be understood more easily. Therefore by these terms the
Prophet designates spiritual sacrifices of prayers and thanksgivings and
similar things, which are often explained by terms of this kind in *Romans
12:18; Philippians 4:18; Psalms 141:2 and 50:23; and Hebrews 13:15.* See the
*Belgic Annotations* at the place. And just as those terms say that there will
be public worship of God under the New Testament, so the Sabbaths say that
under the New Testament there will be certain solemn times, distinguished and
separated from the others and consecrated to God.

*Twelfth,* the same is evident from another chapter of the same Prophecy,
namely, *chapter 58, verses 13–14,* where the duties of one who sanctifies and
of a pious Observer of the Sabbath are set forth. No one will say that all of
these are now entirely obsolete and have no use among us.

[1674 logical printed p. 353 | IA leaf 371 | PDF p. 371]

But if they have some use, in some way concern us, and explain what God
requires of us, they also designate some Time under the Gospel which is thus to
be sanctified and observed. If this is conceded, we have what we want here. But
if these precepts have no use now, any more than other precepts concerning
Levitical offerings strictly considered, it will be easy in this manner to
render the Old Testament entirely empty for us insofar as it concerns our
practice. If someone *says* that these duties can be applied under the Gospel
to any day instituted by man, it will be easy to *answer* that no day instituted
by men can be called *God's holy day, the holy and honored day of Jehovah.* And
who will say that we are bound to delight in such a day instituted by man and
to honor it?

If someone further *says* that by the *Sabbath* here is understood the *annual
fast,* or the day of atonement which is called a sabbath in *Leviticus 23,* it
can be *answered* that this cannot be proved by any sound reason. For although
the Prophet speaks of such a fast in the former part of the chapter, it does
not follow that for this reason he speaks of the same fast at the end of the
chapter—especially since these titles agree much more with the weekly Sabbath
than with that annual fast which recurs only once each year, and other things
are inferred from verses 8 through 13. See the *Belgic Annotations.* And even
if we granted that these things could be applied to that annual day, would the
weekly Sabbath therefore be entirely excluded? Finally, even if nothing else
were understood here besides that annual fast, no sound person will deny that
these words contain a general moral character. But how will this moral
character be applied unless some Solemn Time under the New Testament is
conceded? And then it will further follow that this Solemn Time is distinguished
from other times and far to be preferred to them. For these Epithets and the
other appended ones will have to be applied more particularly to that Time,
whatever it finally is, than to any other Time.

*Thirteenth,* the same can be proved from the same prophecy, namely from its
*final chapter, verse 23: And it shall be—from Sabbath to another Sabbath, that
all flesh may come to bow down before me, says Jehovah.* Here he openly speaks
of the times of the New Testament, for he says *all flesh,* which must be
understood not only of the Jews but also of the Gentiles, as is evident from
verse 20. The words of the preceding verse demonstrate the same, for *those new
heavens and new earth* which Jehovah was going to make point to the Time of the
*Messiah,* as is evident from *chapter 65:17.* For then God was going to abolish
all the ancient ceremonies and institute spiritual worship, *John 3:16–17 and
4:21, 24; Acts 2:17, 33; and 2 Corinthians 3:16, 18.* Hence it is evident that
the Prophet had predicted that there would be a Sabbath even under the New
Testament. But perhaps *you will say* that it no more follows from this than
that there will be new moons, since he also mentions them. *Answer.* I am not
speaking here specifically of the weekly Sabbath. The very New Moons mentioned
here sufficiently prove what I now want: namely, that there is some solemn Time
under the New Testament and therefore that all Time is not equal. Further, this
text will no more support *new moons* than it supports *priests and Levites,*
who are mentioned in verse 21. Therefore if by those priests and prophets are
understood pastors and teachers, or the Church's Leaders under the New
Testament, why should we not also understand by the new moons a Solemn Time
instituted by Christ? For, as was just said, it is customary for Prophets to
use the idiom of the Old Testament when they speak of the Times of the
*Messiah.* This abundantly supports us: the Prophet here predicts that there
will be some Solemn Times under the New Testament, just as he predicts that
there will be Pastors

[1674 logical printed p. 354 | IA leaf 372 | PDF p. 372]

and other governors of Christ's Church. For those times, of whatever kind they
are, will be sacred and placed before the others. Thus it appears plainly false
to say that all times are now equal. And it is evident that there is a
distinction of days under the Gospel, and that this distinction will remain as
long as those new heavens and that new earth endure.

*Fourteenth,* the same can be proved from the prophecy of *Ezekiel 46:1, 3, 4,
and 12,* where Sabbaths and other Solemn Times are mentioned. Commentators
acknowledge that this prophecy concerns the times of the *Messiah.* And from
this prophecy, though obscure, arguments are customarily taken for the Gospel
ministry, the power of Ecclesiastical Leaders, Ecclesiastical worship, and so
forth. Why not also for Solemn Time? Let no one *say* that the Sabbath is no
more proved from this to have a place under the New Testament than the Kalends
and New Moons. For it is sufficiently evident that these have been abolished,
whereas the Sabbath is disputed. And it cannot be denied that by these Sabbaths,
New Moons, and so forth, some Solemn Time under the New Testament is indicated,
which is enough for our Proposition. For if under the New Testament there is
some Solemn Time distinguished from every other, dedicated to God, and to be
passed particularly in pious exercises, we have what we want: namely, that all
times under the New Testament are not equal.

*Fifteenth,* that statement of *Psalm 118:24* can also prove this. For the
context shows that these things must be understood of Christ as the antitype.
For he is the true head of the corner, *Matthew 21:42; Mark 12:10; Luke 20:17;
Acts 4:11; and 1 Peter 2:4.* And when by the resurrection he was declared to be
the Son of God, it was a work worthy of admiration. That day—namely, the day of
the resurrection, when he was declared in a particular way to be the head of
the corner—is understood here. The builders despised him when they affixed him
to the cross; but when he rose from the dead, he was powerfully declared to be
the Son of God in *Romans 1:4,* and the head of the corner. And this day is
emphatically denoted here by זה היום, *this is the day:* namely, the day on which
Christ rested from the work of his passion, and which Jehovah made—that is,
honored in a particular way and rendered notable. See the *Belgic Annotations.*
And in it everyone ought to rejoice and be glad. Hence it is evident that a day
particularly separated to God and to be passed in the solemn praises of the
Church is designated here, even under the New Testament. And thus it is
evident that all times are not equal.

*Sixteenth,* there is some Time under the New Testament which is called *the
Lord's Day in Revelation 1:10.* And this day—whether it is one out of seven, an
annual day, the day of Christ's nativity, death, or resurrection, which we do
not now determine because the matter will be disputed more fully later in *book
5*—is distinguished from all others and adorned with this title in a particular
way, and is thus declared sacred. Therefore all Times are not now equal, for
this title, which denotes something peculiar, does not belong indiscriminately
to every day. Since the author of this title is the Holy Spirit, it is not
applied in vain but for weighty reasons and for a particular end; and thus it
undoubtedly designates a day dedicated to the Lord and appointed for pious
exercises. Ravanella says under the entry *day:* *In memory of Christ's
resurrection it was called the Lord's Day in Revelation 1:10. (For some
wrongly think that ἡμέραν κυριακὴν here signifies the judgment of the Lord, as
if γενόμενον ἐν πνεύματι ἐν τῇ ἡμέρᾳ κυριακῇ, “having come to be in the
spirit on the Lord's Day,” were the same as “having been caught up in the spirit
to contemplate the judgment of the Lord,” or “to the divine judgment,” which
cannot be maintained without absurdity.) And it was taken in place of the
Sabbath of the former age: 1. because since the Lord's*

[1674 logical printed p. 355 | IA leaf 373 | PDF p. 373]

*resurrection is like a second creation of another, spiritual world—as the
prophets speak in Isaiah 65:17 and 66:22 and Revelation 21:1, and so forth—it
pleased the Apostles, undoubtedly at the Holy Spirit's dictation, to take the
first day of this new world in place of the Sabbath of the former age, on which
not that corporeal and corruptible light created on the first day of the old
world, but that heavenly and eternal light, shone forth. 2. So that this day,
which we call the Lord's from the Lord's resurrection, might recall to memory
the mystery and benefit of the Lord's resurrection, and so forth.* Beza has the
same on the passage.

*Seventeenth,* this same title says that this day belongs to the Lord and is
appropriated and set apart to the Lord. For κυριακὸς means pertaining to the Lord.
Therefore all days under the Gospel are not now equal. For *the Lord's Day*
sounds very different from *the day of the Lord,* just as δεῖπνον κυριακὸν in
*1 Corinthians 11:20* designates a particular Supper known to all and
distinguished from every other Supper in which the Lord took part. Ravanella
says under the entry *Feast:* *In place of the Jewish Feasts now abolished, one
Feast Day must be celebrated by us today under the Gospel and dedicated to the
public and solemn worship of God by force of the fourth command of the Law,
namely, the first day of the week. This day is commended to Christians in
Scripture, both by the example of Christ himself, who rose from the dead on
this day and thereby testified that he had completed the work of our redemption
—— and from these things it is evident that the observance of the Lord's Day is
customary in Scripture.*

*Eighteenth,* further, this title says that this day was instituted by Christ
himself, our Lord. For the phrase κυριακὴ ἡμέρα denotes this, just as does
δεῖπνον κυριακὸν. And this word κυριακὸς is used nowhere else in the New
Testament. Therefore, since it is certain in *1 Corinthians 11:20* that this
word denotes something instituted by Christ when it is applied to the
sacrament of the Supper, why not here also, where it is applied to this day?
There is no circumstance of the text which could compel us to take this word
differently here than in that passage. Therefore it is evident that by this day
some Time is denoted—and which one it is will be seen later in its proper place—
separated and instituted by Christ himself, and thus necessarily sacred and to
be spent in divine exercises, and not to be applied by anyone to other uses.
Therefore it is manifest that all Times under the New Testament are not now
equal.

*Nineteenth,* when Christ said in *Mark 11:27 that the Sabbath was made for man,*
he clearly indicated that man would always need a Sabbath and that the Sabbath
would endure as long as man existed, for whose good it was instituted.
Therefore even now under the Gospel there is a distinction of days and some
Sabbath for promoting man's spiritual good. Therefore all Times are not now
equal.

*Twentieth,* it appears quite absurd to say that now there is no determined and
Solemn Time for worshiping God and promoting our salvation, since the Jews had
such a Time recurring every week. It has seemed good to God to pour out a
greater and more abundant measure of grace now than he poured out of old. What?
Is God therefore to be acknowledged and glorified less by us? Are we therefore
more free from thanksgiving? Or should it be said that God now esteems the
celebration of his praise less? What? Have we been granted greater liberty—or
rather license—to seek our own things and prefer the things which concern this
temporal life to God's glory? But since these things conflict not only with
Scripture but also with

[1674 logical printed p. 356 | IA leaf 374 | PDF p. 374]

right Reason, with what probability shall we affirm that God has determined
absolutely no Time under the Gospel in which he wishes his name to be praised,
his virtues to be celebrated, and his goodness to be acknowledged, and so
forth?

*Twenty-first,* the Apostles themselves and the Apostolic Church esteemed one
day before another and observed some time dedicated to God and his worship, as
may be seen in *Acts 20:7; John 20:19, 26; and 1 Corinthians 16:1.* Therefore
all Time under the New Testament is not equal. What the Adversaries customarily
object against these testimonies we shall see in its proper place in *book 5.*

*Twenty-second,* from the very times of the Apostles to the present day, the
universal Church of Christ has observed some Solemn Time and esteemed one day
before another, namely, the Lord's Day. This cannot be denied and will be
demonstrated more fully in its proper place. Therefore all Time is not now equal
with respect to sacred uses.

*Twenty-third,* the Ancients confirm this by their testimony, which can be
shown by a few instances. *Athanasius, in the homily On the Sower,* has these
words: *Certainly among those ancient men the Sabbath was once held in the
highest esteem, which solemnity the Lord transferred to the Lord's Day. Yet we
do not disparage the Sabbath by our own authority; rather, it is the Prophet
who rejects it and says, “My soul hates your new moons and your Sabbaths.” For
as long as those things were being done which were worthy of the institution
of the Law—or rather, while the Master had not yet come—the Pedagogical
arrangement retained its force and authority. But when the master was now
present, the Pedagogue became useless and idle, as a candle ceases when the sun
has risen.* Tertullian in *Apology,* chapter 16, says that *Christians devote
the day of the Sun to gladness for a reason far different from the religion of
the sun.* There
he both testifies to the fact and vindicates it from the adversaries' slanders.
Sedulius, speaking of this day in *Paschal Work,* book 5, chapter 21, has these
words: *It attained such great dignity that it deserved first to behold the
origin of the nascent world and the power of the risen Christ.* Augustine,
sermon 154 *On Time:* *Venerable is this day, which is the Lord's Day, and the
first and perfect day, and the bright day, on which the first light was seen;
on which the children of Israel crossed the Red Sea on dry feet; on which manna
rained for the children of Israel in the wilderness; on which the Lord was
baptized in the Jordan; on which wine was made from water in Cana of Galilee;
on which the Lord blessed five loaves, with which he satisfied five thousand
men; on which the Lord rose from the dead; on which the Lord entered the closed
houses where the disciples were assembled for fear of the Jews; on which the
Holy Spirit descended upon the Apostles; and on which we hope our Lord Jesus
Christ will come to judgment. On that day every creature will be reformed for
the better.* Although many things are said here to which I cannot give my
approval, or about which I hesitate in uncertainty, it is nevertheless evident
from this how highly Augustine esteemed this solemnity of Christians and that
he did not regard all Times as equal. And in *sermon 251* he says that the
Apostles ordained that we sanctify this Lord's Day with sacred exercises. And
in *The City of God,* book 22, final chapter, he says that the Lord's Day is as
it were the everlasting eighth day. And in sermon 15 *On the Words of Christ*
he says that Christ's resurrection *promised us the eternal day and consecrated
the Lord's Day for us.* And Chrysostom, in homily 10 on *Genesis,* says that
God taught us this from the beginning: that in the cycle of the week one whole
day must be separated and spent in spiritual work. See also Epiphanius,
*Heresy* 51, and Ignatius *To the Magnesians: But let each one of us rejoice in
the spiritual meditation of the law on the Sabbath, not in bodily relaxation

*remission from works, admiring God's work —— Let every lover of Christ
celebrate the Lord's Day, consecrated to the resurrection, the queen and chief
of all days.* He therefore esteemed one day before another and did not regard
all times as equal; and at the same time he shows what the Church's practice
was. Dionysius of Corinth, in the *Letter to the Romans,* in Eusebius,
*Ecclesiastical History,* book 4, chapter 22: *Today* (he says) *we have kept the
holy Lord's Day.* The Ancients adorned that day with similar titles. For
Eusebius calls it *the Principal and First day;* Chrysostom, *the Royal day;*
Gregory Nazianzen, *higher than the highest, admirably admirable, above all
other days.* Justin Martyr, *Apology* 2: *But on the day of the Sun* (he says) *we all
commonly hold an assembly, since it is the first day, on which God, having
changed the darkness and matter which he had previously created, made the
world; and Jesus Christ our Savior rose from the dead on that very day.* Jerome
in the *Letter to Eustochium:* *On the Lord's Days* (he says) *they devote
themselves only to prayer and readings.* Therefore all Time was not equal for
them.

*Twenty-fourth,* the contrary opinion is the very opinion of the *Familists,
Antinomians, Anabaptists, Lutherans, and Socinians,* and is therefore all the
more to be rejected. Schlichtingius speaks thus against Meisner: *It is evident
that he thinks this precept about observing the Sabbath is still in force,
although the fact itself teaches that it has become obsolete, since no
Christians observe the Sabbath. For if you say that Christians observe the
following day in place of the Sabbath, I say in turn that it is evident from
this that this precept has been abrogated; for how would it be lawful to observe
another day in place of the Sabbath? —— But Paul also openly teaches that in the
Christian religion distinctions of days have been removed just as distinctions
of foods have, in Romans 14, Galatians 4, and Colossians 2. This is also taught
by the very nature of the Christian religion, in which nothing ceremonial has
force, but only faith working through love, or the new creation.*

What the *Anabaptists* think we can learn from Cloppenburg's *Anabaptistic
Gangrene,* disputation 10 of conference 3, and from Calvin's little book
against the Anabaptists. Wendelin teaches what the *Lutherans* think in
*Exercise CXXXVIII,* which is on the *morality of the Sabbath.*

The Antinomians distinguish between the *Sabbath* and the *Sabbath day.* They
maintain that the former is of divine institution but the latter only of human
constitution. So Henry Denne. And Saltmarsh, in the little book entitled
*Sparkles of Glory,* p. 265, maintains that the Father's bosom is the Christian
Sabbath, prefigured by the seventh day of creation. And he maintains
that the six working days are a type of the administrations of Christ in the
flesh by which he fulfilled the Law, and also of Christians while they are
under the administrations both of the Law and of the Gospel. Such are all
forms of worship, Christian duties, graces, prayers, Christ's institutions, and
so forth.

Since, therefore, this opinion is condemned by the orthodox in all those
people, why should it not be condemned in any others?

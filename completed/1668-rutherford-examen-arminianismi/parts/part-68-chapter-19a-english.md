CHAPTER XIX

ON THE MAGISTRATE

ROMANS 13:4

“For he is God's minister for your good. But if you do what is evil, be afraid,
for he does not bear the sword in vain. For he is God's minister, an avenger
for wrath upon him who has done what is evil.”

It is asked whether the magistrate may punish notorious criminals and
murderers with capital punishment. Some

[1668 KBNL/UvA witness | logical printed p. 729 | PDF p. 778 | split word joined: Remonstrantes]

Remonstrants deny it, such as Henricus Welsing, *On the Duty of a Christian
Man*, page 1; Henricus Slatius, *Open Declaration*, page 53; Joan. Geisteranus,
*On the Duty of the Magistrate*; and the Socinians: Ostorodius, *Institutes of
Religion*, chapter 28, and Smalcius, disputation 6 on the civil magistrate
against Frantzius. They deny that the magistrate may shed blood, and they deny
that wars are lawful for Christians. The Remonstrant Society does not indeed
deny this in its *Confession*, but neither does it assert the contrary plainly
enough, as the Leiden Professors rightly noted. Stung by their censure, the
Remonstrants speak more openly in the *Apologia*, chapter 12, page 141. But
since they knew that some among them had denied the point in published
writings, they say that those who hold that a magistrate may not punish the
guilty by bloodshed and capital punishment are to be tolerated.

We affirm that this is altogether lawful, and we openly condemn and reject the
contrary opinion as erroneous.

1. Because Genesis 9:6 says, “Whoever sheds man's blood, by man shall his blood
be shed.” Exodus 21:14 says, “But when anyone acts presumptuously against his
neighbor, killing him by guile, take him from my altar to death.” Numbers 35:31
says, “You shall not accept a ransom for any killer who is guilty and worthy of
death, but he shall certainly be delivered to death.” That this is not a
judicial and temporary law but belongs to the law of nature is clear from its
effect: innocent blood cannot otherwise be expiated, something never said of
the

[1668 KBNL/UvA witness | logical printed p. 730 | PDF p. 779 | split word joined: Ceremoniali]

ceremonial or judicial law. Verse 33 says, “You shall not pollute the land
itself in which you will dwell, because that blood would pollute this land;
expiation could not be made for that land except by the blood of the one who
shed it.” Matthew 26:52 says, “All who take the sword will perish by the sword.”
Yet not all killers in fact perish by the sword. Therefore Christ speaks of the
right that the magistrate possesses.

2. Because the Remonstrants press Genesis 9:6 not only against killing but also
against every shedding of blood. Yet no distinctions can evade the fact that
under the New Testament it is lawful for the magistrate to bear the sword. By
divine institution, Romans 13, he bears the sword as God's minister for
vengeance. But the sword is an instrument not of a monetary fine but of
bloodshed.

Moreover, there are many New Testament passages which, if pressed literally as
the Arminians interpret them with the Anabaptists, clearly prove that no
vengeance, whether monetary or in any way corporal, may lawfully be taken upon
those who kill their fathers or mothers, or upon the most infamous assassins
and regicides. It will thus be necessary for the magistrate to repay parricide
and regicide with goodness and love, as is expressly stated in Matthew 5:39,
43. Indeed, one must not resist a person who wishes by the sword, fire, and
burning alive to remove king, parents, and fatherland from existence; and for
such

[1668 KBNL/UvA witness | logical printed p. 731 | PDF p. 780 | split word joined: sicariis]

assassins one must only pray well and do good. Hence the magistrate bears the
sword only so that with speeches, sweet entreaties, and gentle words he may
cajole all the accursed plagues of church and commonwealth. And Christ himself
acted against his own most gentle precepts when, after making a whip from
cords, he drove the sellers and buyers out of the temple. Nor will they give me
a passage in the whole New Testament from which they can conclude that the
magistrate is anything other than an unarmed admonisher who can rightfully use
neither the sword nor any force against the most accursed criminals and guilty
persons—unless the passage in Matthew 5 is interpreted as concerning private
persons who seize private vengeance by force. Let them produce the passage!
Indeed, on this account, food must not be denied to the thief who does not work
with his hands, nor may any punishment be inflicted upon anyone. Instead, a
thunderbolt from heaven must be awaited upon the heads of all the guilty. Thus
“magistrate” and “king” will be mere names. For where in the New Testament are
there laws punishing with death incest, adultery, theft, sodomy, and monstrous
lust between human beings and beasts?

3. We must submit to kings and governors who are sent to punish evildoers and
praise those who do well, 1 Peter 2:13–14.

4. If killing people in war were unlawful, John the Baptist would have
answered the soldiers badly when they asked the way to eternal life, by
allowing them to live in that unlawful way of life without reproof and by

[1668 KBNL/UvA witness | logical printed p. 732 | PDF p. 781]

teaching only the manner and method by which they should live amid their
abominable killings, Luke 3:14. Likewise, Peter would have cared too little for
Cornelius's salvation, Acts 10. Neither the faith of the centurion, greater
than any in Israel, Matthew 8, nor the righteousness and true conversion to God
in Cornelius could have coexisted with daily engagement in war if war were
unlawful.

5. Because in Revelation many wars are waged against the dragon and the beast
with Christ the Lamb as leader, sponsor, and approver. So also 1 Corinthians
9:7; Luke 3:14; and 14:31–32.

6. The opponents teach that the magistrate ought to impose punishments in
proportion to the fault. Therefore murder must be punished unto death by the
shedding of blood.

7. It is a law of nature to repel force with force and, in the just defense of
life, to kill rather than be killed.

It is asked: Is it not the magistrate's office to punish a heretic? Or is it
consistent with the laws of our most merciful Savior Jesus Christ for the
magistrate to tolerate in the commonwealth Jews, Turks, Roman Catholics,
Arians, Socinians, Libertines, and all heretics who err from a pure error of
the mind?

In this question the following distinctions must be observed. 1. With
Bullinger, decade 2, sermon 8, a distinction must be made between a heresiarch
and people who have been led astray.

2. With Polyander in the *Synopsis of a Purer Theology*,

[1668 KBNL/UvA witness | logical printed p. 733 | PDF p. 782]

disputation 50, thesis 57, between a blasphemer and another heterodox person.

3. Between a universal apostate from Christianity and one who attacks or
denies a fundamental article.

4. Knowledge is one thing; conscience, a second; and outward profession, a
third.

5. A positive profession is one thing; a negative profession, another.

6. An ordinary punishment is one thing; capital punishment, another.

7. A curable error is one thing; an incurable error, another.

8. Cumulative authority is one thing; privative authority, another.

Assertion 1. Freedom of pure knowledge and opinion must be granted by the
magistrate to all people, to this extent: the magistrate cannot compel anyone
to think or hold this or that opinion in religion. For acts of the mind, since
they are inward, are not subject to the magistrate's authority. The magistrate
can command that the mind be instructed through teachers and pastors, but he
cannot command the mind's opinion.

Assertion 2. The magistrate has no direct and immediate authority over the
conscience. Indirectly and secondarily, however, the magistrate has cumulative
authority over the conscience, because he can command a person earnestly and
diligently to employ all the means of orthodoxy. But he has no privative
authority, because neither a king nor a

[1668 KBNL/UvA witness | logical printed p. 734 | PDF p. 783 | split word joined: tyrannus]

tyrant nor any created power can deprive the conscience of the freedom to hold
right views about God.

Assertion 3. The magistrate ought to compel a heretic to a negative profession
of the sound faith; that is, he ought and is able to compel a heretic not to
profess, teach, or disseminate outwardly in the exercise of religion anything
contrary to the sound faith. Hence we think that all who openly and publicly
profess a false religion are to be punished according to the character of the
offense.

1. Because the magistrate bears the sword for the punishment of evildoers,
Romans 13:4; 1 Peter 13–14. But such heretics are evildoers, and heresy is a
work of the flesh, Galatians 5:20. A king is not God's minister if he does not
restrain those who think and profess perverse things about the divine majesty.

2. Those whom the church ought to punish ecclesiastically as scandalous, the
Christian magistrate ought to punish with civil penalties in his capacity as
guardian, foster father, tutor, defender, and protector of the church. The
office of guardian, tutor, and protector requires this. But the church ought
to convince those who contradict, excommunicate the obstinate, and avoid them
as heretics.

3. A public false religion directed toward God either is a public sin or is
not. If the latter, people can be saved in any sect or religion, which is
absurd, since there is one faith, Ephesians 4:5, and salvation is through the
name of Jesus alone, Acts 4:12. If the former, God's minister ought to punish
and restrain it.

[1668 KBNL/UvA witness | logical printed p. 735 | PDF p. 784]

4. Because if the Remonstrants' reasoning were valid—that heretics must not be
restrained by the sword because religion is commended by reason alone, not by
iron, flame, and force—then this reasoning would prove that not even
blasphemers and heresiarchs are to be restrained. For in their erroneous
consciences they believe themselves bound under penalty of the greatest crime
if they do not teach their deadly heresies about Christ as a false prophet,
even in public places. Yet the Remonstrants teach that the magistrate may and
ought to bar teachers of deadly errors from churches and public places. But on
their principles the answer must be that such a heresiarch should be persuaded
by reason not to teach in public places the doctrine he believes saving and
necessary for the salvation of souls; he must not be barred from public
churches by force and the sword.

Assertion 4. The magistrate cannot directly compel a heretic to a positive
profession of the sound faith and to profess as saving truth what he considers
blasphemy, because he could thereby compel him to hypocrisy. But he can compel
him toward laying aside an erroneous conscience by requiring him to weigh,
hear, and learn its causes, means, reasons, and motives.

Assertion 5. The magistrate ought to punish with death blasphemers, those who
profess heretical and blasphemous things about God, and those who fall away
from Christianity to Judaism or another false religion.

1. Because God commands this, Exodus 22:20: “He who

[1668 KBNL/UvA witness | logical printed p. 736 | PDF p. 785 | split word joined: sacrificat]

sacrifices to other gods besides God alone shall be destroyed as one accursed.”
The law in Deuteronomy 13 is that the dreamer who tempts someone to serve
foreign gods is to be killed without any indulgence, and that a worshiper of
the sun and moon is to be overwhelmed with stones. Leviticus 24:16 says,
“Whoever curses his God shall bear his sin; and whoever blasphemes the name of
the Lord shall be punished with death.”

These laws indeed do not apply to us as judicial laws, as Beza notes in *On
the Punishment of Heretics*: 1. because they are prescribed by Moses, not by
our magistrate, and to the Israelites, not to us; 2. because they speak of
bodily sacrifices, and so forth; and 3. because they were made against
dreamers. These things now cease among us. But as regards natural equity, it
cannot be proved from Scripture that these laws are among those abolished by
Christ's death—unless they say that Christ's death has made blaspheming God's
name, which was a sin among the Jews, lawful or *adiaphoron* for us; or, if it
is a sin, that it is a sin abolished by Christ's death, at least to the extent
that it ought not be punished by the magistrate.

2. Because the edicts of King Nebuchadnezzar, which did not belong to the
political and judicial law of the Jews, are praised, Daniel 3:29; as are those
of Artaxerxes, Ezra 6:11 and 7:25–26, decreeing death and eradication for those
who blaspheme Jehovah and

[1668 KBNL/UvA witness | logical printed p. 737 | PDF p. 786 | split word joined: recusârint]

refuse to obey God's law. So also 2 Chronicles 15:13; Ezra 10:8.

3. This is confirmed in no small measure by the examples of Jehu, who
slaughtered Baal's priests; Josiah, who killed the priests of the high places;
Elijah, who killed Baal's priests at the brook Kishon; Peter, who struck down
Ananias and Sapphira; and Paul, who struck Elymas the sorcerer with blindness.
All these prove from a lesser case that it is much more lawful for a Christian
magistrate to condemn blasphemers to death, since justice is natural in both
cases and since this sin is most grave, no less than parricide.

Assertion 6. Where people who have been led astray by others err, the matter
must be handled with great patience and all gentleness. Those who repent must
be forgiven, and those who err must be restrained with some lighter
punishment.

It is asked whether no one deliberately errs or persuades himself to err when
his eternal salvation is at issue. The Remonstrants say so, *Apologia*, folio
278. We deny it.

1. Because Jeroboam deliberately erected the golden calves to establish his
kingdom, and Solomon knowingly sacrificed to foreign gods. Both knew that
eternal salvation was at issue.

2. Because otherwise no one could lie, for the sake of filthy gain or empty
glory, that God is corporeal or is the author of sin. This is contrary to
experience.

It is asked whether that heretic is not blasphemous who strives

[1668 KBNL/UvA witness | logical printed p. 738 | PDF p. 787 | split word joined: nititur]

to prove his blasphemy from God's Word and is moved by such reverence for the
divine Word that he would rather undergo a thousand deaths than abandon it.
The Remonstrants hold such a heretic to be innocent, upright, and pious,
*Apologia*, chapter 24, folio 263.

We deny it. 1. Because those whose conscience is seared with a hot iron can be
judicially hardened by God so that they believe their heresy conforms to God's
Word and undergo death for it. This is evident in Baal's priests and in the
Jews, who contend from the Old Testament that Mary's Son is an impostor and
are prepared to die for that religion of theirs.

2. Because on this reasoning no blasphemer abominable to God would ever be
found in the world: with a deluded conscience, he might believe Christ to be a
deceiver and the fiercest enemy of God the Father, Christ's religion to be a
fable, and killing Christ and the apostles to be lawful and even pious. This
contradicts Scripture. For what must be said about those who believe that the
sacrifice of their children to Molech is religious obedience to God of the
kind God commanded Abraham? Jeremiah 7:31. Likewise, what must be said about
the killers of Christ and the apostles? Matthew 26:65–66; 1 Corinthians 2:8;
John 16:2–3.

3. Because the principal argument why the magistrate may not punish a heretic
with death is that neither the magistrate nor any mortal can judge the hidden
heart to determine whether he holds that blasphemy against the dictate of his
conscience. But in that case, someone who maintains that God is a body to be
worshiped in an idol, that the Son of God is an impostor, and that there are
three

[1668 KBNL/UvA witness | logical printed p. 739 | PDF p. 788]

gods cannot but be tolerated. Indeed, one who infects others with the gangrene
of his false doctrine cannot be barred from public places by bodily force,
contrary to what they themselves teach in the *Apologia*, chapter 24, folio
257. For only the “knower of hearts” knows whether inward obstinacy accompanies
the blasphemy. Thus Moses, the kings of Israel, and the people could not have
stoned someone who enticed others to worship false gods, because it was
possible that this heresy was itself piety and innocence. What? Did the people
know the secrets of the heart? Nor can any heretic, even Hymenaeus, who made
shipwreck of the faith, be excommunicated. Indeed, he could not be publicly
rebuked on the charge of heterodoxy or marked by any censure, because only the
“knower of hearts,” not the church or any mortal, knows whether he is obstinate
or has been led astray by a simple error of the mind.

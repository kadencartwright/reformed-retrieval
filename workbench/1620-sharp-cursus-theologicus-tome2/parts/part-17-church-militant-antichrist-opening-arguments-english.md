QUESTION VI.

Whether the Pope is that great Antichrist of whom Scripture treats.

THE WORD *ANTICHRIST* (which occurs in Scripture only in John, in his First
and Second Epistles) is used in two ways, according as the preposition ἀντὶ
has two meanings. For, first, ἀντὶ means *for*, or *in the place of another*
(whether by force and fraud or legitimately), as in Matthew 17:27, Mark 10:45,
and likewise Luke 11:11. Thus one is called an ἀντιβασιλεὺς or
ἀντιστράτηγος who is not equal to a commander or king, but subordinate to
him.

[1620 BSB Tome II | canvas 85 | printed col. 172 | bsb10175568]

Bellarmine therefore errs in book 3, chapter 1, *On the Supreme Pontiff*, when
he denies that ἀντὶ signifies substitution or subordination.

Second, ἀντὶ is taken to mean *against*. We call the Pope Antichrist in both
senses jointly, because, although he is Christ’s enemy, he loudly claims to
act in Christ’s place so that he may deceive more easily. In this he imitates
Antiochus Epiphanes, whom they hold to have been a type of Antichrist. As
Jerome testifies on Daniel 11, when Antiochus pretended to be Ptolemy’s
guardian, he subjected the kingdom of Egypt to himself by fraud.

The word *Antichrist*, insofar as it means Christ’s adversary, is also used in
two ways—or, according to Bellarmine in book 3, chapter 2, in the same ways as
the word *Christ*. The word *Christ*:

1. Is used generally for all those who have a likeness to Christ with respect
to anointing, as in the case of prophets, kings, and priests, Psalm 105:15.

2. Is used properly and particularly for the preeminent and unique Christ
Jesus. So too the word *Antichrist* is used generally, either for open enemies
of Christ or for hidden ones. The open enemies are either outside the Church,
such as Jews, Turks, and the false Church, who do not embrace true doctrine,
Luke 11:23; or within the Church, such as open heretics, who strike against
the truth of the doctrines of faith, and schismatics, who strike especially
against charity—whether from ambition, hatred, impatience with the Church’s
order, or on the pretext of scandals that occur in the Church. The hidden
enemies are those who attack Christ under his own name: the fools of Proverbs
9:6, those who come in his name and seek their own glory, John 5:43 and 7:18.
They are called false prophets and savage wolves, Matthew 7:15 and Acts 20:29,
and false Christs, because they pass themselves off as Christ;

[1620 BSB Tome II | canvas 86 | printed col. 173 | bsb10175568]

yet at first they are not recognized as such. All these are called
antichrists in the general sense, 1 John 2:18.

Second, the word *Antichrist* is used properly and particularly for a certain
preeminent enemy of Christ. Concerning him, as even Bellarmine testifies from
2 Thessalonians 2, it is foretold that he will be the head of that universal
apostasy. All the preceding antichrists paved the way for him, as it were, but
he completes the fullness of impiety and is called κατ’ ἐξοχὴν ὁ
ἀντίχριστος—*Antichrist preeminently*—in 1 John 2:18, 22 and 4:3.

It is about Antichrist in this sense that we and the Papists dispute whether
the Pope is Antichrist. They deny it; we affirm it with the following
arguments.

It must be noted, however, that when we call the Pope Antichrist we do not
mean all the bishops who have been at Rome since the apostles’ times, because
many of the first bishops were holy martyrs. We mean only all the Popes after
Antichrist was disclosed, namely, those who have occupied the see from the
year of Christ 600. At that time Boniface III, who killed his predecessor,
began to arrogate to himself the title of universal bishop and head of the
Church, which he obtained from Emperor Phocas.

Argument 1. He alone to whom the name *great Antichrist* applies is that great
Antichrist. But such is the Pope, because he alone pretends and boasts that he
acts in Christ’s place and is his vicar, although he is utterly contrary to
Christ, as is evident from the following arguments.

Argument 2. He alone to whom all the parts of the scriptural description of
Antichrist apply is that great Antichrist. For when a definition applies to
someone, the thing defined also applies to him. But all the descriptions of
Antichrist apply to the Pope alone. Therefore.

[1620 BSB Tome II | canvas 86 | printed col. 174 | bsb10175568]

The assumption is proved from the description itself and its application to
the Pope. The description is this:

That great Antichrist is that man of sin, stirred up through Satan’s working,
who after the general ἀποστασία, or apostasy, sits in God’s temple as God and
exalts himself above everything called God. At one time he is one
collectively, but in succession he changes in his see, 2 Thessalonians 2:3,
4, 6, 7, 9; 1 John 4:3; Revelation 13:4 and 18:2. He professes Christ’s name,
2 Thessalonians 2:8 and Revelation 13:11, but in reality teaches doctrines of
demons, 1 Timothy 4:1, and opposes the true doctrine of Christ’s person and
offices, and therefore denies that Jesus is the Christ, 1 John 2:22. He
tyrannically exercises a twofold power, civil and ecclesiastical, Revelation
9:11 and 18:11. He works lying miracles, 2 Thessalonians 2:9, Revelation
13:13 and 16:14. He is the mother of fornication and idolatry, Revelation
17:2, 5 and 18:3; that two-horned beast, utterly impure and cruel under the
pretext of religion, so that it resembles the Lamb; and he persecutes the
Church, Revelation 11:3; 14:18; 17:6; and 19:2. His seat is Rome, Revelation
15:18 and 17:9. He is revealed at his own time, 2 Thessalonians 2:8, and is to
be destroyed by a twofold judgment in this life:

1. By the spiritual judgment of the Spirit of the Lord’s mouth, that is,
through the preaching of the gospel, 2 Thessalonians 2:8.

2. By a most severe temporal judgment together with his adherents,
Revelation 17:16 and 18:2. Finally, he is to be completely abolished at
Christ’s glorious coming, 2 Thessalonians 2 and Revelation 20.

From the application of this definition we draw the following arguments.

Argument 3. He who sits in the temple of God and exalts himself above God and
everything called God or worshiped as God is that great Antichrist, 2
Thessalonians 2:4. But such is the Pope.

[1620 BSB Tome II | canvas 87 | printed col. 175 | bsb10175568]

The assumption is proved.

The Papists say that the Pope is above all laws and that it is not permissible
to disapprove what the Pope approves, according to Gratian; and in the gloss
he says that everyone can be judged by him, but he by no one.

2. They say that all power in heaven and on earth has been given to the Pope,
as Zodericus Zamorra says in book 2, chapter 1, and that in the Pope is power
above all powers of both heaven and earth, as Stephanus Pans says in an
oration at the Lateran Council under Leo X.

3. He arrogates to himself titles belonging to God and Christ. Thus he is
called the God of vengeance, according to Cuperius, page 61, number 51, and
also King of Kings and Lord of Lords in the *Extravagantes*, *On Superiority
and Obedience*, chapter *Solita*.

Likewise, a canonically elected Pope is called God on earth by Albanus in
book 1, number 22 of *On the Power of the Pope*. So too in the *Clementines*
and in the preface he is called “Our Lord God the Pope.” At session 4 of the
Lateran Council Christophorus Marcellus says, “You are another God on earth.”
Likewise Anthoninus, part 3, title 22, chapter 5, says that the Pope is called
lord of those in heaven, on earth, and in the underworld. So too Caietanus,
treatise 16, question 6, says that Christ removed the impediment of original
sin, while Peter and the Pope remove that of actual sin through the keys.

4. He has usurped power over kings and emperors, who are called gods. Indeed,
he has even forced emperors to kiss his feet, and Alexander III set his foot
upon the neck of Emperor Frederick Barbarossa while pronouncing these words:
“You will walk upon the asp and the basilisk.” Hence, concerning Pope
Nicholas, distinction 96 says: “It is shown plainly enough that the Pontiff
can neither be bound nor loosed at all by the secular power, since it is
agreed that he was called God by

[1620 BSB Tome II | canvas 87 | printed col. 176 | bsb10175568]

Emperor Constantine, and it is manifest that God cannot be judged by men.”
Likewise Pope Sixtus IV permitted a triumphal arch to be erected for himself
with the inscription:

“By your oracular voice you guide the reins of the world,

And deservedly you are said to be God on earth.”

And book 1 of the ceremonies, title 7, says: “On the night of the Nativity the
Pope blesses a sword, which he afterward gives to some prince as a sign of the
infinite power conferred upon the Pontiff, according to the saying, ‘All power
in heaven and on earth has been given to me.’” Likewise, “He will rule from
sea to sea.” When Pope Pius V deposed Elizabeth of England, he misused in his
bull the testimony of Jeremiah 1:10: “Behold, I appoint you today over nations
and kingdoms.”

Argument 4. If the Pope arrogates to himself authority above God’s laws and
word, then he is that great Antichrist. But the former is true. Therefore.

The assumption is proved. Gratian says in the gloss, “I say that the Pope can
dispense against natural law and against the Apostle.” It is also commonplace
in the pontifical court that the Pope can dispense against Paul’s Epistles.
Capistranus, in *On the Authority of the Pope and the Church*, folio 95, says
that the Pope can dispense against the Apostle’s authority because the Pope
is greater than the Apostle. Pope Martin V approved marriage with a full
sister. They also enroll and canonize parricides among the saints, and remit
even sins not committed to those who do not repent, and so forth.

Argument 5. If it is safer to violate God’s laws than the Pope’s, even when
the latter conflict with God’s law, then the Pope is that great Antichrist.
But the former is true. Thus it is safer to violate the Sabbath than a feast
day or Lent, and so forth. Likewise, if someone has polluted himself with
fornication, adultery, theft, and so forth, he is absolved for a little

[1620 BSB Tome II | canvas 88 | printed col. 177 | bsb10175568]

money. But if someone eats meat on Friday, reads Scripture in the vernacular,
or omits idle and even impious ceremonies, he pays with his life. Therefore.

Argument 6. He who falls away from God and teaches doctrines of demons in the
last times is that great Antichrist. But such is the Pope. Therefore. The
assumption is proved. Forbidding marriage and commanding abstinence from
foods is a doctrine of demons, 1 Timothy 4:1–3. But the Pope teaches this.
Therefore.

Argument 7. He who prefers any creature to Christ or makes it equal to him is
Antichrist. But such is the Pope. Therefore. The assumption—that he makes
himself equal to Christ—was proved in the third argument. He also makes Mary
equal to Christ, if he does not prefer her to him, when it is said, “By a
mother’s right command the Redeemer,” and when he calls her Queen of Heaven,
Gate of Paradise, Refuge of Sinners, Mediatrix of Men, and so forth.

Argument 8. He who assumes all the titles that belong to Christ alone is that
great Antichrist, because we read nothing of the sort about the other heretics
or enemies of the Church who were lesser antichrists. But such is the Pope,
because he wishes to be called Great Shepherd of the Sheep, Universal
Shepherd, Prince of Shepherds, Bridegroom and Head of the Church—titles that
are attributed to Christ alone, Hebrews 13:20; 1 Peter 5:4; 2 Corinthians
11:2; and elsewhere. Therefore.

Argument 9. If the Pope does and says things that not even Christ himself
would say or do if he were on earth, then he is Antichrist. But the former is
true, because the Pope transfers kingdoms and deposes kings; Innocent compares
the Pope to the sun and the emperor to the moon; and the gloss adds, from
Ptolemy, that the sun is 7,744 times larger than the moon. See Gregory’s
*Decretals*, book 1, title 33, *Solita*, and so forth; Pope Innocent III, tome
2, page 644.

Likewise, the Pope remits sins, even those not committed, provided the money
has been duly paid;

[1620 BSB Tome II | canvas 88 | printed col. 178 | bsb10175568]

and Hostiensis says that the Pope can by law do everything God can do.
Distinction 40, *Si Papa*, also says, “If the Pope should draw innumerable
peoples with him in crowds into hell, it is wrong to say, ‘What are you
doing?’”

Argument 10. If the Pope rehashes the doctrines of almost all the ancient
heretics and forces them upon the Church, then he is that great Antichrist.
But the former is true.

Proof. Long ago the Valentinians, Marcionites, and other heretics, according
to Irenaeus, book 3, chapter 1, and Tertullian’s *On Prescriptions*, when they
were refuted by the Scriptures, accused the Scriptures of imperfection and
obscurity and said that they possessed no authority. So do the Papists. Long
ago the Pelagians held that free choice remained in fallen man, according to
Augustine against the Pelagians. So do the Papists. Long ago the Marcionites,
Messalians, and others said that baptism removes only preceding sins and
original sin. So do the Papists. Long ago Marcion taught that women could
baptize in a case of necessity, according to Epiphanius, Heresy 42. So do the
Papists. Long ago the Pelagians taught that a person could fulfill the law
perfectly and be perfectly righteous, as Jerome testifies on Jeremiah 13. So
do the Papists. Long ago the Pelagians denied that original sin exists in
infants. So the Papists hold that it is completely removed through baptism.
Long ago the Pelagians taught that people are predestined because of good
works. So do the Papists. Thus Catharinus, on Romans 8, teaches that many
Papist doctors have erred in this matter. Long ago the Basilidians and
Carpocratians wished their doctrines to remain unknown to the common people
and forbade the laity to read Scripture, according to Irenaeus, book 1,
chapter 13. So do the Papists. Long ago the Marcionites and Manichaeans
imagined that Christ had an imaginary and phantom body. So do the Papists
when they imagine that Christ’s body exists under the species of bread and
wine without its own properties. Long ago the Manichaeans

[1620 BSB Tome II | canvas 89 | printed col. 179 | bsb10175568]

offered bread alone in the Supper, according to Augustine, Heresy 71. So do
the Papists. Long ago the Manichaeans and Tatianists forbade priests to marry,
according to Augustine, Epistle 74 and Heresies 40 and 46. So do the Papists.
Long ago the Manichaeans abstained from meat during a fast, according to
Augustine, book 2 of *On the Morals of the Church*, and Montanus the heretic
was the first to establish laws concerning fasting, according to Eusebius,
book 5 of the *Ecclesiastical History*, chapter 17. So do the Papists. Long
ago, according to Pope Leo IV in his sermon on Lent, “there were heretics who
did not take food with other people.” But the Pope creates such people as
Carthusians, hermits, and so forth. See Augustine, Heresy 39. Long ago the
Collyridians, or Mariamites, worshiped and invoked the Virgin Mary; for this
reason Epiphanius calls them idolaters in Heresy 79. So do the Papists. Long
ago the Angelici worshiped angels, as Augustine teaches in Heresy 93. So do
the Papists. Long ago the Marcionites preferred virginity to everything, as
Epiphanius testifies in Heresy 22. So do the Papists. Long ago the
Marcellians had several images of Christ, just as the Gnostics and
Carpocratians had images of Christ and Paul, according to Irenaeus, book 1,
chapter 24; Epiphanius, Heresy 27; and Augustine, Heresy 17. So do the
Papists. Long ago the Apostolici admitted no one into their society unless
they had deprived themselves of their goods, bound themselves to certain
rules, and renounced marriage, as Augustine says in Heresy 68. So do the
monks within the papacy. Long ago the Armenians venerated the Lord’s cross,
and therefore were called σταυρολάτραι, that is, worshipers of the cross. So
do the Papists, and so forth.

Argument 11. He who denies that Jesus is the Christ is that great Antichrist,
1 John 2:22 and 3:3. But such is the Pope. Therefore.

The assumption is proved. He denies the reality of Christ’s person and, as
far as he can, abolishes his offices. Therefore. The antecedent is proved.
First, concerning the person, because through

[1620 BSB Tome II | canvas 89 | printed col. 180 | bsb10175568]

transubstantiation the Papists hold that Christ’s body exists without the
properties of a true body and consequently is not a body, according to
Augustine and others, as was proved in the locus on the sacred Supper. But
when the reality of the human nature is removed, the reality of the
Mediator’s person is removed.

2. It is proved concerning the prophetic, priestly, and royal offices.

1. He attacks the prophetic office, because Scripture testifies that Christ
perfectly revealed the Father’s will, John 15:15 and 16:13; Acts 20:2;
Hebrews 1:1. But the Pope invents another doctrine in addition to this one
and wishes it to be necessary for salvation, as will be evident from the
antithesis between the doctrine of Christ and that of Antichrist.

2. He attacks the priestly office, because Scripture teaches that Christ is
that one priest who by his one offering made satisfaction for all sins,
Hebrews 10:14 and 1 John 1:7. But the Pope creates an infinite number of other
priests to offer Christ daily, and wishes sins to be purged by other means
than Christ. Next, he attacks Christ’s intercession, because Scripture says
there is one mediator between God and men, 1 Timothy 2:5. But the Pope wishes
there to be an infinite number of other mediators.

3. He attacks Christ’s royal office because the Pope claims for himself
things that belong to Christ because of this office. Thus session 10 of the
Lateran Council says that in the Pope is all power above all powers of both
heaven and earth. Next, he arrogates to himself a right over souls after this
life, so that he can bring them up from hell and thrust them down to hell.
Thus Clement VI says in his bull, “We grant that if a person dies on the way
after making confession, he is immune from all his sins, and we command the
angels to bring the soul, entirely absolved from purgatory, into the glory of
paradise.” Likewise Troilus, treatise *On the Canon*, doubt 3, says that the
Pope has power in

[1620 BSB Tome II | canvas 90 | printed col. 181 | bsb10175568]

heaven to canonize whatever dead person he wishes and enroll him among the
saints, even against the will of the bishops and cardinals. Finally, he reigns
as a king—or, rather, a tyrant—in the Church.

Argument 12. He whose doctrine and life are in every respect diametrically
opposed to Christ’s doctrine and life is that great Antichrist. But such is
the Pope. Therefore. The assumption is proved.

1. Concerning doctrine. Scripture, which is Christ’s voice, teaches that
everyone must search the Scriptures, John 5:35. But the Pope keeps people
from reading them.

2. Scripture teaches that human traditions nullify God’s commandments, and
that those who observe them worship God in vain, Matthew 15:3, 9. But the
Pope wishes traditions to be received with the same affection as Scripture.

3. Scripture says that God’s word contains everything that must be done and
believed, and therefore that it is perfect and clear. But the Pope loudly
calls it imperfect, obscure, a dead letter, a leaden rule, a wax nose, and so
forth.

4. Scripture says that God alone must be worshiped and served. But the Pope
bestows δουλεία, or service, upon the saints.

5. Scripture teaches that original sin, with respect to its inherence, cannot
be removed in this life, Romans 7. But the Pope holds that it is entirely
removed in baptism.

6. Scripture teaches that covetous desire is sin in the regenerate, Romans
7:7. But the Pope teaches the opposite.

7. Scripture teaches that the wages of every sin is death, Romans 6:23. But
the Pope holds that many sins are venial.

[1620 BSB Tome II | canvas 90 | printed col. 182 | bsb10175568]

8. Scripture teaches that the unregenerate are dead in sins, Ephesians 2:1.
But the Pope holds that they have residual powers for cooperating with grace
in the first conversion.

9. Scripture teaches that we are justified freely because of God’s grace
alone in Christ. But the Pope attributes the second justification to our
merits.

10. Scripture teaches that believers are certain of salvation, Romans 8 and
elsewhere. But the Pope teaches that one must always doubt.

11. Scripture teaches that the law is impossible even for the regenerate,
Romans 8:3 and Acts 15:10. But the Pope teaches that it is possible.

12. Scripture teaches that the cup must be given to everyone in the Supper, 1
Corinthians 11 and elsewhere. But the Pope teaches the opposite.

13. Scripture teaches that we were not redeemed from sins with gold or
silver, 1 Peter 1:18. But the Pope absolves sins in return for money duly
paid. The remaining matters that pertain to this are to be sought from the
state of the particular question between us and the Papists.

Second, concerning life. Christ came in humility and modesty, but the Pope
proudly casts kings down from their thrones.

2. Christ refused to worship the devil, although the devil offered him all
the kingdoms of the world. But Platina and other writers on the Popes testify
that twenty-two Popes gave themselves to the devil in order to attain the
papacy, and that several were necromancers.

3. Christ drove the buyers and sellers out of the temple, but the Pope
established buying and selling in the temple through the purchase and sale of
masses and indulgences.

4. Christ washed his disciples’ feet, although they were fishermen, but the
Pope forces emperors to kiss his feet.

[1620 BSB Tome II | canvas 91 | printed col. 183 | bsb10175568]

5. Christ approved God’s command that the wages of a prostitute must not be
brought into his house, and condemned the act itself. But the Pope approves
brothels and receives a large income from them. Historians in the lives of
the pontiffs teach that four Popes were incestuous, eleven were sodomites, and
one—Pope Joan—was a prostitute.

6. Christ paid tribute to the emperor, but the Pope took kingdoms from the
emperor. Christ did not have the means to pay tribute, but the Pope has a
daily income of 18,000 crowns. Christ was both God and man, but the Papists
loudly claim that he is neither God nor man, and so forth.

Argument 13. Antichrist is foretold to come through Satan’s working with all
power and lying signs and wonders, because his kingdom will be established
and propagated through false miracles by which he will deceive, if it were
possible, the elect themselves, Matthew 24:24; 2 Thessalonians 2:9;
Revelation 13:13 and 16:13. But such is the Pope. Therefore. The assumption is
proved. Nicholas of Lyra says on Daniel 4, “In God’s Church the people are
often deceived by priests through fabricated miracles for the sake of gain,
as when they cause images to sweat, weep, laugh, and so forth, and when they
pretend to cast out demons.” Next, the miracles found in legends and
elsewhere are fabrications and monks’ dreams. Likewise, Scripture does not
foretell that any miracles will occur in the last times except Antichrist’s,
because the gift of miracles was temporary. Indeed, the fathers and some
Papist writers testify that miracles are now unnecessary in the true Church.
Thus Chrysostom, Homily 49 on Matthew, says: “We now recognize Christ’s
ministers not because they perform beneficial signs, but because they do not
perform these signs at all.” Gregory, Homily 29 on

[1620 BSB Tome II | canvas 91 | printed col. 184 | bsb10175568]

the Gospel, says that miracles were necessary only in the planting of the
Church. Stella, on Luke 11, says that the gospel was confirmed through
miracles and asks whether it must still be confirmed by miracles. He answers
no, “because now,” he says, “they would do more harm than good.” Thus
Augustine rightly says in book 22 of *The City of God*, “Miracles were
necessary so that the world might believe, but the person who asks for
miracles now that the world has believed is himself a great wonder.” Finally,
the Jesuit Acosta, book 6 of *On the Salvation of the Indians*, chapters 4,
12, and 17, teaches that Papist miracles are false and fabricated, and
therefore are Antichrist’s.

Argument 14. Antichrist will arrogate to himself a twofold human power, civil
and ecclesiastical, Revelation 13:16, 19, where the false prophet is said to
exercise all the power of the first beast and of the Roman Empire, according
to Bellarmine, book 3 of *On the Roman Pontiff*, chapter 5. But such is the
Pope. Therefore.

Argument 15. Antichrist is foretold to be extremely wealthy, Daniel 11:38,
43 and Revelation 17 and 18:3, to be worshiped with gold and silver, and to
be clothed in purple, scarlet, and so forth, Revelation 17:4. But such is the
Pope. Therefore.

Argument 16. Antichrist is foretold to exercise great cruelty against those
who will not worship the image of the beast—that is, the Pope, because he is
the image of the beast, representing the Roman Empire—and to cause them to
receive the beast’s character and mark on their forehead and right hand, so
that no one can buy or sell unless he has the beast’s mark, Revelation 13:16.
But such is the Pope, who has stirred up the most cruel persecutions and
slaughters against the saints, imprints the chrism of confirmation upon
everyone’s forehead, and causes no one to be able to buy or sell without his
mark;

[1620 BSB Tome II | canvas 92 | printed col. 185 | bsb10175568]

finally, he imprints the mark of the tonsure upon his clergy. Therefore.

Argument 17. Antichrist’s general seat is foretold to be εἰς τὸν ναὸν τοῦ
Θεοῦ, “in the temple of God,” 2 Thessalonians 2:4, which signifies not so much
*in* the temple as *over* the temple. The temple signifies, according to
Anselm, the minds of unbelievers, in which Antichrist is said to sit when he
has deceived them; or, according to Augustine, Antichrist together with his
people, who would wish to appear to be God’s temple; or, according to
Chrysostom, the Christian churches that Antichrist will command to serve him.
But the seat is foretold to be at Rome, Revelation 17:9, 18, which is called
spiritual Babylon in Revelation 18:2, 10. Bellarmine interprets Babylon as
Rome in book 2 of *On the Roman Pontiff*, chapter 10. He says that the Pope
sits in these seats. Therefore.

Argument 18. Antichrist’s temporal seat must be that seven-hilled city,
Revelation 17.

The testimonies by which the preceding position is confirmed follow.

1. Bernard, Sermon 33 on the Song: “Woe,” he says, “to this generation
because of the leaven of the Pharisees, which is hypocrisy—if, indeed, it
ought to be called hypocrisy. Today a putrid infection creeps through the
whole body of the Church. They are Christ’s ministers and serve Antichrist.”
But who then presided over the Church except the Pope? And so forth.

2. Pope Gregory, book 4, Epistle 34, concerning John of Constantinople: “He
alone,” he says, “attempts to be called bishop; but what is signified in this
pride of his other than that the times of Antichrist are already near?” The
same author, book 6, Epistle 30: “I confidently say that whoever calls himself
universal priest, or desires to be so called, is by his pride the forerunner
of Antichrist.” But if the man of Constantinople, who arrogated this title to
himself for a time, was the forerunner

[1620 BSB Tome II | canvas 92 | printed col. 186 | bsb10175568]

of Antichrist, is not the Roman who has always used this title since the time
of Boniface III Antichrist?

3. The bishops of Cologne and Trier to Pope Nicholas I, in Aventinus’s
*Annals*, book 4: “He who is the servant of servants desires to be lord of
lords just as though he were God. He speaks great things, alters laws,
establishes his own, defiles, plunders, despoils, defrauds, kills—that ruined
man whom they deservedly customarily call Antichrist. On his forehead the
name of blasphemy is written: ‘I am God; I cannot err.’ He sits in God’s
temple and rules far and wide,” and so forth.

4. Bishop Arnulph addresses the fathers of the council about the Pope in this
way at the Council of Reims held in 992: “Reverend fathers, what do you judge
this man to be, seated on a lofty throne, clothed in purple and gleaming with
gold? What, I ask, do you judge him to be? Surely if he lacks charity and is
puffed up with knowledge, he is Antichrist sitting in God’s temple and

5. Franciscus Petrarcha of Padua, around the year of Christ 1350, addresses

From what has been said, it follows that the following err:

1. The author of a certain book *On Antichrist* falsely attributed to
Augustine, who says that Antichrist will be born of a virgin by the devil’s
working, just as Christ was born of a virgin by the Holy Spirit.

2. Hippolytus in his discourse *On the Consummation of the World*, who thinks
that Antichrist will have no mother, but will be the devil himself, who will
assume false flesh from a false virgin.

3. Those who, according to Jerome on Daniel 7 and Bede on Revelation 13, say
that Antichrist will be at once a true man and a true devil, just as Christ
was true God and man.

4. Those who think that Nero will be resurrected and become Antichrist, or
that Nero is hiding somewhere and will finally persecute the Church.

5. Those who think he will be the son of a prostitute and will be born from
fornication, as Damascene says in book 4 of *On the Orthodox Faith*, chapter
28.

6. Those who think that Antichrist is the Turk; and finally, the Papists who
think that Antichrist will be born from the tribe of Dan and will be a Jew,
an opinion Bellarmine says is highly probable in book 3, chapter 12.

[Book I, printed p. 68; BSB canvas 00094]

Nor is the antecedent restricted: “Antichrist does not exist.” A proposition of this kind is true
simply, and what is said simply is not said with a restriction. Again, the consequent is inferred in
no other sense than that Antichrist is therefore not to be judged to terminate God’s intuitive
knowledge simply. For it cannot happen that what simply does not exist should, for as long as it
does not exist, be known by God as simply existing. Nevertheless, we do not deny that it can,
notwithstanding this, be known by God as existing in a qualified respect, whether in willed being
before God or in known being. But on your opinion this is not to terminate God’s intuitive
knowledge—as if only present things, and not future or past things, were presented to divine
intuitive knowledge.

Assertion 8. Hence it is asked whether Christ the husband gave the Roman Church
a bill of divorce, as he has now given the Jewish Church. Reply. As a matter of
right, Christ has legally given a bill of divorce, so that before God's
tribunal she has ceased to be a wife and is now Babylon and that purple-clad
harlot with whom the marriage has now been entirely dissolved, if we speak of
the papal assembly as such. But as a matter of fact, God has not altogether
given a bill of divorce, because she still possesses the marriage documents,
the Word of God; she still has the seals of the covenant and holds many
fundamentals concerning the one and triune God and concerning Christ and his
natures. 2. An assembly of believers lies hidden among them. 3. Active calling
exists there, because we Protestants call by voice, writings, and martyrdom,
and entreat by the Lord that they return to the husband and the husband's
house and abandon idols and unworthy men. Yet one pastoral act does not
formally make the Church a teaching Church, or the Church according to pastoral
being, just as an outward profession of marital consent, not one or two kisses,
makes a marriage. This is evident in Chaldea, where Jeremiah prophesied,
according to the saying, *“We would have healed Babylon, but she is not
healed,”* and active calling was there on the part of the prophet Jere-*

[Printed p. 78 | Google/KBNL-UvA PDF 129 | GB 9IhiAAAAcAAJ]

*miah. Paul likewise preached Christ at Athens, yet neither Chaldea nor the
Athenian assembly is therefore a Church. And although the teaching of the Old
Testament—which implicitly is the whole Word of God and which they acknowledge
as God's Word—is in the Jewish Church, it nevertheless lacks two things which
make a teaching Church, or a Church according to ecclesiastical and pastoral
being, and which are present in the Roman Church. 1. Virtual consent to the
marriage. Therefore they can in no manner or sense be called Christ's bride,
except improperly and as they are in God's decree, because they are beloved on
account of the fathers, Romans 11. For the Jews do not even outwardly profess
the Messiah or Mary's son to be the Savior, which the Romanists nevertheless
do. 2. They directly and expressly oppose the cardinal principle and sole
foundation of the Christian religion. *For no one can lay another foundation
besides the one laid, Jesus Christ.* They profess that Christ is an impostor
and false prophet. But teaching concerning Christ's person, natures, and office
can be possessed in the Roman Church. Add that active calling to the Christian
religion as Christian has now expressly and formally ceased in the Jewish
Church. Acts 13:46: *“Since you reject the Word and judge yourselves unworthy
of eternal life, behold, we turn to the Gentiles,”* says Paul. Therefore there
is not even outward calling to Christianity among the Jews, except the virtual
and bare-*

[Printed p. 79 | Google/KBNL-UvA PDF 130 | GB 9IhiAAAAcAAJ]

*letter of the Old Testament; but there is outward calling in the Roman
Church, as was said above. Therefore the essence of the teaching Church, or of
the Church according to pastoral and ecclesiastical being, is preserved there,
which is not present in the Jewish assembly.

Assertion 9. Therefore salvation is in the Roman Church, and orthodox faith is
in it as in seed. 1. Because the whole Word of God is among them. 2. Because
they deliver many fundamentals, especially that Church before the Councils of
Trent, Lateran, and Florence, from which a sound faith can be drawn. Therefore
many are saved in the Roman Church.

But on these points we differ from the innovators. 1. They maintain that the
Roman Church itself indeed teaches errors, but not fundamental errors, except
reductively and improperly—errors which overthrow the foundation. Our writers,
however—Perkins, Pareus, and Whitaker—have proved that the papal Church as such
does not teach the doctrine of salvation, but overthrows the very fundamental
principles of the Christian religion.

2. They maintain that the controversies between us and the Romanists are not
fundamental, and that neither this nor that opinion, neither ours nor theirs,
strikes at the fundamentals, but that both can be held and defended without
damage to faith or loss of salvation. We, however, judge the errors of the
Roman Catholics to be so formally fundamental—especially their idolatry,
pagan superstition, and blasphemies against Christ's person and offices—that
we consider a papist, as a papist, to overthrow the foundation itself.

[Printed p. 80 | Google/KBNL-UvA PDF 131 | GB 9IhiAAAAcAAJ]

3. They maintain that one need not repent of Roman Catholic errors as of other
errors concerning religion, because many have held dangerous opinions against
Holy Scripture who nevertheless can be saved, provided that they repent
confusedly and generally, even though they are never imbued with the contrary
truth. For if they hold the foundation, their errors are to be burned like hay
and stubble, while they themselves, repenting, are to be saved, 1 Corinthians
3. But those who believe it lawful to worship a creature and invoke saints are
so ignorant of the fundamentals—namely, that God alone is to be worshiped—that
unless they are imbued with the contrary truth and repent of such things in
particular, they cannot be saved. Thus God foretells through Zechariah that the
Jews will repent of piercing and crucifying Christ, so that they must repent
specifically of that sin and be instructed in the contrary truth. Therefore he
says, *“They will look upon me whom they pierced,”* that is, they will look
upon me with the knowledge of true saving faith, Zechariah 12:10. So also a
Roman Catholic who wishes to be saved must repent of errors that are primarily
fundamental, such as the worship of creatures and the divine graces and glory
ascribed to the Virgin Mary. For God expressly excludes idolaters from the
kingdom of heaven, 1 Corinthians 6:-*

[Printed p. 81 | Google/KBNL-UvA PDF 132 | GB 9IhiAAAAcAAJ]

*10; Galatians 5:19–20. Repentance requires their acknowledgment and
abhorrence, Ezekiel 36:25, 31–32; Isaiah 2:20.

Assertion 10. Therefore our theologians rightly teach that the essential marks
of the Church do not belong to the Roman Church, because, as Calvin says in the
cited place, *conversely, those marks which we ought chiefly to regard in this
discussion of the Church have been erased there.* For the preaching of the
Word and active calling through God's Word is a mark of the Church *in
becoming*, considered according to ecclesiastical and pastoral being, because
in it is the voice of the bridegroom wooing the virgin, even if thus far he has
made too little progress to obtain the girl's consent by entreaty. In this
manner the promulgation of the Word and active calling by Protestants inviting
the Romanists to return to the husband, and through God's Word which is among
them, is a mark of the Church. But the preaching of the Word with fruit is a
mark of the Church considered according to Christian being, according to John
10:27: *“My sheep hear my voice, and I know them, and they follow me.”* This
mark does not belong to the papal Church as distinguished from the assembly of
weak believers in the Roman Church. The Church according to ecclesiastical
being, as distinguished from the Church according to Christian being, is less
properly the Church. Therefore, simply and without qualification, and once the
distinction is applied, the Roman Church must not be called the Church.

Assertion 11. Even if the Roman Church taught all the fundamentals as true in
general, it nevertheless is not a true Church, because those fundamentals,
explained and understood in its own sense, are no longer God's Word but the
devil's word; not Christ's but Antichrist's; not the fundamentals of the
Christian religion, but things destructive of the fundamentals.

Assertion 12. Whatever Potter and others may allege to the contrary, we
Protestants do not possess real union in faith and charity with the Roman
Church. 1. Because our faith and theirs differ in their formal objects. The
Roman Catholics wish their Church's interpretation to be the infallible
testimony of the Holy Spirit on which their faith rests; our faith rests on
God's Word insofar as it is God's Word. 2. The essence of faith is not
preserved in an assembly that believes things which overthrow the foundation.
But the Roman Church believes such things.

1 CORINTHIANS 2:14

*The natural man is not capable of the things of the Spirit of God, for they
are foolishness to him. Nor can he know them, because they are spiritually
discerned.*

The apostle teaches three things here concerning the natural man. 1. He denies
him actual knowledge of the things of the Spirit of God. 2. He posits in him
an ignorance arising from a depraved disposition toward them: *For they are
foolishness to him*; his mind is hostilely disposed toward them. 3. He denies
him the power to know them.

[Printed p. 83 | Google/KBNL-UvA PDF 134 | GB 9IhiAAAAcAAJ]

Hence it is asked whether the Arminians rightly teach in their *Confession*,
section 14, and *Apology*, chapter 1, folio 33, and Arminius in private
disputation 8, thesis 4, that *Scripture can be understood by everyone, even
the simplest, provided that they possess the use of reason and are endowed
with common sense, sufficiently with respect to the meanings necessary for
eternal salvation.* And Episcopius, disputation 5, thesis 3: *No supernatural
light infused into the faculty and elevating the mind is required for
understanding and believing Scripture.* In the same place in the *Apology*
they deny that *the light of the Spirit or spiritual evidence is needed to
understand the meanings of Scripture.* And on folio 29: *The inward testimony
of the Spirit is not needed for believing Scripture to be divine, because
people believe before this testimony is given to them.*

But we teach the contrary. For to me revelation is threefold: 1. Of the
letter. 2. Literal. 3. Spiritual and supernatural. The first belongs to God
alone, who devised the doctrine of the law and Gospel from the treasury of his
infinite wisdom. No created wisdom could devise so perfect a doctrine of law
and Gospel. And although the doctrine of the law is in some manner natural to
a rational creature, as the rational soul itself is, nevertheless to devise it
originally is a work of infinite wisdom, no less than creation of the rational
soul is a work of infinite wisdom and omnipotence. Literal revelation is
*information and instruction concerning the literal and grammatical sense of
the letter of the Word already devised.* It can be provided by a pas-*

[Printed p. 84 | Google/KBNL-UvA PDF 135 | GB 9IhiAAAAcAAJ]

*tor and the doctors of the Church to people lacking supernatural revelation.
Pharisees, heretics, and profane teachers can both expound and understand the
meanings of Scripture, but this knowledge does not suffice for salvation.
Another revelation is supernatural. It is indeed a *disclosure and opening*,
not of a new meaning of Scripture distinct from its grammatical and literal
meaning, as the Remonstrants babble—for Scripture has only one genuine
meaning—but a *spiritual opening and declaration of the literal meaning which
flows from the superinfused Spirit of revelation given to the intellective
faculty*, Ephesians 1:17 and 2:9; Colossians 1:9, and from actual grace
concurrently operating.

Thus we are taught that the Arminians err when they deny that a superinfused
light of the Holy Spirit is required for understanding Scripture *to the
degree sufficient for salvation.* 1. Because *only the person who has the
Spirit*, not the natural man, *knows the things of the Spirit of God*, 1
Corinthians 2:12–13.

2. Because otherwise Paul would pray in vain for *the Spirit of wisdom and
revelation through the acknowledgment of the truth, that they may know what is
the hope of Christ's calling and what is the wealth of the glory of his
inheritance in the saints*, and so forth, Ephesians 1:16–18, if this knowledge
were possessed through grammar and an acquired knowledge of the literal
meaning. For then that same spirit would be needed for understanding
Aristotle's esoteric lectures. Those who argue in this way lack the Spirit.

3. Because there would be no privilege in Christ's *sending to the saints the
Holy Spirit who would teach them all things*, John 14:26, and giving them *that
anointing which teaches them all things.*

[Printed p. 85 | Google/KBNL-UvA PDF 136 | GB 9IhiAAAAcAAJ]

But this action is plainly distinguished from the literal instruction which
we receive from pastors or human teachers. 1 John 2:27: *“But the anointing
which you received from him remains in you, and you have no need for anyone to
teach you.”*

4. Because otherwise Christ would give thanks to the Father in vain, Matthew
11:25–26, for literal information. For this was not lacking to the prudent and
wise of this age. Otherwise, if they did not grasp the doctrine of the cross
even with literal knowledge, they would in no manner disdain and disregard it.
Therefore he gives thanks to the Father for supernatural revelation.

5. Because Christ would speak badly in Matthew 16:17 when he says that *flesh
and blood did not reveal to Peter that Christ was God's Son, but only the
heavenly Father*, if the Holy Spirit were not needed for perceiving Scripture
spiritually. For with respect to literal revelation, that is revealed through
flesh and blood; nor did the Pharisees and all those who obstinately denied
Christ to be God's Son lack literal knowledge.

6. Nor would it be necessary for us to pray for Christ's eye salve, Revelation
3:18, and the opening of the eyes of the mind, Psalm 119:18. Nor would pastors
and doctors need to implore the light of the Holy Spirit for delivering sacred
sermons and teaching pub-*

[Printed p. 86 | Google/KBNL-UvA PDF 137 | GB 9IhiAAAAcAAJ]

*licly in the schools. For sufficient revelation would be obtained through
truly human instruction.

7. If a superinfused light were not needed in the intellective faculty, but
only *a clear presentation of the truth* sufficed for understanding Scripture,
as they say in the *Synodical Writings*, articles 3 and 4, p. 164, the natural
man would be no more blind in grasping the mysteries of God's kingdom than an
untutored student is in grasping Aristotle's esoteric works and his
metaphysical and logical disputations. Christ's anointing, the Holy Spirit's
eye salve, and the Spirit of wisdom and revelation would then be needed for
learning Aristotle's *Organon*, for in such matters *a clear presentation of
the truth* is especially necessary.

8. Because all corruption in the will arises from the darkening of the mind;
and if this darkening is not so great that *God's superinfused light* is
needed, then the natural man will be able not only to grasp but also rightly to
discern, approve, will, and do the things of God's Spirit. Yet the natural man
is dead in sins and unfit for understanding, discerning, approving, believing,
and doing every spiritual truth and good in a way in which an untutored novice
is dead and powerless to understand, explain, and defend Aristotle's first,
natural, and moral philosophy.

9. According to this *Arminian* theology, *Turks, Jews, Americans*, and indeed
everyone can understand and be-*

[Printed p. 87 | Google/KBNL-UvA PDF 138 | GB 9IhiAAAAcAAJ]

*lieve that the Scriptures are divine. No inward testimony of the Holy Spirit
is needed, if the Remonstrants are to be believed.

10. This is the doctrine of the Socinian *Ostorodt*, who ridicules the inward
illumination of the Holy Spirit, *Institutes*, book 1, chapter 5.

## 2 Corinthians 4:3

*But if our gospel is veiled, it is veiled to those who perish.*

It is asked whether our theologians defend the obscurity of Holy Scripture
together with the papists because we teach that no one can understand it to
the degree sufficient for salvation without the Holy Spirit's inward light.
The Remonstrants say so in *Apology* 1, fol. 33, and book 2 against Vedelius,
chapter 2.

We deny it. 1. Because the question between us and the papists concerns
Scripture's *literal and grammatical perspicuity*, not its *supernatural
perspicuity*. We teach that the Scriptures are perspicuous in themselves, but
that the gospel is nevertheless veiled to the blind person and the one who
perishes, and that a veil lies over the Jews' heart when the Old Testament is
read, 2 Corinthians 3:15.

2. Because the Remonstrants teach that the Scriptures are so perspicuous that
they will allow nothing to be required for their inward and spiritual
understanding beyond literal knowledge, which belongs even to devils,
heretics, and *those in whom Satan, the god of this age, has blinded minds so
that the illumination of Christ's glory should not shine upon them*, 2
Corinthians 4:4. But we teach that Scripture is obscure to devils and blinded
repro-*

[Printed p. 88 | Google/KBNL-UvA PDF 139 | GB 9IhiAAAAcAAJ]

*bates in the same way that the sun is obscure to a blind person, although it
is exceedingly bright and visible in itself.

3. Because the Remonstrants slanderously teach that we fashion two meanings
for Scripture, one *literal and grammatical* and another *supernatural*
meaning added to it. This is utterly false. A person with inflamed eyes and a
person with a lynx's sight see the same sun in different ways, but we do not
therefore say either that there are two suns or that there are two perspicuous
visions of the sun. We maintain that believers and reprobates grasp one and
the same meaning of Scripture by natural understanding, while the believer
grasps that same native meaning of Scripture in a more eminent manner, by a
superinfused power and the Spirit of revelation, in a way in which the
reprobate never perceives it. We therefore assert two modes of seeing one and
the same literal meaning. Hence God's Word, perspicuous in itself, is clear and
readily apparent to the believer in both ways, by *natural and supernatural
evidence*. It is perspicuous to the reprobate by *natural evidence*, which we
say against the papists, but veiled and obscure by *supernatural evidence*,
which we say against the Arminians.

But the Remonstrants say in book 2, chapter 10 against Vedelius that *the
papists concede that God's Word is not obscure but perspicuous to the
illuminated*. I reply that this is false. For they say that it should not be
read by any laypeople, even the truly regenerate, because

[Printed p. 89 | Google/KBNL-UvA PDF 140 | GB 9IhiAAAAcAAJ]

by reason of its obscurity it can produce heresies in them, and that it must
therefore be expounded by the Church alone—that is, by the pope, councils, and
doctors.

## Colossians 2:6–7

*Walk in him, rooted, built up, and established.*

Hebrews 13:9: *Do not be carried about by various and strange doctrines.*

It is asked whether a solid faith in Scripture can coexist with a *liberty of
prophesying* by which one may investigate new interpretations of Scripture
every day and perpetually doubt all disputed matters, or matters capable of
being disputed. The Remonstrants say so in the *Apology*, chapter 24, fol. 275.

We deny it. 1. Because then we would necessarily be carried about continually
by new doctrines, which the apostle forbids.

2. Because we ought to be rooted and established in faith and to abound in all
knowledge of Christ, and it is not fitting that we be driven about by every
new wind.

For their *liberty of prophesying*, the Remonstrants cite Paul's words in 1
Thessalonians 5:19–20: *Do not quench the Spirit; do not despise prophecies.*
From this they conclude that everyone should be permitted in the Church to
teach *that to be Scripture's meaning which he believes to be true by his own
spirit of prophesying*. If either the magistrate or ecclesiastical discipline
prevents anyone from teaching and disseminating such prophecies, then they say
the Spirit's power is quenched and prophecies are de-*

[Printed p. 90 | Google/KBNL-UvA PDF 141 | GB 9IhiAAAAcAAJ]

*spised. They teach that *spirit* is taken in this sense in 2 Thessalonians
2:2: *Do not be quickly shaken from your mind, whether by a word or by a
spirit*; and in 1 John 4:1: *Do not believe every spirit.* But, as Beza
interprets it, this means dreams and inventions.

We say that liberty here is threefold. 1. One is the liberty of *bare
knowledge*. 2. Another is the liberty of *conscience*. 3. Another is the
liberty of *profession or exercise*, both publicly and privately. Liberty of
bare knowledge ought to be granted to everyone in a commonwealth in this
sense: the magistrate cannot compel anyone to hold this or that opinion in
religion. This belongs to God alone. Through ministers and doctors, the
magistrate can command that the mind be informed, but he cannot command an
opinion.

2. The magistrate cannot deprive the conscience of its liberty to think
rightly about God. Neither a king, a tyrant, nor any created power can compel
or restrain the conscience. 3. But insofar as conscience displays itself in
the exercise of religious liberty, the magistrate can establish and defend
the public profession and exercise of the orthodox faith, prohibit the
exercise of heterodox religion, and command subjects to hear God's Word from
the mouths of orthodox doctors. He can command this *primarily*, and therefore
*secondarily and consequently* a right conception of God.

This is our position concerning liberty of prophesying, but we condemn the
Remonstrant license of prophesying.

[Printed p. 91 | Google/KBNL-UvA PDF 142 | GB 9IhiAAAAcAAJ]

1. Because the Holy Spirit would thus forbid the quenching of spirits—that is,
*false dreams and inventions*—and consequently God would will us to reverence
fanatical and false interpretations of the Word, which is blasphemous.

2. A false prophet who sells his dreams as God's oracles would not be rebuked
but honored, and this by God's command: *do not despise prophecy*.

3. Because they will us to pray that God open the eyes of our mind and teach
us his statutes; they will us to be admonished by God *to hear, attend, judge,
and test the Scriptures, so that we may be wise and prudent, not children but
adults in knowledge*, in order that we may investigate new prophecies and new
opinions in religion while renouncing the prophecies we formerly honored.
That is, they will the Spirit of God to suggest to us an annual, monthly, or
hourly faith. For the wisdom opposed to childhood is a fruit of the Spirit.
But this spirit of wisdom is God's Spirit, who today suggests that this is the
true meaning of a disputed Scripture. Yet we must abandon that interpretation
if the spirit of wisdom suggests a new interpretation opposed to it. For this
is the Remonstrants' doctrine: *in all controversies no one is to be so certain
in his faith that he is not ready to believe a better interpretation*—which
he believes to be true by his own spirit of prophecy, which must not be
despised—unless he dares to oppose the spirit of prophecy and therefore God
himself teaching the truth. And-*

[Printed p. 92 | Google/KBNL-UvA PDF 143 | GB 9IhiAAAAcAAJ]

*thus the Holy Spirit suggests that contradictories are true: namely, that
this interpretation was true last year, but now its contradictory is true.

4. Because if a person fluctuates in this way concerning disputed matters,
and whatever each person thinks true in his private conscience is true, then
Scripture is proved obscure and insufficient for deciding controversies.
Every disputed matter is here resolved into the erroneous and erring dictate
of conscience, and the human being, through a dreaming spirit—not the Holy
Spirit himself in the Word—is established as the judge of controversies.

5. These disputed matters concern either useful or useless truth. If the
former, no infallible rule for knowing every useful truth has been left to us,
which wrongs the Holy Spirit and the Scriptures. If the latter, how did God
write so many useless things in the Scriptures of the Old and New Testaments
while writing only four or five fundamental articles? This seems unworthy of
God's wisdom.

6. It is permissible to doubt disputed matters and to incline toward this or
the contrary position. But they teach in the *Apology*, chapter 24, fol. 276,
that *by disputed matters are understood those that either are disputed or can
be disputed*. Yet whether there are three Gods, as there are three persons of
the Deity, can be disputed no less; and it is disputed whether Christ is true
God. Tritheists have as much color from the Scriptures as

[Printed p. 93 | Google/KBNL-UvA PDF 144 | GB 9IhiAAAAcAAJ]

the Arians have. It can also be disputed whether the happiness of the
glorified and the torments of the damned are eternal in the same way in which
circumcision is called *eternal* in the Old Testament, and whether the soul is
not mortal, as Smalcius says in *On the Arian Error*, book 1, chapter 14. The
Arminian John Geisteranus said that he did not know whether souls after death
are living or dead, mortal or immortal.

Their assertion does not remove the argument: *fundamentals are not by their
nature matters capable of being disputed, because there has never been a
heretic who called into doubt whether thieves, drunkards, robbers, revilers,
the greedy, and idolaters will obtain the inheritance of eternal life.* The
Antinomians or Libertines recently arisen in England prove this false, for
they contend that Scripture proves that the regenerate cannot sin and that
they can lie, get drunk, rob, shed innocent blood, remain engrafted into
Christ, and be saved. 2. The papists, who are idolaters, earnestly hope that
they will obtain God's kingdom—and that they alone will obtain it. What is as
disputed as the definition of idolatry? Or the definition of theft in the
general subject of usury and restitution? What fundamental article cannot be
disputed with as much appearance of reason as the articles concerning the
perseverance of the saints, original sin, the irresistibility of grace, the
absolute decree of contingent things, and the divine predetermination of the
human will, together with many other things which the Arminians call disputed
and nonessential?

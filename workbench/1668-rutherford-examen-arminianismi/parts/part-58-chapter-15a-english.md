CHAPTER XV

ON

The Church and Its Marks

MATTHEW 18:17

“If he neglects to hear them, tell the church.”

“Tell the church,” that is, tell the bishops and presidents, as Chrysostom
expounds it. Pareus speaks more clearly: “Tell the presbyters, the pastors who
represent the church.” It is not necessary, however, for us to linger here in
refuting the Roman Catholics, who wrench these words to establish their
hierarchy. For Lyra, raving here, says, “Tell the church,” that is, “tell the
prelate by public denunciation.” And Cajetan, in his commentary on this
passage, says, “Neither reason nor practice advises that the church should be
assembled to correct an impenitent brother solely for a sin committed against
me; rather, both reason and the most ancient custom hold that it should be
reported to the head of the church.” These claims are examined and refuted
elsewhere by our theologians. Here our business is with the Remonstrants,

[1668 KBNL/UvA witness | logical printed p. 643 | PDF p. 692 | split word joined: Remonstrantibus]

who assert an exposition of this passage different from both ours and that of
the Roman Catholics.

It is therefore asked whether the Remonstrants rightly define the visible
church in *Apologia*, chapter 22, as “a gathering of believers who profess
saving doctrine.” They say, “Grant that such people do not truly believe;
they are nevertheless the church as far as we are concerned, although not in
God's court.”

Response: Such a gathering cannot be regarded as the church even as far as we
are concerned. It is true that we cannot know *a priori*, certainly and with
the certainty of faith, whether such people truly believe or not, because
whether another person believes savingly is not the formal object of my
faith. But we can know the church *a posteriori*, because we can and must know
with the certainty of faith whether the doctrine which this gathering
professes is saving doctrine. Yet no gathering or multitude can profess sound
doctrine without the true church of the elect and called necessarily being
present there, although very many hypocrites may also be there. But this is
not the true church even as far as we are concerned. For the Remonstrants
define believing as firmly resolving that we can attain
salvation in no other way than the one prescribed by Jesus Christ. Yet
Anabaptists, Roman Catholics, Socinians, Antitrinitarians, Tritheists, Arians,
and others believe this and can believe it.

2. Nor is this the visible church, which, as the most learned Leiden
professors rightly observed, is always defined in Scripture both by its
internal and by its

[1668 KBNL/UvA witness | logical printed p. 644 | PDF p. 693 | split word joined: semper]

external state. The latter includes Christ the head, the whole sacred
ministry, and the means of gathering the church. And such a church is the kind
which, by ministerial power—that is, the power of the keys conferred by
Christ—has authority to remove stumbling blocks. But this gathering is not
such a church.

3. If a gathering that merely professes saving doctrine is such a church,
granting that none in it are elect or effectually called—and they acknowledge
that we ought to join ourselves to every church which is true as far as we are
concerned—it follows that we ought to join ourselves to a church and
gathering of hypocrites. But if such a gathering consists of no elect or
effectually called persons, it is no more a church than a painted man is a
man.

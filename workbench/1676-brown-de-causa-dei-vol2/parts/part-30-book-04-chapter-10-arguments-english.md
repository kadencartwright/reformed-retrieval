# CHAPTER X (continued)

With these points laid down, it is easy to show that the covenant made with
the Israelites was not the covenant of works. Indeed, the arguments by which
we proved above that it was the covenant of grace abundantly confirm this.
Nevertheless, we shall make it plain in a few arguments.

*Argument 1.* Once broken, the covenant of works cannot be restored. But this
covenant, after it was violated by idolatry in Exodus 33:1 and following, was
renewed again in Exodus 34:1–7. Indeed, it was renewed more often after it was
subsequently violated. See Joshua 24:14–17, 21–22, 24–25; Judges 10:16; 1
Samuel 7:3–5; 2 Chronicles 15:12; 2 Kings 23:3; 2 Chronicles 34:31; and
Nehemiah 10:31.

*Argument 2.* The covenant of works is properly a covenant of friendship. But
that covenant which Jehovah made with the Israelites was not a covenant of
friendship, but rather of reconciliation. For it was contracted with humanity
not in integrity, but fallen, corrupted in many ways, and liable to divine
wrath. It was therefore not the covenant of works.

*Argument 3.* Not a few obtained eternal salvation by virtue of that covenant
even though they did not fulfill the condition stipulated in the covenant of
works—that is, perfect and altogether complete obedience. For this was
impossible for a sinner to do, and all the Israelites were sinners. It was
therefore not the covenant of works, which never conferred salvation upon
anyone and could not confer it after the fall.

*Argument 4.* If that which was made with the Israelites was the law of works,
or the covenant of works, then righteousness would have come by the law and a
law would have been given which could impart life. But the Apostle judges both
of these conclusions absurd in Galatians 3:21.

*Argument 5.* If that covenant was the covenant of works, it would be contrary
to God's promises. For by *God's promises* is meant the covenant of grace,
which is also called *the promise* in Galatians 3:17 and consists wholly of
promises, or is, as it were, one single promise. And it is plain that the
covenant of grace and the covenant of works are contrary to each other. The
Apostle, however, rejects this with abhorrence in Galatians 3:21: *Is the law
therefore added contrary to the promises of God? By no means.*

*Argument 6.* If that covenant made with the Israelites had been the covenant
of works, the law would have rendered useless and even abolished the compact
previously ratified by God with reference to Christ, as well as the promise.
For if the inheritance had come by the law, it would not have come by the
promise (Galatians 3:18). The Apostle openly denies this in Galatians 3:17.
Thus his entire argument in the passage just cited is this: *The compact
previously ratified by God with reference to Christ is not rendered useless
by the law, which appeared four hundred and thirty years later, so as to
abolish the promise. For if the inheritance is by the law, it is no longer by
the promise.*

*Argument 7.* If it had been the covenant of works, it would not have been a
tutor unto Christ,

[Printed p. 114 | IA PDF 122 | IA leaf 122 | IA decausadei02brow]

that they might be justified by faith, for the covenant of works had nothing
in common with Christ. Those who are justified through the covenant of works
cannot be justified by faith, since justification by works and justification
by faith are opposed to each other, as the Apostle often shows. See Galatians
3:12 and Romans 3:21. But according to the Apostle in Galatians 3:24, the law
was a tutor leading them to Christ so that they might be justified by faith.
Therefore, and so forth.

*Argument 8.* If it was the covenant of works, the Jews would not have been
kept under the law's custody and confined until that faith which was to be
revealed, as the Apostle says in Galatians 3:23. The reason is that the law,
insofar as it is the covenant of works, directs no one to Christ, who can here
be understood by *faith*, just as elsewhere he is called *our hope* (1 Timothy
1:1) because he is the special object of faith. And *the law is not of faith*
(Galatians 3:12), because it does not require faith in Christ the mediator as
the condition for acquiring righteousness and life.

*Argument 9.* Furthermore, if it had been the covenant of works, Paul would
not have said that Israel, in pursuing the law of righteousness, did not
attain to the law of righteousness because they sought it not by faith but as
though by works of the law. For by reason of the covenant of works, they would
have been bound to pursue righteousness not by faith—which, as we have just
shown, the covenant of works or law does not require—but by works of the law.
Yet the Apostle openly declares this in Romans 9:31–32. They would not have
been guilty of any offense in seeking righteousness by works of the law if
that covenant had been the covenant of works. Nevertheless, the Apostle
accuses them for this very reason in Romans 9:31.

*Argument 10.* If so, the Apostle would not have blamed them for trying to
establish their own righteousness in ignorance of God's righteousness, as he
does in Romans 10:3. For by reason of the covenant of works they would not
have been bound to acknowledge another righteousness of God or to submit to
God's righteousness—that is, as the same Apostle explains in Philippians 3:9,
to that righteousness which is through faith in Christ, the righteousness
which is from God through faith. Rather, they would have been strictly bound
to seek their own righteousness, which is from the law.

*Argument 11.* The purpose of the covenant of works was to continue and
establish Adam in the happiness which he enjoyed. But the purpose of that
covenant made with the Israelites was the restoration of humanity to its lost
state, so that they might become God's peculiar treasure and a priestly
kingdom to God. It was therefore not the covenant of works.

*Argument 12.* In the covenant of works, God's justice held first place, and
God's mercy forgiving sins had no place. But in this covenant made with the
Israelites, God's mercy forgiving sins had a place: *He is gracious to whom he
wills and has mercy upon whom he wills to have mercy* (Exodus 33:19). *He
keeps kindness for thousands and forgives iniquity, transgression, and sin*
(Exodus 34:7).

*Argument 13.* Not all the Israelites were under the curse. But if that
covenant had been only the covenant of works, all would have been under the
curse. For according to the Apostle in Galatians 3:10, *As many as are of the
works of the law are under the curse, for it is written: Cursed is everyone
who does not continue in all things written in the book of the law, to do
them.*

*Argument 14.* Some were justified of old under the covenant made with the
Israelites. But the Apostle says in Galatians 3:11 that it is plain that no one
is justified before God through the law, or the legal covenant. That covenant
was therefore not the covenant of works.

*Argument 15.* The covenant of works had no promises of grace or promises
corresponding to the commands. But this covenant had such promises, as may be
seen in Deuteronomy 30:6 and Psalm 84:11.

*Argument 16.* The condition of the covenant of works is now altogether
impossible for fallen humanity (Romans 3:10; 8:3). But according to Moses in
Deuteronomy 30:12–14, the condition of that covenant made with the Israelites
was not altogether impossible. The word was near, in the mouth and in the
heart. It was therefore not the covenant of works.

*Argument 17.* The condition of this covenant was opposed to the condition of
the covenant of works. For the condition of the covenant of works was, *Do
this and you will live*, or, *The person who performs these things will live
by them* (Romans 10:5). But the condition of the Israelite covenant was faith.
Paul testifies in Romans 10:6–9 about *the righteousness which is by faith*:
*It says, Do not say in your heart, Who will ascend into heaven?—that is, to
bring Christ down from above—but what does it say? The word is near you, in
your mouth and in your heart; that is, the word of faith which we preach*, and
so forth. He cites these words from Deuteronomy 30:12–14. It was therefore not
the covenant of works.

*Argument 18.* In this covenant the required condition itself is promised, as
is clear from the passages just cited. But no such promise exists in the
covenant of works. It was therefore not the covenant of works.

*Argument 19.* That whose end is Christ for righteousness to everyone who
believes is not the covenant of works. But the end of the covenant made with
the Israelites was Christ for righteousness to everyone who believes.
Therefore, and so forth. The major premise is certain. The minor is Paul's in
Romans 10:4.

*Argument 20.* Those to whom adoption, glory, covenants, and promises belonged
were not under the covenant of works. But according to the Apostle in Romans
9:4, all these things belonged to the Israelites. Therefore they were not
under the covenant of works. Consequently, that covenant was not the covenant
of works. The inference of the major premise needs no proof.

*Argument 21.* The covenant of works is common to the whole human race, for it
was made with Adam as the head and common parent of all. But this covenant
made with the Israelites was not common to all, but was a special privilege
founded upon special love (Romans 9:4; Deuteronomy 5, 6, 7, 8).

*Argument 22.* The covenant of works, or of nature, is eternal and even now
written in substance upon human hearts, so that it cannot be erased. For even
the *Gentiles show the work of the law written upon their hearts, their
conscience bearing witness together with it and their thoughts accusing or
even defending one another* (Romans 2:15). They retain an awareness of rewards
and punishments, which sufficiently proves that the law of nature has not yet
been erased, even insofar as it expressed the covenant. But this covenant made
with the Israelites has now become old, insofar as it was the Old Testament,
and is now removed as useless (2 Corinthians 4:11; Hebrews 8:6–8; compare
9:1 with 9:15).

*Argument 23.* The covenant of works rests upon the benefits of creation and
the general preservation of humanity. But this covenant made with the
Israelites had a very different foundation: namely, God's peculiar election,
by which he took them as his peculiar treasure and preferred them to all other
nations.

*Argument 24.* [The covenant of nature, says Apollonius in the work cited
above, p. 170, presents God as, by his sovereign authority and from wise and
just counsel, requiring perfect obedience from humanity for justification by
works and, through the ministry of death, denouncing condemnation upon those
who transgress the covenant. But the Mosaic covenant contains a certain
preliminary view

[Printed p. 116 | IA PDF 124 | IA leaf 124 | IA decausadei02brow]

of the gospel, so that after a person has learned from the teaching of the
law, in examining himself, the law's powerlessness for salvation, and has thus
been driven to deny himself and his own righteousness, it may present God as
not willing that the sinner who turns from sin should die, and as inviting
all who labor and are burdened to his mercy and goodness, so as to ward off
despair just as the law wards off false security. Without this preliminary
view of the gospel, nothing connected with the eternal salvation of sinful
humanity can be accomplished through the legal theology of the covenant of
nature.]

*Argument 25.* The same thing is proved by all those passages of Scripture
which assert that Christ is the sole mediator, such as Acts 4:12: *There is
salvation in no one else, for there is no other name under heaven given among
people by which we must be saved.* This was no less true formerly under the
law than it is now under the gospel. And John 14:6 says, *No one comes to the
Father except through me.* He alone was the Lamb slain from the laying of the
world's foundations (Revelation 13:8).

*Argument 26.* Since it is certain that fallen humanity did not have the power
to fulfill the condition of the covenant of works, we must not say that God
ever willed to make such a covenant with fallen humanity, much less with the
Israelites, toward whom he professed to bear the greatest love. For this would
be to mock them.

*Argument 27.* When the Apostle says in Galatians 3:16 that the promises were
spoken to Abraham and to his seed, not *to seeds*, as of many, and so forth,
he plainly shows that all Abraham's seed were justified in one and the same
way: namely, in the same way in which Abraham himself had been justified
before God. But if that covenant made with the Israelites had been the
covenant of works, they would have been justified differently from Abraham
himself and differently from the way believers are justified today under the
New Testament. Consequently, Abraham's Israelite and evangelical seed—or his
seed among Israelites and Gentiles—would be justified in different ways. He
would therefore have said *and to seeds*, as of many, not *and to your seed*,
as of one, *who is Christ*. See Beza on the passage.

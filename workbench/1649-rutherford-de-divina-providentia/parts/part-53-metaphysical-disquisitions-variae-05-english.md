[Various Questions on Adam’s fall; printed page 597; IA PDF page 671; BSB canvas
647]

Question. Did Adam sin freely if some influx of God was withheld without which
it was impossible for him not to fall necessarily? I answer affirmatively. For
a free act is not free in relation to GOD’s predetermining influx; it is free
in this respect: by an elective power, following a prior judgment, the agent
could break forth into that act which, once such influx was withheld, was
necessarily evil and forbidden by GOD’s law. Indeed, interpretatively Adam
also willed the absence of that predetermining influx which was necessary for
the simple being of Adam’s obedience. Those who uphold the contrary position
deny that God decreed all free acts from eternity and deny GOD’s providence;
with the Socinians and Arminians they conspire against GOD’s cause.

[Marginalia: Adam sinned freely even though GOD’s influx was withheld, without
which he could not avoid falling.]

Question 27. Was the new withholding a punishment of sin which preceded the
sin? I answer negatively. It was neither the evil of fault nor a punishment,
but the free denial of an influx which was not owed. *Who first gave to God,
and it will be repaid to him?*

Question 28. How, then, did Adam possess the power to will to stand, if he had
willed it? I answer: in the fullest sense, because he retained his power in
relation to his own free act. He must not be said to have retained GOD’s
power, as the Arminians jest, because he never possessed it. For in that case
Adam would have been the absolute and independent lord of the divine
concurrences, decrees, and dispensations—a proud position into which, by GOD’s
grace, I hope never to enter. All the adversaries’ arguments would also prove
that God did not possess freedom to furnish or not furnish his concurrence. I
would rather stand by God than by men.

[Question 28 ends within printed p. 597. Question 29, “If a future contingent
event occurs necessarily,” is excluded.]

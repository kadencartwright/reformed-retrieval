# On Certain Somewhat Unleavened Points of Doctrine

This directory presents the complete extant Latin-form text and a new English translation of the undated consilium printed by the *Calvini Opera* editors as *Ad quaedam doctrinae capita aliquatenus infermentata*.

## Work identity

| Field | Value |
|---|---|
| Author | John Calvin (collection attribution) |
| CO consilium | I.4, *Ad quaedam doctrinae capita aliquatenus infermentata* |
| Date | Undated |
| Language of the work | French, not extant in the witness used by CO |
| Language presented here | Beza's Latin translation from French |
| Genre | Pastoral and doctrinal advice |
| Subject | Exile, compelled subscription, baptism, the Lord's Supper, Christ's human nature, and “Sacramentarians” |
| CO locator | X/1, pp. 158–160 |

The Latin heading belongs to the collected presentation rather than to a recoverable French archetype. Its rare adjective *infermentata* literally means “unleavened”; the English display title deliberately retains that metaphor instead of presenting a conjectural paraphrase as certain.

## Edition and transmission

The transcription follows Wilhelm Baum, Eduard Cunitz, and Eduard Reuss, eds., *Ioannis Calvini opera quae supersunt omnia*, vol. X/1 (*Corpus Reformatorum* XXXVIII) (Brunswick: C. A. Schwetschke, 1871), pp. 158–160.

CO locates the text in the Geneva, Lausanne, Hanover, Chouet, and Amsterdam editions of the collected *Epistolae et responsa*. Its only source note says that Beza attests he translated the piece from French. No French manuscript, addressee, place, date, or subscription is supplied here. The Latin is consequently a historical translation witness, not Calvin's recoverable original-language wording.

The public facsimile is supplied by the University of Geneva's [complete *Calvini Opera* collection](https://archive-ouverte.unige.ch/unige:650) and its [direct CO X/1 PDF](https://access.archive-ouverte.unige.ch/access/metadata/f526a338-c95c-4f87-b600-04df723f502b/download). The text appears on PDF pp. 100–101 of the captured surrogate. That file was 33,386,580 bytes with SHA-256 `025973d6e4533769d0c2b870dd324497943bc14050eb2bcbcc4c964d4f2a1777`.

## Scope and collation

`latin.txt` includes every word from *Dilectissimi fratres* on p. 158 through *usum corrumpunt* on p. 160. It excludes the CO heading and source note, running heads, page numbers, and the adjacent consilia sharing the facsimile spreads.

The Latin was checked line by line against 300-DPI renderings of both spreads. Printer line-end divisions are joined, and CO spelling and punctuation are otherwise retained. The p. 159 marker precedes *quid*, the first word printed on that page. The p. 159/160 turn divides printed *Con-fitemur*; following repository policy, the p. 160 marker precedes the complete normalized word *Confitemur*, and the English marker likewise precedes “We confess.” No body text is illegible or conjecturally supplied.

## Translation status

The item was translated in Mary D. Beaty and Benjamin Wirt Farley's copyrighted *Calvin's Ecclesiastical Advice* (1991); that translation supplied no wording here. No complete public-domain English version was located in the audit. The English is an **AI-assisted scholarly draft**, translated afresh from the normalized Latin and checked against the full facsimile. Because the Latin itself is Beza's translation, this English is necessarily a translation through an intermediate language. It has not received independent review by a human Latinist; cite the Latin and CO page for exact-wording claims.

Suggested citation:

> John Calvin, *Ad quaedam doctrinae capita aliquatenus infermentata*, undated, French work surviving here in Theodore Beza's Latin translation, *Calvini Opera* X/1 (1871), 158–160, normalized Latin and draft English translation, *Reformed Retrieval*.

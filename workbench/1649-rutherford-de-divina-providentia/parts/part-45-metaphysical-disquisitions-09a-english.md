### QUESTION IX

Whether GOD’s free good pleasure is the cause of essences, degrees of essence,
and specified forms in natural, moral, supernatural, and artificial things.

Since it is known that for many years, from my youth, I have inclined toward the
affirmative side of the question, it must be investigated here whether I can
make the opinion of pious and learned men my own (I do not mean the Jesuits).
For I judge with charity that pious men err only with the greatest reluctance.
I premise the following.

1. Aristotle, *Metaphysics*, book 8, chapter 6: ᾗ τὸ δυνάμει ᾗ τὸ ἐνεργείᾳ ἓν
πῶς, “in some respect what exists potentially and what exists actually are one.”
The saying that *act and potency do not make a plurality* is attributed to
Averroes. The philosophy of the royal Prophet is the same in Psalm 139:
לֹא נִכְחַד עָצְמִי מִמֶּךָ אֲשֶׁר עֻשֵּׂיתִי בַסֵּתֶר רֻקַּמְתִּי בְּתַחְתִּיּוֹת אָרֶץ,
“My substance was not hidden from you, when I was made in secret and embroidered
in the depths of the earth.” The Septuagint has οὐκ ἐκρύβη τὸ ὀστοῦν, and so
forth, “My bone was not hidden.” The Chaldean paraphrase has לָא אִתְכַּסִּי
גַרְמִי מִנָּךְ דִּי אִתְעֲבֵיד בְּסִתְרָא אִצְטַיַּרִית בְּכַרְסָא דְאִמִּי׃
*My substance is not
hidden from you, because I was made in secret and formed in my mother’s womb.*
Verse 16: *Your eyes saw my body, and in your book of remembrance all my days
were written,* and so forth. The Syriac version: *Nor are my bones hidden from
you, and my descent into the lowest parts of the earth.* The Arabic version:
*My bone, which you created in secret, is not hidden from you, and my person was
in the deep places of the earth.* Hence, according to all these versions, it
was somehow (πῶς) the same bone and the same body of David potentially and
actually: numerically the same David whom GOD saw when he was not yet born and
did not yet have existence, and David once born. It was the same person of David
to be created potentially and actually created, as the Arabs rightly intimate.

[Marginal summary: According to Scripture, what exists potentially and what
exists actually are in some respect the same.]

1. Therefore every created obedience—not stealing, doing good, believing,
praying—and likewise heaven, sun, human being, and tree, exist first in GOD’s
active power before they are actually created from nothing.

2. The first efficient cause is actually all that is potentially. For every
possible thing is prior to that which exists actually, because it can exist by
another’s power; but God does not beg another’s power, and therefore God is not
first able to exist before he actually exists.

[Marginal thesis: Act is prior to potency.]

[Metaphysical Disquisitions, Question IX, Part A; printed page 564; IA PDF page 638; BSB canvas 614]

Every generated thing and every created thing exists in potency before it is
actually generated or created. But nothing begets or creates unless it simply
exists actually. Therefore act is prior to potency. Yet among created things,
possible things and actual nonbeings are prior to actually created beings.
Nonbeings that were not created existed from eternity and, as it were, were
coeternal with God; but God, who is being simply and in whose power all beings
are possible—that is, are actually nonbeings—is simply prior to those nonbeings.
For nonbeings are possible nothings in the First Being, from whose omnipotence,
as it were, all possibilities spring forth. Just as they were confined in the
darkness of nonbeings through a positive decree that they were not yet to be
created, so, posterior to God, at the beginning of time they germinated into
entitative acts of this and that kind. Genesis 1:2. GOD’s positive will is
therefore prior to all created beings and nonbeings, just as a potter who has
decreed both to shape these vessels at this hour and positively not to shape
them before that hour is prior to all those works of clay.

3. Aristotle rightly says that what exists potentially is one with what exists
actually, not simply but πῶς, in a qualified respect. For to feign numerical
entity in a being that exists potentially together with an actually existing
being is to attribute unity and diversity to nonbeing, which is absurd.

Hence one consideration applies to the human being who is materially and
potentially a human being—or to a human being taken incomplexly and to be
identified with all the degrees of essence: animal, living thing, body,
substance, and being. A very different consideration applies to a human being
formally constituted in the formal being of a human being, insofar as a human
being is logically resolved into subject and attributes by saying (*A human
being is an animal*) and (*A human being is a living thing, substance, and
body*). Or a human being—David—is considered as one to be freely fashioned and
joined together in his mother’s womb by GOD’s free efficiency before he existed
or had a body, as just stated from Psalm 139:15–16; or as David now living and a
rational animal and composing that divine Psalm. Or thus, the human being David
is referred either to the efficient cause, GOD, before he comes to be, or to his
physical or metaphysical parts, that is, to his internal causes. So a chair not
yet formed is referred to the artisan freely producing it before it is made, or
it is referred to the stone and wooden parts of which it consists. The same
distinction holds in natural, moral, and artificial being. For a rational
creature’s love toward the First Lovable is a created being, and is therefore
referred to God, who fashioned this act of such praiseworthy obedience, and to
the causes

[Marginal distinction: A materially creatable thing and a created thing are
distinguished.]

[Metaphysical Disquisitions, Question IX, Part A; printed page 565; IA PDF page 639; BSB canvas 615]

that internally and formally constitute that obedience. If advocates of the
contrary opinion admit that being in potency and being in act are given, they
must acknowledge this distinction. Hence these two statements are true:

1. *Through free creative efficiency GOD made that creature, the human being,
who would be a rational animal.*

2. *And on the supposition that GOD had now decreed to create a human being who
is actually and formally a human being, he could not fail to create a rational
animal.*

Hence the following are also true. 1. *A human being, related to GOD as the
efficient cause creating most freely, becomes a human being most freely, and by
the same free act becomes and is created most freely as a living animal and a
body. But a human being, related to his internal metaphysical parts—namely genus
and difference—does not become, nor is he freely, a rational animal, but is a
rational animal by necessity of nature and essentially. For it is repugnant for
a human being to be and not to be a rational animal. Nor can GOD create a human
being and not create a rational animal, because in creating the same thing he
cannot fail to create the same thing. A rational animal is the same as a human
being. But no reason appears to me why this distinction should not hold in every
created being and consequently in created moral rectitude.*

[Marginal thesis: GOD freely made that creature materially considered, a
rational animal.]

2. *Or thus: that creature which is only in potency—or rather will materially
be a human being—is a rational animal freely, not by necessity of nature, in
relation to the First Efficient Cause.* I say the same of every created moral
rectitude, for rectitude is either created or uncreated, or perhaps mixed, as
has been said. The same must be said of artificial things.

3. Or thus: *The creature that is actually and formally a human being is a
rational animal by necessity of nature—namely, of the internal parts considered
physically or metaphysically—not freely. Thus Abraham’s act of slaying his son,
which materially is only the killing of a child, in relation to GOD commanding
it is not necessarily, but freely, a forbidden killing of a child.* See Genesis
22:1. And the act of slaying children which GOD forbids, Jeremiah 7:30–31 and
Psalm 106:37–38, *in respect of its internal causes is an impious and horrible
killing of children, and that by necessity of nature.*

We must demonstrate the truth of the first statement.

I.

GOD is free to create a human being, for from the free abundance of his goodness
he made all things. Therefore he could have refrained from creating. And if
GOD’s free exercise in the creation of a being must not be denied, neither must

[Metaphysical Disquisitions, Question IX, Part A; printed page 566; IA PDF page 640; BSB canvas 616]

it be denied in the creation of a being with such and so great a degree of
essential perfection. For if we speak of a human being materially and
potentially, as a physical being, God did not by necessity of nature fit an
elementary body and a rational soul to him. Did he fashion such a body in order
to teach humility? Yet he would equally have taught spiritual humility and
gratitude if he had bestowed a supra-elementary body, of the fifth essence (as
they call it), together with a spirit more excellent than that in the angel
Gabriel. For 1 Corinthians 15:38 says: *GOD gave a body as he willed*—what
necessity of nature is there here?—*& to every seed its own body.* Likewise,
*one kind of flesh belongs to human beings, another to beasts*; *one glory
belongs to celestial bodies, another to terrestrial bodies, another to the sun,
and another glory to the moon.* Verse 39. 4. No necessity, but overflowing
goodness and freedom, moves the Creator to this variety. Since the genus and
specific difference of a human being, which consist of metaphysical parts, are
attributed to the same freedom of GOD and not to necessity of nature, no less
than the essential physical parts are, this must hold in all created things and
acts of free obedience. I admit that a human being consisting of another
supra-elementary matter and physical form would differ in species from a human
being created in this order of things. But this favors us, because we are now
speaking of a human being who has such an essence materially and potentially,
not actually.

[Marginal thesis: GOD gives the physical parts and a body to every seed as he
pleases; therefore, according to his free will, he also grants such a nature and
essence to a human being and to all things in potency.]

2. I argue from the manner of creation. The creation of Adam is essentially a
free action of GOD, who out of his lavish goodness toward creatures gave a
gratuitous gift which he could have withheld. By one free act God created the
whole being and all of the being; indeed, by the same free act he created a
human being, animal, living thing, body, substance, and being. He did not make a
human being by one free creative action, an animal by another, a body by another,
and a living thing by another, so that acts of creation would have to be
multiplied according to the multiplication of essential attributes. Rather, by
a free act, not by necessity of nature, God created a human being, animal,
living thing, and body; and consequently by free efficiency he joined all those
essential degrees in the creature who is potentially and materially a human
being.

[Marginal thesis: By the same free creative action of GOD, GOD created Adam—a
human being, an animal, and so forth.]

3. I argue from the freedom of an earthly potter. Whatever freedom is granted
to an earthly craftsman must be attributed in a far greater degree as
αὐτεξουσία, self-determining authority, to the Creator, the supreme Craftsman of
things who is to be praised forever. Jeremiah 18:6; Romans 9:20–21. But free
exercise intervenes in the earthly craftsman, and freedom reigns in forming the
essences of artificial things—in their matter and form,

[Metaphysical Disquisitions, Question IX, Part A; printed page 567; IA PDF page 641; BSB canvas 617]

and, I add, in their height, shape, beauty, and ugliness, as the Holy Spirit
speaks in Romans 9. Therefore God will enjoy greater freedom in fashioning and
making the thing which will be a human being, so that “rational animal” is its
essence, and its height and stature (so to speak) are metaphysical and
essential.
If a craftsman has decreed to fashion a marble statue, he cannot fashion a
wooden one without retracting his plan, for in the artisan’s mind being a marble
statue has its formal being or nature according to his idea. Yet he could simply
have fashioned the potential statue (grant the expression), which is materially
a statue, as marble, wooden, iron, or silver. Thus, on the supposition that God
determined to create Solomon adorned with so many ornaments and virtues and
eminent in illustrious gifts of mind, the Strength of Israel, in whom there is
no repentance, cannot fail to create Solomon in that way. But God freely created
the creature who would be Solomon in that way—not as a stone, an ass, or the
lowest man among the common people. Oviedo perhaps saw this: *A human being* (he
says) *is rational from himself and through his essential predicates. GOD is the
efficient cause of an existing human being who is a rational animal; but he
cannot be the cause of an existing human being who is a horse.*

[Marginal thesis: The supreme freedom of the supreme Craftsman is proved when
he freely creates a thing consisting of any essence whatsoever.]

[Marginal reference: Oviedo, *Metaphysical Course*, controversy 10, point 5,
section 1, no. 11.]

4. I argue from GOD’s liberal goodness. It must be ascribed to GOD’s grace and
his free goodness and generosity—not to matter or the necessity of nature—that
God made human beings as rational creatures to glorify God in a far more
excellent manner than worms do. By natural instinct human beings recognize that
GOD freely made them rational beings, not fungi, stones, or senseless animals,
while nevertheless all things were created for GOD as the final end and by him
as a free efficient cause. Revelation 4:11; Romans 1:36–37; Proverbs 16:14.
Therefore, just as God freely determined the end and its measure and perfection,
so he must freely determine the means and their measure and perfection.

[Marginal thesis: It must be ascribed to GOD’s free grace, not to the necessity
of nature or matter, that GOD made creatures to glorify him in this and no other
manner.]

5. Let us see how beings outside GOD act upon him, the most glorious agent.
Nothing possible or impossible necessitates him or constrains him to form such a
work; indeed, nothing outside GOD restricts his wisdom or infinite freedom. Let
us posit that the human being was created by him who made all things good
(וְהִנֵּה טוֹב מְאֹד, “and behold, it was very good,” Genesis 1:31). With regard
to the degrees of essential perfection, either (4) God could have made him with
a variation of species as (5) or (3), or something prevented it. If he could,
the thesis is established. If something prevented it, then the nature of the
human being outside God prevented it; but thus it would have been impossible

[Marginal thesis: A thing of such an essence does not necessitate God to create
it.]

[Metaphysical Disquisitions, Question IX, Part A; printed page 568; IA PDF page 642; BSB canvas 618]

for God to have created another world—either more perfect or less perfect than
the present one—because the world outside himself, and therefore the number of
degrees of essential perfection and consequently the determinate number of
species that now obtains in this order of things, neither greater nor smaller,
would have necessitated GOD to create only these things: no more, no more
excellent, no fewer, and no less excellent than those actually created. But
then the goodness, order, multitude, and essential perfection of things would
exist antecedently to GOD’s free will, which is absurd. For these beings, which
are supposed to necessitate and constrain GOD to create as things prior to him,
are either uncreated or created. If uncreated, they are God, which is blasphemous;
if created, then they are beings by participation and therefore created most
freely. Consequently such effects, since they are created beings, could not be
the cause why God freely made them, for then they would act as causes and
exercise acts of causation upon the free will of the First Cause.

Furthermore, things do not pass from the state of possibility to the state of
futurition by necessity of nature; therefore, by GOD’s will they pass to a state
of futurition with such a degree of perfection that this possible creature is
the sun, that one a worm, this one a human being, that one an angel, this one an
ass, and that one a fish. Likewise, the reason why these creatures excel in
these gifts and those creatures in other virtues cannot be ascribed to created
things or to the necessity of matter. For Papists and Arminians impiously
ascribe Adam’s concupiscence to the necessity of nature, whereby God is made the
author of sin. If the workmanship is evil by necessity of nature, fault is
imputed to the supreme Craftsman who created nature from nothing, while the
obedience opposed to concupiscence would have to be ascribed to necessity of
nature rather than to the Creator’s gratuitous gift; and this too is absurd.

[Marginal clarification: I call it a “passage” improperly, because it is not a
movement or real migration of real things. I mean this: for a thing to be
possible from eternity—namely, from omnipotence—is one thing; for it to be
future—namely, from GOD’s free will—is another.]

6. I argue from the ideas. Because he so willed, God formed within himself, in
his essence representable in infinitely possible creatures, the ideas of all
created things. He does not beg the ideas and practical images and forms of
things from the things themselves. Rather, created things exist because such
ideas first existed in GOD; the ideas are not such because things outside God
first existed in that way, for then things would first be possible and future
outside GOD and antecedently to God. Instead, things outside God are such in
their degrees of essential goodness, natures, perfections, virtues, gifts, and
ornaments as God freely formed their ideas. For the opponent Fonseca admits that
*all things come to be according to the ideas in GOD’s mind and that the being
of things is also virtually*

[Marginal inference: The ideas of things were formed in GOD’s mind according to
his free good pleasure; therefore, the essences of things were also so formed.]

[Metaphysical Disquisitions, Question IX, Part A; printed page 569; IA PDF page 643; BSB canvas 619]

*in the First Efficient Cause* (he says), *who exemplifies or prescribes to each
thing its proper nature and definition.* And that the ideas are formed according
to the prescription of free good pleasure is evident. 1. The ideas are not formed
from things outside God, as has been said, because the ideas are eternal and
prior to things existing either potentially or actually. Nor are the ideas
formed from GOD’s nature or according to GOD’s nature antecedently to GOD’s free
good pleasure. For since created things must be conformed to their ideas, if the
ideas were conformed to GOD’s nature, all things would thus be made by necessity
of GOD’s nature, not according to God’s free prescription, but in the likeness
of the divine nature naturally pouring forth the eternal ideas of things and
antecedently to God’s free prescription. In that case, all the essences of
things and all degrees of essential perfections would have welled forth from
GOD’s essence by natural emanation, not by free creative efficiency. But then
the essences of things could not have failed to be coeternal with GOD, for there
is no reason why they should have begun to exist at this point in the beginning
of things rather than at another. Just as, if the sun had been eternal, its
illumination would have been coeternal, so on this account the eternity of the
world must be asserted.

Nor is it necessary that we demonstrate the truth of the second statement. For
by the same decree by which God determined to create a formally constituted
human being, he also determined to create a rational animal. Yet God did not
properly decree that a human being be an animal, because the object of the
divine decree is that which is of itself future or nonfuture, or that which is
not so necessarily future that its not coming to be is repugnant. Thus he
decreed tomorrow’s illumination by the sun and combustion effected by fire, and
by decree established the successions of summer and autumn. These are not so
necessary that they are not, as it were, contingent to God and able not to be.
But God cannot decree that a human being be an animal, because it implies a
contradiction for a human being not to be an animal.

[Marginal thesis: Things that are necessary and belong to an essential
connection—such as a human being’s being an animal—do not fall under GOD’s
decree.]

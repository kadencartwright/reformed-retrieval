It is asked whether the Covenant of Grace was made with each and every mortal,
even with those who have heard nothing about Christ. The Remonstrants say so.
We deny it. For we teach that the doctrine concerning the Covenant of Grace is
set before everyone in the Visible Church, but that with respect to God's
counsel and eternal Election, the Covenant of Grace was made with the elect
alone. This is evident:

1. From God's own practice. He entered this covenant only with Noah and his
descendants, with Abraham and his descendants, and with the Jewish people and
not with others. God himself testifies that in this the people

[1668 KBNL/UvA witness | logical printed p. 433 | PDF p. 484]

of the Jews were distinguished from all other nations, Deuteronomy 7:7 and
Psalm 147:19–20. Therefore, the Apostles were forbidden to preach the gospel
to the Gentiles, Matthew 10:5–6, and they were forbidden to evangelize
Bithynia. This ought not to have been done if the Gospel Covenant had been
made with everyone.

2. This is clear from the words of the Covenant, Jeremiah 31:31: “Behold, days
are coming, says Jehovah, when I will enter a Covenant with the house of
Israel and with the house of Judah.”

3. The promises of the Covenant are not universal. Therefore, neither is the
Covenant itself. The antecedent is proved: God promises only certain people,
and that without regard to their obedience or disobedience, that solely for
his own name's sake he will remove the stony heart and put in a new heart and
a new spirit, Ezekiel 36:22, 26, 32. Therefore, not all are children of the
promise; only the elect are reckoned in the seed, Romans 9:8.

4. Some are said to be “without Christ, alienated from the commonwealth of
Israel, and strangers as regards the promise of the covenants.” And as a
special and singular grace, God enters this covenant with some, not others,
Genesis 17:7 and Ezekiel 168: “Indeed, I swore and entered a Covenant with you,
says Jehovah, and you became my people,” Zechariah 13:9; 1 Corinthians 6:16;
Hebrews 10:11.

5. Those with whom this New Covenant of Grace was entered will never depart
from God, Jeremiah 32:40–41. God will never reject them

[1668 KBNL/UvA witness | logical printed p. 434 | PDF p. 485]

because of all that they have done. Rather, they will remain in the Covenant
as immovably as God's natural covenant with the sun and moon, with day and
night, Jeremiah 31:35, 37. They will no more fall from this Covenant than the
world will be overwhelmed by the waters of Noah, which God swore would never
happen, Isaiah 54:9–10. Nor will covenantal mercy be taken away from them any
more than God can profane his Covenant, alter the declaration of his lips,
retract what he swore by his holiness that he would do, and thus lie, Psalm
89:34–36. Therefore, they will never perish. But among mortals there are very
many who depart from God, whom God rejects and abandons, who fall from the
Covenant externally set before them and perish eternally. Therefore, this
Covenant was not entered with all mortals.

6. The distinction could not stand between the Covenant of Grace, which is
eternal and inviolable, if this Covenant were entered with the many who break
it, and the Covenant of Works entered with Adam—or this Covenant insofar as it
is set forth legally. Yet that distinction necessarily remains, as the Prophet
says in Jeremiah 31:31–33.

7. It is foreign to God's wisdom that God should enter the Covenant with
those Nations and present Christ, the Covenant's surety, as having died for
them, when they nevertheless have heard nothing, even by report, about that
Covenant, the Covenant's promise, or the grace and

[1668 KBNL/UvA witness | logical printed p. 435 | PDF p. 486]

mercy of the Surety. Indeed, it is foreign to God's wisdom that God should not
only hinder them by his providence but expressly prohibit the promulgation of
the Covenant, Acts 16:7 and Matthew 10:4–5, and yet regard as violators of the
Covenant of Grace and despisers of Christ's death, blood, and grace those
whose ears have not taken in even a sound or syllable concerning God's
gracious Covenant.

8. It would be necessary for all infants who died before the use of reason to
be saved eternally, whether they were born to believers or to those outside
the Church, to Pagans or Atheists, because they never transgressed this
Covenant of Grace which the Remonstrants will have entered with them. They
also utterly deny that these infants are guilty of violating the Covenant of
Works or of any sin, and therefore they do not need reconciliation in Christ.
Thus all of them without exception must be saved. But Scripture teaches that
God retains his freedom of Election and Reprobation before infants have done
anything good or evil, Romans 9:11.

It is asked whether grace determining the will to good and supernatural acts
is a thing promised by God in the New Covenant. The Remonstrants deny it. We
affirm it.

But it must be known that predetermining grace is twofold. One kind
predetermines the will as to specification, namely, to actions that are good
and supernatural in kind.

[1668 KBNL/UvA witness | logical printed p. 436 | PDF p. 487]

This predetermination flows from the infused habit of supernatural grace,
such as believing, hoping, and repenting. The other is grace predetermining as
to exercise, when the will is predetermined to this or that particular act.
Hence our position is established by these assertions:

Assertion 1. Predetermining grace as to specification is promised in general
to all who are truly covenanted, namely, a circumcised heart, a new spirit,
and a new heart, Deuteronomy 30:6 and Ezekiel 36:26–27.

Assertion 2. Predetermining grace as to specification is not promised to the
covenanted in every particular act. For then the covenanted could not sin,
which is contrary to Scripture and experience.

Assertion 3. Predetermining grace as to specification is promised to all the
covenanted in certain acts: namely, the grace by which it comes about, and has
come about, that the truly regenerate cannot fall into the sin against the
Holy Spirit or into final Apostasy; nor can they commit some great sin with
the whole exertion and impulse of their will, because “the seed of God remains
in them,” 1 John 3:9; “they have God's anointing remaining in them,” 1 John
2:29; and the fear of Jehovah is promised to them so that they may never
depart from him, Jeremiah 32:40.

Assertion 4. But against the Socinians, Arminians, and Papists, we maintain
that in this New Covenant such grace is promised that from a

[1668 KBNL/UvA witness | logical printed p. 437 | PDF p. 488]

supernatural habit they cannot but love God in most of their acts, believe in
Christ, subdue the flesh and the affections, repent, and persevere in the state
of grace.

1. In this covenant grace is promised by which we may never depart from God,
Jeremiah 32,43, and by which the Spirit will never depart from the covenanted
and their seed, or from their seed's seed forever, Isaiah 59.20:21.

2. Circumcision of the heart is promised, “to love God with the whole heart
and the whole soul,” Deuteronomy 30:6; the inscription of the Law on the
heart, Jeremiah 31:33; and God's Spirit, by whom God causes us to walk in his
commandments, Ezekiel 36:27.

3. Unless such predetermining grace were promised, our state in Christ would
be no more stable than in the first Adam, and it would be equally easy in the
one as in the other to fall from God's favor and the state of grace. Nor would
Christ here, the surety of the New Covenant, Hebrews 2:12, be more powerful
than Adam's free will, by which he destroyed himself and all his descendants.

4. The Covenant of Grace would not be an eternal Covenant on both sides,
contrary to the clear testimonies of Scripture in Jeremiah 31:35–37; Jeremiah
32:40–41; Isaiah 54:10; and Isaiah 59:21.

5. The perseverance of the Saints would be neither promised by God nor sure
and established. We will demonstrate afterward from the Scriptures that this
is false.

6. Unless such grace were promised to us,

[1668 KBNL/UvA witness | logical printed p. 438 | PDF p. 489]

a person would have no ground on which to pray for the constant inclination
of his will toward faith and obedience, to trust in God because of such an
inclination, or to give thanks for such an influence—contrary to the
Scriptures: Psalm 119:27, 29, 33–37, 43, 66; Psalm 86:11; Song of Solomon 1:2,
7; Matthew 6:10; Ephesians 1:16–17; Ephesians 3:14–16; Colossians 1:9; 1
Thessalonians 5:23; and 2 Thessalonians 1:3.

It is asked whether the Covenant of Grace is eternal, or whether our remaining
in this Covenant so depends on the inclination of free choice that we can
remain in it or, according to our own choice, fall from it. The Remonstrants
deny that this Covenant is eternal. We affirm that this Covenant is immutable
and eternal.

1. The eternity of the Covenant on both sides is established in express
words. On God's side, Jeremiah 32:40: “And I will enter an everlasting Covenant
with them, so that I will not turn away from doing good to them.” Its eternity
on our side is established in the same place, and that from God's infallible
promise: “And I will put reverence for me in their mind, so that they do not
depart from me.”

2. Final perseverance in grace is promised to the Covenanted: Jeremiah
32:40–41; Jeremiah 31:33–35; Isaiah 59:21; Hosea 2:18; Psalm 1:5; 1 John 4:14;
Matthew 16:18; John 6:37; 10:28–29; and 14:16.

3. This Covenant is as sure, constant, and inviolable as God's natural
Covenant concerning the perpetuation of day and night and the

[1668 KBNL/UvA witness | logical printed p. 439 | PDF p. 490]

revolutions of the sun and moon, Jeremiah 31:35–37; as sure as that which is
known to be most true with God's holy oath interposed, Hebrews 6:18; and as
sure as that the world will not be overwhelmed by Noah's waters, Isaiah 54:10.
Therefore, it is established in the immutability and truthfulness of God, who
cannot lie, Psalm 89:34–37.

4. It rests upon that holy Covenant, which can never be violated, entered
between the Father and his Son concerning the giving to him of long-lived
seed, Isaiah 53:10; children, Isaiah 8:18 and Hebrews 2:13; dominion from sea
to sea, Psalm 72:8 and Psalm 2:8–9; and a willing people, Psalm 110:3, provided
that he lays down his life for his own. But this Covenant between the Father
and the Son can in no way be made void.

5. Christ's death and the shedding of his blood, by which the Testament of the
Covenant was confirmed, would be in vain; and there would be room for another
Covenant and another Surety who would confirm that other Covenant with other
blood, if this Covenant could be made void.

6. Predetermining grace, pardon for falls, and repentance are promised in this
Covenant.

7. The distinction established by the Holy Spirit would perish between that
Covenant which Jehovah entered with the house of Israel on the day he led them
out of Egypt and this New Covenant: that the former was made void and
violated, but the latter is eternal and inviolable, Jeremiah 31:31–33.

ROMANS 9:8.

But the children of the promise are reckoned as the seed.

It is asked whether the promises of the New Covenant are universal and extend
equally to all by God's primary intention and antecedent will. The
Remonstrants say so.

We deny that by God's decree and intention the promises pertain to any but the
elect, although the things promised are offered under a condition to each and
every person in the Visible Church.

1. By God's intention the promises cannot extend to those who neither hear
nor can hear such promises—indeed, to those who cannot hear because God's
providence prohibits and hinders it. For it is an axiom of the Jurists:
“Unpromulgated promises are no more binding toward performing the condition
of the promises than an unpromulgated Law hidden in the mind of the Lawgiver.”
For who would imagine God speaking in this way: “Pagans and Americans, I
promise you remission of sins, Christ's Righteousness, and eternal life
obtained for you by Christ's death, and I decreed all these things for you from
eternity if you have believed. Meanwhile, I decreed never to reveal such a
promise to you; indeed, with my Providence hindering it, I decreed to prevent
it from being revealed to you.”

2. The promises concerning the inscription of the Law on the heart, Jeremiah
31:33 and Hebrews 8:10, and concerning the giving of a new heart, Ezekiel

[1668 KBNL/UvA witness | logical printed p. 441 | PDF p. 492]

36:26, and a circumcised heart, Deuteronomy 30:6, are not made to the
reprobate by God's intention and decree. For either they are made absolutely
to the reprobate, and thus God would engender a new heart and a new spirit in
all the reprobate, which is contrary to experience and the Scriptures; or
conditionally, God would give the reprobate a new and circumcised heart. But
this is found nowhere in the Scriptures: “Whoever has performed this or that
duty, God will give him a new heart and a new Spirit.” Further, God calls us
not from works that we have done, but makes those dead in sins alive. Add that,
according to the Arminians, the obligation to have a new heart binds everyone
equally. Therefore, the adversaries do not rightly infer God's decree from the
obligation imposed on human beings, namely: “By his obligating will God wills
that each and every person believe and obey God and be saved. Therefore, he
decreed that each and every person should believe and obey.” And thus, if by
God's counsel these promises do not extend to each and every person, neither
do the rest.

3. In the promises God either wills salvation and remission for each and
every person if they believe, so that the condition suspends his volition and
decree to remit and save; or the condition suspends not God's decree but
actual remission and glorification. The first cannot be said, because it is
unworthy of the infinite perfection of the divine will that his fixed and
eternal decrees should be suspended on the unstable inclination and temporal
act of created free

[1668 KBNL/UvA witness | logical printed p. 442 | PDF p. 493]

choice. But if the condition suspends only the actual remission and temporal
conferral of eternal life, then by God's counsel the promises concerning the
giving of remission and eternal life pertain only to those who fulfill the
condition, that is, only to believers.

1. The decree to glorify is as restricted as glorification itself, as
Arminius himself admits in his *Declaration*. For just as God glorifies no one
but a believer, so he decreed to glorify no one but a believer; and just as he
condemns no one but one who is finally impenitent, so he decreed to condemn no
one but such a person.

2. As the Jurists rightly teach: “A conditional volition and a promise under
the supposition of such a condition posit nothing unless the condition is
first posited in reality. Likewise, a conditional gift is not a gift unless
the condition is first fulfilled. Likewise, a conditional promise does not
obligate the promisor unless the condition is posited.” Since, therefore,
Judas the traitor and the reprobate never believe, there never was in God any
volition for the salvation of Judas and the reprobate.

3. According to the sum of the gospel, God does not intend to give the
reprobate just any salvation and remission considered vaguely, but this
particular salvation obtained under the manner, or by way, of a reward under
the condition of faith, as the Arminians teach everywhere. For in the gospel
he does not intend salvation for the reprobate absolutely and under no

[1668 KBNL/UvA witness | logical printed p. 443 | PDF p. 494]

condition. Thus if the condition of Faith is never posited—as certainly it is
not in most of the reprobate according to the adversaries, and in none
according to us—such a condition is mere nonbeing and nothing, since they
never believe. Therefore, it is false that in the promises God intends for
Judas the traitor and those who never believe remission and eternal life by
way of a conditional reward.

But the adversaries say that God's intention in promising salvation to the
reprobate is suspended until all the reprobate actually and finally believe.
But this is to imagine in God an intention suspended from eternity and to
imagine God intending salvation for them by an incomplete, suspended,
doubtful, and indeterminate act until they all actually believe, which God
foreknows will never happen. Therefore, it is certain that God did not intend
equally, and that he did not by his primary will promise, remission and
eternal life to each and every person.

4. By God's intention the Gospel promises extend no more widely than God
himself extends them in the gospel. But in the gospel God himself extends them
only to believers: “He who believes will be saved,” and likewise with the
threat, “He who does not believe will be condemned.” For salvation and faith
extend equally far. No one is saved unless he believes, and no one who
believes is not saved. Therefore, God's saving intention is directed to
believers alone.

[1668 KBNL/UvA witness | logical printed p. 444 | PDF p. 495]

If they say that God intends to save more people, provided more believed, than
are actually saved, and that he does so by an antecedent will and intention,
because by such an antecedent intention he intends to save each and every
person provided each and every person believed:

I answer as above that a conditional intention is nothing and posits nothing
unless the condition is first posited. But that condition is never posited,
for never does everyone believe. Therefore, there never is such an antecedent
intention in God. 2. Nowhere in the Scriptures does God say that he wills to
save any other than believers. Otherwise, this would be the gospel promise:
“Before the condition of faith is posited, God wills, intends, and promises to
save each and every mortal,” which is false.

5. If by God's intention the promises of the gospel pertained to each and
every mortal, and therefore God willed by an antecedent intention to save each
and every person, then by the same reasoning the threats of the gospel would
pertain to each and every mortal. Thus God would intend by an antecedent
intention that each and every mortal perish eternally, and would antecedently
destine each and every person to eternal destruction. The reason is that
God's will is as conditional in the threats as in the Gospel promises, and God
is as inclined toward Justice as toward Mercy.

[1668 KBNL/UvA witness | logical printed p. 445 | PDF p. 496]

6. There are countless places in both the Old and the New Testaments where
Remission, Righteousness, and Salvation are promised to the believer and the
repentant. But there is no passage in which God says, “I decree and will that
each and every person should have remission and eternal salvation, provided
they believe. Likewise, I decree and will that each and every person should
perish eternally, provided they do not believe.” But our position is in the
Scriptures; theirs by no means is. Matthew 11:28: “Come to me, all”—not simply
all, but “all who labor and are burdened.” John 3:16: “For God so loved the
world that he gave his only-begotten Son, that everyone”—not simply every
person, but “everyone who believes—should not perish but have eternal life.” So
also John 5:24; John 7:37; Isaiah 55:1; John 11:26; Romans 3:22; Galatians
3:22; Acts 10:43; Acts 2:38–39. Therefore, from the bare word “all,” the
adversaries commit a fallacy from divided terms to conjoined terms. For
Prosper rightly observes in *The Calling of the Gentiles*, book 1, chapter 3,
that “the people of God has its fullness.” And Scripture expressly
interprets the Gospel promises concerning all believers, those who thirst,
and those burdened by consciousness of sins.

7. That doctrine is not to be admitted which extends Christian consolation
equally to all human beings—murderers, adulterers, Pagans, Turks, and so
forth—by this Syllogism:

The promises of God pertain to all who are human beings, whether they are
Pagans, Turks, parricides, and so forth.

[1668 KBNL/UvA witness | logical printed p. 446 | PDF p. 497 | ends before MATT. 10:5]

But I am a human being, a Turk, a parricide, and so forth.

Therefore, the promises of God pertain to me.

8. Those to whom by God's intention the promises of the New Covenant pertain
are truly covenanted and restored to grace, and for them Christ acquired by
the merit of his death and blood the promised spiritual goods of the New
Covenant. But each and every mortal is not covenanted, restored to grace
through Christ, and reconciled to God. The reason for the major premise is
that the promises of the Covenant would pertain by God's intention to devils
if devils were covenanted, restored to grace through Christ, and reconciled.
But the minor premise is evident, because it is monstrous to say that Pharaoh,
Herod, and others were restored to God's grace, and that the Sodomites were
bought by the blood of the Lamb.

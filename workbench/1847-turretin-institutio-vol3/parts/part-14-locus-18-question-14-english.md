QUESTION XIV.

Can the present-day Roman church be called the true church of Christ? We deny
against the papists.

I. From what was said in the thesis concerning the marks of the church, it is
easy to gather what must be thought in the particular case, whether concerning
the Roman church or concerning the Reformed and Evangelical church. We will
treat this distinctly, beginning with the Roman church.

[Marginalia: The state of the question.]

II. The question does not concern every Roman church, including that which
flourished in the age of the apostles and whose faith was celebrated throughout
the whole world in Paul’s time (Romans 1:8). It concerns the present-day papal
church, which is subject to the dominion and power of the pope. The papists
contend not only that it is true, but also that it is the catholic and only
church on earth, outside which

[1847 vol. III printed p. 104 | IA leaf 138 | PDF p. 138]

no salvation can be given. We, however, judge that it is neither the one and
only catholic church nor can be or be called true.

III. The Roman church can be viewed under a twofold relation: either as
*Christian*, by reason of its profession of Christianity and the evangelical
truths it retains; or as *papal*, by reason of its subjection to the pontiff and
the capital corruptions and errors in both faith and morals which it has mingled
with and built upon those truths, beyond and contrary to God’s word. We can
therefore speak about it in different ways. In the former respect, we do not
deny that there is something true within it. But in the latter respect, under
which we consider it here, we deny that it can be called Christian and
apostolic; rather, it is antichristian and apostate. In this sense we admit that
it can still be called a Christian church improperly and in a qualified sense
in three respects. First, by reason of God’s people, or the elect, who still
remain in it and are commanded to come out of it even at the time of Babylon’s
destruction (Revelation 18:4). 2. By reason of its *external form*, or certain
ruins of the scattered church in which its traces can still be seen today, both
with respect to God’s word and its preaching, which, though corrupted, still
remain in it, and with respect to the administration of the sacraments,
especially baptism, which is still preserved there intact as to its substance.
3. By reason of the Christian and evangelical truths concerning the one and
triune God, Christ the God-man and Mediator, his incarnation, death, and
resurrection, and other heads by which it is distinguished from assemblies of
pagans and unbelievers. But we deny that it can properly and absolutely be
called a true church, much less that it is the one catholic church, as they
would have it.

IV. To demonstrate this, the single point we proved above could suffice: the
proper and essential mark of the true church is nothing other than the
conformity with God’s word of the doctrine retained in an assembly. A comparison
of the two sides makes it clearer than the midday light that the Roman church’s
doctrine in many of its most serious heads fights diametrically against
Scripture, whatever paints and colors may be laid over it to disguise its
foulness and soften its harshness. It is therefore evident from this very fact
that it is not the true church and cannot be called so except falsely.

V. But so that this may appear more distinctly, we must consider the various
characteristics and rays of divinity which shine in the word and by which the
salutary and fundamental doctrine drawn from God’s word and taught in the true
church must be marked and proved: namely, *truth in faith* free from heresy;
*purity in worship* cleansed from every superstition and idolatry; *liberty in
government* free from bondage and tyranny; *holiness in morals* opposed to the
filth of vices and the uncleanness of the flesh; and *certainty and consolation*
far removed from doubt and distrust. Therefore, just as the church which retains
this foundation of salvation and whose doctrine displays these characteristics
cannot but be called true, so we rightly contend that any church lacking them,
such as we judge the Roman church to be, cannot be called true.

[Marginalia: The Roman church is proved not to be a true church. 1. Because it strikes against the foundation.]

VI. 1. It is not only *not built upon the foundation of the prophets and
apostles* and does not rest on it alone, as the true church of Christ does
(Ephesians 2:20; Galatians 1:8–9), but strikes against that very foundation in
many ways, both by addition and by subtraction. First, *by addition*: joining
unwritten human traditions, which Christ himself rejected (Matthew 15:9), to
the canonical Scriptures given to us as the rule of things to be believed and
done; laying another foundation besides the one laid (1 Corinthians 3:11);
devising another head of the church besides Christ, other merits besides his
obedience alone, another propitiatory sacrifice besides his cross, another
purgatory besides his blood, and other names through which we may be saved

[1847 vol. III printed p. 105 | IA leaf 139 | PDF p. 139]

besides the name of Jesus; and proposing more doctrines of this kind which are
plainly incompatible with the fundamental articles and evidently overthrow them
by a direct and immediate consequence. Nor should those be heard who think that
these things can be harmonized “because things which are subordinate do not
conflict,” or that adding something to the foundation does not immediately
overthrow the foundation itself. Certainly, one who adds to the foundation
things homogeneous with it does not overthrow the foundation. But one who adds
contrary and incompatible things overthrows it by adding; for he joins things
which cannot be combined and cannot stand together. Thus one who adds unwritten
traditions, to be received with the same affection and reverence as Scripture,
thereby denies that Scripture is a rule to which nothing must be added and from
which nothing must be taken. One who adds another propitiatory sacrifice to the
sacrifice of the cross thereby denies the unity and perfection of Christ’s
sacrifice. One who sews other merits onto Christ’s merit denies that his
righteousness is sufficient for us. Thus, since the unity of the foundation
belongs to its nature, so that no other can be laid besides what has been laid
(1 Corinthians 3:11), and salvation is given in none other than Christ (Acts
4:12), no one can add anything to it as a foundation without destroying by
adding and opposing by combining. Hence Paul so often teaches the incompatibility
of our own righteousness with Christ’s righteousness and of the merit of works
with grace and faith (Romans 10:3; 9:6; Galatians 5:4). He also declares that
those who joined other objects of worship to Christ did not hold the Head
(Colossians 2:19). Second, *by subtraction*: denying the perfection, integrity,
and authority of Scripture; rejecting the imputed righteousness of Christ alone
for our justification before God; removing the second commandment of the
Decalogue; and taking the eucharistic cup away from the laity.

[Marginalia: 2. Because it is heretical.]

VII. Second, it is *apostate and heretical*, because it has fallen away from
the faith once delivered to the saints and teaches various deadly heresies,
forcing them upon people to be believed under pain of anathema. Such are the
doctrines concerning justification by works and their merits, human
satisfactions and indulgences, transubstantiation and the sacrifice of the
Mass, sin and free choice, sufficient grace, the possible observance of the
law, the ecumenical pontiff and the primacy of the pope, the church’s nature,
infallibility, and authority, the efficacy and number of the sacraments, and
other erroneous doctrines of this kind which prevail among them. Foremost are
those which the apostle numbers among doctrines of demons, namely, the choice
of foods and the prohibition of marriage (1 Timothy 4:1), as we have already
proved in their proper places or will prove in what follows.

VIII. The adversaries object here in vain. 1. “These errors, if there are any,
are not fundamental.” But the thing itself cries out that they attack the
primary heads of faith, such as the perfection of the word, Christ’s mediation,
the unity of his sacrifice, the efficacy of his grace, and other things of this
kind in which the essence of religion consists. Further, even if each one by
itself and separately were not capital, jointly they become such by the very
fact that they place them among necessary articles of faith proposed for belief
under pain of anathema, with consciences bound to them by force but not
convinced. Finally, although they were not all equally fundamental, it would be
enough for one or another to be so. Indeed, if even a single one occurred, it
would suffice to make the religion intolerable and the church false, so that
salvation could not be obtained in it. 2. “Private opinions of doctors are
often presented as the church’s position.” But the sanctions of councils,
decrees of pontiffs, other authentic writings of this kind, and the practice
and usage of the whole church testify that the errors noted above are not merely
the private errors of certain people, but the public errors of the entire
church. By that practice they are prescribed to each and all for belief and
profession as the common and publicly received doctrine. 3. “We wrongly
conceive the Roman church’s mind and ascribe a false meaning to it.” But anyone
who wishes attentively to conduct this examination

[1847 vol. III printed p. 106 | IA leaf 140 | PDF p. 140]

will readily see that nothing could be said which is more false and contrary to
reason. Nor can the expositions and explanations which some men seek out with
subtle art to cloak the errors, conceal the poison, and evade the charge of
idolatry demonstrate this, as was recently done by the bishop of Condom in his
*Exposition of the Catholic Faith*. Whatever color ingenious writings of this
kind introduce to coat the errors and wipe away the stains which have hitherto
defiled the Roman church’s face, it is not difficult to gather either that there
is a deceptive coloring and malicious fraud by which fine words seemingly
approaching the truth are offered, while meanwhile nothing is relaxed concerning
the source of all the errors—namely, the church’s infallibility and the pope’s
primacy—so that the unwary may more easily be snared and deceived; or that they
depart from the church’s common and constant doctrine and practice and ascribe
to it a meaning different from that displayed by the decisions of councils,
declarations of pontiffs, public confessions and formulas, and other symbolic
writings of this kind, and different from what has hitherto prevailed among the
Roman church’s primary pillars and chief doctors. No one can persuade himself
that these men have hitherto either not adequately understood or not adequately
explained their church’s position.

IX. 4. “The errors ascribed to them do not flow from their doctrine except
through inferences, arguments, and consequences deduced from it, and these are
indeed rejected and disapproved; consequently, they cannot be attributed to it
without the stain of calumny,” as the same bishop says in the *Exposition*,
pages 11–12. But (1.) it is here gratuitously supposed that their writers
directly and formally teach nothing which strikes against the foundation. Yet
the matter itself openly testifies to the contrary. What then? Do we charge the
papists merely through twisted and disapproved consequences with wishing
traditions to be received with a firm faith and the same affection of piety and
reverence as the written word; with holding the Mass to be a true, proper, and
propitiatory sacrifice; with holding that transubstantiation and purgatory
exist; that a person is justified not by faith alone but also by works; that
the pope is the head of the church; that the sacrament must be adored with the
worship of *latria*; that one must bow down with religious worship before images
of God, Christ, the Virgin, and the saints; that the saints must be worshiped
and invoked; and with other theoretical as well as practical doctrines of this
kind? Do the papists not profess such things directly and positively in doctrine
and practice? If certain consequences are urged against them, these are not
remote, forced, and obscure consequences which should be disregarded, but
necessary, proximate, evident, and inherent ones which flow spontaneously from
their principles and possess the force of a firm and necessary proof. Thus from
the doctrine of transubstantiation, the sacrifice of the altar, the adoration of
the host, and the invocation of saints, we infer by a necessary consequence that
the nature of Christ’s body is overthrown, the most perfect sacrifice of the
cross is impaired, and the highest honor owed to God is given to a creature.
Thus from communion under one kind we gather that the sacrament is mutilated
and Christ’s institution violated. For from the assertion of one contradictory,
the removal and overthrow of the other necessarily follows, unless we wish to
make a contradiction in terms and set an opposite in apposition. The necessity
or evidence of these consequences cannot be obscured or weakened by empty
little distinctions, like dust thrown into the eyes—distinctions which Scripture
does not teach, earlier ages did not recognize, ordinary people do not
understand, and the introduced practice does not observe, but openly destroys.
(2.) The stain of calumny is present only when consequences are deduced not
from received and asserted principles but from fictitious and denied ones; not
from the church’s public and constant position and practice, but by violence
continually done from the beginning to its words, confessions, and declared and
explained mind. Such are those consequences which the papists most unjustly
charge against us from a thoroughly distorted version of our position.
Therefore, as forced and utterly false, we not only do not acknowledge them but
reject and condemn them with our whole heart. Yet the Roman church’s

[1847 vol. III printed p. 107 | IA leaf 141 | PDF p. 141]

definitions, solemn practice, and constant discipline prove sufficiently,
even while we remain silent, that we do nothing of the kind here.

[Marginalia: 3. Because it is idolatrous.]

X. Third, it is *idolatrous and superstitious*, both by reason of the object it
worships and by reason of the manner in which it worships. By reason of the
*object*: besides God, who alone, as omniscient, omnipotent, and supremely good,
ought to be the one object of worship and invocation, it also venerates and
adores creatures which are not gods by nature (Galatians 4:8), such as the
Blessed Virgin, angels, departed saints, the consecrated host, the sacrament,
the cross, the pontiff, and the relics of Christ and the saints. By reason of
the *manner*: in the manufacture, worship, and adoration of statues and images,
which are so solemnly prohibited by God’s law. These things are established not
from the private opinions of doctors, but from public sanctions and constant
practice, so that we invent nothing against them here.

XI. The various evasions which the adversaries are accustomed to grasp here do
nothing to weaken the force of this argument or disguise the shame of their
error. 1. “All this worship which is offered to creatures is called religious
because it necessarily terminates in God,” as the bishop says in section 3 of
the *Catholic Exposition*. It is not enough to make worship lawful that it
terminates in God as its final end while meanwhile being extended to other
objects besides God. It must be offered to God alone, to the exclusion of
creatures, because God’s jealousy does not permit his glory to be given to
another. Further, by this pretext the pagan idolaters could readily have been
excused. They did not terminate their worship in the wood, stone, or gold from
which the idols were made, but contended that it was referred and terminated in
the deities represented by the idols. Nor would the Israelites have sinned in
worshiping the calf, since they terminated that worship in Jehovah, who had led
them out of Egypt.

XII. 2. “Invocation directed to the saints is performed in the same spirit of
love by which the faithful living on earth are asked to pray with us and for
us, and the manner of praying to the saints differs greatly from the manner of
praying to God. God is prayed to that he may have mercy upon us, while the
saints are asked only to pray for us.” Thus the same bishop speaks in the
*Catholic Exposition*, pages 19–21. But these things are gratuitously said to
deceive the simple. (1.) The civil service of Christian society and mutual help
among the living and present are wrongly confused with an act of religion,
invocation, adoration, trust, vow, and sacrifice, whose objects are the dead,
far removed from civil intercourse and present neither by nature, operation,
nor knowledge. Nor has anyone ever been commanded to invoke them, much less in
the manner and for the end which present-day Rome approves. Who does not see
how great the difference is between these things? Who has ever said that the
living are invoked religiously? (2.) It is also supposed that the saints are
not approached as principal causes which either give good things or deliver
from evils, and are not addressed with the same formulas of prayer as God. But
the Tridentine fathers decree that they must be invoked as suppliants and that
recourse must be had to their help and assistance. Hence they are given every
part of divine worship: invocation, adoration, supplications, vows, feasts,
temples, altars, incense, offerings, and thanksgivings. Hence the sacrifice of
the Mass, in which Christ is said to be offered to the Father, is offered in a
saint’s honor, although nothing can be judged more religious than it. Hence the
public formulas, whatever the adversaries contend, in which they are prayed to
that they may have mercy, spare, deliver, save, hear, blot out sins, illuminate
hearts, bestow spiritual grace, and receive people at the hour of death. These
occur repeatedly in litanies and prayers to the Blessed Virgin, Peter, Paul,
and others. In the reformed office of the Blessed Mary, published by order of
Pius V, in a prayer to the Blessed Virgin, they address her thus: “Consolation
of the desolate, way of the wandering, salvation of all who hope in you.” And
in the *Garden of the Soul*, published at Antwerp in 1557: “Hail,

[1847 vol. III printed p. 108 | IA leaf 142 | PDF p. 142]

Mary, hope of the world. Hear me, a wretched sinner trusting in your mercy and
compassion; Mother of mercy, Mother of grace, hope of all the desolate,
consoler of all who despair, salvation of all who hope in you.” They invoke her
not merely as their hope but also as their *only hope*: “You are the only hope
of sinners; through you we hope for forgiveness of offenses, and in you, most
blessed one, is our expectation of rewards.” *Roman Breviary*, September 9.
Hence Coster, expounding in his work on the hymn “Hail, Star of the Sea” the
words “loose the chains of the guilty,” comments: “Loose them by prayers, loose
them by merit, loose them by authority, loose them by command.” Is this only to
ask the Virgin to pray for us? Is it not also to ask that she have mercy upon
us and confer grace itself through herself? And if nothing else is intended in
the worship of the Virgin than that she pray for us, why could the *Book of
Salutary Counsels to Indiscreet Worshipers of the Blessed Virgin*, published at
Cologne, not escape the censor’s mark at both Rome and the Spanish Inquisition?
It aimed only to direct the worship of the Virgin to that end and to try to
correct the superstitious and idolatrous practice which prevails everywhere,
yet its author was treated as guilty “of impiety against the Scriptures, the
Virgin, the saints, and images.” In contrast, Crasset’s book on the worship of
the Virgin, published against it in 1679 with the provincial’s approval and
under the patronage of the archbishop of Paris, repeats and approves whatever
the more deranged Mariolaters had not earlier blushed to say: “that omnipotence
in heaven and on earth belongs to her; that nothing is impossible for her; that
she can save those in despair; that she approaches God’s tribunal not only as a
maidservant who petitions her Lord, but as a mother who commands her son,” page
28, and very many other things of this kind which cannot be read without holy
indignation. See also what Rivet wrote in the *Apology for the Blessed Virgin*
and Drelincourt in *On the Honor Due to the Blessed Virgin*. Nor is their
practice toward the remaining saints any different. They invoke them
religiously not only as intercessors, but as protectors from evils and bestowers
of both grace and glory.

XIII. 3. “They do not offer the saints *latria*, or the highest worship, but
only *dulia* or *hyperdulia*, a subordinate and lower worship.” We have
examined the emptiness of this distinction elsewhere, Locus XI, *On the Law*,
Question VII, section 10. It is sufficiently refuted by the fact that it
supposes degrees or kinds of religious worship can be given, whereas we contend
that it is one and indivisible and belongs no less properly to God alone than
conjugal affection is owed to a husband alone. 4. Concerning images: “They
ascribe no divinity and no power to them except that of arousing remembrance of
the prototypes. The worship and honor owed to them are referred to the
prototypes, so that the images themselves are not so much worshiped as the
prototypes before them, nor are they honored in themselves with absolute but
only relative worship.” But if such an exception is valid, the pagans should be
excused from idolatry. Everyone knows that before Tertullian, Origen, Minucius,
Arnobius, Lactantius, and Augustine they disguised the worship of images with
the same colors, saying that they did not attribute divinity to images but
merely worshiped the gods in them and the deity signified through the idol;
that they were regarded as symbols of divinity and as signs and images of the
gods; that they were worshiped in God’s honor; and more things of this kind.
The papists themselves do not conceal that in many respects there is no
difference between their opinion concerning the saints and what the Gentiles
thought concerning their gods, and that the papists seem to have omitted
nothing which the pagans were accustomed to do in the highest adoration of
their statues. Polydore Vergil notes this in book 6, chapter 13; Vives on
Augustine, book 8; Cassander, *Consultation*, article 21; and others. Concerning
adoration, although not a few appear to shrink from it and speak only of
veneration and worship, they cannot deny it in view of the decree of the Council
of Trent, which commands that the veneration sanctioned by the second Council
of Nicaea be given to images. There *proskynesis*, adoration, is expressly
commanded and indeed opposed to mere *timē*, or

[1847 vol. III printed p. 109 | IA leaf 143 | PDF p. 143]

veneration, as is found in its fourth action. This is also the position of
Thomas, Bonaventure, Cajetan, Biel, Vasquez, Bellarmine, and others, and is
likewise confirmed by the universal practice and blind superstition of the
people.

[Marginalia: 4. Because its doctrine is doubtful and opposes certainty of salvation and peace of conscience.]

XIV. Fourth, because the Roman church’s doctrine *fosters doubt and opposes
certainty of salvation and peace of conscience*, which can be obtained only in
the true church. The former is gathered not only from the countless capital
controversies debated among the papists which the pontiff himself, though the
supreme judge of controversies, has not yet been able to settle, but is
especially evident from these two articles. 1. Concerning the church’s claimed
*infallibility*: since the parties have not yet agreed among themselves where
it resides, whether in the pontiff or the council, nothing certain can be held
even concerning any doctrine dependent upon that infallibility. 2. Concerning
the *minister’s intention*, necessarily required in every sacrament according
to the Council of Trent, Session 7, canon 11: since such an intention is hidden
and known to no one except God, who searches hearts, whatever is done by such
ministers must also be doubtful and uncertain. Hence no one can be certainly
persuaded about his own baptism—whether he is a Christian and truly baptized—
because he does not know whether the bishop or priest who baptized him had the
intention of baptizing him, or whether such a bishop or priest was duly ordained
with the ordainer’s intention. Thus the human mind is entangled here in
inextricable knots and possesses nothing in which it may rest. Hence it also
arises that a person laboring under this uncertainty is daily exposed to the
most immediate danger of idolatry in the adoration of the host, being uncertain
of Christ’s presence. He cannot know whether the priest had the intention to
consecrate; and if he had it, whether he has the power to consecrate, that is,
whether he was duly ordained by a bishop; and other things of this kind make his
mind perpetually doubtful and anxious. The latter—the inefficacy of pontifical
doctrine to quiet a sinner’s conscience before God—is abundantly evident from
their doctrine concerning “uncertainty of faith and salvation and perpetual
doubt” and the *suspense of judgment* in which a person is commanded to remain,
as we saw in its proper place, Locus XV, Question 17. Consequently the
conscience terrified by the sense of sins and fear of divine judgment can
obtain solid peace and tranquility neither in life nor in death. Instead, it is
continually tossed by the surge of doubts and distrust because it cannot know
with certainty whether it is faithful and in a state of grace, in God’s love or
hatred, to be received into heaven or cast down into hell. Nor is this
surprising, since they abandon trust in Christ alone, his most perfect
righteousness, and his most fragrant sacrifice, which alone can appease God’s
wrath, satisfy his justice, and expiate sins. They wish instead to rest upon
the rotten and fig-wood foundation of their own righteousness and merits, human
satisfactions, and papal indulgences. Since these cannot bear examination by
the divine judgment or protect and defend the sinner against the missiles of
divine wrath and curse, they hold him overwhelmed and enslaved by perpetual
terror. This is increased considerably by the fear of purgatory’s burning
flames and dreadful torments, by which souls must be tortured for many ages
after death. The conscience cannot but be miserably tormented by these things
and all but driven to despair.

[Marginalia: 5. Because it opposes piety and good morals.]

XV. Fifth, because it *opposes piety and good morals in many ways* through
various doctrines which conflict with decency and open a broad door to sins.
Such are: 1. The *prohibition of marriage and law of celibacy* imposed on the
clergy contrary to God’s word. According to it, those who do not possess the
gift of continence are better off not “marrying rather than burning,” as Paul
prescribes (1 Corinthians 7:9), but burning and fornicating and defiling
themselves with unspeakable lusts rather than

[1847 vol. III printed p. 110 | IA leaf 144 | PDF p. 144]

marrying. It can scarcely be said how many and how monstrous were the
abominations of lust and pollution that flowed from this sewer, so that Pius
II, the Roman pontiff, said with good reason according to Platina: “Marriage was
taken away from priests for a great reason but should be restored for a greater
one.” 2. *Monastic vows*, by which even underage young people are withdrawn
from paternal authority and the obedience owed to parents, and the yoke of
blind obedience and servitude is imposed upon people in such a way that,
without any examination or inquiry, they are bound immediately and without
hesitation to perform whatever is commanded by their superiors, whether just or
unjust. Nothing more wicked or more contrary to the light of reason and the
holy liberty of the faithful can be imagined. 3. *Auricular confession*, which
experience has taught only too well to be a torture chamber of consciences, a
school of crimes and impurities, a device of pontifical tyranny and rapacity,
a key perfectly fitted to search and penetrate the secrets of families and
kingdoms, and a teacher of the foulest conspiracies, treacheries, and regicides.
This is especially because of the seal of confession, which may not be broken
for any cause, however serious, even if it concerns the murder of a father or
king or the overthrow of the commonwealth. “There can be no evil so great,”
Eudæmono-Johannes says in *Against Cotton*, chapter 13, “that confession may be
betrayed for the sake of avoiding it.” And Toletus, *On the Instruction of
Priests*, book 3, chapter 16: “It must be observed that the obligation of the
seal is so great that for absolutely no cause—not even to save one’s own life
or for the safety of the entire commonwealth—can a confessor reveal the
penitent’s sin.” Emanuel Sa has the same under the word “Confessor” in the
*Aphorisms*, and Suarez, *On Penance*, disputation 33, section 1. 4. The most
shameful doctrine of *equivocations and mental reservations*, to be practiced
even in court. By it they contend that, when the matter requires, one may not
only lie but even perjure oneself for the sake of a greater good, with a mental
reservation, so that nothing is any longer certain in pacts and treaties.
Indeed, they say a Christian may abjure his religion before a pagan judge by
saying, “I am not a Christian,” while supplying mentally, “so as to tell it to
you.” See what was said at Locus XI, Question 12. 5. Closely related to this is
the treacherous doctrine that *faith need not be kept with heretics*. Although
certain private doctors have been ashamed of such a disgraceful doctrine, it
is known to have been publicly received, declared by the Council of Constance
in Session 19, proved by a deadly practice in the cases of John Hus and Jerome
of Prague, and repeatedly confirmed elsewhere in the most dreadful massacres
and slaughters of the faithful carried out contrary to a pledge of faith. See
Locus XI, Question 11.

XVI. 6. The monstrous doctrine concerning *the pope’s power to depose kings
and absolve subjects from their oath of fidelity*. Through it, a mortal human
being not only sacrilegiously invades God’s rights and impiously presumes to
dispense from an indispensable bond, but a broad door is also opened to the
most appalling rebellions against superiors and abominable regicides. According
to this principle, after a sentence of excommunication is issued, a king ceases
to be legitimate and can no longer occupy the kingdom except tyrannically. “He
begins to be a tyrant in title because he is not the legitimate king,” Suarez
says in book 6 of the *Defense*, chapter 4. “But any person among the people can
kill one who tyrannically occupies power if there is no other remedy, for he is
a public enemy.” For according to Urban II’s canon *The Excommunicated*, cause
23, question 5, chapter 4, one who kills an excommunicated person is not a
murderer: “We do not judge those to be murderers who, burning with zeal for
their catholic mother against the excommunicated, happen to have slain some of
them.” President Forgetus graphically describes the atrocity of this most
pestilential error in an oration given in the Sorbonne school, reported by
Thuanus in book 114: “From this error,” he says, “as if swords had been thrown
into the kingdom with which citizens might fight among themselves, and so
forth; from this fire a torch was lit which afterward roamed through all France
and almost reduced it to ashes; from it arose the deadly counsels considered
concerning exposing the kingdom as

[1847 vol. III printed p. 111 | IA leaf 145 | PDF p. 145]

plunder and betraying it to foreigners. From these beginnings the detestable
conspiracy against the most Christian and truly catholic King Henry III took
its origin, and he was finally killed by an abominable parricide when the mind
of the criminal deserter had been preoccupied by the poison of this deadly
opinion. From it arose flame, blood, slaughter, sacrilege, robbery, barbarity,
monsters, prodigies, and Iliads of evils which have afflicted the wretched
kingdom for seven years now, and so forth.” Although it must not be denied that
not a few papists detest this impious doctrine and that the Parlement of Paris
in particular has repeatedly promulgated the most severe edicts against it, it
is nevertheless certain that it has been commonly and publicly received and
repeatedly sanctioned by various decrees of pontiffs and canons of councils.
Thus Bellarmine, Baronius, and Perron contend that this is “the position and
voice of the whole catholic church,” which they confirm with a great abundance
of testimonies. To the same end Gregory VII numbers these among the *Dictates
of the Pope*: “That the pope is permitted to depose emperors; that he can
absolve the subjects of wicked men from fidelity.” The same Gregory, presiding
over a Roman synod, confirms this in the canon *We of the Saints*: “Holding the
statutes of our holy predecessors, by apostolic authority we absolve from their
oath those who are bound to the excommunicated by fidelity or sacrament, and so
forth.” Gelasius confirms this in the *Decretum*, cause 15, question 6, canon
“Another, and so forth”; Gregory IX in the *Decretals*, book 5, title 7,
chapter 16; and the Lateran Council under Innocent III in 1215. History
testifies that all these things have repeatedly been proved by the most cruel
practice: by Gregory VII against Emperor Henry IV; Innocent III against Otto IV;
Innocent IV against Frederick II; Boniface VIII against Philip the Fair, king
of France; Julius II against Louis XII; Pius V against Elizabeth, queen of
England; Sixtus V against Henry III; and Gregory XVI [sic] against Henry IV. See more
about these matters in Molineus, *Hyperaspistes*, book 3, chapter 1.2; the
French treatise *On the Bloody and Factious Doctrine and Practice of the Roman
Church*; and our fifth disputation *On Separation from the Roman Church*.

XVII. 7. The abominable doctrine concerning *dispensation from divine laws*,
according to which the pope arrogates to himself the power of dispensing from
God’s law, that is, of granting release from obedience and freeing from the
necessity of obeying God. For he dispenses from vows and oaths; frees underage
children from parental authority and subjects from the subjection they swore to
kings and princes; dissolves marriages duly joined; and, excepting the first
degree between a parent and children, dispenses in all the other degrees of
consanguinity prohibited by God’s law, even between brothers *and* sisters
[sic]. “The supreme pontiff,” Bertach says in the *Repertory* under the word
“Pope,” from Antoninus, Counsel 33, “can for an important reason grant a brother
permission to marry his deceased brother’s wife.” King Henry VIII of England
obtained this permission, as did others repeatedly. The same Antoninus, part 3,
title 1, chapter 2, section 1, says “that Martin V granted a dispensation to a
man who had contracted and consummated marriage with his own sister, whom he
had known in fornication.” Papal decretals and glosses on the canons affirm
that “the pope dispenses against the apostle,” the gloss on canon *The Reader*,
distinction 34. And *On the Concession of Prebends*, title 8, chapter *He
proposed*: “According to the fullness of his power, by law he can dispense
beyond the law.” The gloss interprets this: “The pope dispenses against the
apostle, likewise against the Old Testament, and likewise in a vow and an
oath.” And the gloss on the canon *There are certain things*, cause 25,
question 1: “The pope dispenses in the gospel by interpreting it.” For if
Toletus is to be believed, *On the Instruction of Priests*, book 1, chapter 68,
“Not all the things the apostles instituted were instituted by divine law.”
Hence Andradius says in book 2 of the *Defense of Trent*: “Those did not err
who said that Roman pontiffs can sometimes

[1847 vol. III printed p. 112 | IA leaf 146 | PDF p. 146]

dispense from Paul and the first four councils.” Nor should anyone find this
surprising, since, as Bellarmine says in *Against Barclay*, chapter 31, “In a
good sense Christ gave Peter, and consequently the pontiff, the power to make
sin not sin and what is not sin, sin.” And in book 4 of *On the Pontiff*,
chapter 5: “If the pope erred by commanding vices or prohibiting virtues, the
church would be bound to believe that vices are good and virtues evil, unless
it wished to sin against conscience.”

XVIII. 8. The *foul trafficking and marketing of sacred things*, through which
the pope restores the money changers’ tables overturned by Christ, making a
shop where there ought to have been a sanctuary. Æneas Sylvius, or Pope Pius II,
could not conceal this infamous corruption. In epistle 66 to John Peregallus he
says, “There is nothing the Roman Curia grants
without silver; even the laying on of hands and the gifts of the Holy Spirit are
sold, nor is forgiveness of sins granted except to those with money.” Hence
this clause is always inserted in indulgence bulls: those indulgences are given
to “those who extend helping hands.” Espensæus bitterly deplores the foulness
of this crime in his second digression on the epistle to Titus, relating
complaints about it from various authors and chiefly Mantuanus:

> Whoever seeks to procure sacred wealth with profane money,
> let him go to Rome; sacred things are for sale at Rome.
> Temples, priests, altars, sacred rites, crowns,
> fires, incense, prayers—heaven and God are for sale.

[Marginalia: Likewise, things for sale to us.]

Ferus confirms this on 2 John: “Who does not see the insatiable greed and
frauds of priests, religious, and pontiffs? Among them everything is for sale,
not only sheep and oxen—that is, the benefices and offices, as they call them,
both greater and lesser—but also doves, that is, the gifts of the Holy Spirit
and spiritual goods.” Theodoric of Niem had long before complained gravely
about it after others. No witness could have been better informed, since he was
secretary and privy counselor to three popes. *The Grove of Union*, treatise 6,
chapter 37: “The Apostolic Camera,” he says, “is compared to the sea, into which
all things enter but which does not overflow. Thus many thousands’ weight of
gold are carried into it from various parts of the world, and yet it is not
filled. In it are many leeches saying, Bring, bring, and so forth.” Whoever
wishes to see a description of this infamous profit should consult the *Tax of
the Apostolic Chancery and Penitentiary*. It accurately reports what must be
paid for benefices, provisions, dispensations, absolutions even from the most
serious sins, and other immunities and privileges of this kind, and cannot be
read without horror. Who would say that these things arise from the character
and disposition of the true church of Christ and from the doctrine of Simon
Peter, who, according to Christ’s command, gave freely what he had freely
received (Matthew 10:8), rather than from the disposition of Simon Magus, who
thought the gift of God could be acquired with money (Acts 8:18), and from the
nature of Babylon, which conducts the foulest traffic in sacred things and
human souls (Revelation 18:13), whose entire kingdom is sheer commerce designed
to make a profit and scrape together wealth from every direction? See
Espensæus’s complaint concerning this in his work on Titus 1.

XIX. Many other things could be brought forward to show the Roman church’s
impurity in both doctrine and practice. Complaints about them are repeatedly
read in Bernard, Theodoric of Niem, Alvarus Pelagius, Petrarch, Nicholas of
Clémanges, Wessel Gansfort, Mantuanus, and others who wrote about that church’s
utterly corrupt condition. The same could be confirmed from the permission of
the most foul brothels which exist at Rome, and the most shameful profit
gathered from them through the taxes exacted

and transferred into the church’s treasury, although God once prohibited
bringing “the hire of a prostitute and the price of a dog into the gift of God,
because these things are alike an abomination to Jehovah” (Deuteronomy 23:18).
I pass over the countless other abominable lusts, fit to be expiated by fire
alone, which rage there with impunity and, I shudder to relate, are even
publicly extolled with praises in published books. The writers cited above do
not remain silent about these, and they are mentioned in the *Response to the
History of Calvinism*, part 1, chapter 10. This could be further proved from the
Jesuits’ impure moral theology, which through the doctrines of probability,
direction of intention, attrition, and other principles of this kind appears
wholly designed to diminish and excuse every crime. Thus cushions are sewn
under sinners’ arms so that they may more readily indulge their inclinations
and rush headlong into any sins, as is shown at length in the *Provincial
Letters*, the *Censure of Amadæus Guimenius and Vernantius*, and other writings
published against them. But what has been said can suffice to show that the
Roman church’s doctrine in various heads opposes piety and good morals.

[Marginalia: 6. Because it opposes liberty through tyranny.]

XX. Sixth, because it *opposes the liberty* Christ acquired for us through the
savage *tyranny* which it introduces and exercises over both bodies and souls,
and which is most foreign [the Latin prints *alienissma*, sic] to the spirit of
Christianity and Christ’s doctrine. That doctrine wishes “faith to be
persuaded, not commanded,” and its “weapons are not carnal, but mighty through
God for destroying strongholds and taking every thought captive to the
obedience of Christ” (2 Corinthians 10:4–5). The apostles founded and propagated
religion not by force and sword, but by the light of truth alone and the
strength of unconquered power; not by killing, but by dying; not by inflicting
persecution, but by suffering it and bearing the cross after Christ. Ancient
Christians also followed this path, and the sounder adversaries have repeatedly
testified that it must be followed, admitting “war is not a lawful means for
removing schism from the church,” as the eminent Thuanus says in his preface.
The matter must be conducted with reasons and a demonstration of the truth by
which consciences may be convinced, not with force which compels bodies. We
must fight with arguments, not weapons; error must be slain by the sword of the
divine mouth, not human beings killed by the sword’s edge. But the mind of the
Roman church and the pontiffs is different. Distrusting the goodness of their
cause, they have thought no means more effective for establishing their
tyrannical rule than violence and cruelty, raging everywhere with sword and
flames, slaughter and blood, and savaging the faithful in the most dreadful
persecutions. Even if we remain silent, the many utterly destructive tortures
instituted against the Albigenses and Waldenses cry too loudly for this to be
unknown; the horrible massacres practiced upon the faithful with treachery more
than Punic and savagery more than bestial in France, Calabria, Bohemia,
Piedmont, Ireland, Spain, the Low Countries, and many other places; the
countless most cruel punishments inflicted upon so many myriads of saints; and
the thousand arts of doing harm by which, both long ago and still today, the
Congregation for the Propagation of the Faith and the Extirpation of Heretics
strives to destroy Christ’s genuine disciples solely from hatred of the truth
and religion they profess. In their number must be placed the bloody counsels
suggested to princes for religion’s destruction and the deadly edicts extracted
from them, by which, contrary to the public faith of treaties, privileges once
granted and repeatedly confirmed are taken from the faithful, temples are
demolished, flocks dispersed, pastors driven into exile or condemned to death,
and the liberty of sacred exercises and consciences—far dearer than all the
most precious things—is seized from them. If to these we add tyranny over
consciences through the continual imposition of the intolerable yoke of various
human traditions and heterodoxies, through the thunderbolts of the most unjust
anathemas, snares cast upon souls, the torture chamber of the Spanish
Inquisition, and countless other instruments

[1847 vol. III printed p. 114 | IA leaf 148 | PDF p. 148]

of raging violence, we will readily conclude that no sect, not even paganism,
was ever more cruel. This is proved fully and accurately by the celebrated
author of the *Response to Maimbourg’s History of Calvinism*, part 2, in the
*History of Papism*, recriminations 1 and 2.

[Marginalia: 7. Because the Antichrist sits in it.]

XXI. Seventh, because *the Antichrist sits in it*, the author of the great
apostasy described by the apostle (2 Thessalonians 2). While under the pretext
of his vicariate he professes himself to be “in place of Christ,” by impiously
usurping his Lord’s authority he reveals himself truly to be “against Christ,”
his Lord’s rival and enemy, “the one opposing and exalting himself,” who, sitting
in God’s temple as if he were God, exalts himself above everything called God,
namely, the emperors, kings, and princes of the earth and the departed saints in
heaven, and presents himself as God. A comparison of the two makes it easy to
gather that all these criteria of the Antichrist are found in the Roman pope,
as we proved fully in Disputation VII, *On Separation*.

[Marginalia: 8. Because it is Babylon.]

XXII. Eighth, because it is *mystical Babylon*, from which the godly are
commanded to come out (Revelation 18:4) as from a thoroughly corrupt society
which is diametrically opposed and incompatible with mystical Zion, the true
church of Christ. That this designates none other than Rome is proved by John’s
description, and the adversaries themselves do not deny it. John’s description
(Revelation 17) fits it alone exactly, especially in the two characteristics by
which he marks it: that it is the *seven-hilled city*, which “sits upon seven
mountains” (verse 9), and that it “holds rule over the kings of the earth”
(verse 10). It is established that Rome is seven-hilled and that in John’s time
no city other than Rome was the mistress of the world, the world’s capital, and
the queen of nations, which is therefore called by the Greeks “the ruling
city.” Bellarmine acknowledges this in *On the Roman Pontiff*, book 2, chapter
2: “Throughout the Apocalypse John calls Rome Babylon, as Tertullian observed,
and as is openly gathered from Revelation 17, where great Babylon is said to
sit upon seven mountains and to hold rule over the kings of the earth. For in
John’s time no city other than Rome held rule over the kings of the earth, and
it is very well known that it was built upon seven hills.” Baronius, at the year
45; Ribera on Revelation 14, numbers 27 and 30; Viegas on Revelation 17;
Lessius; Cagnæus; and not a few others agree with him.

XXIII. Nor can they escape by saying that pagan Rome, not papal Rome, is meant,
or Rome as it will fall back into paganism. A mere reading of the words proves
the contrary and teaches that things are attributed to this Babylon which do
not at all fit pagan Rome but fit papal Rome perfectly: that it would make the
nations and kings of the earth drunk and bewitch them with the golden cup of
its fornications (Revelation 17:2; 18:3); that they would fornicate with it;
that it would traffic and profit in souls (Revelation 18:13); that it bears the
name of *mystery* inscribed upon its forehead (Revelation 17:5); that after its
destruction it must become a dwelling of demons (Revelation 18:2); that the
kings of the earth who fornicated with it must lament its destruction
(Revelation 18:9); that it boasts in its infallibility and majesty as “a queen
who will not see mourning” (Revelation 18:7); that it must bear the horns of the
Lamb and have the mouth of the dragon (Revelation 13:11); that it must be drunk
with the saints’ blood (Revelation 17:6); that in it are found the bodies of the
Lord’s prophets; and other things of this kind which are so clear in themselves
that they scarcely, if at all, need application. Especially should we add that
John was greatly amazed when he saw it (Revelation 17:6). There was no reason
for amazement in seeing pagan Rome worshiping idols or persecuting saints. But
when he understood that Rome, converted to Christ and styling itself the mother
and teacher of all churches, was represented to him in this vision, then he
rightly marveled that the see they call apostolic would not only become

[1847 vol. III printed p. 115 | IA leaf 149 | PDF p. 149]

apostate, but would so degenerate from its former piety that it would become in
the Christian world the mother and teacher of all superstitions and
fornications, the cause of persecutions against God’s holy martyrs, and the seat
of the Antichrist himself. Just as ancient pagan Rome cannot be understood by
it, neither can the new Rome which Lessius and Ribera imagined will fall back
into paganism under the Antichrist. This fiction is not only *unwritten*, having
no foundation in Scripture [the Latin prints *Scripturo*, sic], but also
*contrary to Scripture*, because the Antichrist had to establish his rule in
Christianity and sit in God’s temple, as was said. It is therefore rejected by
Justinianus, Suarez, Malvenda, and others. Elsewhere, in Disputation VI, *On
Separation*, we showed how aptly Rome is designated by Babylon in its pomp and
pride, tyranny and cruelty, errors and idolatry, impurity and filth, and other
attributes of this kind. It suffices to note this one point now: because it is
such, we cannot hold it to be a true church, but must hold it to be false and
apostate. Just as our ancestors had the most just causes to separate from it,
so all who rightly decide that Christ must be preferred to the Antichrist,
rational worship in spirit and truth to idolatrous and superstitious worship,
evangelical and orthodox truth to erroneous and heretical doctrines, and the
holy liberty Christ acquired for us by his blood to intolerable tyranny, are
bound by the duty of conscience to renounce it and divorce themselves from it.

[Marginalia: Sources of the solutions.]

XXIV. It is one thing to retain *the foundation alone* without addition, *the
whole* without subtraction, and *the pure and uncorrupted* without corruption;
it is another to retain it mixed, mutilated, and adulterated. A church which
retains the foundation in the former sense is a true church, but not one which
retains it only in the latter sense, as does the Roman church. Although it
professes to retain the foundation, it does not retain it *alone*, because
besides the fundamental heads about which there is agreement between us, the
adversaries add very many other articles concerning things to be believed and
done and command them as necessary to faith and morals under pain of anathema.
It does not retain the *whole*, because they remove various heads from it; nor
the *pure*, because they corrupt it in different ways. What benefit is it to
retain certain heads of Christianity if other heads of antichristianity are
added; to set out wholesome food if it is infected by added poison; to possess
certain truths but more errors? 2. It is one thing to retain *something of the
true church*, another to *be a true church absolutely*. In the same way it is
one thing for a body to possess some healthy parts, another for the body to be
healthy absolutely. The Pharisaic church [the Latin prints *Ecclesiæ
Pharisaica*, sic] retained some things belonging to the
true church in Christ’s time, yet was not therefore a true church. The same
judgment applies to the Arian, Donatist, and other factious churches, whose
baptism and ordinations the catholic Christians did not repeat.

XXV. It is one thing to retain the Creed, the Lord’s Prayer, and the Decalogue,
in which the substance of the Christian religion is contained, only with a
literal faith, not a mental one, on the lips and as to the words and profession;
it is another to retain them with a sincere heart as to their meaning and
truth. The Roman church can be said to hold them in the former manner, not the
latter, because it sins in many ways in its exposition of these heads. Nor is
the inference valid from profession of all the Apostles’ Creed’s articles to
the truth of a church, not even according to the papists. They do not wish us
to be a true church although we receive the entire Creed; nor are the Socinians
regarded as a true church by either the papists or us although they profess the
Creed, because they retain it in words, not in meaning.

XXVI. If certain of our writers have said that the fundamentals remain in
papism, they did not therefore judge the Roman church to be a true church in
which salvation can be obtained,

[1847 vol. III printed p. 116 | IA leaf 150 | PDF p. 150]

for with so much zeal they defend the contrary, urging separation from it as a
matter of the utmost necessity for salvation. They intended only to demonstrate
the truth of our faith from the fact that, with respect to purely affirmative
articles, we believe nothing which the papists do not likewise retain. Thus it
is unnecessary to occupy ourselves with proving our faith where the adversaries
do not dispute it. Nevertheless, they establish a great difference both in the
manner in which these fundamentals are retained on the two sides and in the
negative articles, in which we differ through and through.

XXVII. The validity of *baptism* indeed proves the truth of a church with
respect to Christianity in general, in opposition to assemblies of unbelievers,
but not with respect to a pure Christianity cleansed of the errors of heretics.
True baptism can be found among heretics who are not a true church, just as true
circumcision and sacrifices consecrated to the one God existed in the church of
the ten tribes, which was not therefore a true church. Nor can the adversaries
deny this, since they acknowledge our baptism as true but nevertheless deny us
the name of the true church.

XXVIII. Although the Roman church was once a true church in Paul’s time and
the following ages, it does not follow that it was always true and is still so
today. No particular visible church has been granted the privilege of
infallibility, and a comparison of the two churches establishes that the
present-day church has fallen away from the faith of the ancient one and
introduced countless capital errors into religion, both theoretical and
practical, which conflict diametrically with the doctrine delivered to the
Romans by the apostle and received and believed by them.

XXIX. We do not deny that where there is a *pure and uncorrupted ministry*, at
least in fundamentals, there is also a true church. But this does not hold for
a *corrupt and impure ministry*, such as we assert the papal ministry to be.

XXX. The plea of *long possession* which the papists allege to demonstrate the
truth of their church and avoid examination of their doctrine cannot grant
them a sufficient title, even if in law the possessor’s condition is preferred.
1. Nothing can prescribe against the truth, nor can possession of false
doctrine make its profession lawful. A falsehood is always falsehood, whatever
antiquity may commend it, and a heresy, however old, is no less damnable. Nor
can or should things which might have a place in civil and positive law obtain
likewise with respect to divine and natural law. The rationale of an estate and
earthly possession is one thing; that of articles of faith and religious
doctrines another. In the former prescription through long possession has a
place, so that ownership may be certain and lawsuits not eternal. But it does
not in the latter, where the matter concerns divine and indispensable law
respecting heavenly truth and the salvation of souls. Whatever time has passed
since the rise of an error, it is always lawful to oppose it. 2. By this plea
every religion throughout the world and every heresy and superstition would be
safe. After a long time they would remain certain and fixed by law itself, with
all later dispute, scrutiny, and examination about them removed. Neither Christ
nor the apostles would have had the right to oppose pagan idolatry, and the
Gentiles could with equal right as the papists urge their own long possession
to demonstrate the truth of their religions, as they repeatedly do in
Tertullian, Cyprian, Origen, Minucius, and Arnobius. 3. A possessor requires a
just title or just cause of possession. But this is the very question between
us and the papists: by what right do popes, cardinals, and priests hold the
power they claim for themselves? Was it given by Christ or the apostles? Where
and when was it done? 4. Possession must be peaceful to establish prescription;
otherwise, if contradiction and opposition intervene, prescription is thereby
removed. Yet as for

[1847 vol. III printed p. 117 | IA leaf 151 | PDF p. 151]

the rights usurped by the pope, the ecumenical pontificate, supreme power over
kings and emperors, clerical celibacy, the worship of images, invocation of
saints, the bodily presence of Christ in the Eucharist, and other Roman errors
of this kind, who is so ignorant as not to know, or so shameless as to deny,
that they have been publicly and repeatedly contradicted in both the eastern
and western worlds? 5. Since the origin of every lawful possession in religion
must be traced back to the first institution of Christ and the apostles,
possession for many ages is alleged in vain unless one ascends to the first age
and shows that it already obtained in the time of Christ and the apostles.

XXXI. Although we acknowledge that before the Reformation the godly could
obtain salvation in the Roman church, it does not follow that that visible
church, as papal, was true. Although they remained in it bodily with respect to
their location, they did not remain in mind with respect to faith, separating
from it negatively if not positively. Further, the rationale of their times is
one thing, that of ours another. Even if negative separation could suffice for
them, it cannot also suffice for us in so great a light of truth and liberty,
as was said above.

XXXII. Although Rome boasts that it is the only true church, like the Jews who
cried “The temple of the Lord” (Jeremiah 7:4), it cannot therefore secure our
trust. No one can be judge in his own cause, and it is one thing falsely and
rashly to attribute something to oneself, another actually to possess it.
Whatever deceptive coloring and paints are applied to it—which the adversaries
try to do so diligently by presenting it to us in deceitful mirrors and
covering it with a false and empty mask—it can never truly be held to be
Christ’s holy and true bride.

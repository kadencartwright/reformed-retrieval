CHAPTER SIX

THE BLESSEDNESS of rational creatures is enjoyment of the Highest Good,
namely God. Or, what comes to the same thing, it is rational creatures’
communion with God the Father, the Son, and the Holy Spirit, who alone is
truly their Highest Good and makes them eternally blessed. It is called God’s
salvation. Psalm 50:23: “He who sacrifices praise honors me, and to him who
orders his way I will grant enjoyment of God’s salvation.” Jeremiah 3:18:
“Surely Israel’s salvation is in Jehovah our God.” First John 1:3: “So that
you too may have communion with us; and our communion is with the Father and
with his Son Jesus Christ.”

[1615 printed locator H]

It has two parts: freedom from every evil and possession of all true goods
which rational creatures enjoy in God. Such goods are the vision of God,
conformity with God, sufficiency in God, and certain knowledge of their own
eternal felicity.

The vision of God is knowledge of God the Father, the Son, and the Holy
Spirit through a supernatural light kindled by the Holy Spirit in the minds
of rational creatures to make them alive with everlasting life.

This is the first and chief part of blessedness. Hence blessedness is
commonly defined as the vision of God, or as the perfect act

[1615 printed locator I]

by which God is known.

In Christ’s soul this vision is as open and perfect as can belong to a
rational creature, so that with ineffable clarity and perfection Christ’s
soul sees God face to face and is blessed by that vision of God.

Christ’s soul was indeed made blessed with uncreated blessedness by the
personal union itself with the Word (*λόγος*), but it could not have been
blessed with created blessedness without the vision of God. Its vision,
however, differs from the vision by which the Deity sees himself. 1. The
Deity sees himself *ὅλην καὶ ὅλως*, whole and wholly; Christ’s soul sees the
whole Deity, indeed, but not

[1615 printed locator K]

wholly. 2. The Deity sees in himself all things, even those which will never
come to be, provided they are not simply impossible; but Christ’s soul sees
in the Deity only all things which have been, are, and will be. 3. The Deity
sees himself and all things in himself with clarity absolutely infinite,
because he sees by uncreated and therefore infinite light, that is, by his
own essence; but Christ’s soul sees with finite clarity, because it sees by a
created and finite light.

In the angels likewise the chief part of blessedness is the vision of God,
and in them it is always clear. Matthew 18:10: “See that you do not despise
one of these little ones, for I tell you that their angels in heaven behold
the face of my Father who is in heaven at all times.” Through this vision
they see and know God’s essence—not, to be sure, the whole of it as Christ’s
soul does, much less wholly and in every manner, but according to their
capacity. For finite minds do not fully grasp what is infinite, nor can what
is created comprehend the Creator plainly and perfectly.

As for man, the vision of God is likewise necessary to his blessedness and
is its primary part. This vision certainly is true wisdom, of which Proverbs
3:13 says, “Blessed is the man who attains wisdom and the man who advances
understanding”; and verse 18, “Everyone who takes hold of her is blessed.”
Matthew 16:16–17: “Simon Peter answered and said, You are the Christ, that Son
of the living God. Then Jesus answered and said to him, Blessed are you,
Simon Bar-Jonah, because flesh and blood did not reveal this to you, but my
Father who is in heaven.” John 17:3: “This is eternal life, that they know
you, the only true God, and Jesus Christ whom you sent.”

The vision of God in man, however, is either obscure or clear.

The obscure vision of God belongs to this life, while the soul still exists
in a mortal body and knows God through faith, as through a riddle and a
mirror. First Corinthians 13:12: “For now we see through a mirror and through
a riddle, but then we will see face to face. Now I know to some degree, but
then I will know more fully, according as I will have been more fully taught.”

The clear vision of God belongs to the other life, when the soul, both while
separated from the body and when reunited to it after the universal
resurrection, will know God face to face. Thus First Corinthians 13:12; First
John 3:2: “We will see him as he is”; and Revelation 22:3–4: “The throne of
God and the Lamb will be in it, and his servants will worship him. And they
will see his face.”

So much for the vision of God.

Conformity with God is that by which a rational creature is like God in true
wisdom, righteousness, and holiness. First John 1:6–7: “If we say that we
have communion with him and walk in darkness, we lie and do not act
sincerely. But if we walk in the light, as he is in the light, we have mutual
communion with him.” And chapter 3:2: “Beloved, now we are children of God,
but what we will be has not yet been manifested. We know, however, that when
he has been manifested, we will be like him.”

Sufficiency in God is that by which a rational creature so rests in God and
is so content with him that it seeks nothing outside him and desires nothing
more. Psalm 33:12: “Blessed is that nation whose God is Jehovah.” Psalm 62:2:
“My soul rests in God alone; my salvation is from him.” And verse 6: “My soul
rests in God alone, for my expectation is from him.” Psalm 73:25–26: “Whom
would I have in heaven besides you? Besides you I delight in no one on earth.
Though my flesh and my heart fail, God is the rock of my heart and my portion
forever.” *αὐτάρκεια*—sufficiency.

Certain knowledge of its own eternal felicity is that by which a rational
creature not only knows its felicity but also knows with certainty and rests
secure that it will be blessed forever. Romans 8:35–39: “Who will separate us
from Christ’s love? Will affliction, or anguish, or persecution, or famine,
or nakedness, or danger, or sword? (As it is written, For your sake we are
killed all day; we are reckoned as sheep destined for slaughter.) Rather, in
all these things we are more than conquerors through him who loved us. For I
am persuaded that neither death nor life, neither angels nor principalities
nor powers, neither things present nor things to come, neither height nor
depth, nor any other created thing can separate us from God’s love, which is
in Christ Jesus our Lord.”

This blessedness is spiritual because it consists in goods that properly
belong to the mind, which is spirit. It is divine because it comes from God
alone and concerns the enjoyment of God. And it belongs properly to the
Church, that is, to the holy angels and the men elected to eternal life.
Psalm 33:12: “Blessed is that nation whose God is Jehovah, the people he
chose as a possession for himself.” Psalm 144:15: “Blessed is the people for
whom it is thus; blessed, therefore, is the people whose God is Jehovah.” Song
of Songs 6:8: “They will proclaim her blessed.” Malachi 3:12: “Therefore all
nations will proclaim you blessed.”

It alone is true, solid, and eternal.

It is begun in regenerate men in this life and will be perfected in the
other. Revelation 20:6: “Blessed and holy is he who has a part in the first
resurrection. The second death has no power over him, but they will be priests
of God and Christ.” And chapter 22:14: “Blessed are those who perform his
commandments, so that they may have a right to the tree of life and enter the
city through the gates.”

The efficient cause by which we become its participants is the free
forgiveness of sins on account of Christ’s righteousness imputed to us by
God. Psalm 32:1–2 and Romans 4:7–8: “Blessed is he relieved of transgression,
whose sin is covered. Blessed is the man to whom Jehovah does not impute
iniquity and in whose spirit there is no deceit.”

Its instrumental cause is saving faith. Luke 1:45: “Blessed is she who
believed.” John 20:29: “Blessed are those who did not see and believed.”

The infallible sign which shows that a man is blessed is threefold. The first
is effectual calling to Christ, through which we are members of God’s true and
pure Church and participants in the goods God dispenses in his Church. Psalm
65:5–6: “Blessed is he whom you choose and bring near to dwell in your courts.
We are satisfied with the good of your house, the holy things of your temple.
In righteousness you speak awesome things to us, O God of our salvation,
confidence of all the ends of the earth and of the distant seas.”

[1615 BSB witness | digital canvas 34 | printed p. 10]

Psalm 84:11: “One day in your courts is better than a thousand

[1615 printed locator A]

elsewhere. I choose to frequent the threshold in the house of my God rather
than live in the tents of wickedness.”

The second sign is zeal to avoid sins and do the good works commanded by God.
Psalm 1:1–2: “Blessed is that man who does not walk in the counsel of the
wicked, does not stand in the path of sinners, and does not sit in the company
of mockers, but whose delight is in Jehovah’s law and who meditates on his law
by day and night.” Psalm 2:12: “Blessed are all who trust in him.” Psalm 40:5:
“Blessed is that man who puts his trust in Jehovah and does not look to the
proud or to those

[1615 printed locator B]

turning aside to falsehood.” Psalm 84:6: “Blessed is the man who has strength
in you to walk your paths according to his heart’s desire.” And verse 13: “O
Jehovah of hosts, blessed is the man who trusts in you.” Psalm 89:16: “Blessed
is the people who recognize the trumpet sound, who walk in the light of your
face, Jehovah.” Psalm 112:1: “Blessed is everyone who reveres Jehovah and
delights greatly in his commandments.” Psalm 119:1–2: “Blessed are the
blameless in their way, who walk in Jehovah’s law. Blessed are those who keep
his testimonies, seeking him with their whole heart.” Proverbs 8:32, 34:
“Blessed are those who observe my ways. Blessed is the man who listens to me,
diligently attending at my doors daily and watching the posts of my
entrances.” Proverbs 14:21:

[1615 printed locator C]

“He who shows favor to the poor—oh, blessed is he!” Proverbs 16:20: “He who
trusts in Jehovah—oh, blessed is he!” Proverbs 28:14: “Blessed is the man who
is always exceedingly afraid, but he who hardens his heart will fall into
evil.” Proverbs 29:18: “When there is no vision, the people are stripped bare,
but he who observes the law—oh, blessed is he!” Isaiah 30:18: “Jehovah is a
God of judgment; blessed are all who wait for him.” Isaiah 56:2: “Blessed is
the mortal who does righteousness and the son of man who lays hold of it,
keeping the Sabbath so as not to profane it and keeping his hand from doing
any evil.” Matthew 5:3, 5–8: “Blessed are the poor in spirit, for theirs is the
kingdom of heaven. Blessed are the meek, for they will possess the earth by
right of inheritance. Blessed are those who hunger and thirst for
righteousness, for they will be satisfied. Blessed are the merciful, for
mercy will be given to them. Blessed are the pure in heart, for they will see
God.”

[1615 printed locator D]

Luke 11:28: “Blessed are those who hear God’s word and observe it.” John
13:17: “If you know these things, you will be blessed if you do them.” James
1:25: “But he who has looked into that perfect law of liberty and continued
in it, because he was not a forgetful hearer but a doer of the work—he, I say,
will be blessed in his deed.” Revelation 1:3: “Blessed is he who reads, and
blessed are those who hear the words of this prophecy and observe the things
written in it.” And chapter 16:15:

[1615 printed locator E]

“Behold, I come like a thief. Blessed is he who watches and keeps his garments,
lest he walk naked and his shame be seen.” And chapter 22:7: “Behold, I come
quickly. Blessed is he who observes the words of the prophecy of this book.”

The third sign is the cross and affliction, by which God at one time
fatherly chastens the godly, at another tests them, and at another makes them
martyrs to his truth. Psalm 94:12–13: “Blessed is that man whom you chasten,
Jah, and whom you teach from your law, to give him rest from the days of
adversity while a pit is dug for the wicked.” James 1:12: “Blessed is the man
who endures temptation,” and so forth. And chapter 5:11: “Behold, we proclaim
those blessed who endure adversities.

[1615 printed locator F]

You have heard of Job’s endurance and have seen the Lord’s end, for the Lord
abounds in tender mercy and is compassionate.” Matthew 5:4, 10–11: “Blessed
are those who mourn, for they will receive comfort. Blessed are those whom men
persecute for righteousness’ sake, for theirs is the kingdom of heaven.
Blessed will you be when they revile and persecute you and, lying, say every
evil thing against you because of me.” First Peter 3:14: “But even if you
suffer anything for righteousness, you are blessed.” And chapter 4:14: “If
you are reviled in Christ’s name, you are blessed.”

Besides this, however, there is a certain temporary and bodily blessedness of
men which concerns the enjoyment of goods belonging to this bodily life.

[1615 printed locator G]

It is called human blessedness because it consists in things which pertain to
this human life and have a place in this life as an *ἐπίσαγμα*, a kind of
addition that serves for the consolation of the godly. It is described
throughout Sacred Scripture; in the Psalms and elsewhere, for example, these
parts of bodily felicity are proclaimed.

I. Nobility and distinction of family and birth. Proverbs 17:6: “Fathers are
the ornament of their children.” Hence the genealogies of the godly are
recounted throughout Scripture with praise. Christ himself and the apostles
also grant the Jews the honor of their descent from Abraham according to the
flesh. John 8:37: “I know that you are Abraham’s seed.” Acts 3:25: “You are
children of the prophets and of the covenant God made with our fathers,
saying to Abraham, And in your seed all the families of the earth will obtain
a blessing.” Romans 9:4–5: “They are Israelites, to whom belong the adoption,
the glory, the covenants,” and so forth, “to whom belong the fathers and from
whom is Christ according to the flesh.” Legitimacy of birth also belongs
here, since those born from a dishonorable bed are barred from public offices
and honors. Deuteronomy 23:2: “A bastard shall not enter Jehovah’s assembly;
even his tenth generation shall not enter Jehovah’s assembly.”

[1615 printed locator H]

II. The fame and distinction of one’s fatherland or native soil. Psalm
127:4–5: “How could we have sung Jehovah’s song in the land of foreigners? If
I forget you, O Jerusalem, let my right hand forget itself.”

[1615 printed locator I]

III. A good education. Proverbs 3:13: “Blessed is the man who attains wisdom
and the man who advances understanding.” First Kings 10:8: “Blessed are your
men; blessed are these servants of yours who stand before you continually and
hear your wisdom.” Ecclesiasticus 25:12–13: “Blessed is he who has attained
prudence and recounts it in the ears of hearers. How great is he who has
attained wisdom! But no one surpasses

[1615 printed locator K]

the one who reveres the Lord.” And chapter 50:28–29: “Jesus son of Sirach of
Jerusalem has inscribed instruction in understanding and knowledge in this
book; he poured out a shower of wisdom from his heart. Blessed is he who
occupies himself with these things, and he who puts them in his heart will be
wise.”

IV. The necessary supports of this life. Psalm 33:19: “To rescue their soul
from death and preserve them alive in famine.” Psalm 36:8–9: “How precious is
your lovingkindness, God, when the sons of men take refuge under the shadow
of your wings. They are abundantly filled with the richness of your house,
and you give them drink from the river of your delights.” Psalm 37:11: “The
meek will possess the earth by right of inheritance and delight themselves in
an abundance of peace.” Verses 21–22: “The wicked borrows but cannot repay;
the righteous man, however, shows favor and gives. For those blessed by him
possess the earth by right of inheritance, but those cursed by him are cut
off.” Verses 25–26: “I was young and have also grown old, but I have not seen
the righteous so forsaken that his seed sought bread. All day he shows favor
and lends, and his seed is blessed.” Verse 29: “The righteous will possess the
earth by right of inheritance and dwell upon it forever.” Matthew 5:5:
“Blessed are the meek, for they will possess the earth by right of
inheritance.” And chapter 6:33: “Seek first God’s kingdom and his
righteousness, and all these things will be added to you.”

V. A happy marriage. Ecclesiasticus 26:1: “Blessed is the husband of a good
wife, and the number of his days is doubled.” Nevertheless, celibacy, if
someone is adorned by God with the gift of continence, is placed among no
small part of blessedness. First Corinthians 7:40.

VI. The increase and prosperity of one’s family and descendants. Thus Leah,
the wife of the patriarch Jacob, says in Genesis 30:13: “It is well with me,
for women will proclaim me blessed.” Psalm 112:2: “His seed will be mighty on
earth; the generation of the upright will be blessed.” Psalm 127:3–5:
“Behold, children are Jehovah’s possession; the fruit of the womb is a reward.
As arrows in a mighty man’s hand, so are sons. Blessed is that man who has
filled his quiver with them. They will not be ashamed but will destroy their
enemies in the gate.” Psalm 128:2–3: “You will be blessed, and it will be well
with you. Your wife will be like a fruitful vine at the sides of your house;
your children will stand around your table like olive plants.” Psalm 144:12:
“May our sons be like well-nurtured plants in their youth, and our daughters
like carved cornerstones, the structure of a temple.” Proverbs 17:6:
“Children’s children are the crown of old men.” Proverbs 20:7: “The righteous
man who walks continually in his integrity is blessed; blessed are his
children after him.” Isaiah 65:23: “They will not beget children for
disturbance, for they will be Jehovah’s blessed seed and their offspring with
them.” Yet not just any children and descendants are to be counted among
human blessedness, but wise and godly ones, not born for disturbance.
Otherwise, “He who begets a fool begets sorrow for himself, and the father of
a fool will not rejoice,” Proverbs 17:21. And “A foolish son is an indignation
to his father and bitterness to his mother,” Proverbs 17:25.

VII. Increase of power and riches. Psalm 112:3: “Wealth and riches are in his
house, and his righteousness endures forever.” Psalm 144:13–15: “May our
storehouses, being full, continually bring forth food; may our flocks bear by
thousands and increase by myriads in our folds. May our cattle be very fat;
may there be no invasion, no going out, and finally no complaint in our
streets. Blessed is the people for whom it is thus.”

VIII. Preservation, comfort, and help in adversity. Psalm 41:2–3: “Blessed is
he who attends to the weakened man, saying, In the time of evil Jehovah will
deliver him. Jehovah will preserve him and restore him to life; he will be
made blessed on earth. Likewise, do not hand him over to his enemies’ desire.”
Psalm 112:4: “Light arises in darkness for the righteous; the righteous man
is gracious and merciful.” Deuteronomy 33:29: “You are blessed, Israel. Who is
like you, a people saved by Jehovah, your helpful shield, whose sword is your
excellence? Thus your enemies will be humbled before you, but you will tread
upon their high places.” Daniel 12:12: “Blessed is he who waits and reaches
one thousand three hundred and thirty-five days.”

IX. Peaceful government and comfortable habitation under a prudent and good
prince or magistrate. Ecclesiastes 10:7, or verse 14 in Tremellius’s version:
“O blessed land, whose king is born of the most illustrious and whose princes
eat at the proper time for strength and not for drinking.” Ecclesiasticus
25:11: “Blessed is he who lives with an understanding wife, does not stumble
with his tongue, and does not serve a man unworthy of him.”

X. Security from fear and trembling, troubles and bodily miseries. Psalm
112:7–8: “He does not fear an evil report, his heart erect and trusting in
Jehovah. His heart is sustained; he does not fear until he sees evil upon his
enemies.” First Corinthians 7:40: “But she is more blessed if she remains so,
according to my judgment, and I think that I too have God’s Spirit.”

XI. Joy flowing from the enjoyment of our labors and the goods God has given.
Ecclesiastes 2:23: “There is no good in man except that he eat and drink and
make his heart enjoy good from his labor. I also saw that this is from God’s
own hand.” And verse 27: “For to the man who appears good in his sight, God
gives wisdom and knowledge with joy.” Acts 14:17: “God fills our hearts with
food and gladness.” Isaiah 65:13–14: “Thus says the Lord Elohim: Behold, my
servants will eat, but you will hunger. Behold, my servants will drink; they
will rejoice and sing for gladness of heart.” Verses 21–22: “Those who build
houses will inhabit them, and those who plant vineyards will eat their fruit.
They will not build and another inhabit; they will not plant and another
eat.” Here also belongs joy flowing from the destruction of God’s and the
Church’s enemies. Psalm 112:8: “His heart is sustained; he does not fear until
he sees evil upon his enemies.” Ecclesiasticus 25:10: “The man who rejoices in
his children is blessed, as is he who lives to see the ruin of his enemies.”

XII. Long life. Exodus 20:12: “So that your days may be prolonged upon that
land which Jehovah your God gives you.” Isaiah 65:22: “As the days of a tree,
so will be the days of my people.”

XIII. A peaceful bodily death in the faith of Christ. Revelation 14:13:
“Blessed from this time are the dead who die for the Lord’s cause. Yes, says
the Spirit, so that they may rest from their labors, and their works follow them.”
Isaiah 57:1–2: “Upright men are taken away with no one noticing; the righteous
man is taken away before the coming of evil, enters peace, and rests in his
bed.”

XIV. Perpetual glory and an honorable reputation remaining even after death
because of piety, virtue, and deeds rightly done. Psalm 112:6: “Because he is
not moved forever, the righteous man is in perpetual remembrance.” Verse 9:
“He distributes and gives to the needy; his righteousness endures forever;
his horn is exalted with honor.”

[1615 printed locator B]

Proverbs 31:28–31 says of a woman who manages her household well and reveres
God: “Her children rise and proclaim her blessed; her husband likewise
praises her, saying, Many women have acted valiantly, but you surpass them
all. Favor is deceitful and beauty is vain; the woman endowed with reverence
for Jehovah obtains praise for herself. Give her from the fruit of her hands,
and let her deeds praise her in the gates.” Revelation 21:24: “The nations
which have been saved will walk by its light, and the kings of the earth will
bring their glory and honor to it.”

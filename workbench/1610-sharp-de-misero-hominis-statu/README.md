# John Sharp, *Tractatus de misero hominis statu sub peccato* (1610)

> **Working edition begun:** This directory contains an AI-assisted,
> diplomatic-normalized Latin transcription and an independent close English
> translation. Both are unreviewed working files, not a published critical
> edition. Every page in Parts 01–02 was read directly from facsimile images; OCR
> was used only as a finding and drafting aid.

## Work identity

- **Author:** John Sharp (Johannes Scharpius, c. 1572–c. 1648)
- **Full title:** *Tractatus de misero hominis statu sub peccato, in duos
  libros distinctus, quorum priori de peccato eiusque poena, posteriori vero
  de viribus in homine per peccatum reliquis, seu de libero arbitrio agitur*
- **Edition:** Geneva, Pierre and Jacques Chouët, 1610
- **Extent:** 220 numbered pages, octavo
- **Structure:** Book I treats sin and its punishment; Book II treats the
  powers remaining in fallen humanity, or free choice.

## Editorial method

Long *s*, ligatures, line-end divisions, and typographic `u/v` are normalized.
Edition spelling, syntax, numbering, biblical references, Greek, and Hebrew
are retained. Running heads, signatures, catchwords, ornaments, and printed
page numbers are omitted except through markers. When a word crosses a page,
it is joined and the new marker precedes the complete word. The English marker
is placed at the corresponding translated phrase.

The English is a fresh translation from the accompanying Latin, checked
against the images. It is full text rather than summary and has not used a
modern English translation as a wording source.

## Part 01 boundary

Part 01 is the first coherent fifteen-page unit, printed pp. 21–35 / Google
Books `PA21`–`PA35`. It begins with Book I and includes all of Chapter I (*De
malo in genere*), all of Chapter II (*Divisio mali, ubi de malo culpa*), and
the opening positive account in Chapter III (*De causis peccati, et primo de
causa efficiente*). It ends after Sharp's eleven arguments that God is not the
author of sin, at the terminal clause `contrariamque naturam haberet, &c.` on
p. 35. The `Obiectiones` beginning on p. 36 are excluded.

- Latin: `parts/part-01-book-01-chapters-01-03a-latin.txt`
- English: `parts/part-01-book-01-chapters-01-03a-english.md`
- Both files contain fifteen synchronized markers: printed pp. 21–35 / Google
  Books `PA21`–`PA35`.
- Direct image control confirmed all pages, fourteen page joins, the Greek and
  Hebrew expressions, and the exact stop before the objections.
- The source numbers the five ways one actual sin causes another as I, II,
  III, IIII, V; that witnessed sequence is retained.
- Several printed biblical locators are anomalous (among them `Gen. 2.6`,
  `1. Ioh. 2.6`, `Rom. 1.12`, and `1. Ioh. 8.8`); they are transcribed as
  printed rather than silently emended.

## Part 02 boundary

Part 02 resumes at the `Obiectiones` on printed p. 36 and runs through printed
p. 51 / Google Books `PA36`–`PA51`. It completes Chapter III's objections on
the efficient cause of sin, includes all of Chapter IV on the material cause,
and closes with the complete Chapters V–VI on the formal and final causes. The
sixteenth page was retained to reach that authorial boundary: p. 51 ends
Chapter VI with `reatus & poena`, while p. 52 begins the ruled `CAPUT VII` and
a new division on the sin of angels.

- Latin: `parts/part-02-book-01-chapters-03b-06-latin.txt`
- English: `parts/part-02-book-01-chapters-03b-06-english.md`
- Both files contain sixteen synchronized markers: printed pp. 36–51 / Google
  Books `PA36`–`PA51`.
- All sixteen included pages and the excluded opening of p. 52 were checked
  directly against full-size images. The joins in *adiutor*, *Aegyptiorum*,
  *caecitas*, and *mortuus* place each new marker before the complete joined
  word and its corresponding English phrase.
- The Greek forms `ἀνομία`, `ἀνωμαλίαν`, `ἐνεργεῖ`, and the forms of
  `ἀταξία` were read from the images rather than inferred from OCR.
- Apparently anomalous printed biblical locators, including `Ioh. 1.13.27`,
  `Psal. 51.40`, `Matth. 15.29`, and `Heb. 5.9`, are retained without silent
  emendation.

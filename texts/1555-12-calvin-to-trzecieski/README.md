# Calvin to Andrzej Trzecieski, Letter 2372 (1555)

This directory presents the complete normalized Latin and a new English
translation of Calvin's appeal to Andrzej Trzecieski to translate Scripture
into Polish.

## Work identity

| Field | Value |
|---|---|
| Author | John Calvin (1509–1564) |
| Recipient | Andrzej Trzecieski the younger (c. 1525–1584), Polish humanist, poet, and Reformation supporter |
| CO number | Letter 2372 |
| Date and place | Geneva, late December 1555; place is authorial, date is editorial |
| Original language | Latin |
| Genre | Humanist and reform correspondence |
| Subject | A proposed Polish translation of the Bible; vocation, ability, and reform |

The letter is one item in a coordinated cluster written as Francis Lismanino
prepared to return to Poland. It is not a preface to a completed Bible and
does not establish that Trzecieski ever produced the translation Calvin
urges him to undertake.

## Edition and witness

The transcription follows Wilhelm Baum, Eduard Cunitz, and Eduard Reuss,
eds., *Ioannis Calvini opera quae supersunt omnia*, vol. XV (*Corpus
Reformatorum* XLIII; *Thesauri epistolici Calviniani* VI) (Brunswick: C. A.
Schwetschke, 1876), letter 2372, printed pp. 910–912.

CO reports a relatively recent apograph in Paris, Collection Dupuy 102, fol.
35v, the same codex and folio cited for letter 2365. This project transcribes
the CO edition state, not the Paris copy independently. The surviving text
includes an address and a closing place but no date or subscription.

The public facsimile is supplied by the University of Geneva's [complete
*Calvini Opera* collection](https://archive-ouverte.unige.ch/unige:650) and
its [direct CO XV PDF](https://access.archive-ouverte.unige.ch/access/metadata/b873ef04-63a3-4f30-8d07-f55388ab850e/download).
The relevant spreads are PDF pp. 468–469 in the captured surrogate. The
captured file was 47,262,286 bytes with SHA-256
`7cd272773162dd4b9a1b8f43e224647f42a6a0bb3045df2f79781fe9fa88ae77`.
UNIGE can regenerate the wrapper, so later whole-file hashes may differ.

## Scope and collation

`latin.txt` includes every authorial word printed by CO, from the address
*Generoso viro D. Andreae Tricesio* on p. 910 through the closing *Genevae*
on p. 912. It excludes the nineteenth-century letter number, editorial title
and summary, witness note, running furniture, and the biographical footnote
that continues below the letter.

The transcription was checked word for word against 600-DPI renders of both
relevant facsimile spreads. It removes printer line wrapping and joins
line-end divisions while retaining CO spelling and punctuation. The Greek
maxim is encoded in Unicode. Page markers reproduce the actual printed
boundaries: the address alone falls on p. 910, the prose begins on p. 911,
and p. 912 begins with *sic*, completing the final sentence of argument.
Nothing remains illegible or conjecturally supplied.

CO prints neither a day nor a month. Its chronological placement among the
letters carried or occasioned by Lismanino's departure supports “late
December 1555,” but that date is explicitly treated here as editorial. The
letter must not be cited as though Calvin wrote a full internal dateline.

## Translation status

No complete public-domain English version was located in the correspondence
audit. The English here is an **AI-assisted scholarly draft**, translated
afresh from the normalized Latin and checked against the complete facsimile.
It has not received independent review by a human Latinist. For exact-wording
claims, cite the Latin and CO page.

Suggested citation:

> John Calvin to Andrzej Trzecieski, late December 1555, letter 2372,
> *Calvini Opera* XV (1876), 910–912, normalized Latin and draft English
> translation, *Reformed Retrieval*.

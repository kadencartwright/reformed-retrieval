# Francis Turretin, *Institutio theologiae elencticae*

> **AI-assisted, unreviewed workbench edition:** This directory contains an
> incomplete diplomatic-normalized Latin transcription and a fresh English
> translation. Neither has been independently reviewed or approved for
> publication or quotation.

## Work identity

- **Author:** Francis Turretin (1623–1687)
- **Title:** *Institutio theologiae elencticae*
- **Original publication:** three parts, Geneva, 1679–1685
- **Edition used:** Edinburgh, John D. Lowe, 1847
- **Structure:** twenty theological *loci*, subdivided into questions

## Controlling witness and rights

The controlling witness for Part 01 is the Princeton Theological Seminary
Library copy of volume I digitized by the Internet Archive as
[`institutiotheolo01turr`](https://archive.org/details/institutiotheolo01turr).
The record contains 702 raw scan frames; its 700-page access PDF omits the two
color-card frames. It also supplies plain OCR, page-aware DjVu XML, and scan
metadata. The 1847 Latin edition and Turretin's seventeenth-century text are
public domain. Facsimile images, not OCR, govern the transcription.

The 1847 printing presents the work in four physical volumes while retaining
the original three-part structure. Part 01 lies in volume I, *Pars prima*,
which contains loci I–X. The other Internet Archive volume records will be
mapped in detail only as work reaches them; no claim of a complete four-volume
source audit is made here.

## Independence from modern English

The Giger/Dennison English edition (1992–1997) remains copyrighted and was not
consulted or used as a wording source. The English in this directory was
translated independently from the paired Latin transcription and checked
against the public-domain 1847 facsimile.

## Part 01 boundary

Part 01 contains the first complete topical sequence of Locus I, Questions
I–V, fifteen printed pages in all:

- printed pp. 3–4: Question I, on the word *theology*;
- printed pp. 4–7: Question II, whether theology exists and how it is divided;
- printed pp. 7–10: Question III, whether natural theology exists;
- printed pp. 10–16: Question IV, whether natural theology is sufficient for
  salvation; and
- printed pp. 16–17: Question V, on God and divine things as theology's
  object.

The unit begins at `LOCUS PRIMUS—DE THEOLOGIA` and `QUAESTIO PRIMA` on
printed p. 3 / IA leaf 57 / access-PDF p. 57. It ends with Question V at
`licet praecipuum ejus subjectum sit homo sanandus` on printed p. 17 / IA
leaf 71 / access-PDF p. 71. The next page, printed p. 18 / IA leaf 72 /
access-PDF p. 72, begins `QUAESTIO VI` and `DE THEOLOGIA GENERE` and is
excluded.

- Latin: `parts/part-01-locus-01-questions-01-05-latin.txt`
- English: `parts/part-01-locus-01-questions-01-05-english.md`
- Both files contain the same fifteen source markers, one for every printed
  page from 3 through 17.

## Editorial method

Latin long *s*, ligatures, line-end divisions, and typographic `u/v` are
normalized. Edition spelling, substantive wording, Greek, headings, biblical
references, and marginal section labels are retained. Running heads,
signatures, catchwords, page numbers outside the source markers, and ornaments
are omitted. At split-word page boundaries, the word is joined and the marker
is placed before the complete word.

The English is a full close translation rather than a summary. Its markers
repeat the Latin sequence and are placed at corresponding source boundaries.
Both files remain AI-assisted and unreviewed and require full comparison by a
qualified Latin editor before publication or quotation.

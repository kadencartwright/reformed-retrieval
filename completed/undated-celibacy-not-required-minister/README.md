# That Celibacy Is Not to Be Required of a Minister in This Way

This directory presents the complete Latin letter and a fresh English
translation of the item printed under the editorial title *Caelibatum in
ministro non ita requirendum esse* at CO X/1 226–229.

> **Editorial status:** This is an AI-assisted draft transcription and
> translation checked line by line against high-resolution facsimile images,
> not a peer-reviewed critical edition. A qualified Neo-Latinist should
> independently collate it before formal publication or reliance on a
> disputed reading.

| CO title | *Caelibatum in ministro non ita requirendum esse* (editorial, not part of the letter body) |
| Date | Undated; necessarily early if the statement “I have never married” is taken literally |
| Place | Not stated |
| Original language | Latin; CO gives no notice of translation from another language |
| Genre | Private ecclesiastical letter later transmitted among Calvin's *consilia* |
| Subjects | Ministerial celibacy and marriage, ecclesiastical discipline, a recently published confession, church order, unity |
| Printed locator | CO X/1, pp. 226–229 |

This is not a detached tract on compulsory celibacy. It begins with a personal
benediction, explains why Calvin writes although he does not know the recipient
by sight, praises the order of the recipient's churches, comments on “your
confession which was recently published,” and closes with a personal request
for greetings. Neither the confession, the recipient, nor the intermediary
represented by `N.` is identified in the CO main text. The letter has no
transmitted date, place, formal signature, or final *Vale*.

The autobiographical sentence *uxorem nunquam duxi: et an ducturus aliquando
sim, nescio* (“I have never married, and I do not know whether I shall ever
marry”) gives an early relative date. CO's own note merely says, *Vides hanc
epistolam antiquioribus adnumerandam esse*—“You see that this letter must be
numbered among the earlier ones.” This edition does not turn that inference
into an exact date.

## Edition and printed transmission

The transcription follows Wilhelm Baum, Eduard Cunitz, and Eduard Reuss,
eds., *Ioannis Calvini opera quae supersunt omnia*, vol. X, part 1, *Corpus
Reformatorum* XXXVIII (Brunswick: C. A. Schwetschke, 1871), pp. 226–229.

CO's title note records the earlier Geneva *Epistolae et Responsa*, p. 336;
Lausanne, p. 641; Hanover, p. 721; Chouet, p. 462; and Amsterdam, p. 211. It
does not identify an autograph, manuscript copy, named recipient, or textual
variants. The present edition therefore follows CO's printed main-text witness
and keeps work identity distinct from the nineteenth-century editorial title
and transmission.

## Authorial and editorial matter

[`latin.txt`](latin.txt) begins with *Gratia tibi et pax a Deo patre nostro*
and ends with *quos sperabis aequo animo salutem accepturos.* It includes the
entire transmitted salutation, letter, and closing request. It excludes the CO
heading, running heads, foliation, title-note cue and bibliography, and the
chronological comment attached after Calvin says he has never married. That
comment is an editorial inference, not a sentence of the letter. CO prints no
variant apparatus for this item.

The material before the heading on p. 226 belongs to the preceding counsel.
The material below the letter on p. 229 begins *Ad quasdam piorum fratrum
controversias* and is a separate work. Nothing within the letter's main-text
boundaries is omitted.

## Normalization and textual cruxes

The transcription follows [`TRANSLATION_POLICY.md`](../../TRANSLATION_POLICY.md):
printer line wrapping is removed, edition punctuation and spelling are
retained, and each printed page is explicitly marked. Two page turns divide
words and are normalized under the new page:

- pp. 226–227: *vo-lunt*, normalized as *volunt* under p. 227;
- pp. 227–228: *in-commoditates*, normalized as *incommoditates* under p. 228.

The pp. 228–229 turn does not divide a word: p. 228 ends *et tantum otii* and
p. 229 begins *haberes*. Forms such as *literis*, *ubicunque*, *Caeterum*,
*commendarit*, *nunquam*, and *conspirare* are retained as printed.

Interpretive points include:

- *Necessitudo* in the opening means an established personal connection, not
  the abstract necessity discussed later in relation to marriage.
- The letter deliberately grants real advantages to continence before warning
  that excessive honor can function as an unwritten law and deter suitable
  ministers from necessary marriage.
- *In ista actione incontinentiae meae praetextum capto* concerns the course
  of marrying that he has just contemplated: he denies pleading personal
  sexual incontinence as a pretext.
- *Conspirare* in the closing has its positive early-modern sense, “to act in
  concert” or “breathe together,” reinforced by the military image of united
  minds fighting Satan and all his ranks.

## Translation independence

Mary D. Beaty and Benjamin Wirt Farley published a copyrighted translation in
*Calvin's Ecclesiastical Advice* (1991). It was not consulted or used for the
wording of this translation, which was made afresh from `latin.txt` and then
checked against the facsimile.

Suggested citation:

> John Calvin, *Caelibatum in ministro non ita requirendum esse*, undated
> private letter, *Calvini Opera* X/1 (1871), 226–229, normalized Latin and
> draft English translation, *Reformed Retrieval*.

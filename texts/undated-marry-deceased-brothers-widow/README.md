# Whether It Is Lawful to Marry a Deceased Brother's Widow

This directory presents the complete Latin text and a new English translation
of the consilium headed *An liceat fratris mortui viduam ducere in uxorem* in
the *Calvini Opera*.

The displayed heading is the organizing title in CO and the collected-print
tradition. No authorial title, addressee's name, signature, or date is supplied

opinion responds to an intended marriage involving a prince, but the witness
does not identify him. No historical identification is supplied here by
conjecture.

## Edition and transmission

The transcription follows Wilhelm Baum, Eduard Cunitz, and Eduard Reuss,
eds., *Ioannis Calvini opera quae supersunt omnia*, vol. X/1 (*Corpus
Reformatorum* XXXVIII) (Brunswick: C. A. Schwetschke, 1871), pp. 235–238.

CO reports the item in the 1575 Geneva *Epistolae et responsa* at p. 357 and
in the Lausanne, Hanover, Chouet, and Amsterdam collections at pp. 668, 751,
491, and 224 respectively. Its note cites no autograph, manuscript, variant,
or date. This directory consequently presents the CO main-text edition state,
not an independently verified authorial witness.

The public facsimile is supplied by the University of Geneva's [complete
*Calvini Opera* collection](https://archive-ouverte.unige.ch/unige:650) and
its [direct CO X/1 PDF](https://access.archive-ouverte.unige.ch/access/metadata/f526a338-c95c-4f87-b600-04df723f502b/download).

## Scope and collation

`latin.txt` contains every word from *Quid hac de re pronunciaverit Deus* on
p. 235 through *quod non expedit* on p. 238. It excludes CO's heading and
source footnote, running furniture, and both adjacent French documents.

Every line and page turn was checked against 600-DPI renderings of the two
complete facsimile pages. Printer line wrapping and line-end divisions are
removed; CO's spelling, capitalization, punctuation, biblical locators, and
Greek `ἁπλῶς` are otherwise retained. None of the three printed page turns
divides a word: they fall between *in / promptu* (235–236), *profanas /
insolubile* (236–237), and *affectusque / omnes* (237–238). Both Latin and
English preserve those transitions. No reading is illegible or conjecturally
supplied.

## Textual and interpretive cautions

- CO unmistakably prints the anomalous form *claro* in *qua Deus claro
  populum suum retrahit* on p. 237. The Latin preserves it. The English
  renders the sense “by which God clearly withdraws his people,” for which
  *clare* would be the expected adverb, without silently changing the printed
  witness.
- Calvin reads “brother” broadly as “kinsman” in Deuteronomy 25 while taking
  the prohibition concerning a brother's wife in Leviticus 18 to include a
  full brother. The translation preserves that argumentative distinction
  rather than harmonizing the two uses.
- The closing Greek `ἁπλῶς` means “simply” or “without qualification.” It
  marks the transition from the general argument to the appeal directed to
  the unnamed prince.
- The polemic makes sweeping negative claims about eastern peoples,
  Egyptians, Canaanites, and “profane nations.” These are translated as
  historical rhetoric, not endorsed as reliable ethnography.
- The work is a sixteenth-century theological and natural-law opinion. It is
  not current legal advice concerning marriage, affinity, or incest rules.

## Translation status

John H. Leith's edited volume *Calvin's Ecclesiastical Advice*, translated by
Mary D. Beaty and Benjamin W. Farley (1991), contains a copyrighted modern
translation of the *consilia*. It was not consulted and supplied no wording
or interpretation here. No complete public-domain English version of this
item was located in the research audit.

`english.md` is an **AI-assisted scholarly draft**, translated afresh from
the normalized Latin and checked against every printed line. Biblical
language is translated from Calvin's Latin rather than copied from an English
Bible. The draft has not received independent review by a human Latinist;
cite the Latin and CO page for exact-wording claims.

> *Calvini Opera* X/1 (1871), 235–238, normalized Latin and draft English

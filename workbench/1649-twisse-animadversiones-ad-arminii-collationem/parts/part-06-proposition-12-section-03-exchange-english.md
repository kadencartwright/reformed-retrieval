SECTION THREE.

JUN.

Let the latter argument be as follows: Sin is the meritorious cause of that negative act. A man in
the purely natural state has no sin, and therefore no meritorious cause; consequently God had no
cause for that negative act. The whole prosyllogism is granted, but the consequence is denied,
because it infers from a particular. It would indeed be true if God's negative act arose only from
a meritorious cause, but that assumption is most remote from truth. The cause of every negative act
is either in God or in the creature. The same therefore holds for this act. But the cause of this
act is not in the creature; it is therefore in God. No one will deny the prosyllogism. The cause why
you are not an Apostle, or do not live in the age of Adam or Methuselah, lies in God's will alone.
In man, iniquity is the cause why he is distant from God and God from him—in the respect in which
Isaiah spoke. Otherwise iniquity is not the only cause, but God's will too; for if he willed, he
would remove iniquity like a cloud and draw the man to himself.

ARM.

My latter argument also remains firm, but let it be arranged correctly in this manner: The act of
divine good pleasure by which God determines to deny spiritual or supernatural blessedness to a
particular man arises from a meritorious cause, which is sin. But preterition is such an act.
Therefore preterition arises from sin as its meritorious cause. The reason for the major is that
this denial

[1649 BSB witness | first body | printed p. 52 | BSB canvas 64]

cannot be considered otherwise than as punishment; but sin precedes the denial itself as its proper
cause according to the manner of merit. It follows that in this act God cannot deal with a man
considered in the purely natural state apart from sin.

2. I shall briefly confirm the major and its reason and then examine your response. I prove the
major: what God's providence prepared for man under a condition, preterition does not deny him
unless the condition has not been performed or has been violated. But God prepared supernatural
blessedness for man by his providence, and so forth. Next, the passage in Isaiah plainly indicates
that God would not have deserted the Jews unless they had merited it by their iniquity. I prove the
reason for the major: whatever is contrary to the good of blessedness prepared, promised, and
therefore conditionally owed to man as created in God's image can be considered only as punishment.
But the denial of supernatural blessedness is contrary to the good of blessedness prepared for man
as such, for supernatural blessedness too is prepared for him as such. Therefore its denial is
punishment. Furthermore, I confidently say that there is no passage of Scripture from which it can
be taught that this denial was or can be considered otherwise than as having the character of
punishment. If it is punishment, it is therefore prepared only for sinners; for truth establishes
for us that punitive justice has no place except concerning sinners.

3. Now to your response. My syllogism did not infer from a particular. For the negative act of God
with which we are concerned arises from a meritorious cause alone; that is, it exists only from
that cause, and that act of God would not exist if the cause did not exist. The particle “alone”
does not serve to exclude the will of God, and so forth.

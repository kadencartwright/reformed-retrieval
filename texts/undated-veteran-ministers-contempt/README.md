# When Disturbances Had Unfortunately Arisen in the Church from Contempt for a Veteran Minister (22 December 1551)

This directory presents the complete surviving Latin presentation and a fresh
English translation of the advice printed under the CO heading *Quum ministri
veterani contemptu turbae incommode in ecclesia subortae essent* at CO X/1
218–220.

> **Editorial status:** This is an AI-assisted draft transcription and
> translation checked line by line against high-resolution facsimile images,
> not a peer-reviewed critical edition. A qualified Neo-Latinist should
> independently collate it before formal publication or reliance on a
> disputed reading.

The requested directory slug says `undated`, but the transmitted body is not
undated: it closes *Genevae, 22. Decembris 1551.* That internal date controls
the metadata and English heading.

The CO title is sometimes easy to misparse. *Turbae* is nominative plural and
the subject of feminine plural *subortae essent*; *ministri veterani* is
genitive singular. Thus it refers to “disturbances” arising from contempt for
“a veteran minister,” not to several veteran ministers arising or to the
contempt of a crowd. *Incommode* is adverbial: the disturbances arose
unfortunately or harmfully.

## Edition, transmission, and language state

The transcription follows Wilhelm Baum, Eduard Cunitz, and Eduard Reuss,
eds., *Ioannis Calvini opera quae supersunt omnia*, vol. X, part 1, *Corpus
Reformatorum* XXXVIII (Brunswick: C. A. Schwetschke, 1871), pp. 218–220.

CO's title note lists the earlier Geneva *Epistolae et Responsa*, p. 349;
Lausanne, p. 227; Hanover, p. 254; Chouet, p. 479; and Amsterdam, p. 219. It
then states *Gallicum archetypum deperditum est*: “the French archetype has
been lost.” CO supplies no manuscript siglum or variant apparatus for the
item. [`latin.txt`](latin.txt) accordingly represents the transmitted Latin
translation/presentation, not Calvin's recoverable original-language wording.

The unsigned body supports, but does not independently prove, the traditional
attribution: its singular speaker addresses the recipients authoritatively,
refers to a report received from brother `N.`, and closes “my colleagues greet
you from the heart” at Geneva. This edition reports CO's Calvin attribution
rather than upgrading the Latin witness to an autograph.

## Authorial and editorial matter

[`latin.txt`](latin.txt) begins with *Plurimum dolet, fratres dilectissimi*
and ends with *Genevae, 22. Decembris 1551.* It includes the entire address,
argument, farewell, colleagues' greeting, place, and date. It excludes the CO
heading, running heads, foliation, footnote cue, and bibliographical/language
note. No signature follows the date in CO.

The heading and title note on p. 218 are editorial. The material on p. 217 is
the end of *De certorum ministrorum ΑΤΑΞΙΑΙ*. The new heading and work that
begin below this item's date on p. 220 are also editorially distinct. Nothing
inside the main-text boundaries is omitted.

## Normalization and textual cruxes

The transcription follows [`TRANSLATION_POLICY.md`](../../TRANSLATION_POLICY.md):
printer line wrapping is removed, edition spelling and punctuation are
retained, and all printed pages are marked. Two page turns divide words, and
both are joined under the new-page marker:

- pp. 218–219: *il-lius*, normalized as *illius* under p. 219;
- pp. 219–220: *sal-tem*, normalized as *saltem* under p. 220.

The English markers follow the same semantic turns. Other points requiring
notice are:

- CO's `N.` placeholders conceal the deceased pastor, the witness who had
  reported peace, and the veteran pastor under dispute. Their identities are
  not recoverable from this main text alone.
- *Habitatis istic ut precario* says that the recipients live there “as if on
  sufferance.” The reference to benevolent senators confirms civic precarity,
  but the body does not name the city and this edition does not guess it.
- The Guelphs and Ghibellines function as a proverbial example of adversaries
  uniting against a new common enemy; the analogy does not identify the
  recipients' contemporary opponents.
- *Pastum ... subducendum* can denote the threatened withdrawal of pastoral
  or spiritual feeding, with the recent death of one pastor supplying the
  immediate context.
- The “final remedy” (*ultimum remedium*) is not defined in the surviving
  body. The translation leaves it general rather than identifying it with a
  new election, outside mediation, or expulsion.

## Translation independence

Mary D. Beaty and Benjamin Wirt Farley published a copyrighted translation in
*Calvin's Ecclesiastical Advice* (1991). It was not consulted or used for the
wording of this translation, which was made afresh from `latin.txt` and then
checked against the facsimile.

# Anthony Tuckney, *Praelectiones theologicae* (1679)

> **Working edition in progress:** This directory contains an AI-assisted,
> diplomatic-normalized Latin transcription and an independent close English
> translation. Both are unreviewed working texts and are not publication-ready.

## Work identity

- **Author:** Anthony Tuckney (1599–1670)
- **Title:** *Praelectiones theologicae, nec non determinationes quaestionum
  variarum insignium in scholis academicis Cantabrigiensibus habitae; quibus
  accedunt exercitia pro gradibus capessendis*
- **Edition used:** Amsterdam, Stephanus Swart, for Jonathan Robinson and
  George Wells, 1679
- **Form:** a posthumous anthology of theological lectures, determinations,
  degree exercises, and sermons, transcribed by the editor from Tuckney's
  autograph manuscripts

## Controlling witness and rights

The controlling witness is the Early English Books Online microfilm scan at
the Internet Archive,
[`bim_early-english-books-1641-1700_prlectiones-theologicae_tuckney-anthony_1679`](https://archive.org/details/bim_early-english-books-1641-1700_prlectiones-theologicae_tuckney-anthony_1679).
The catalog identity is independently confirmed by the
[Folger Shakespeare Library record](https://catalog.folger.edu/record/430688).
The 1679 book and Tuckney's underlying text are public domain. Internet
Archive page images control the transcription; its page-structured OCR is
used only as a navigation and collation aid.

The scan contains 678 image leaves and several independent pagination
sequences because the volume is an anthology, not a single continuous
treatise. Parts therefore follow complete lectures or other coherent items,
not a volume-wide page quota.

## Editorial method

Long *s*, ligatures, line-end divisions, and typographic `u/v` are normalized.
Substantive spelling and wording are retained. Greek and Hebrew are retained
where legible; a damaged or genuinely doubtful reading is marked explicitly
rather than silently invented. Running heads, printed page numbers,
signatures, catchwords, ornaments, and copy-specific stamps are omitted.
Marginal source citations are retained in bracketed blocks. Source markers
give the 1679 printed page and zero-based Internet Archive leaf number.

The English is a fresh, full translation from the paired Latin, not a summary.
No modern English rendering was used as a wording source. Both files remain
AI-assisted and unreviewed pending complete comparison by a qualified human
editor.

## Part 10: ninth unit of the Romans 1:17 lecture

Part 10 opens the separate inquiry whether Paul's exclusion of works of the

question `An cum Apostolus nos ex operibus legis justificari negat, sola opera
Ceremonialia excludat` on printed p. 98 / IA leaf 106 and ends with the two
brief corroborating appeals to Romans 4 and Philippians 3 partway down printed
p. 106 / IA leaf 114. The next sentence, `Restant tandem ad hanc absolvendam
controversiam quaedam Adversariorum argumenta diluenda`, opens the answers to
the opposing arguments and is excluded.

Part 59 states Castellio's full composite objection: purification at death
can occur neither by faith, whose acts fail in the dying, nor by bodily
death, which cannot itself sanctify; appealing to Christ appears merely to
renew the dilemma. Tuckney's general answer distinguishes certainty about a
revealed fact from knowledge of its manner. Regeneration, conception,
sleep, awakening, and final purification can be real although their precise
mode remains obscure. It runs from `At hoc adhuc velitationis praeludium
fecit` on printed p. 281 / IA leaf 289 through the general response on
printed p. 282 / IA leaf 290. The detailed reply beginning `Ad illam, quam
objicit`, is excluded.

## Part 60: thirty-sixth unit of the Romans 4:5 lecture

Part 60 answers Castellio's claim that the dying lack the faculties needed
for faith to complete sanctification. Tuckney first adduces lucid deathbeds;
then argues that God can act in believers who are unconscious or suddenly
dying, just as he implants grace in infants who cannot exercise reason; and
finally turns the objection back upon Castellio by considering late
conversion and imperfect saints overtaken by lethargy or sudden death. It
runs from `Ad illam, quam objicit` on printed p. 282 / IA leaf 290 through
the close of the reversed objection on printed p. 284 / IA leaf 292. The
next argument, beginning `Tertio accedamus ad secundum ejus Argumentum`, is
excluded.

# Samuel Rutherford, *Examen Arminianismi* (1668)

> **Working edition in progress:** This directory contains an AI-assisted
> diplomatic-normalized Latin transcription and independent close English
> translation of Rutherford's lecture text in Matthias Nethenus's posthumous
> recension. It is not independently reviewed or publication-ready.

## Work and edition

- **Author:** Samuel Rutherford (c. 1600–1661)
- **Editor:** Matthias Nethenus, with the manuscript transmitted and the
  project encouraged by Robert MacWard
- **Title:** *Examen Arminianismi*
- **Edition:** Utrecht, Antonius Smitegelt, 1668
- **Structure:** twenty chapters, printed pp. 1–761, followed by editorial
  indexes

The title calls the work Rutherford's written and dictated lectures but also
states expressly that Nethenus reviewed and edited them. Nethenus reports that
he supplied chapter and paragraph organization, resolved doubtful manuscript
readings, omitted discussions outside the Arminian controversy, and arranged
the indexes. The body is therefore identified as Rutherford's lecture text in
Nethenus's posthumous recension, not as an unmediated holograph edition.

## Controlling witness and rights

The primary controlling witness is the public-domain Google Books scan
`9IhiAAAAcAAJ`, digitized from the KBNL/University of Amsterdam copy:
[`Examen Arminianismi`](https://books.google.com/books?id=9IhiAAAAcAAJ). The
downloaded PDF has 857 one-based frames and embedded OCR. The page images, not
OCR, govern transcription. That exemplar is defective at printed pp. 42–43;
those missing leaves are supplied from the intact British Library exemplar at
Google Books (`mLpoAAAAcAAJ`). The Biblioteca Angelica copy
(`GC9rDo5BOUwC`) is also available for comparison.

- PDF 811–853: editorial chapter/question, Scripture, and subject indexes.
- PDF 854–857: terminal blank matter; excluded.

The body has press-pagination anomalies and one demonstrated lacuna in the
controlling copy. Logical p. 28 is visibly misprinted 58. The KBNL/UvA scan
then passes directly from printed p. 41 / PDF 94 to printed p. 44 / PDF 95;
the failed catchword and the intact British Library copy confirm that complete
printed pp. 42–43 are absent, not merely misnumbered. Those two pages are
therefore supplied under explicit witness markers from the public-domain
British Library Google Books copy `mLpoAAAAcAAJ`. The later numerical sequence
skips 574–575 while the text and catchwords join continuously. Markers record
logical/visible pagination, physical PDF frame, and any substitute-witness
provenance independently.

## Editorial method

Long *s*, ligatures, line-end divisions, and typographic `u/v` are normalized;
substantive wording, headings, marginalia, citations, and foreign scripts are
retained. Nethenus's printed errata are applied transparently, with original
readings recorded where recoverable. Running heads, signatures, catchwords,
ornaments, and page numerals are omitted unless substantively relevant.

The English is a full, independent translation from the accompanying Latin,
not a summary. The modern excerpt and machine-assisted projects are structural
finding aids only and are not wording sources. Both files remain AI-assisted
and unreviewed.

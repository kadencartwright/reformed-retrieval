## Sixth controversy

The sixth controversy is: **Can true faith actually be separated from love and
the other virtues?** Bellarmine, book 1 *On Justification*, chapter 15, affirms
that true faith can be separated from love and the other virtues, and in the
same place he says that it is absurd to affirm that faith cannot exist where
charity does not exist. We, however, assert that true justifying faith never
has existed and cannot exist without love, any more than fire can exist
without heat or the sun without light, although sometimes it appears more and
sometimes less, according as faith has had increases or decreases. We rely on
these arguments.

### Arguments

1. Faith which is without works of charity is feigned and dead, James 2:14,
20; 1 Timothy 1:5. Therefore true faith cannot exist without love.

2. To know God in Christ is to have true faith. But no one knows God in Christ
except the person who keeps the commandments—that is, who loves, for love is
the sum of the commandments—1 John 2:4. Therefore the conclusion follows.

3. To be in the light and to remain in the light is to believe and to
persevere in faith. But no one is in the light except the person who loves,
1 John 2:9–10. Therefore the conclusion follows.

4. Everyone who is born of God—that is, who is

[1609 e-rara witness | canvas 94 | image 15575066 | printed p. 92]

a son of God—loves God. But everyone who believes—that is, who has true
faith—is a son of God, 1 John 5:1. Therefore he loves God. But the person who
loves God also loves his neighbor, verse 2 of the same chapter. Therefore the
person who has faith has love.

5. Love, as the Roman Catholics maintain, is the form of faith. But a form
gives being to the thing whose form it is. Therefore true faith without love
has no being, and consequently does not exist without love.

6. The person who believes abides in God, and God in him, 1 John 4:15. But in
the person in whom God abides there is love, verse 16 of the same chapter.
Therefore the person who believes loves, and consequently faith does not exist
without love.

7. If God judges in Christ in this life, and will judge at the end of the age,
concerning faith and love as by an infallible and inseparable sign, then faith
cannot exist without love; rather, when faith is given, love is given. But the
former is true, Matthew 26:34; Luke 7:47. Therefore the conclusion follows.

8. True faith and true believers cannot exist without that which Christ sets
down as an infallible sign of true believers. But he sets down love as an
infallible sign of true believers, John 13:35. Therefore the conclusion
follows.

9. That which is always efficacious and works through love cannot be separated
from love. But faith is such, Galatians 5:6. Therefore the conclusion follows.

10. Justifying faith is given only to the elect, and this so that it may
benefit them. But faith which is without charity does not benefit, 1
Corinthians 13:2. Therefore justifying faith which is without charity is not
given. But once true charity has been given, it is never lost or falls away,
verses 8 and 13 of the same chapter. Therefore the conclusion follows.

### Objections

#### Objection 1

From John 12:42–43: very many chiefs of the priests had true faith, but they
did not have love. Therefore true faith can be separated from love.

Response 1: it says only that they believed, but not that they had true
justifying faith, for their faith was only temporary. It is therefore a
fallacy from the equivocation of “faith.”

2. Even if it is granted that they believed, the minor premise is false. It is
not denied simply that they loved, but comparatively: “They loved,” it says,
“the glory of men more than the glory of God.” But it does not follow, “They
loved the glory of men more than the glory of God; therefore they did not love
the glory of God.” This is a fallacy from what is said in a qualified sense to
what is said simply. A similar case: a boy loves his father more than his
other friends; therefore he does not love his other friends.

#### Objection 2

From 1 Corinthians 13:1–2: “If I have faith without charity, I am nothing.”
Therefore faith can exist without charity.

Response 1: I deny the inference. For the apostle is speaking from a
supposition, but a supposition affirms nothing and posits nothing in the actual
existence of the thing.

3. He speaks there not about true faith but about the faith of miracles, which
is not justifying and can be separated from charity. It is therefore a fallacy
from equivocation. He teaches, moreover, that neither miracles, nor prophecy,
nor works of charity benefit without true inward and spiritual love.

#### Objection 3

From James 2:24: “What will it benefit,” he says, “if someone says that he has
faith and does not have works?” Therefore he teaches that faith does not
justify without works, and yet can exist without works.

[1609 e-rara witness | canvas 96 | image 15575068 | printed p. 94]

Response: it is a fallacy from the equivocation of “faith,” because James
speaks about historical and feigned faith, while the conclusion concerns
truly justifying faith. The apostle does not assert that true faith can exist
without works, but speaks about those who boast that they have true faith. He
proves that they do not have it, because they do not have works, since works
infallibly follow and demonstrate faith. Thus this passage is turned back
against the Roman Catholics. The apostle proves that the faith is not true
because it does not have the works of love joined to it; therefore that faith
is true which has love joined to it.

#### Objection 4

Those who are in the church are believers. But many who are in the church do
not have love, since both the evil and the good are in the church, Matthew
3:10, 12; 22:10; and 25:2. Therefore faith, from which they are called
believers, can be joined with sin, from which they are called evil, and
consequently faith can be separated from love.

Response 1: the major premise, taken universally, is utterly false, as is
clear from the same passages cited by Bellarmine. For there are many in the
visible church who are not true believers.

2. Faith and love can be joined with sin in one and the same person. Therefore
it follows badly: faith can exist with sin in believers; therefore it can
exist without love.

An instance: but in Matthew 22 those invited to the wedding are rebuked because
they did not have a wedding garment. That garment was charity; and in Matthew
25 the virgins are rebuked because they did not have oil—that is, works of
mercy—not because they did not have faith. And the incestuous man in 1
Corinthians 5 is rebuked, not because he lacked faith, but on account of his
sin. In Acts 8 Simon Magus had faith and not works. Therefore faith can exist
in sinners.

[1609 e-rara witness | canvas 97 | image 15575069 | printed p. 95]

Response 1: this does not conclude what is at issue. For we grant that faith
exists in certain sinners, as in that incestuous man, who, although he sinned
gravely, nevertheless lost neither faith nor charity, as is evident from his
repentance, 2 Corinthians 2:1.

2. There are also many in the church who sin and do not have true justifying
faith, such as Simon Magus, who had only historical faith, not true faith and
charity.

3. The passages from Matthew are falsely interpreted, as though the wedding
garment and the oil signified works alone. For they signify first and chiefly
faith itself, and works together with faith.

#### Objection 5

If faith and charity cannot be separated, this is either because one belongs
to the essence of the other or because one arises from the other. But neither
can be said. Not the first, because they are two distinct virtues; nor the
second, because although charity arises from faith, it nevertheless does not
arise as a proper affection which necessarily flows from its subject, but as a
virtue toward which faith disposes and inclines the believer—namely, to love
the one whom he believes. But a disposition does not compel the person who
loves, but leaves him free to love if he wills and not to love if he does not
will. Therefore faith and charity can be separated.

Response 1: we grant Bellarmine the first part of his distinction—namely, that
love arises from faith. For love is born from faith as light from the sun or
heat from fire. He falsely asserts, however, that it arises from faith only as
from a bare antecedent disposition. For it arises from faith as from an
antecedent cause which effectually produces it. As soon as anyone believes,
at that moment he also loves the one whom he believes, so that if he believes
he cannot fail to love, and conversely.

[1609 e-rara witness | canvas 98 | image 15575070 | printed p. 96]

2. Although faith does not compel the person who loves to love, it nevertheless
does not follow that faith can be separated from love, since the person who
believes, although not by compulsion, nevertheless loves necessarily. And if
he loves necessarily, faith is not separated from charity. Finally, he falsely
asserts that charity is born from faith as from a bare antecedent disposition.

#### Objection 6

If faith and love are necessarily joined, so that one does not exist without
the other, then if justification depends upon faith, it also depends upon
love. But the latter is false according to the testimony of our adversaries.
Therefore the former is also false.

Response: I deny the entailment, because many things are necessarily joined
which have different offices and operations. Thus in the soul there are the
intellect and the will, which are necessarily joined. But it does not follow,
“Therefore whatever the intellect does, the will also does.” Rather, the
necessity of the conjunction does not confound the diversity of offices and
operations.

#### QUESTION 2: Whether a Monarchy Was Established by Christ in the Person of Peter

Or,

Whether Peter was constituted by Christ as monarch and head of the Church in
Christ’s place.

Bellarmine, in book 1 *On the Roman Pontiff*, chapter 10, maintains the
affirmative position; we maintain the contrary with these arguments.

Argument 1. That alone is the head of the Church from which life, sensation,
and motion flow into the body, as nature itself teaches. But life, sensation,
and motion flow into the Church from Christ alone, John 1:4 and 6:15.
Therefore.

Argument 2. That one alone should be regarded as the head of the Church whom
Scripture teaches to be the head of the Church, 2 Timothy 3:16. But it teaches
that Christ alone is the head of the Church, 1 Corinthians 12:27; Ephesians
1:22 and 5:23; Colossians 1:18, and nowhere teaches this of Peter. Therefore.

Argument 3. According to Bellarmine, chapter 10, to be the head of the Church
and to be its foundation are the same, because the foundation is to a house
what the head is to the Church and a king to a kingdom. But the Church has no
foundation besides Christ, 1 Corinthians 3:10, where the universal negative
particle should be noted. The prophets and apostles, however, are called the
foundation in Ephesians 2:20 by reason of their doctrine, whose sum was
Christ; also because they founded churches and governed them in Christ’s
name. In this Peter had no prerogative above the other prophets and apostles,
and therefore they are called the foundation collectively.

Argument 4. If Peter had been constituted head of the Church by Christ, the
other

[1620 BSB Tome II | canvas 54 | printed col. 108 | bsb10175568]

apostles would not have been ignorant of it but would have acknowledged it.
But they did not acknowledge it; indeed, while Christ was still alive they
contended among themselves about the primacy, Matthew 20:15; Mark 10:49; Luke
22:25. Therefore.

Argument 5. If Peter had been constituted by Christ as head of the others,
then when this controversy concerning the primacy arose, Christ would have
decided it and declared that it belonged to Peter—unless perhaps someone says
that Christ was unwilling to reveal this to the disciples and thus remove the
matter of contention. But Christ did not do this; instead, he dissuaded them
all from every ambition for the primacy and exhorted them to humility and
equality. Therefore.

Argument 6. If more than one are called supreme apostles and pillars—even
though when they are mentioned Peter is not put in first place—then Peter
alone was not constituted as head in Christ’s place. But the former is true,
2 Corinthians 11:5; Galatians 2:9. Therefore.

Argument 7. If Paul was not inferior to the supreme apostles, then Peter was
not constituted head of all and monarch. But the former is true, 1
Corinthians 11:5. Therefore.

Argument 8. A person who is sent is not superior to the one who sends him,
for the sender has authority over the person sent, or is at least his equal.
But Peter was sent by the apostles, Acts 8:14. Therefore.

Argument 9. A person who, when accused before others, pleaded his case was not
their superior; indeed, by his submission he testified that he was inferior
to them in their capacity as a council. But Peter did this, Acts 11:1.

Argument 10. If Peter had been constituted head of the entire Church, then
the gospel of the uncircumcision and of the Circumcision—that is, of the
entire Church—would have been entrusted to him. But the latter is false,
because the Circumcision, which is the smaller part, was entrusted to Peter,
and the uncircumcision, the larger part, to Paul, Galatians 2:7. Therefore.

Argument 11. If Peter had been constituted

[1620 BSB Tome II | canvas 55 | printed col. 109 | bsb10175568]

bishop and head of the entire Church, how did he dare to enter into that
compact with Paul in Galatians 2, around the eighteenth year after Christ’s
ascension, and thus restrict and abandon the ministry entrusted to him? But he
did this in the cited passage. Therefore.

Argument 12. If one of the apostles were to be appointed head of the Church,
then it would be the one who was constituted apostle of the Gentiles, among
whom the Christian Church was chiefly to be propagated; who rebuked others,
especially Peter; who was sent to Rome, resided and preached at Rome, and
from there wrote various epistles to the churches, and so forth. But these
things are said of Paul in Scripture, Acts 23:11; 27:24; 28:30; Galatians 2;
and elsewhere. Therefore.

Argument 13. If anything had been granted to Peter above the others, the
evangelists would not have kept silent about it, for by the inspiration of the
Holy Spirit they wrote everything necessary for faith and salvation. But they
wrote nothing of the kind; indeed, they recount that the common power of the
keys was given to all, John 20:23.

Argument 14. If Peter had been constituted monarch by Christ, then at some
point he would have exercised the functions of his monarchy. But he never
exercised them, either when the brothers were present or in his epistles,
because he does not command as a superior but courteously exhorts colleagues,
as is customary among equals. Therefore.

Argument 15. The Fathers testify to the same thing. Cyprian, in his book *On
the Unity of the Church*: *The other apostles assuredly were what Peter was,
endowed with an equal fellowship of honor and power*.

Jerome, on Matthew, chapter 16, says that the Church is founded upon Peter,
although elsewhere the same thing is done upon all the apostles, and the
strength of the Church is founded upon them equally.

Ambrose, on 2 Corinthians 11, therefore says this because he is not inferior,
either in

[1620 BSB Tome II | canvas 55 | printed col. 110 | bsb10175568]

preaching, or in performing signs, to the apostles who preceded him, nor in
dignity, but only in time.

Augustine, *Retractions*, book 1, chapter 22, says that he had expounded the
passage in Matthew 6 to mean that Peter was the rock, and then adds: *But I
know that I afterward expounded it so that “upon this rock” would be understood
as the one which Peter confessed; for it was not said to him, “You are the
rock,” but “You are Peter,” while the rock was Christ*.

The interlinear Gloss on Matthew 16 says: *The others have the same judicial
power, to whom after the resurrection Christ said, “Receive the Holy Spirit,”*
John 20:22. *All churches also have the same power in their presbyters and
bishops*, it says.

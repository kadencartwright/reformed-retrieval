# Whether a Man May Marry His Deceased Wife's Sister

This directory presents the complete source-language French response printed
under CO's title *S'il est licite à un homme de prendre en mariage la soeur de
sa femme trespassée*, with a fresh English translation.

> **Editorial status:** This is an AI-assisted draft transcription and
> translation checked line by line against 600-DPI facsimile images, not a
> peer-reviewed critical edition. A qualified specialist in sixteenth-century
> French and Reformation marriage law should independently review it before
> formal publication or reliance on a disputed reading.

| Date and place | Not stated |
| Original language | French |
| Genre | Polemical theological and legal counsel on prohibited degrees of affinity |
| Reported witness | Bibliothèque de Genève, Cod. 145, fol. 142, an old copy according to CO |
| Printed locator | CO X/1, pp. 233–235 |

The response repeatedly says *nous*—“it has seemed to us,” “we would wish,”
“we do not answer,” “we confess,” and “we have tried.” CO supplies neither a
subscription nor the names of any possible co-speakers, the opposing writer,
or the intended recipients. Its placement in Calvin's *Consilia* supports a
Calvin association, but the witness information neither establishes a
corporate authorship nor justifies silently turning every plural statement
into an individually signed Calvin letter.

## Edition and transmission

The transcription follows Wilhelm Baum, Eduard Cunitz, and Eduard Reuss,
eds., *Ioannis Calvini opera quae supersunt omnia*, vol. X, part 1, *Corpus
Reformatorum* XXXVIII (Brunswick: C. A. Schwetschke, 1871), pp. 233–235.
CO's sole title note identifies an old copy at Geneva Cod. 145, fol. 142. It
does not report an autograph, a date, a signature, a named addressee, or a
variant witness. The present edition has collated CO's French facsimile, not
the manuscript itself.

The French response is a distinct work. The Latin text above it on p. 233
concludes advice about an underage betrothal. The Latin item beginning on p.
235, *An liceat fratris mortui viduam ducere in uxorem*, addresses marriage to
a deceased brother's widow and is not a continuation or translation of this
French response.

## Completeness and normalization

[`french.txt`](french.txt) begins *Sur la question qui est auiourdhuy debatue*
on p. 233 and ends *le cognoistront* on p. 235. Every word between those
boundaries is retained. The CO title, witness footnote, running heads, printed
page numbers, preceding Latin counsel, and following Latin counsel are
excluded.

Printer line-end divisions are joined while historical spelling and
punctuation are retained. No printed-page turn divides a word:

- p. 233 ends *se pourroit dire de* and p. 234 begins *la mere et de la fille*;
- p. 234 ends *nous ne pensons pas avoir rien* and p. 235 begins *avancé*.

The corresponding English markers occur at the same translated phrases.
Forms such as *auiourdhuy*, *ung*, *trespassée*, *ha*, *affermer*, *doibt*,
*lascher*, *soubs*, *trespas*, *restreincte*, *praticquerons*, *meslinge*,
*naifve*, and *cognoistront* are preserved.

## Textual and interpretive cautions

- The opening has no printed stop between *trespassée* and *Il nous ha
  semblé*. The transcription does not insert one, although the English divides
  the clauses for readability.
- *Ung mesme vaisseau* is a coarse “same vessel” argument which the response
  itself calls an indecent covering. The translation preserves the metaphor
  without supplying an anatomical identification not stated in the witness.
- The p. 235 image reads *toutes gons d'esprit moderé*. The apparent *gons*
  error is retained in French and rendered contextually as “all people.”
- The response refers to an unnamed proponent, his preface, two biblical
  passages, rabbinic reasoning, papal action, Roman civil law, and its own
  interpretation of affinity. It does not provide bibliographic locators for
  those claims, and this edition does not turn them into independently verified
  legal history.

The response's language about incest, affinity, polygamy, and supposedly
“disordered” desire records a sixteenth-century controversy. Publication here
is documentary, not endorsement, and the text is not current legal, pastoral,
or marital advice.

## Translation independence

Mary D. Beaty and Benjamin Wirt Farley published a copyrighted translation in
*Calvin's Ecclesiastical Advice* (1991). It was not consulted or used for the
wording of [`english.md`](english.md). The English was translated afresh from
the complete normalized French and checked against the facsimile.

# A Minister Dismissed Without a Hearing

This directory presents the complete Latin-form witness and a new English
translation of the consilium headed *Quum a ministerio quis indicta causa
abdicatus esset* in the *Calvini Opera*.

## Work and witness identity

| Field | Value |
|---|---|
| Author | John Calvin (collection attribution), speaking in a plural voice with unnamed colleagues |
| CO consilium | IV.11, *Quum a ministerio quis indicta causa abdicatus esset* |
| Secure date | Geneva, 10 December; no year supplied |
| Authorial language | French according to CO |
| Language presented here | Latin translated from the French |
| Genre | Collective ecclesiastical admonition in epistolary form |
| Addressees | Unnamed church leaders or congregation |
| Subject | Dismissal of a minister without accusation or stated cause, civil-magisterial intervention, disorder, and church unity |
| CO locator | X/1, pp. 223–224 |

CO's note labels the Latin *Ex gallico*, “from the French.” The normalized
Latin here is therefore a translated witness to a French-language work, not a
reconstruction of Calvin's exact French wording. CO does not name the Latin
translator in this item's note or print the underlying French alongside it.
The first-person plural throughout also matters: the work speaks as a
collective “we,” although it is preserved in the Calvin collection.

The final dateline reads only *Genevae, 10. Decembris* (“Geneva, 10
December”). Neither the body nor CO's source note supplies a year. Dates on
neighboring consilia, their position in the volume, and an earlier provisional
filename are not evidence for a year. This directory consequently uses
`undated-12-10` and reports only the place, day, and month that the witness
actually gives.

The displayed CO heading is an editorial description: “When someone had been
dismissed from the ministry without his case being heard.” It is not treated
as a recoverable authorial French title. The anonymized `N.` in the body is
retained; no identity is supplied conjecturally.

## Edition and transmission

The transcription follows Wilhelm Baum, Eduard Cunitz, and Eduard Reuss,
eds., *Ioannis Calvini opera quae supersunt omnia*, vol. X/1 (*Corpus
Reformatorum* XXXVIII) (Brunswick: C. A. Schwetschke, 1871), pp. 223–224.

CO reports the item in the Geneva *Epistolae et responsa* at p. 348 and in
the Lausanne, Hanover, Chouet, and Amsterdam collections at pp. 659, 741,
478, and 218 respectively. The same note gives the language statement *Ex
gallico* but no year, named translator, manuscript description, or variant.

The public facsimile is supplied by the University of Geneva's [complete
*Calvini Opera* collection](https://archive-ouverte.unige.ch/unige:650) and
its [direct CO X/1 PDF](https://access.archive-ouverte.unige.ch/access/metadata/f526a338-c95c-4f87-b600-04df723f502b/download).
Both printed columns appear on PDF p. 133 of the captured surrogate: the work
begins in the lower part of CO p. 223 and ends on p. 224 before the following
*Ad quaestiones circa disciplinam ecclesiae responsa*. The downloaded PDF
was 33,386,580 bytes with SHA-256
`025973d6e4533769d0c2b870dd324497943bc14050eb2bcbcc4c964d4f2a1777`.

## Scope and collation

`latin.txt` includes every word from *Quum et de turbis quae vestram
ecclesiam vexarunt* through the complete dateline *Genevae, 10. Decembris*.
It excludes the CO heading and its source footnote, running furniture, the
preceding document ending on p. 223, and the following document beginning on
p. 224.

The text was checked line by line against a 600-DPI rendering of the entire
facsimile page. Printer line wrapping and line-end divisions are removed,
while CO's spelling, capitalization, punctuation, anonymization, and Greek
`ἀταξία` are retained. The printed page turn divides *poste-rum*; the
normalized files place the p. 224 marker before the complete joined word
*posterum*. No reading is illegible or conjecturally supplied.

## Translation cautions

The legal-ecclesiastical phrase *indicta causa* is rendered “without a
hearing,” while the body specifies the concrete complaint: `N.` says that no
one accused him, the pulpit was forbidden to him, and no reason was stated.
The word *magistratus* is rendered “civil magistrate” to distinguish the
civic authority from the ministers discussed in the same sentence. The
English supplies no year or personal identification absent from the witness.

## Translation status

John H. Leith's edited volume *Calvin's Ecclesiastical Advice*, translated by
Mary D. Beaty and Benjamin W. Farley (1991), contains a copyrighted modern
translation of the *consilia*. It was not consulted and supplied no wording
or interpretation here. No complete public-domain English version of this
item was located in the research audit.

`english.md` is an **AI-assisted scholarly draft**, translated afresh from
the normalized Latin and checked against every printed line. It has not
received independent review by a human Latinist. Exact-wording claims must
identify the Latin as a translation from French and should not be presented
as direct quotations recovered from a French authorial witness.

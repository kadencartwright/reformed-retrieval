12. Since the elect, when they first hear the promises, are no less ignorant
of the divine intention than the reprobate, how do they know that they ought
to believe? Reply. They must rest upon Christ as Savior of all believers, and
then God's intention will become evident to them. 2. Just as a lamb by instinct
alone recognizes its mother among other sheep, so those born of God rely upon
Christ by supernatural instinct. The reprobate, being destitute of this
instinct, cannot do so. But they say that the price of redemption must
necessarily have been paid for everyone to whom it is offered.

[1651 logical printed p. 269 | IA leaf 287 | PDF p. 287]

Reply. If the discussion concerns an offer from the divine intention, we deny
that such an offer is made to the reprobate. If it concerns the evangelical
offer, we deny the connection. For it is no more absurd that Christ should be
offered in the Word to those for whom he did not pay such a price than that
Pharaoh and Abraham should be commanded to do things which God did not decree
would occur.

13. It cannot be said, without conspicuous insult to God's majesty, that God
intends the obedience of the reprobate and fails of his intended end. But you
will ask: to what end does God exhort in the gospel those whom he foreknows
will never obey? William Pemble of Oxford says that God exhorts to this end:
that the reprobate may be shown their duty and what obedience is pleasing and
acceptable to God. But the learned man seems not to reach the end, for to
exhort is to declare a duty. Thus God declares duty to the reprobate to the end
that duty be declared—but the same thing is not its own end. Others say that
the reprobate are exhorted not properly and for their own sake, but by
concomitance insofar as

[1651 logical printed p. 270 | IA leaf 288 | PDF p. 288]

they are mingled in outward society with the elect. Thus, just as by sending
rain nature intends to water fields, not rocks, God exhorts everyone in the
church so that chiefly the elect may obey and be saved, not for the sake of
the reprobate. These men say something true, for the Word is sent chiefly to
the elect. Hence Paul's epistles are addressed to saints, beloved, called,
sanctified, and faithful brothers. And if there were an assembly in which no
one were elect, it appears that Christ would not light his candle there to
search for the lost coin. But they do not attain the whole truth. For it is not
by chance but for a definite end that God sees to the scattering of seed upon
stony hearts. The question remains: to what end does God exhort the reprobate
mingled with the elect? This was not done by chance but by God's counsel. What
if here, with devout ignorance, we exclaimed with Paul, “O the depth, etc.,”
Romans 11? For there he discusses the external calling of the Jews, among whom
only the elect obtained the promises; the rest were hardened.

14. Setting aside the Arminian end—that God intends the obedience of the
reprobate—it is enough for us to say with Scripture that God exhorts them so
that they may be without excuse, Acts 13:46; that Christ is appointed as a sign
spoken against, Luke 2:34; a stone and rock of offense, 1 Peter 2:7–8; so that
they may know a prophet has been among them, Ezekiel 2:5; so that it may be a
testimony to them, Matthew 24:14; so that they may be judged according to

[1651 logical printed p. 271 | IA leaf 289 | PDF p. 289]

the gospel they have heard, Matthew 14:15; so that they may be hardened, Isaiah
6:9–10; Matthew 13:14–16; and so that the glory of divine power may be declared
in vessels of wrath, Romans 9:22. But Scripture contains not a syllable or jot
about the Arminian end.

It is asked whether, now that the apostles' extraordinary mission has ceased,
the sending of ministers through the church's calling and ordination is
necessary by divine institution. The Remonstrants deny that it is strictly
necessary except in an established church, for the sake of order and decorum,
in *Apologia*, chapter 21, page 227. And Episcopius, in the second collection
of his private disputations, disputation 26, theses 4–5, says that it is right
and lawful for a Christian at any time to teach the divine Word if he is fit to
teach and those who wish to be taught seriously and earnestly request it.
Thus Andreas Radderius, a Socinian, says in *Refutation of the Gordian Knot*,
chapter 4, page 8: “Just as a republic is free to choose someone fit to
undertake an office, so in Christ's church, which is most free, it will also be
permissible to choose for that office someone fit to teach others.”

We, on the contrary, say that not only δύναμιν, ability, and gifts, but also
ἐξουσίαν, authorization, and authority through the church's sending and
calling are necessary by God's institution for someone to be a lawful pastor
of the church. We prove it as follows.

1. From Romans 10:15: “And how will they preach unless they are sent?”
1 Timothy 5:22: “Lay hands hastily on no one, nor share in the sins of
others.” 1 Timothy 4:14:

[1668 KBNL/UvA witness | logical printed p. 685 | PDF p. 734]

“Do not neglect the gift that is in you, which was given to you through
prophecy with the laying on of the hands of the presbytery.” Acts 1:23: “They
therefore appointed two,” and so forth. Verse 24: “And praying, they said,” and
so forth. Verse 26: “And the lot fell upon Matthias, who by common votes was
enrolled with the eleven apostles.” Acts 6:5: “And this saying pleased the
whole multitude present, and they chose Stephen,” and so forth. Even when God
called and sent immediately, the church's sending had to precede; this was
certainly given as an example for us, for otherwise God's immediate calling
could have sufficed. Acts 13:2: “While they were performing their ministry to
the Lord and fasting, the Holy Spirit said, ‘Set apart for me Barnabas and Saul
for the work to which I have called them.’” Acts 14:23: “And when they had
created presbyters for each church by votes and had prayed with fastings, they
commended them to the Lord in whom they had believed.” Therefore, it is much
more necessary now for ministers to be called by churches through prayers and
fastings; and a calling would be profane unless such a sending of the church
preceded it.

2. It cannot be said that God, who is the author of order, permitted just
anyone fit to teach to fly into the pastor's chair without any preceding call
of the church. For it is required that a bishop not only possess gifts of mind
but also be blameless, the husband of one wife, vigilant, temperate,
well-ordered, hospitable, not

[1668 KBNL/UvA witness | logical printed p. 686 | PDF p. 735 | split word joined: vinosus]

given to wine, not violent, and so forth, having a good testimony from
outsiders, and so forth, 1 Timothy 3:2–3. It is absurd to refer this to the
testimony of the man himself who aspires to the chair and not to the votes of
the church which calls him. One who is such a man may desire the episcopate,
verse 1, but may not usurp it without the church's calling.

3. Christ would wrongly rebuke the church of Thyatira for permitting Jezebel,
the pseudo-prophetess, to teach if it did not belong to the calling church to
test and to remove from the church those proved to be pseudo-prophets. The one
who has authority to reject also has authority to receive into fellowship.
The same was the fault of the church of Pergamum, which tolerated champions of
Balaamite doctrine. Nor would Christ rightly praise the Ephesian church
because it could not bear evil people and tested those who repeatedly claimed
to be apostles, though they were not, but were discovered to be liars, if by
God's institution it did not belong to the church to test and call true and
approved teachers and to cast out and reject impostors, as in Revelation
2:1–2, 14, 20.

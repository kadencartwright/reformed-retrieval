# John Sharp, *Tractatus de justificatione hominis coram Deo* (1609)

This workbench begins a diplomatic-normalized Latin transcription and a close,
independent English working translation of John Sharp (Johannes Scharpius),
*Tractatus de justificatione hominis coram Deo* (Geneva, 1609).

## Files

- `parts/part-01-preliminaries-and-foundation-latin.txt` — title, dedication,
  table of controversies, and the foundational doctrinal summary.
- `parts/part-01-preliminaries-and-foundation-english.md` — full close English
  translation of the same unit.
- `parts/part-02-controversy-01-faith-as-knowledge-{latin.txt,english.md}` —
  first controversy, on whether faith is knowledge.
- `parts/part-03-controversies-02-03-and-04a-faith-{latin.txt,english.md}` —
  second and third controversies and the scriptural arguments in the fourth.
- `parts/part-04-controversy-04b-special-assurance-{latin.txt,english.md}` —
  remainder of the fourth controversy, on assurance of particular mercy.
- `parts/part-05-controversy-05-perseverance-{latin.txt,english.md}` — fifth
  controversy, on whether true believers can fall away from faith.
- `parts/part-06-controversy-06-faith-and-love-{latin.txt,english.md}` — sixth
  controversy, on whether true faith can be separated from love and the other
  virtues.
- `parts/part-07-controversy-07a-faith-alone-arguments-and-fathers-{latin.txt,english.md}`
  — opening of the seventh controversy through its arguments and patristic
  testimonies.
- `parts/part-08-controversy-07b-objections-01-22-{latin.txt,english.md}` —
  objections 1–22 in the seventh controversy and Sharp's complete responses.
- `parts/part-09-controversy-07c-objections-23-37-{latin.txt,english.md}` —
  objections 23–37 and the conclusion of the seventh controversy.

## Witness anomaly

Printed p. 8 begins with `bus, & consiliis damnata fuerunt implacabilis ille`,
an intrusive repeated fragment whose grammar and page join are impossible. It
repeats the continuation of `Omnes enim haereses ... quae a piis patri-` from
pp. 5–6 and is a genuine press/composition defect present in both checked
facsimiles. Both paired files preserve it in square brackets as an anomalous
printed repetition; the surrounding dedication reads continuously without it.
The controversy table on p. 10 also prints singular `promissionis` with plural
`sint`; the Latin retains the form, while the English conveys the evident
singular question without silently changing the source transcription.
Printed p. 101 cites `Ioh. 14.5. & 15.14.` for the statement that no one can
bring what is clean from what is unclean. The wording points instead to Job
14:4 and 15:14, but both paired files retain the witness's printed Johannine
references rather than emending them silently. Part 09 likewise retains three
apparently defective printed references: `Luc. 9.50.` beside Mark 5:36 for
“believe only” (apparently Luke 8:50), `Matth. 10.27.` for justification in the
sense of declaring righteous (apparently Matthew 11:19), and `Gen. 15.16.` for
Abraham's prior righteousness by faith (apparently Genesis 15:6).

## Editorial method and status

Long *s*, ligatures, line-end divisions, typographic `u/v`, and unambiguous
abbreviations are normalized. Original spelling and substantive wording are
otherwise retained. Running heads, signatures, catchwords, page numerals, and
ornaments are omitted from the prose but represented by exact source markers.
Biblical references and Greek expressions are retained. Split words at page
boundaries are joined after the new page marker.

This is an **AI-assisted, unreviewed** working edition, not a publication-ready
critical text. Every included page has an identical marker in the paired files,
and the English is a full translation rather than a summary. A qualified human
editor must collate both files against the facsimile before quotation or
publication. No modern English translation was used.

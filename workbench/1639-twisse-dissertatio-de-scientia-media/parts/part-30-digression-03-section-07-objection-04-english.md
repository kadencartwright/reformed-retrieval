NAVARRETE.

Fourthly, God does not produce things in his eternity. Therefore he does not intuit them as present
in that same eternity. The consequence is proved: God’s practical knowledge or cognition produces
the thing known, and for God to produce a thing is to know it practically through the determination
of his will. Speaking of production in itself, this holds on God’s part.

It is confirmed. That intuitive knowledge either presupposes the thing as present in eternity or
produces it. It does neither; therefore it is not terminated at a present thing. The minor is proved
with respect to its first part. Divine cognition does not derive any perfection from things known,
as ours does when it presupposes the known thing because it derives from that thing the character
of intuitive knowledge. With respect to the second part, the minor must be maintained according to
the faith. The major appears evident. For if God has the thing present according to its real being,
either that being is presupposed or it is produced by the power of knowledge.

It is asked whether the gospel ministry and the preaching of the Word are
strictly necessary for salvation. The Remonstrants deny it in *Apologia*,
chapter 22, page 233, in *Synodical Writings*, article 2, page 233, and in
Episcopius, disputation 28, thesis 11. So also do the Socinians in the Racovian
Catechism, *On the Church*, chapter 11, pages 301–302. There they teach that,
now that Scripture has been disclosed and is given to everyone to read, the
ministry is not so much necessary as useful. The Apologists say that it is
necessary under the hypothesis of

[1668 KBNL/UvA witness | logical printed p. 687 | PDF p. 736 | split word joined: temporis]

time, if, for example, the public ministry can be exercised, and under the
hypothesis of the divine will. God, however, they say in the same place in the
*Apologia*, did not will it to obtain as strictly necessary always and forever,
since someone can believe through the Word read and heard.
But in the cited place in the *Synodical Writings* they teach that we do not
know whether God may lead many to Christ without the external ministry of the
gospel.

We say: 1. The question about God's absolute power to save without Christ the
Mediator and without the gospel announced is curious and outside Scripture.
2. We say that it is an unscriptural invention that the substance of the
gospel, if not its words, is instilled through the ministry of angels or by
some other means into those who have never heard. 3. We do not deny that some
believe through the Word when it is read and understood in a manner equivalent
to a sermon. But we teach that, according to the order revealed to us and
prescribed by God, the preaching of the Word is strictly necessary for the
salvation of all adults.

1. Romans 10:14: “How then will they call upon him in whom they have not
believed? And how will they believe in him of whom they have not heard? And
how will they hear without a preacher?” Romans 1:16: “I am not ashamed of the
gospel”—namely, the preached gospel, in which capacity in verse 14 Paul
acknowledges himself a debtor to Greeks and barbarians—“for it is the power of
God for salvation to everyone who believes.” 1 Corinthians 1:21: “It pleased
God through the foolishness of preaching

[1668 KBNL/UvA witness | logical printed p. 688 | PDF p. 737]

to save those who believe.” 1 Corinthians 3:5: “Who then is Paul, and who is
Apollos, except ministers through whom you believed?”

2. One who does not believe cannot be saved. But no one believes without a
preacher, because without one how will they hear? Romans 10:14. Christ's sheep
hear his voice, John 10:27–28.

3. Because God commanded it in Matthew 28:19: “Go and teach all nations.” Mark
16:15: “Preach the gospel to every creature.”

4. From the office of ministers. 2 Corinthians 5:19: “God has placed in us the
word of reconciliation.” Verse 20: “Therefore we act as ambassadors in
Christ's name; and, as though God were entreating you through us, we implore
you in Christ's name: be reconciled to God.” 2 Corinthians 3:6: “Who made us
competent ministers of the New Covenant, not of the letter but of the spirit.”

5. From the practice of the church. Acts 15:21: “For from ancient generations
Moses has had in every town those who preach him, since he is read in the
synagogues every Sabbath.” Nehemiah 8:9: “The Levites read that book, the law
of God, distinctly; and by expounding the sense they gave understanding through
Scripture itself.” That this is to be perpetual is stated in Ephesians 4:11:
Christ, ascending on high, gave gifts to people for the edification of the
church, and so forth.

6. From God's own practice. He did not will to teach Saul and Cornelius by
himself or through an angel, but entrusted Cornelius to Peter's ministry and
Saul to Ananias's, Acts 9 and 10. Justin Martyr, *Apology* 2.

[1668 KBNL/UvA witness | logical printed p. 689 | PDF p. 738]

Tertullian, Chrysostom, and all the Fathers in their own times proclaimed the
necessity of preaching.

In what sense God must be said to will the condemnation of the reprobate

And now, what sort of assertion is this: “God never willed the condemnation of those condemned”? Andreae should at least say that God formerly did not will it when he loved them and called them to salvation, but then at last, with his judgment changed—

[Part II | PDF page 167 | logical source scan 166 | printed page 165 | ends before following subsection on shared page]

—willed them to be condemned for their sins when he condemns them. Unless perhaps these men also hold that, when God condemns unbelievers, he does not sit as judge and avenger of sins, but idly permits sinners marked for condemnation to perish and suffer eternal punishment. And how absurd too it is to think that God is mutable in himself, loving certain people at one moment and holding them hateful at another, willing them saved at one moment and lost at another. And what sort of God would this be, whose decree and judgment, uncertain in itself, depends upon human choice? What sort of potter is this, if judgment rests with the clay?

We taught above the extent to which Andreae’s statement that those who perish by their own will perish against God’s will can be admitted, distinguishing the ambiguity of the word *will*. In this sense Augustine also rightly says what Andreae should not have passed over: “Not even what happens against God’s will happens apart from God’s will.” That is, in a wonderful and ineffable manner even those events which God himself does not do happen and occur not apart from the will of God intervening: he does not permit them unwilling, but willing. Writing about this in the *Enchiridion to Laurence*, Augustine says: “Nothing happens unless the Almighty wills it to happen, either by permitting it to happen”—for it would not happen if he did not permit it, nor does he permit unwilling, but WILLING—“or by doing it himself.”

What, then, you will ask, does he will while not willing it, and not will while willing it? Whatever is evil—but not insofar as it is good that evils too should exist, evils which God justly punishes in the wicked or uses well in instructing his own. In short, he so acts that what he does is good while what the evil instrument does is evil, as I will declare in its place. What, then, is that which he wills by truly willing it? What he himself works in his people by his Spirit and good instruments, as he commands in his law to be done. Finally, what does he not will by truly and properly not willing it? What he forbids to be done in his law and evil instruments do wickedly by their own impulse.

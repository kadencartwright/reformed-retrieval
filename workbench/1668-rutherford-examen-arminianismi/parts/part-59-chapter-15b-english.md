It is asked whether there is in the New Testament a church entirely destitute
of presbyters to which the Lord and Savior committed the power of the keys, as
the Remonstrants maintain in *Apologia*, chapter 22, folio 233.

To resolve this question, we first set down these distinctions. 1. One
ministry is ordinary and another extraordinary. 2. There is a mystical church,
composed solely of believers who profess the faith, and there is a ministerial
church, composed of the flock and pastors. 3. A church may be called such by
anticipation, as in Hosea 12:12: “Jacob served for a wife,” namely, one who was
to be his wife; or a church is called such formally because it is already
constituted in its own being.

[1668 KBNL/UvA witness | logical printed p. 645 | PDF p. 694]

Assertion 1. Sometimes there is a mystical church prior to a ministerial
church. 1. A house can exist before it is furnished with stewards and servants.
2. In the order of nature, Adam and Eve constituted the mystical church before
there was an ecclesiastical ministry in that assembly.

Assertion 2. The ordinary public ministry is prior to the mystical church of
believers. Ephesians 4:11: “He gave some as pastors and teachers”; verse 12,
πρὸς τὸν καταρτισμὸν τῶν ἁγίων εἰς ἔργον διακονίας, “for the equipping of the
saints, for the work of ministry,” and so forth. For since people believe
through the ministry and preaching of the Word, Romans 10:14, the ministry
therefore precedes the assembly of believers. 2. It is necessary in the order
of nature for fathers to exist before they beget sons, and for seed to exist
before the plant springs up. Pastors, however, are fathers, and the preached
Word is the seed of the mystical church.

But in order to discuss this more precisely, it must be understood that a
mystical church destitute of pastors and presbyters neither has the keys
committed to it by Christ nor has any ordinary power to ordain presbyters. Once
these two points have been demonstrated, the arguments by which the
Separatists and Socinians, together with the Arminians, contend that an
assembly of believers destitute of presbyters is that church to which the Lord
and Savior committed the keys of the kingdom of heaven will also be rendered
powerless.

Assertion 1. Christ Jesus immediately, without any

[1668 KBNL/UvA witness | logical printed p. 646 | PDF p. 695 | split word joined: interveniente]

intervening power, instituted presbyters for the church when he ascended into
heaven. Ephesians 4:11–12; 1 Corinthians 12:18. Nevertheless, the designation
of such persons to office takes place through the presbytery, 1 Timothy 4:14
and chapter 5, verse 22.

Assertion 2. It must not be denied that an assembly of believers has a virtual,
though not formal, power in a case of necessity to make good the lack of
ordination through popular election. This occurs if an assembly of believers
is on some island far removed from all pastors. 1. If David, without any
revelation from heaven, ate the bread of the Presence in an extreme necessity
—for every positive law yields to natural law—then believers destitute of
pastors can choose pastors for themselves. 2. Theologians, both Protestant and
Roman Catholic, prove this. Thus Voetius does in *On the Desperate Cause of the
Papacy*, book 2, section 5, chapter 5. Thus Flavian and Diodorus preached at
Antioch without being invested with any priesthood, as Theodoret says in book
2, chapter 24. “For sometimes,” Gerson says in part 2, sermon 2 after Easter,
“God is accustomed to make an immediate interruption in ordinary calling
through pastors.” More on these matters may be seen in Viguier, *Institutes of
Theology*, chapter 15, paragraph 1; Suárez, volume 2, chapter 2; Vásquez on the
*First Part of the Second Part*, disputation 179, chapter 2; and Soto, *On
Justification*, book 2, question 3, article 8.

Assertion 3. The order established and confirmed by Christ for calling pastors
is through other pastors, and thus

[1668 KBNL/UvA witness | logical printed p. 647 (visible 646) | PDF p. 696]

through a succession of pastors.

1. 1 Timothy 5:22: “Lay hands hastily on no one.” 1 Timothy 4:14: “Do not
neglect the gift which was conferred upon you through prophecy with the laying
on of the hands of the presbytery.”

2. Apostolic practice is our most certain rule in this matter. For nowhere in
the New Testament do we read of presbyters ordained by an assembly of believers
alone when it was destitute of presbyters; rather, the ordination was always
performed by pastors. Acts 1:15–16; Acts 6:2–4; Acts 14:23; 1 Corinthians
3:6; 1 Timothy 4:14; Titus 1:5. The Separatists themselves acknowledge this,
because care for all the churches was laid upon the apostles.

3. If, according to the apostle's canons, the ordination of pastors is imposed
upon presbyters as presbyters, then this responsibility is not entrusted to an
assembly of believers as such. But the former is true; therefore the latter is
also true. The major premise is certain, because nothing can be commanded by
virtue of an office as a duty incumbent upon those who are invested with no
office in the church. The assumption is proved because in 2 Timothy 2:1–2 the
power of entrusting the ministry to faithful men who are able to teach others
is given entirely to Timothy, and consequently to other pastors and
presbyters. By the same capacity Timothy is forbidden to entangle himself in
the affairs of this age, so that he may be an approved soldier of God, verse 5;
and by it he is commanded also to warn others not to quarrel about words and to
divide the Word rightly,

[1668 KBNL/UvA witness | logical printed p. 648 | PDF p. 697 | split word joined: tanquam]

“as a workman who has no need to be ashamed,” verses 14–15. But these things
are imposed upon Timothy as a pastor. So too 1 Timothy 5:17 shows the honorarium
owed to faithful προϊσαμένοις, “those who preside.” It also forbids him to
receive an accusation against a presbyter except under two or three witnesses,
verse 19, and to lay hands hastily on any pastor, verse 22. In Titus 1 Paul
wills that Titus, as a pastor, should ordain presbyters in every city, and he
wills that Titus should rebuke the Cretans with pastoral authority. But it is
absurd that each and every person among the people should be admonished that an
honorarium is owed to them as workers; that it belongs to them not to receive
an accusation against a presbyter and to lay hands upon pastors either
deliberately or rashly; and also that they are to ordain presbyters in every
city and rebuke with pastoral authority people such as the Cretans were.

4. The Adversaries' distinctive argument is feeble; therefore their opinion
itself is also feeble. The antecedent is proved because they imagine the
ordination of pastors by pastors to be a papistical succession. Yet they
themselves are compelled to admit a perpetual succession of believers—and
indeed a local one—even from the times of the apostles. For they deny that
anyone is legitimately ordained by pastors unless he has been ordained by
believers. Further, we by no means defend an absolutely perpetual succession
of pastors. We acknowledge that it can be interrupted and that, when pastors
are lacking, an election made by the people in a case of

[1668 KBNL/UvA witness | logical printed p. 649 | PDF p. 698 | split word joined: necessitatis]

necessity supplies the lack of ordination by pastors. 3. We do not champion a
local, cathedral succession of pastors that has always been eminently visible
since apostolic times.

But they object that in Matthew 16 and Matthew 18 Christ conferred the keys of
the kingdom of heaven upon the believing people. Therefore, he also conferred
the power to ordain pastors. Response: Even if the antecedent were true, it no
more follows that the power to ordain was conferred upon the people than that
the power to preach and administer the sacraments was conferred upon them.
What? Because the keys were committed to the people, are they therefore also
able to exercise every act of jurisdiction and order? It by no means follows.
2. Nothing is more false than the antecedent. Scripture neither expressly nor
by any consequence says that the keys were conferred upon the assembly of
believers. For through the keys given to Peter in Matthew 16, Christ conferred
the power to open the doors of the kingdom of heaven, to bind and loose on
earth by administering the Word and sacraments. Was this, however, given to the
assembly of Christians?

Objection 2. Acts 1 says that the 120 men cast votes in the ordination of the
apostle Matthias. Response: This is not in the text. Rather, 120 people were
present at that gathering. Verse 23 indeed says, “Therefore they appointed two,”
but these words must be referred, not to the entire assembly, but to the
apostles enumerated in verse 13 and to the words in verse 17, “He was numbered
with us,” namely, with the apostles.

[1668 KBNL/UvA witness | logical printed p. 650 | PDF p. 699 | split word joined: Neque]

Nor does the sequence of the context refer this ordination to the votes of each
and every person.

With regard to the passage in Acts 1, the Adversaries object that confusion
follows from our presbyterial government no less than from a democratic one.
For verse 23 says, “Therefore they appointed two,” namely, those 120; and Acts
6:5 says, “The entire multitude appointed seven deacons before the apostles,”
and the apostles spoke and prayed in verse 6. Therefore, when the 120 appointed
two, they must have spoken. But did they all speak jointly and at the same time?
Yet this confusion is forbidden in 1 Corinthians 14. Did the women speak? But
this is forbidden in verse 34. Did the children? But this is impossible.
Likewise in Acts 6, did all twelve apostles speak jointly and also pour out
vocal prayers to God at the same time? This too is confusion. Did the entire
multitude that appointed seven deacons also speak at the same time? Certainly
not. Yet by such expressions children and women are completely excluded. Why
not also all the others except the presbyters? And since all the presbyters
cannot speak jointly without the greatest confusion, why are not all excluded
except one prelate? Then he alone will be the church—and thus the pope!

Response 1. This argument especially favors our opinion, because the 120 in
Acts 1 and the entire multitude in Acts 6 cannot jointly lay hands upon deacons
or pastors,

[1668 KBNL/UvA witness | logical printed p. 651 | PDF p. 700 | split word joined: præsertim]

especially if the visible church consists of 3,000 people professing Christ,
as it does in Acts 2. Therefore, for the sake of order, certain chosen persons
—that is, προϊστάμενοι, “those who preside,” and presbyters—must lay on hands
and ordain pastors, and thus administer all the church's public affairs. And
this is our representative church, which the Separatists so vigorously attack
as mere Judaism. For children and women are part of the church of the redeemed,
yet the Adversaries wish them to be excluded. Therefore, even on their own
admission, the keys were committed by Christ to a representative assembly. 2.
Matthew 18: “If he does not hear the church”—namely, the church speaking and
rendering judgment—“let him be to you as a heathen and a publican.” But the
whole church—that is, each and every person, children and women—cannot speak at
all in the church, much less speak jointly. Therefore, in that passage the word
*church* does not signify an assembly of the redeemed gathering into one
visible assembly to perform sacred acts, unless they wish to exclude children
and women from the number of the redeemed and from the number of those
performing sacred acts in the sacred assembly. And this is to acknowledge the
representative church along with us.

The only passage they cite for the exercise of ecclesiastical discipline by
the whole people is Colossians 4:11: “And say to Archippus, ‘See that you fulfill
the ministry which you received from the Lord.’” Therefore, they say, the whole
people—namely, of the

[1668 KBNL/UvA witness | logical printed p. 652 | PDF p. 701 | split word joined: Colossensium]

Colossian saints to whom Paul writes—exercise the power of jurisdiction over
pastors by admonishing them, and therefore also by ordaining and
excommunicating.

Response: Καὶ εἴπατε Ἀρχίππῳ, “And say to Archippus.” Therefore, “Say it to
Archippus judicially and authoritatively” does not follow affirmatively from
genus to species. Likewise Matthew 18:17, Εἰπὲ τῇ ἐκκλησίᾳ, “Tell the church.”
Does each private individual therefore exercise authority over the whole
church? John 8:48: “The Jews said to Jesus”; therefore, they spoke with
authority. 1 John 1:8: “If we say that we have no sin.” 2. The Fathers
Augustine, Chrysostom, Ambrose, and Jerome, and 3. the Scholastics Aquinas,
Suárez, Báñez, and Gregory of Valencia say that this admonition of Archippus is
a fraternal rebuke, not an act of jurisdiction. Cajetan says that “the act of
correction is elicited by prudence and commanded by mercy.” Andrew Duval says
that “rebuking and admonishing an erring brother is an act not only of divine
law but also of natural law,” as in Leviticus 19:17 and Hosea 2:1. But it by no
means follows from this that the whole assembly of Colossians had the power of
jurisdiction over Archippus.

We demonstrate by the following arguments that the keys of the kingdom of
heaven were by no means committed by Christ to the whole assembly of believers.

1. The keys were given to that church which is built upon the rock, as the
household of Wisdom, Proverbs 9:1, and as the house of God, 1 Timothy 3:13 and
Hebrews 3:4. This occurs through the doctrine of the prophets and apostles

[1668 KBNL/UvA witness | logical printed p. 653 | PDF p. 702 | split word joined: prædicatam]

preached by the doctors and pastors given for the building up of the church,
Ephesians 4:11. But this house is not an assembly of believers united in some
fashion and destitute of presbyters; it is the ministerial church. Therefore,
and so forth. The minor premise is proved: An assembly destitute of pastors and
presbyters is not a house built through the ministry of pastors and presbyters,
because the material of the house is not the house itself. For if they lack the
power to preach the Word and administer the sacraments, they lack the power of
the keys. But according to the Adversaries, where the power of the keys is
lacking, there is no visible church. For if the building upon the rock is
promised to the visible church from Matthew 16, no other building can be
understood here than that which occurs through pastors and teachers,
Ephesians 4:11.

2. The keys are promised to those who are stewards of the mysteries of God,
1 Corinthians 4:1; who are servants of the household by office,
2 Corinthians 4:5; and whose task is to open and shut the doors, to conduct
themselves rightly in the house of God, 1 Timothy 3:15, to distribute a portion
to each one in the household at the proper time, Matthew 24:45, and to divide
the Word rightly, 2 Timothy 2:15. But an assembly of people professing faith in
Christ and united by any covenant, yet destitute of pastors and stewards, are
not stewards of the mysteries of God, and so forth. Therefore, the keys were
not given to such an assembly destitute of pastors. The Adversaries do not deny
the assumption, nor does it require proof. The major premise is proved from the

[1668 KBNL/UvA witness | logical printed p. 654 | PDF p. 703 | split word joined: Phraseologiâ]

phraseology of Scripture. Isaiah 22:22: “I will place the key of the house of
David upon his shoulders; when he opens, no one will shut, and when he shuts,
no one will open.” By the consensus of all interpreters, the giving of the
keys is, as Musculus says on the passage, “the badge of authority received.”
The keys are entrusted to the steward and overseer of the house so that he may
administer his authority through them. Thus Calvin in his commentary on the
same passage. Gualther, homily 114: “The keys are the symbol of authority; keys
are presented to kings.” Junius says it denotes full administration. Beza in
his major annotations on Matthew 16 says that the power of ministers is
denoted. Pareus on the passage: “I will make you steward of my house.” Jerome
on Isaiah 22: “The key is the power of preeminence.” Thus Augustine,
Chrysostom, and Fulgentius teach that in Matthew 16 the power of binding and
loosing given to the apostles and ministers is signified by the name *keys*.
So too Isaiah 9:6: “The government is upon his shoulders,” that is, by the
consensus of all interpreters, the keys of David were given to him. Revelation
3:7: “These things says the one who has the key of David, who opens and no one
shuts, and shuts and no one opens.” Revelation 1:18: “I have the keys of hell
and death.” Revelation 9:1: “The key of the bottomless pit was given to the
angel.” In all these passages, by the consensus of all interpreters, the
stewardly power conferred upon kings, overseers, rulers of churches, and even
those who are stewards by office is signified. But believers destitute of
pastors are by no means such persons.

3. In this text, Matthew 16, the keys are given to those

[1668 KBNL/UvA witness | logical printed p. 655 | PDF p. 704]

to whom, by Christ's command, the exercise of the keys is given by binding,
loosing, opening, and shutting the doors of the kingdom of heaven—that is, by
preaching the Word and administering the sacraments. But by Christ's command
this exercise is given only to the apostles and ministers in the person of
Peter, not to an assembly of believers destitute of pastors. The major premise
is clear in the text. The exercise is given by special command to the same
person to whom the power of the keys is given. Verse 19: “And I will give you
the keys of the kingdom of heaven.” Here power is conferred. And he says to the
same man, “Whatever you bind on earth will be bound in heaven,” and so forth.
Here the exercise of using the keys is conferred. Further, if Christ alludes to
Isaiah 22:22, as is plain in the text, then it is certain that official and
stewardly power is denoted by the keys. Further, no clear testimony remains by
which the power of the keys conferred upon pastors as such can be proved if
this passage is taken from us. Therefore, the power conferred upon Peter in
the person of believers, and thus upon all believers, is not denoted here. For
what is conferred upon believers as believers cannot at the same time be
conferred upon pastors, since a contradiction frequently exists between
believers and pastors, as between believers and unbelievers. Further, this
passage is plainly the same as Matthew 28:19 and John 20:23. But in those
passages the power of administering the sacraments and baptizing is given.
Further, binding and loosing

[1668 KBNL/UvA witness | logical printed p. 656 | PDF p. 705 | split word joined: actus]

are acts of jurisdiction and official power communicated in Scripture only to
princes, rulers, and pastors of the people and church of God. Therefore, they
are not communicated to all believers. The antecedent is proved from Isaiah 52
and 49:9, where Christ is said to release the imprisoned. So too in many other
passages of Scripture: Psalm 105:27; Judges 15:10; Psalm 149:8; Matthew 22:13;
Acts 21:11; Acts 22:4; Mark 3. In these passages, *to bind* signifies official
power proper to rulers. So also *to loose* in Leviticus 14:7; Psalm 102:20;
Jeremiah 40:4; Psalm 105:20; Acts 2:24; Romans 7:2; 1 Corinthians 7:27;
Revelation 20:7; Revelation 9:15; Job 12:18.

4. If neither in Matthew 16 nor in Matthew 18 does Christ say that he confers
the keys, with respect to their power and exercise, upon an assembly of
believers destitute of rulers, then there are no testimonies in the New
Testament by which it can be proved that this power was conferred upon
believers. But the latter is true; therefore the former is also true. The major
premise is clear, because they contend that the keys were committed to the
assembly of believers on the strength of only these two testimonies. For in
Matthew 16:18, when speaking of the church built upon the rock, Christ changes
the persons addressed and turns his discourse from the church itself to Peter
in verse 19: “And I will give to you”—Peter, not the church built upon the
rock—“the keys of the kingdom of heaven.” But they say that the keys were given
to Peter as representing the believers because he delivered that notable
confession in the believers' name, and not as representing the

[1668 KBNL/UvA witness | logical printed p. 657 | PDF p. 706 | split word joined: Collegium]

college of rulers. This is utterly worthless. 1. According to the Adversaries,
the keys were not given to believers as such and as the bride of Christ, but as
professing Peter's faith and as united by such a covenant. Therefore, they were
not given because of that confession itself, but because of the profession of
that confession. Yet I ask about that profession on account of which they wish
the keys to have been given to the assembly of believers: Is it a sincere and
true confession, or not? If the former, all those baptized by pastors who do
not profess sincerely have been baptized by men who have no authority and must
be rebaptized. 2. Someone excommunicated with an unerring key by ten men who do
not sincerely profess the faith but are nevertheless united in a visible
church is not bound in heaven, because he was bound on earth by men who had no
authority. But it is absurd for someone justly bound on earth by the church not
also to be bound in heaven. 3. The Adversaries maintain that the power of the
keys is part of the liberty obtained for the redeemed by Christ's blood. Yet
those who do not sincerely profess the faith must not be numbered among the
redeemed. But if an insincere profession constitutes a visible church that has
the power of the keys, then the keys were not committed by Christ to the church
built upon the rock, against which the gates of hell will not prevail. For
nothing is easier according to Scripture than for an assembly of those who do
not sincerely profess Christ to be able, in a time of temptation, actually to
fall away

[1668 KBNL/UvA witness | logical printed p. 658 | PDF p. 707]

from Christ and for the gates of hell to prevail against such a church.

Similarly, in Matthew 18:19 Christ changes the persons addressed and turns his
discourse from the church mentioned in verse 17 to the apostles in verse 18:
“What things you bind on earth will be bound in heaven.” And that whole address,
as is evident from the beginning of the chapter, was delivered to the disciples
or pastors.

But they also argue as follows: The keys of the kingdom were promised to as
many people as were built upon the rock, as the Adversaries argue. Yet this
major premise is neither expressed in the text nor follows by consequence. 2.
This major premise is also false, because the invisible church is built upon
the rock and will never fall away from Christ. Yet the Adversaries deny that
the keys were given to the invisible church; indeed, they maintain that they
were given only to the visible church as such.

But it is clear that in Matthew 16 Christ addresses Peter as representing the
rulers, not the believers. 1. According to the Adversaries, the power of the
keys is denied to very many people—namely, women and children—who nevertheless
make this confession of Peter no less than the apostle Peter does. 2. If
believers, insofar as they make this confession of Peter and insofar as they
are built upon the rock, are that church which must be heard and which has the
official power of binding and loosing, then the ministerial church is as immune
from defection and apostasy as the assembly of the elect and

[1668 KBNL/UvA witness | logical printed p. 659 | PDF p. 708]

true believers. This conflicts with experience and the Scriptures. For the
seven churches of Asia, as well as the Roman, Corinthian, Galatian,
Thessalonian, and Philippian churches, all of which were nevertheless
ministerial churches endowed with the power of the keys, fell away from Christ
the rock and lapsed into grave and capital errors and horrendous idolatry. Yet
we accuse the Roman Catholics on precisely this count, especially Gretser in
*On the Inauguration of Luther's Doctorate*, page 29; Bellarmine, *On the
Authority of Councils*, book 2, chapter 2; Suárez, *On the Three Virtues*,
disputation *On the Church*; Gregory of Valencia, volume 3; Hosius in the
*Polish Confession*; and John of Torquemada, *On the Church*, book 1, chapters
24 and 25, because from this context in Matthew 16 they wish to establish the
infallibility and ἀναμαρτησίαν, “sinlessness,” of the visible and ministerial
church. Their argument is certainly valid in fundamental points if those to
whom the keys were given can never fall away from Christ, and if a ministerial
church possessing the power of the keys is an assembly that will never fall
away from Christ. 3. From these contexts, those to whom the keys are given
represent the person and place of Christ himself, so that it is said of them,
“He who despises you despises me; he who honors you honors me.” This is said
expressly of ministers in Matthew 10:40. And it is said of this church in
Matthew 16:19, “Whatever you bind on earth will be bound in heaven.” Thus those
who have this power are

[1668 KBNL/UvA witness | logical printed p. 660 | PDF p. 709 | split word joined: cooperarii]

God's fellow workers and Christ's ambassadors. But nowhere in Scripture do we
read that all believers are God's ambassadors, or that all who despise any
believers who bind and loose in Christ's name are despisers of Christ himself.

5. Those to whom the keys are committed authoritatively remit and retain sins,
and their acts are valid in heaven according as people believe and repent, or
do not believe and repent, as our theologians teach against sacramental
absolution. But an assembly of believers destitute of presbyters has no such
authoritative power. Therefore, and so forth.

The Adversaries respond that even believers who are neither pastors nor
presbyters have the power to remit sins. Matthew 18:21: “How often,” Peter asks,
“will my brother offend me and I forgive him?” Luke 17:3–4; 2 Corinthians 2:10.

Response 1. The present question is the subject of 2 Corinthians 2:10, and the
passage must be understood of the church's authoritative remission after it
had excommunicated the incestuous man in 1 Corinthians 5. 2. A private
forgiveness of an offense between one brother and another differs greatly from
a public and authoritative forgiveness. Our argument concerns authoritative
forgiveness, for the former is an act of charity commanded by the law of nature
and binds everyone, even outside the visible church and even those who have
been excommunicated and from whom the power

[1668 KBNL/UvA witness | logical printed p. 661 | PDF p. 710 | split word joined: Clavium]

of the keys has been taken away. For excommunication does not release people
from the bond of the law of nature which binds everyone. Matthew 6:12, 14–15;
Matthew 5:44–45. But the church's public and authoritative forgiveness is a
matter of positive law. 2. Private forgiveness must be shown even toward our
enemies; we must not avenge ourselves but must commit vengeance to God, whether
or not the offending party repents. Nor is private forgiveness ratified in
heaven, and no one who has suffered manifest injury is bound to it when the
offending party does not repent, Luke 17:4, except so far as refraining from
vengeance, Romans 12:14, and showing common charity, verse 20 of the same
chapter. Thus the church is by no means bound to forgive an offending party who
does not repent. Indeed, it cannot but retain the sins of those who do not
repent of their sins. But Peter, as a private person offended by a private
brother, must forgive a hundred times if the offending brother repents, Luke
17:4. Yet Peter does this by no power of the keys. Indeed, as far as he himself
is concerned, he can privately forgive the sins of that man which are bound and
retained by the excommunicating church on earth, and also by God in heaven.

6. The keys are committed only to those who have Paul's pastoral spirit for
summoning, examining, passing sentence, and delivering to Satan. But believers
destitute of pastors do not have Paul's pastoral spirit, for they cannot

[1668 KBNL/UvA witness | logical printed p. 662 | PDF p. 711]

preach publicly or administer the sacraments by that spirit. Accordingly,
compelled by this argument, some Separatists acknowledge that certain persons
who are destitute of every pastoral power can nevertheless administer the
sacraments. This certainly follows from their doctrine. For if an assembly of
believers destitute of pastors is a visible church constituted in its complete
being, there is no reason why any one of the believers may not exercise every
specific act of the keys, foremost among which are preaching the Word and

It is nevertheless worth noting that in Matthew 18:16–17 Christ addresses the
disciples of verse 1: “At the same time the disciples came to Christ, saying,
‘Who is greatest in the kingdom of God?’” To them he says in verse 18, “Amen, I
say to you, whatever you bind on earth”; and this passage must be expounded by
Matthew 16, where the keys in a more restricted manner are given to Peter alone
as representing the college of rulers. For the same keys that bind and loose
in one passage remit and retain sins in the other passage, and they were
undoubtedly committed to the church's stewards. Nor is there any reason why we
should expound the church in Matthew 18 as merely an assembly destitute of
rulers. For it is an assembly which must be obeyed

under the peril of excommunication, and that assembly is undoubtedly
ministerial. Nor are we greatly troubled by their claim that in Scripture the
church always signifies an assembly of believing, redeemed people built upon
Christ the rock. For this assembly is the invisible church, which the
Adversaries constantly confuse with the visible church throughout this
controversy.

Finally, all our theologians, together with the Fathers, contend against the
Roman Catholics that in Matthew 16 the keys were given to Peter, not to him
alone—for the papists would forge their papacy from that—but in the person of
Peter to all other bishops, pastors, and presbyters. Thus Nazianzen in Oration
21, *In Praise of Basil*, on the words “I will give you the keys”: “For Peter
became a participant in the same throne with the other pastors, not with the
other believers.” And Cyprian, *On the Unity of the Church*, on these words:
“The other apostles assuredly were what Peter was, endowed with an equal
fellowship of honor and power.” And Basil, *On the Solitary Life*, chapter 21:
“He granted the same power to all pastors and rulers, the sign of which is that
they all bind and loose equally.” Theophylact on Matthew 17: “Although it was
said to Peter alone, it was granted to all the apostles.” Cyril on John: “Christ
deferred the reply to him who was the apostle greater in rank.” Euthymius on
Matthew 16: “I will give you the keys. But this gift belonged to the other
apostles.” Jerome, book 1 *Against Jovinian*: “All

the apostles received the keys, not Peter alone.” Anselm on Matthew 16: “All
the apostles have the same judicial power.” From this it is clear that there is
no visible church destitute of pastors in the New Testament to which the keys
were committed.

It is asked whether the Remonstrants rightly teach in *Confession*, chapter 22,
section 4, that the true church is the one which agrees in the faith and
profession of necessary truth.

We teach that this is false according to their own principles.

1. For them, holding necessary doctrine is holding the very few dogmas approved
by all Christians and called into controversy by almost no assembly.
Consequently, Antitrinitarians, Arians, Socinians, Roman Catholics, and every
impure sect that agrees on certain uncontested dogmas of Christianity will be
the true church.

2. They explain holding necessary doctrine as properly holding that without
which the commands of faith and obedience cannot be duly observed, and
therefore, by God's own judgment, pardon of sins and eternal salvation cannot
be obtained. For since acts of the intellect cannot be subjected to God's
command, those who think heretically about God in their minds, conceiving of
God as an idol and a man venerable for his gray hair—and consequently, because
in their view an erroneous conscience binds to practice, those who worship
idols, such as the Roman Catholics; those who believe Christ to be a mere man,
such as the Arians and Socinians; those who suppose that three Persons are
three infinite Gods;

[1668 KBNL/UvA witness | logical printed p. 665 | PDF p. 714 | split word joined: putant]

and those who believe the Socinian and Vorstian religion to be true—are true
members of the church, provided that they duly perform faith as defined by the
Arminians and the commands of obedience prescribed by Christ. Jacob Arminius,
disputation 20, thesis [number absent in the witness].

3. He regards as Reformed churches all those which reject every supremacy of
the Roman Pontiff and profess that they render faith and worship to God and
Christ. Adolphus Venator, *Against the Dordrecht Brethren*, pages 134–135: “Why
should I condemn the nations to whom doctrines were not revealed as clearly as
to the apostles, and so forth, but who nevertheless led a godly life?” The
Remonstrants, in the dedication of their *Apology against the Censure of the
Leiden Professors*, abhor “fights and contests over nonnecessary dogmas.” Their
theology is practical theology, “located in the reformation of morals and in
the correction and direction of the whole life according to the prescription
of Jesus Christ,” and they command only “the ungodly, profane, worldly,
contentious, abusive, rebellious, and implacable” to stay away. Smalcius has
the same in disputation 8, *On the Church*, page 5; Socinus, *On the Church*,
chapter 2, page 20.

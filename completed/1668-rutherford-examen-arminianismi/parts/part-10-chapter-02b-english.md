ROMANS 9:5

Who is God, blessed for ever.

[1668 KBNL/UvA witness | logical printed p. 149 | PDF p. 200]

It is asked whether the Son is Αὐτόθεος—God from himself—and is God from and
of himself. Arminius, in his *Declaration*, pages 124–125, denies that he is
Αὐτόθεον either as Son or as God. For, he says, in no different respect can
God's essence be said to have been communicated to the Son, because these are
contradictory. He censures the younger Trelcatius for calling the Son, as God,
Αὐτόθεον, because in that case the Father would differ from the Son in name
alone—which Sabellius asserted—and we would fall into tritheism, since there
would thus be three gods possessing the divine essence together and
collaterally. So also Vorstius in *Apology* 4, page 46. The impious Johannes
Geisteranus says in his confession that the Son received deity from the Father
in time. So also Socinus in his *Theological Lectures*, folios 106–107, who in
chapter 18 denies that he is true God.

We maintain the contrary: Christ is essentially and truly God. Isaiah 9:6:
“He shall be called the mighty God.” John 1:1–3: “The Word was God.” Romans
9:5; Colossians 1:15; 1 John 5:20. Therefore, he is God from himself and, as
God, has the divine nature from no one.

2. If the Son, not only as Son but also as God, has an essence communicated by
the Father—because the essence communicated is distinct from the essence
communicating—there will be two essences here, one communicating and one
communicated. The same must be said of the Holy Spirit; and thus there will be
three essences, three deities, and three gods. Arminius, not Trelcatius, is the
tritheist.

[1668 KBNL/UvA witness | logical printed p. 150 | PDF p. 201]

3. If the Son as God is not God from himself, he is not God essentially. For
to be God and to be from another is to be God and not to be God, because God
essentially is independent. The Son will thus be a god of lesser rank than the
Father. There will be one supreme God and two little gods, the Son and the Holy
Spirit.

4. It is customary for Arminius to fashion contradictions in his own brain.
But if it cannot be said in different respects that the essence is
communicated to the Son and is not communicated to the Son, as Arminius
dreams, then in no different respects is the essence ἀγέννητος (“unbegotten”)
in the Father and γεννητός (“begotten”) in the Son, which the church has
constantly taught against Sabellius. In no different respects is the divine
essence incarnate in the Son and not incarnate in the Father. In the Son it is
personally incarnate; in the Father, taken absolutely, it is not incarnate.

PHILIPPIANS 2:6

Who, when he was in the form of God, did not consider it robbery to be equal
with God.

It is asked whether Christ is true God, ὁμοούσιος (“of the same essence”) and
consubstantial with the Father, according to the mind of the Arminians.

Some of them say so in words but deny it in fact. We therefore rightly and
deservedly hold them highly suspect of Arianism.

1. When the most distinguished Leiden professors fault the Remonstrants
because in their *Confession*, which ought to

[1668 KBNL/UvA witness | logical printed p. 151 | PDF p. 202]

contain the heads of faith and fundamental articles, they abstain from the word
ὁμοούσιος and from “consubstantial,” yet answer nothing in the *Apology*.
Indeed, they say that they do not wish to anathematize those who think wrongly
about the Trinity and the person of the Son and the Holy Spirit, and that the
ancient church maintained communion with those who believed Jesus Christ to
be nothing but a man from among men. And in the *Apology*, chapter 3, folio
50, they say, “An
irrefragable argument for proving the deity of the Son cannot be drawn from
divine worship.”

2. Vorstius, *Response to the Apology*, article 10, chapter 3, says that in the
Scriptures the Father is rightly called God κατ᾽ ἐξοχήν (“preeminently”), that
he truly is the fountain of all deity, and that he has a certain prerogative
over the Son. This is the prerogative of deity by which he is the fountain of
the Son, by which the Son is God but not God κατ᾽ ἐξοχήν. In their exposition
of the Lord's Prayer, the Remonstrants want that true God, the Father of our
Lord Jesus Christ, to be hallowed, saying nothing about the Son and the Holy
Spirit. They also refer Isaiah 6:3, “Holy, holy, holy,” to the Father alone.

3. In the *Apology*, chapter 18, folio 190, they explain the glory which
Christ is said to have had with the Father before the world existed, John
17:5, not as the glory of deity but as glory bestowed on Christ in time, which
Christ had from eternity in God's decree, not actually and really. In the
*Confession*, chapter 19, section 2, they assign to Christ only the Father's
judgment seat, as though by the very fact that he was judge of all he were not
that only true God.

[1668 KBNL/UvA witness | logical printed p. 152 | PDF p. 203]

4. They say in the *Apology*, chapter 2, folio 39, that they do not concern
themselves with such vanities: Is the worship to be given to Christ the same
as the worship of God? Second, is the foundation of that worship Christ's
divine nature, or also some other dignity in which the human nature also
shares? Then, does it terminate upon the person as such? Or upon the person in
so far as the divine nature is in him? Certainly, if it is vain to say that
Christ is worshiped because the divine nature is in him, they indicate plainly
enough that in Christ there is not the excellence of the divine nature on
account of which he is worshiped. And if it is true that the same adoration is
owed to Christ as is owed to the Father, they doubt whether the divine nature
in Christ is the same as that in the Father. For their position is that an
inferior adoration and a subordinate veneration are owed to Christ. They
therefore establish a special chapter *On the Veneration of Jesus Christ*.
Hence it is evident that they make him a God inferior to the Father and to be
worshiped with an inferior adoration, the kind of adoration which the Arians
thought was owed to a most holy man. So Smalcius, *Response to the New
Monsters*, chapter 23, page 207; Socinus, *Against Wieck*, chapter 4, folio
160: “For no other reason ought we to trust in Christ than as in a second, or
instrumental, cause of our salvation.”

5. In the *Apology*, chapter 121, folio 138, they speak in Socinus's express
words: “For that honor”—given to Christ—“is not an honor by which Christ as
Mediator is worshiped above God himself, or as the highest and supreme Lord to
whom that honor is owed from himself. Far be it. This honor,

[1668 KBNL/UvA witness | logical printed p. 153 | PDF p. 204]

this glory, God always reserves whole to himself alone.” If they acknowledge
Christ to be God, consubstantial with the Father, first, why is honor shown to
Christ not honor owed to him as the supreme Lord? Second, why do they not say
that God, and Christ as God, reserves this glory whole to himself alone? But
they say that God—understanding the Father while not naming the Son—always
reserves this glory whole to himself. Third, they say it is vain to dispute
whether mediatorial honor is owed to him in so far as the divine nature is in
him or in so far as the human nature is in him. But here, with the
protestation “far be it,” they say that it is sacrilegious, and therefore
idolatrous, to give Christ the Mediator the same glory which the Father
reserves to himself. Yet it would not be idolatry unless they believed the
deity which is in the Mediator to be a participated and created deity.

6. If it would be ἐθελοθρησκεία (“will-worship”) to adore the Son unless there
were a positive command in the New Testament to adore him, then the Son is not
to be adored because of the deity which is in him, but because of God's
command. Arminius expressly states the former in private disputation 39.1 and
disputation 34.1, where he says that two things are required in order that
Christ be worshiped religiously: first, God's command to honor the Son;
second, that the Son undertake certain offices for the sake of human beings to
procure salvation for them. Therefore, Christ is not adored for the same
reason for which God the Father is adored—that is, not because of deity. For
the Father

[1668 KBNL/UvA witness | logical printed p. 154 | PDF p. 205]

is to be adored by virtue of the first precept of the Decalogue, although
there were no new command in the New Testament to adore him and he did not
undertake any offices for the sake of human beings. God alone is to be adored
with religious adoration, Matthew 4:10. Therefore, if Christ is not to be
adored unless he undertook certain offices for the sake of human beings, he is
not God; rather, he is adored because of those offices undertaken and the
Father's positive command. In the same place Arminius also maintains that
Christ is only the secondary object of religion, but God the primary object.
Yet if Christ is God for Arminius, he too is the primary object of religion.

7. In disputation 4 Arminius says nothing about Christ's essential kingdom,
which belongs to him as God and is common to him with the Father, but speaks
only about the mediatorial kingdom.

8. Arminius says in disputation 44, thesis 3, that trust must be placed in
Christ “as in him who was made the author of salvation, so that in the
covenant he might propose to us the way to eternal life.” But in this way
trust must be placed in Moses as one proposing the way to eternal salvation.

9. Arminius denies that Christ as God is Αὐτόθεον, as we saw above.

10. The Remonstrants say that many of the fathers deny Christ to be
consubstantial with the Father. But I think this happened for two reasons.
First, Hilary in *On the Synods* and Athanasius in his letter *On the Synods*
teach that the fathers at the Council of Antioch denied that the Son was
ὁμοούσιον with the Father according to a

[1668 KBNL/UvA witness | logical printed p. 155 | PDF p. 206]

participated deity, as the Samosatene said. I believe the Arminians think in
this way, but in this way he is ὁμοούσιος with the Father by grace. Second,
Jerome writes in *Against the Luciferians* that “the whole world groaned and was
astonished to find itself Arian.” At Constantius's command a synod was held at
Nicaea in Thrace, in which it was determined that the Son was not
consubstantial with the Father but like him. When the fathers at Ariminum,
induced by fear of the emperor, had subscribed at the Synod of Sirmium, they
determined that the Son was neither ὁμοούσιον (“of the same essence”) nor
ὁμοιούσιον (“of like essence”) with the Father, but merely like him in all
things. This synod was wholly Arian, and Pope Liberius and Hosius, bishop of
Córdoba, subscribed to it. I add that Hilary understood ὁμοούσιον by
ὁμοιούσιον, and that Augustine, against Pascentius the Arian, explained it as
likeness in substance.

HEBREWS 1:6

And let all the angels of God adore him.

It is asked whether Christ as man is adored, and whether as man he is the
formal object of adoration. The Remonstrants affirm it, chapter 16, § 4.

We deny it. 1. No creature, but God alone, is to be adored. Deuteronomy 6:13;
Matthew 4:10. But Christ the man, or Christ's humanity, is a creature; and
adoration is God's proper glory, which God does not will to give to another.
Isaiah 42:8.

2. Christ's humanity is not an idol.

3. It favors the Arians and Socinians to wrest from us the argument by which
we prove Christ to be true

[1668 KBNL/UvA witness | logical printed p. 156 | PDF p. 207]

God, namely, that all the angels adore him, Hebrews 1:6.

If they say that an inferior worship is owed to the humanity, I respond:

1. If this is understood of the worship of religious adoration, as it must be
understood here, it is said against the Scriptures.

2. It is against the consensus of the orthodox church, which taught that
Christ is worshiped with the same honor as the Father.

3. A window would be opened to all idolatry of images and angels if it were
lawful to render to creatures an honor of inferior adoration.

4. Scripture recognizes no honor of religious adoration owed to Christ on
account merely of an office imposed on him by the Father, or bestowed on him
from bare grace, because of the mediatorial office.

ROMANS 9:5

Who is God, blessed for ever.

It is asked whether a twofold communication of deity must be acknowledged in
Christ: one through secret and ineffable generation, the other through a
gracious communication of divine power and supreme glory, and therefore a
twofold sonship. The Remonstrants affirm it in the *Apology*, chapter 3, folio
48.

We deny it. 1. If deity was communicated to Christ once from eternity and once
in time, there will be two deities in Christ, one eternal and the other
temporal. Thus Christ will be two gods, which is blasphemous.

[1668 KBNL/UvA witness | logical printed p. 157 | PDF p. 208]

2. Through the incarnation and resurrection from the dead, Christ did not
acquire a new deity which he did not have before. Rather, he was only
“declared powerfully to be the Son of God, through the Spirit of sanctification
and the resurrection from the dead,” Romans 1:4. Therefore, the manifestation
of a preexistent deity, not the bestowal of a new deity, must be understood
here. For he raised himself from the dead through his own deity, John 2:19,
and he himself assumed our flesh into himself, Philippians 2:7.

3. Scripture does say that he received power and glory from the Father through
the resurrection from the dead. Yet this is not a temporal deity bestowed on
Christ, for then the Father would not glorify his Son with the glory which he
had with the Father before the foundations of the world were laid, John 17:5,
but with another, new glory of deity which he had acquired.

4. Nowhere in Scripture is Christ said to have been made God or deified
through the incarnation and resurrection from the dead, but only to have been
exalted, made prince, and obtained a name above every name. For now, the state
of emptying having been laid aside, he is declared the consubstantial Son of
God; and in each nature he is declared prince, supreme Lord of the church, and
judge, although the world had treated him with death, ignominy, and insults.

5. Two sonships presuppose two persons. And although what was born of Mary

[1668 KBNL/UvA witness | logical printed p. 158 | PDF p. 209]

is called the Son of God, Luke 1:35, it does not follow that in that nativity
a new Son and person was born. Rather, in that nativity the Son of God assumed
the human nature in the unity of his preexistent person.

6. Nothing of new deity or power could be added to Christ, the Son
consubstantial with the Father, because nothing can be added to God unless God
is changed. Therefore, a temporal deity cannot be added to God; consequently,
there is only a manifestation of divine glory.

But they say that Scripture asserts that this glory was given to Christ and
that Christ received a name and power. Response. To give glory to God and to
ascribe power to him do not signify a real giving of glory to God. Rather,
“to give” here means only to declare the glory given, or the glory previously
inherent, as Joshua says to Achan, “My son, give glory to God,” Joshua 7:19.
Nothing is more frequent in the Psalms and in the whole of Scripture.

7. “Deity communicated in time” and “temporal deity” are contradictions in
terms; they are deity and non-deity. For what begins in time is not deity.
Hence it is evident that when the Remonstrants assign two deities to Christ,
one through ineffable generation and the other through a temporal bestowal of
glory, they do not assert that the Son of God is true God. For nothing can be
added to or taken away from true God in time, and the

[1668 KBNL/UvA witness | logical printed p. 159 | PDF p. 210]

communication of deity belongs to Christ as God. Thus, according to the
Remonstrants, he would not be God from himself.

8. It is absurd that Christ should be adored χάριτι τῷ Θεῷ (“by the grace of
God”), as though the true formal object of adoration, namely deity, were not in
him.

But they will say that a twofold adoration belongs to Christ: one which
belongs to him as God, and another which belongs to him as Mediator. But this
is said without Scripture or reason. For the adoration owed to him as God
passes to the person, and to the humanity κατ᾽ ἄλλο (“in relation to something
else”). Therefore, another adoration is fashioned in vain, no more than two
bloods, two crosses, and two healings of the leper, the demoniac, and the dead
person—one which belongs to him as God and another which belongs to him as
man, or even as Mediator. Thus there is no twofold adoration either.

9. Against Nestorius, who divided the persons and attributed two kinds of
adoration to him, the ancient church defined that he is one person, has two
natures, and as Θεάνθρωπον (“God-man”) is adored with one and the same
adoration.

It is asked whether Christ as Mediator is to be adored. Response. “As,” or “in
so far as,” is taken either reduplicatively or specifically. Reduplicatively,
it denotes the formal ground of adoration and states the ground on account of
which someone is always and exclusively to be adored. God alone, but not the
Mediator alone, is to be adored; nor is mediatorial dignity the formal and

[1668 KBNL/UvA witness | logical printed p. 160 | PDF p. 211]

adequate ground why the Son is to be adored, because the Father is no less to
be adored than the Son, and so also the Holy Spirit is to be adored. But taken
specifically, the word does not state the ground on account of which someone
is always and exclusively to be adored. For example, a man as man is
snub-nosed, sees, is a geometer, and is a musician; nevertheless, not every
man, man alone, and man always is snub-nosed, is a geometer, and sees. If,
therefore, “as” is taken specifically in this question, Christ certainly is to
be adored as Mediator. That is, that person of the deity who is Mediator is to
be adored, and indeed also for this reason: he is Mediator and has undertaken,
performed, and continues to perform the mediatorial office for our good. But
if “as Mediator” is taken reduplicatively, he is not to be adored, because the
mediatorial office is not the proper, formal, and adequate ground of
adoration. Otherwise, neither the Father nor the Holy Spirit would be to be
adored.

MATTHEW 28:19

Baptize all, in the name of the Father, the Son, and the Holy Spirit.

It is asked whether the Holy Spirit is true God, consubstantial with the
Father and the Son. The Remonstrants say that they cannot prove the Holy
Spirit to be God from his being adored, since Scripture is full of the
adoration of the Father and the Son but contains not even the least thing
about the Holy Spirit. In the *Confession*, chapter 2, and the *Apology*,
chapter 2, folio 51, they say that because the Son or the Holy Spirit is called
Jehovah, it does not prove that the Son or

[1668 KBNL/UvA witness | logical printed p. 161 | PDF p. 212]

the Holy Spirit is God, since the name Jehovah is often taken nuncupatively
and is often attributed to created angels. It will thus be easy to evade every
passage in which the Son and the Holy Spirit are called God and Jehovah.

We maintain the contrary. 1. Isaiah 48:16: “The Lord Jehovah and his Spirit
sent me.” Isaiah 63:14: “The Spirit of Jehovah led them quietly; so thou
leddest thy people, to procure for thyself a glorious name.” Therefore, the
Holy Spirit is to be praised and adored as the leader of the people out of
Egypt—unless they mean “Spirit” to signify not a person, but God's power and
operation, as the Sadducees, antitrinitarians, and Sabellians understand the
term “Holy Spirit.”

2. The Creator is to be adored by all creatures because he is Creator. The
Sanctifier is to be praised and glorified, and hope is to be placed in him who,
as the supreme and intrinsically infallible teacher, leads us into all truth,
and who, as the principal cause, sends the church's ministers. But the Holy
Spirit, as the supreme and intrinsically infallible teacher, leads us into all
truth, John 16:13, and as the principal cause sends the church's ministers,
Acts 13:21 and 20:28.

3. He in whose name we are baptized is to be adored. But we are baptized in
the name of the Holy Spirit, Matthew 28:19.

4. He who distributes gifts to each person in the church as he wills is to be
adored. But the Holy Spirit does this, 1 Corinthians 12:11.

[1668 KBNL/UvA witness | logical printed p. 162 | PDF p. 213 | stop before TITULUS QUARTUS]

5. Song of Songs 4:15: “Awake, O north wind, and come, O south wind, and blow
through my garden”—that is, O Holy Spirit, who blowest where thou wilt, John
3:8—“make my garden fruitful.” Indeed, an example of the church adoring the
Holy Spirit shines forth to us here, as in 2 Corinthians 13:14; Acts 13:2–3;
Acts 4:24; Revelation 1:4 compared with Isaiah 6:3; Hebrews 3:7; and Acts
28:25.

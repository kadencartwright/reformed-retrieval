Question 14. By what kind of power does the good of GOD’s displayed glory
result from sin? I answer that, on the part of the creature that sins, it
results by a merely obediential power and accidentally, for the creature does
nothing in the formal act of punishing or pardoning sins, but undergoes it. Yet
on GOD’s part it cannot be designated by the name “obediential power,” because
GOD of himself, with an intention equally primary, punishes and pardons, and
indeed saves, shows mercy to, or converts sinners.

[Marginalia: By what kind of power the good of God’s glory results from sin.]

Question 15. By what kind of knowledge does GOD know conditionally future
contingents—for example, that David would be delivered into Saul’s power if he
remained among the inhabitants of Keilah? I answer that here the concatenation
or connection between the remaining and the delivery must be considered. 1.
The delivery
itself must be viewed not simply, but as conditionally future. The concatenation
in turn is viewed either as removed from every decree of GOD, antecedently to
and apart from GOD’s decree, or as joined with it. In the former manner, because
there is no necessary connection, GOD recognizes this concatenation, like all
other possibilities, by natural knowledge and the knowledge of simple
intelligence. But in my judgment, all these concatenations are absolutely
decreed by God, and thus GOD knows them by free knowledge, or the knowledge of
vision. Many therefore torment themselves in vain about the mirror in which God
beholds these things.

But the actual delivery, considered in itself, was only possible if the
condition were posited—namely, David’s remaining—which condition GOD decreed
would never be posited, by a positive decree, so that David’s safety and
well-being would thus be provided for. GOD knows that delivery by the knowledge
of simple intelligence. Yet that same delivery, as concatenated with David’s
remaining among the inhabitants of Keilah—about which David so anxiously
consulted the oracle—and as concatenated with and clothed in that condition of
remaining, was
actual, certain, determinate, and known to GOD by free knowledge, or the
knowledge of vision. Thus it is as foolish to say that anxious David consulted
GOD about a merely possible delivery—which was by no means hidden from David,
for otherwise he would never have consulted GOD—as it is blasphemous to say
that GOD replied concerning a possible and conjectural delivery: because 1.
David did not consult him about such a delivery. 2. Nor would a reply about such
a conditional, possible, and uncertain delivery have satisfied GOD’s servant or
provided for his safety. For the delivery was just as possible as the
non-delivery,

Question 16. Is the decree by which GOD determined a hypothetical connection
futile when the connected extremes will never be future? I answer: the decree
is by no means therefore void, because all possibilities that will never be
future fall under GOD’s most actual and positive decree, as has often been said
above. And since all beings either cohere or do not cohere, providence would not
be infinite apart from such a decree concerning such coherences. For since
Scripture says that certain future contingents cohere and they are
determinately foretold by GOD to be—and consequently they are foreknown to
be such that they cannot fail to be—they must possess determinate truth. Yet
they do not possess such truth from themselves and apart from GOD’s decree.
Therefore they possess it from GOD’s decree alone.

2. Nothing can exist, happen, or cohere with something else fortuitously by
brute fate, blind fortune, or empty chance. Therefore it occurs through GOD’s
providence. 3. It must necessarily be confessed that connections of this kind
are decreed by GOD. Otherwise, God could not even be said to will the
concatenation or coherence of eternal salvation with the faith and obedience
of the reprobate, even though the connected extremes—namely, the obedience and
eternal salvation of the reprobate—are never decreed by GOD. But it is
evident from GOD’s words to Cain, Genesis 4:7, that GOD willed such a
concatenation by his approving will and therefore decreed it: *If you do well,
will you not be accepted?* And Proverbs 1:24: *Since I call and you refuse, I
stretch out my hand and no one attends.* Verse 26: *I too shall laugh in your
calamity.* Acts 13:46: *Since you reject it (the word of life) and judge
yourselves unworthy of eternal life, behold, we turn to the Gentiles.* See also
Matthew 23:27; John 8:21–23; John 5:39–40; Acts 18:4–6; Acts 28:23–26; and 1
Thessalonians 2:13–16. Nor is GOD’s decree void even if GOD never decreed the
connected extremes, because in these things GOD intends the glory of justice
and never intends the final obedience and salvation of the reprobate.

[Marginalia: The decreed concatenation of extremes that will never be future in
the nature of things demonstrates the most perfect providence in God; such
decrees are not void.]

Question 17. In the glorious GOD, are the necessity of nature in outward action
and hypothetical necessity compatible with each other, and can they be the same

necessities? I answer affirmatively. For in respect of the mode of acting and of
the divine constancy and immutability, GOD necessarily creates the world if he
has decreed to create it; and yet he creates freely in respect of the thing
produced outwardly. How freedom and immutability consist together in GOD is a
mystery which I believe, and I either profess or glory that I do not comprehend
it.

# How a Godly Man May Withdraw from the Superstitions of the Papacy

This is a fresh English translation of the complete Latin presentation at CO
X/1 188–189. According to CO's source note, Calvin wrote the advice in French
and Theodore Beza published it in Latin. The French archetype was not used or
reconstructed here: this English translates Beza's historical Latin witness.
No wording from Beaty and Farley's modern translation supplied the English.

## Translation

### [CO X/1, p. 188]

I thank the Lord, dearest brother, because he has led you to a sincere
knowledge of his truth and rescued you from those errors into which he
sometimes allows even his own people to fall, in order to humble them in this
way. See, therefore, that you walk in this simplicity of faith in such a way
that the Lord may confirm you in it more and more. Since you are already
united with our Lord Jesus Christ out of this world and exist as a member of
him—insofar as he put on our flesh in order to enter into full brotherhood
with us—order your life according to the Gospel's rule. Its sum is that we
seek solid salvation in him alone and thus at last attain that life which is
laid up for

### [CO X/1, p. 189]

the faithful in heaven, in the hope and expectation of resurrection to
eternal glory. Indeed, that fixed hope must remain with us as long as our
bodies rest on earth: our souls will live with him in heaven.

As for what you ask me—whether a Christian may partake of the Lord's Supper
administered there—the answer is plain: he certainly may, provided that what
is in question is Christ's Supper. But when you weigh the matter as you
should, you will find no more agreement between the Lord's Supper and the
papal mass than between light and darkness. It is established that human
wickedness or ignorance cannot make the Lord's ordinance worse. But I utterly
deny that the mass can be referred to Christ as its author. On the contrary,
I affirm that Satan himself fabricated it as an insult to Christ and as the
most certain overthrow of the holy Supper. It stands diametrically opposed to
the Supper when it is classed as a sacrifice and the power and efficacy of the
Lord's passion are transferred to a recklessly invented action.

Moreover, the most open idolatry exists there, not only where the bread is
adored, but also because prayer is offered for the dead, the merits and
intercessions of the saints are invoked, and many things of this kind are done
there which the Lord expressly condemns. The faithful are therefore no more
permitted to participate in that superstition than they were once permitted
to sacrifice at Bethel. It is wholly contrary to the confession of faith
which the Lord requires from us.

Because this argument has been treated more fully in books published on the
subject, I ask you to examine them diligently. Meanwhile, I commend you to the
Lord's grace, that he may teach you his will and open your eyes to distinguish
black from white. I trust that in this way you will not lack something in
which to rest. For your part, meanwhile, deny yourself every liberty to
dissemble by appearing in any way to approve impiety. May the Lord increase
his gifts in you and, when he has shown what is right, also give you a steadfast
spirit so that, once you have recognized it, you may adhere to it as you
should. Geneva, 15 June.

Yours, Calvin.

## Translator's notes

1. Beza's notice that the work was written in French makes the printed Latin a
   translation witness, not Calvin's recoverable original wording. The
   English therefore stays accountable to the Latin printed by CO while
   identifying its derivative language state.
2. The compressed *iam ex hoc mundo cum Domino nostro Iesu Christo unitus sis*
   is rendered “already united ... out of this world.” It can suggest both
   separation from the present world and a union already effected during the
   present life; the Latin translation does not make that relation fully
   explicit.
3. Calvin's initial permission to share the Lord's Supper is conditional on
   the rite actually being Christ's Supper. The following contrast denies
   that the papal mass satisfies the condition; it is not a general permission
   to attend the mass.
4. “Deny yourself every liberty to dissemble” renders *tibi ... omnem
   licentiam praecidas simulandi*. The following indirect statement identifies
   the dissimulation as outwardly appearing to approve the impiety Calvin has
   just rejected.
5. The closing date supplies day and month but no year. No recipient's name is
   preserved in the body; *frater carissime* establishes only that Calvin
   addresses a beloved male fellow Christian.

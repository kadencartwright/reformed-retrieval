# John Brown, *Libri duo contra Wolzogium et Velthusium*, Volume II (1670)

> **Working edition in progress:** This directory contains an AI-assisted,
> unreviewed diplomatic-normalized Latin transcription and independent close
> English translation. It is not a critical edition or publication-ready text;
> every reading and translation should be checked by a qualified human editor
> against the cited ONB facsimile.

## Work and volume identity

- **Author:** John Brown of Wamphray (c. 1610–1679), printed as Johannes Broun
  in the ONB catalog record
- **Collective work:** *Libri duo. In priori, Wolzogium ... In posteriori,
  Lamberti Velthusii sententia Libertino-Erastiana ...*
- **Volume used here:** Volume II only, with the divisional title
  *Libertino-Erastianæ Lamberti Velthusii Sententiæ, De Ministerio, Regimine, &
  Disciplinâ Ecclesiasticâ ... Confutatio*
- **Date:** 1670
- **Cataloged collective imprint:** Amsterdam, Henricus ab Aquisgrano
- **Subject of Volume II:** Lambert van Velthuysen’s position on ecclesiastical
  ministry, government, and discipline

The divisional title leaf says that the refutation was written “by the same
author” but does not repeat Brown’s name or the collective imprint. Those data
come from the ONB record for the bound two-volume work. No text from Volume I,
Brown’s work against Wolzogen, is transcribed or translated here. Wolzogen is
mentioned once within the selected Volume II text because Brown compares his
treatment of orthodox theologians with Velthuysen’s.

## Part 01 boundary and source map

Part 01 is Brown’s first complete argumentative unit in the body: his statement
and synopsis of Velthuysen’s position, the first of the three steps Brown
announces on printed p. 4.

- It begins with the body heading and `Narus Satanas quàm necessarium...` on
  printed p. 1 / ONB canvas 9.
- It carries Brown’s introduction and his detailed extraction of Velthuysen’s
  claims concerning church power, excommunication, ministry, ordination,
  preaching, sacraments, and the secular magistrate.
- It includes Brown’s final compact synopsis and ends with the complete sentence
  `Plus potest unus aliquis ... quàm plures docti multarum horarum, imò, &
  dierum spacio, confirmare ac demonstrare possunt.` at the top of printed p. 20
  / ONB canvas 28.
- The next paragraph, `Nos tamen compendiosam aliquam inibimus rationem...`,
  begins Brown’s transition to his second announced step, comparison with the
  Reformed confessions, on the same page and is excluded.

The unit therefore spans exactly twenty printed pages, pp. 1–20 / ONB canvases
9–28, with only the upper portion of p. 20 included. The unnumbered divisional
title on canvas 7 and its verso on canvas 8 are mapped here as preliminary
matter but are not part of the twenty-page body unit.

## Part 02 boundary and source map

Part 02 begins with `Nos tamen compendiosam aliquam inibimus rationem...` in
the lower portion of printed p. 20 / ONB canvas 28. It starts Brown’s comparison
of Velthuysen’s position with the Reformed confessions and continues through
the Helvetic, Gallican, Belgic, Augsburg, and Saxon testimonies. It ends in the
upper portion of printed p. 37 / ONB canvas 45 after Brown’s application of the
Saxon Confession and before the Württemberg Confession begins. Its paired files
contain eighteen synchronized markers for printed pp. 20–37.

## Part 03 boundary and source map

Part 03 completes the confessional survey and then contains Brown’s first two
assertions concerning Christ’s mediatorial kingdom.

- It begins with `Confess. Wirtemberg cap. de ordine` in the lower portion of
  printed p. 37 / ONB canvas 45.
- It completes the Württemberg and Bohemian material, cites Polish synods and
  the Basel Confession, and concludes the survey with chapters 23, 30, and 31
  of the British Confession.
- `ASSERTIO I` proves that Christ as mediator is king and possesses a kingdom;
  `ASSERTIO II` distinguishes that mediatorial kingdom from his universal
  kingdom as God.
- It ends with the complete sentence `De secundâ assertione jam satis.` in the
  upper portion of printed p. 57 / ONB canvas 65. The following heading,
  `ASSERTIO III`, and its text on the same page are excluded.

The unit has twenty-one synchronized markers for printed pp. 37–57. Because
only the lower portion of p. 37 and upper portion of p. 57 belong to it, the
unit comprises approximately twenty full pages of text without splitting
Assertion II.

## Editorial and translation method

Long *s*, ligatures, line-end divisions, and typographic `u/v` are normalized.
Obvious typographic contractions are expanded, while common reference
abbreviations are retained. Substantive spelling, wording, names, numbering,
quotations, and foreign-language matter are retained. Running heads,
bare page numbers, signatures, catchwords, ornaments, and non-textual bleed from
adjacent pages are omitted. Page markers identify the volume, printed page,
and one-based ONB canvas. The duplicate ampersand printed on p. 1 is retained.

The short Greek expression on p. 9 is retained in the Latin and explained in a
translator’s note. Brown’s quotations from Velthuysen are distinguished
typographically, but their wording remains Brown’s Latin presentation rather
than a new transcription of Velthuysen’s Dutch books.

The English is a full translation of the accompanying Latin, not a summary.
It preserves Brown’s polemical attributions as historical claims rather than
endorsing them. Biblical quotations are translated directly from Brown’s
printed Latin wording. No modern translation has been used as a wording source.

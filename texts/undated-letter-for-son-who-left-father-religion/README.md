# A Letter Written in the Name of a Son Who Had Withdrawn from His Father for Religion's Sake

This directory presents the complete Latin-form witness and a new English
translation of the consilium headed *Epistola cuiusdam nomine scripta, qui se
a patre religionis nomine subduxerat* in the *Calvini Opera*.

## Work, voice, and witness identity

| Field | Value |
|---|---|
| Historical composer | John Calvin (collection attribution) |
| Represented writer | An unnamed young man on whose behalf Calvin wrote |
| Addressee | The represented writer's unnamed father, repeatedly addressed as *observande pater* or *mi pater* |
| CO consilium | II.1, *Epistola cuiusdam nomine scripta, qui se a patre religionis nomine subduxerat* |
| Date and place | Not preserved |
| Authorial language state | French, according to Theodore Beza as reported by CO |
| Witness edited here | Beza's Latin translation, reprinted by CO |
| Genre | Proxy or ghostwritten filial appeal in letter form |
| Principal topics | Filial obedience, conscience, the rule of Scripture, corrupt worship, the mass and Lord's Supper, and a proposed family hearing |
| CO locator | X/1, pp. 179–181 |

The title's *cuiusdam nomine scripta* controls the first-person voice: Calvin
composed the letter in another person's name. The “I” who has left home, calls
the recipient father, refers to his own youth, and asks that a paternal uncle
be admitted is the represented son. Those details are evidence about the
rhetorical situation Calvin was asked to voice, not autobiographical evidence
about Calvin.

The language-state distinction is equally important. CO's source note says
that Beza included the letter among the epistles and called it translated from
French. CO does not print a French archetype here. Accordingly, `latin.txt`
edits Beza's historical Latin translation and [`english.md`](english.md)
translates that Latin; neither file purports to reconstruct Calvin's unlocated
French wording.

## Edition, headings, and transmission

The transcription follows Wilhelm Baum, Eduard Cunitz, and Eduard Reuss,
eds., *Ioannis Calvini opera quae supersunt omnia*, vol. X/1 (*Corpus
Reformatorum* XXXVIII) (Brunswick: C. A. Schwetschke, 1871), pp. 179–181.

The item stands first beneath the collection division *De mutata aut mutanda
religione*. That divisional title, the displayed item heading, and CO's
numbered source note are collection or publication paratext, not words spoken
by the son to his father. The historical address begins *Quod facti mei
rationem scripto et absens* and ends with *Bene vale, observande pater* and the
request that the father acknowledge his *subductitium*. It has no transmitted
salutation, date, place, personal name, or signature.

CO's note records Beza's Latin in the Geneva edition at p. 143, Lausanne p.
317, Hanover p. 354, Chouet p. 194, and Amsterdam p. 88. It reports no
manuscript, French copy, or body variant. The note's description of the text as
*e gallico versam* supplies the French-to-Latin witness relationship but not
the wording of the French source.

PDF p. 111 suppresses visible folio numbers on the divisional opening, but the
continuous CO pagination fixes its left and right columns as pp. 179 and 180;
the following spread visibly begins at 181. Every body line was checked
against 300-DPI, 2500 × 3150-pixel renderings. Extracted text was used only
for orientation.

The p. 179/180 boundary falls between *et* and *communi*; the p. 180/181
boundary falls between *molestiam* and *augere*. Neither divides a word. Latin
and English preserve the exact marker sequence 179, 180, 181, with the English
p. 181 marker placed immediately before “increase,” the rendering of *augere*.
The transcription excludes all headings, CO's source note, running and page
heads, and the following work. No body reading is illegible, conjectural, or
silently supplied.

## Translation status and textual cruxes

Mary D. Beaty and Benjamin Wirt Farley's copyrighted *Calvin's Ecclesiastical
Advice* (1991) translates the complete 46-item *consilia* collection. The
bibliographical audit located no complete reusable public-domain English
version of this letter. Their wording was not consulted or reused; the English
here was translated afresh from the transcribed Latin and checked against the
facsimile.

CO prints the grammatically awkward *in cultus Dei ratione quem pro vera
habes*: masculine *quem* follows feminine *ratione*. The transcription does
not emend it to *quam*; the translation gives the evident reference to the
form of worship the father regards as true and records the anomaly in a note.
Two other compressed constructions remain visible in the notes: *quod qui a
Deo edoctus sim* and concessive or hypothetical *ut aliis sua ignorantia ...
fraudi etiam non sit*.

The closing also resists flattening. *Tuum ... subductitium* is an unusual
substantive for the father's own person who has been withdrawn, deliberately
echoing the title's *subduxerat*. The proposed *patruus* is naturally the
son's paternal uncle, but the Latin does not state whether he is to mediate,
serve as witness, or participate in a doctrinal hearing.

This is an **AI-assisted scholarly draft** checked against every printed line,
not an independently reviewed critical edition. Exact claims should cite the
Latin witness and printed CO page, particularly where the relationship to the
lost French text or the represented son's voice matters.

Suggested citation:

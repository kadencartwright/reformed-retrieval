# Whether It Is Lawful to Make Images to Represent God

This directory presents the complete direct-French witness and a new English
translation of Calvin's answer to the immediately preceding paper of the
inquisitor Horrys.

## Work and witness identity

| Field | Value |
|---|---|
| Principal author | John Calvin |
| CO dossier item | III.3, *S'il est licite de faire des images pour representer Dieu* |
| Interlocutor | Brother Matthieu Horrys/Horris/Orris/Oris, an inquisitor; spellings vary across the dossier and witnesses |
| Date, place, addressee, signature | Not supplied in the body |
| Original language/form | French |
| Principal witness | Bibliothèque de Genève, Cod. 145, fol. 118, in Charles de Jonvilliers's hand; not Calvin's autograph |
| Additional witness | Another copy at Cod. 145, fol. 121; CO says it calls the inquisitor *Oris* |
| Genre | Polemical response to a theological memorandum |
| Topics | Continuing moral law; the Second Commandment; natural reason; visible representations of God; cherubim; idolatry; New Testament evidence |
| CO locator | X/1, pp. 199–202 |

CO reports that the title is written on the back of the principal copy with
the note “Question proposée par frère Matthieu Horris inquisiteur.” The text
answers the argument printed immediately before it, but it is a distinct work:
the Horrys paper ends on p. 199 before this title and response begin. Scribal
transmission through Jonvilliers's hand should not be confused either with
authorship of the argument or with a Calvin autograph.

## Source and transcription

The transcription follows Wilhelm Baum, Eduard Cunitz, and Eduard Reuss, eds.,
*Ioannis Calvini opera quae supersunt omnia*, vol. X, part 1 (*Corpus
Reformatorum* XXXVIII; Brunswick: C. A. Schwetschke, 1871), printed pp.
199–202. The volume is available through the University of Geneva's [complete
Calvin collection](https://archive-ouverte.unige.ch/unige:650) and as a [direct
volume X/1 PDF](https://access.archive-ouverte.unige.ch/access/metadata/f526a338-c95c-4f87-b600-04df723f502b/download).

| UNIGE PDF page | Printed CO pages | Text represented |
|---:|---:|---|
| 121 | 199–200 | Opening of III.3 on p. 199 and continuation on p. 200 |
| 122 | 201–202 | Continuation and conclusion of III.3 |

Both openings were inspected at the facsimile's native 300-DPI resolution.
The PDF has 362 pages, is 33,386,580 bytes, and has SHA-256
`025973d6e4533769d0c2b870dd324497943bc14050eb2bcbcc4c964d4f2a1777`.
The body begins *La question est, Ascavoir* and ends *en le voulant ainsi
représenter*. Every word in that interval was collated. The CO heading,
manuscript note, p. 201 editorial footnote, running heads, page numbers,
terminal rule, preceding Horrys paper, and following work are excluded and
documented separately.

No page turn divides a word. The boundaries are *garde* / *les commandemens*
(199/200), *consequemment* / *Que tous* (200/201), and *hommes* / *Touchant*
(201/202). The French and English preserve the same four-marker sequence.

## Textual and interpretive cautions

- On p. 201 CO prints *qu'il ne fault iuger par la veue des aveugles*. The
  negation makes the analogy work against its context; CO's footnote asks
  “*nous? me?*” The French retains *ne*. The English gives the sense apparently
  required—judging by the blind person's sight—and explicitly marks the crux
  in its translator's notes.
- The response itself moves among *Horris*, *Orris*, and *Oris*. The spellings
  are not normalized, and the additional manuscript's *Oris* is witness
  evidence rather than a new person.
- Polemical terms such as *cafart* and assertions that God makes opponents
  senseless are part of the historical rhetoric. They are translated rather
  than silently softened, without implying editorial endorsement.
- Forms including *Signeur*, *Chorubins*, and *seulloment* were verified as
  printed. No illegible lacuna was supplied and no conjectural emendation was
  introduced into `french.txt`.

## Translation status and use

Mary D. Beaty and Benjamin Wirt Farley's copyrighted *Calvin's Ecclesiastical
Advice* (1991) translates the consilia collection. The project's bibliography
audit located no complete reusable public-domain English version of this
response. That modern translation was not consulted or reused;
[`english.md`](english.md) is a fresh translation from the French witness.

This is an AI-assisted working edition checked against the complete
facsimile, not a peer-reviewed critical edition. Readers should preserve the
dossier boundary, Jonvilliers copy-witness status, variant name spellings, and
absence of a date or subscription when citing it.

CHAPTER THREE

ON ELECTION

ROMANS 9:11

It is asked whether there is a twofold election to salvation: one indefinite
and general, namely, by which God elects all who believe to salvation without
considering these or those persons; another definite and special, by which God
elects certain persons by name to glory and views them as finally believing.
The Arminians affirm it. We deny it.

1. For indefinite election is not election, because in it no singular person
is ordained to salvation. Only the way of attaining salvation is prescribed
indiscriminately to all, and this can stand with the eternal reprobation of all
and the eternal damnation of the same persons. But the election spoken of in
Scripture does not stand with the damnation of the elect. Romans 8:30: “Those
whom he predestined, he called, justified, and glorified.”

2. For indefinite reprobation also stands with the eternal salvation of all,
which is absurd.

3. Election is a singular, indivisible act concerning the ordaining of
determinate human beings to glory, just as it is the preparation of such a
portion of clay for

[1668 KBNL/UvA witness | logical printed p. 239 | PDF p. 290]

honor, Romans 9:21.

4. For in this way human beings would be elected twice: once in general
election and again in special election.

5. For election is an act of divine love which separates these persons from
those for glory, Ephesians 1:4: “Just as he elected us in him,” and so forth;
Romans 9:30. But by this general election God pursues Judas and Simon Magus
with as much love as Peter and Paul.

It is asked whether any election or predestination in Scripture is other than
that of singular, definite, and certain persons, or whether it is of all
indiscriminately, if they should be willing to believe. The Arminians in the
*Hague Conference*, page 34, and Corvinus, *Against Tilenus*, page 53, say that
the decree of election is nothing other than the decree by which God decreed
to justify and save believers in Christ.

We, on the contrary, teach that election is only of certain persons.

1. So Scripture teaches, Matthew 20:16: “Many are called; few are elect.”
Matthew 24:22: “For the sake of the elect those days will be shortened.” Romans
9:11: “When the children were not yet born and had done nothing good or evil,”
and so forth. Romans 11:5: “Thus, therefore, also at this time a remnant has
been made according to gracious election.” John 15:16: “You did not elect me,
but I elected you and ordained you to go and bear fruit.” Therefore, they were
elected in order that they might believe, not because they had believed.

2. For they are defined by certain pronouns.

[1668 KBNL/UvA witness | logical printed p. 240 | PDF p. 291]

Romans 8:30: “Those whom he predestined,” and so forth.

3. For the number of the elect is certain and is as many as believe in time,
neither more nor fewer. Acts 13:48: “As many as were ordained to eternal life
believed.”

4. For the elect are inscribed in the book of life, Luke 10:20; Philippians
4:3; Revelation 21:27. But those who are thus designated in God's mind are not
lunar human beings wandering in a common notion, uncircumscribed by the
Arminians' “provided that” and “if.”

5. For that election belongs to each and every person if they should believe,
whereas scriptural election does not belong to everyone, because not everyone
is elect and not everyone is given by the Father to the Son.

6. For Christ determinately acknowledges the elect as his own, as his sheep,
John 10:16; as his peculiar flock; and as those given to him by the Father,
John 17:9.

7. For this election is God's will concerning the means of salvation.
Therefore, it is not election. For by the decree of election faith is given to
or withheld from whomever God wills, according as he elects or does not elect
human beings. But by the will concerning the means, faith is given to no one.

8. For this election can stand with the eternal salvation of all. Thus it is
not election and the assumption of some while others are passed over. It can
also stand with the eternal damnation of all.

It is asked whether God destines some to glory incompletely, revocably, for a
time, and not peremptorily—namely, those who believe only for a time—but

[1668 KBNL/UvA witness | logical printed p. 241 | PDF p. 292]

destines others to glory completely, irrevocably, and peremptorily—namely,
those whom he foreknows as finally believing and dying in the faith. The
Arminians affirm it, *Apology*, chapter 9, folio 104.

We judge revocable and mutable election to be plainly blasphemous.

1. For “God's counsel for the heirs is immutable,” Hebrews 6:17, and for a
certain particular person, namely Abraham, verses 13–15. Yet according to the
adversaries he could have fallen away, and not all his obedience after God's
oath was constrained but free. And Romans 11:29 says, “For God's gifts and
calling are ἀμεταμέλητα,” “irrevocable”—things of which God cannot repent.
Therefore, the Jews are not utterly rejected but are still beloved by God with
respect to election. Likewise Malachi 3:6: “Because I, Jehovah, do not change,
you sons of Jacob have therefore not been consumed.” Therefore, if those whom
God had decreed would never be consumed had been consumed, Jehovah would have
changed. And if those whom God destined to glory did not attain glory, God
would have changed.

2. For Romans 8:30 says: “Those whom God predestined, he called; those whom he
called, he justified; those whom he justified, he glorified.” But if a human
being were not peremptorily elected before the end of life, God would
predestine many whom he would never glorify. The apostle's order would then be
inverted, and it would have to be said, “Those whom God justified, those and no
others he predestined.”

[1668 KBNL/UvA witness | logical printed p. 242 | PDF p. 293]

3. For no one would be elect in this life or have eternal glory in this life,
since he would be just as revocably and incompletely reprobate as revocably and
incompletely elect. But many are elect in this life, Ephesians 1:4; Matthew
20:16; Matthew 24:25. Indeed, Christ willed the disciples to rejoice in this
life “because their names were inscribed in the book of life,” Luke 10:20.

4. For revocable election is a mutable volition and a mutable love in God,
contrary to Scripture, which says that God's love is eternal and that he loves
to the end those whom he loves.

5. For seven and a hundred times in one person, as often as a human being
believed or did not believe, God would establish, rescind, and newly change his
eternal counsels and decrees.

6. For new decrees would begin and end in God in time, and old ones would
depart.

7. For no one would have hope, confidence, joy, and Christian consolations in
the sense of election, though this is commanded to the elect in the Word, Luke
10:20: “Rejoice that your names are inscribed in heaven.”

8. For if Pilate said, “What I have written, I have written,” much more does
God say: “Those whom I destined to eternal glory as persons enrolled in the
book of life, I have destined to eternal glory.”

9. For Christ would falsely say to some, Matthew 7:23, “I never knew you,”
because he at one time knew as his own those revocably elected to glory, for

[1668 KBNL/UvA witness | logical printed p. 243 | PDF p. 294]

whom he met death and whose salvation on his part he seriously intended just
as much as that of others who were saved after constant faith and godliness.

10. For Christ would poorly prove that he had to call many from among the
Gentiles because, he says, “They are my sheep,” John 10:16—namely, in my
eternal predestination. But there is no consequence if they are only revocably
elect and are not certainly elect before the end of life. For then they were
not Christ's until they died in faith. But what need have the dead of a call to
faith?

11. If we are elected “before the foundations of the world were laid,”
Ephesians 1:4, and “grace was given to us according to the purpose before
secular times,” 2 Timothy 1:9, then there is no suspended and wavering election
dependent upon the last acts of free choice in which human beings die.

12. For given complete and incomplete election, no election to adoption, and
therefore to faith, of which Paul speaks in Ephesians 1:5, can be conceived.
For according to the adversaries, we are elected for one time because we
believe, and we are not elected for another time because we do not believe.

13. For no wise person destines for a time to an end someone whom he foreknows
will never attain the end, as though God were to say: “I foreknow that Judas
will be eternally damned and will never attain eternal glory. Nevertheless, I
destine him to glory for a time, and I know well enough that I will eventually
retract and change my

[1668 KBNL/UvA witness | logical printed p. 244 | PDF p. 295]

destining will.”

14. For there would be such a promise in Scripture: “Do this, and you will be
irrevocably elect”—namely, “After death you will be elected to glory,
peremptorily and irrevocably.” But such a promise is found nowhere in
Scripture.

15. For the statement in Psalm 33:10–11 would be false: “The counsels of the
nations are mutable and can be dispersed, but God's cannot be annulled and
stand immutable through all generations.” For the counsels of God destining
Judas to glory can be established and annulled before the end of Judas's life,
since these counsels are revocable according as Judas pleases.

16. For Peter, by finally believing, would irrevocably elect God and would be
the cause of peremptory election. Nor would more thanks be owed to God here
because Peter is irrevocably elect than because Judas is irrevocably elect.
For Judas, if he had indeed been willing, could have been irrevocably elected
just as well as Peter. For in this general decree—“I will to elect to grace,
irrevocably and peremptorily, all who finally die in faith: Peter if he wills,
and Judas if he wills”—God contributed nothing more to Peter's irrevocable
election than to Judas's.

EPHESIANS 1:3

“Just as he elected us in him before the foundations of the world were laid,
that we should be holy.”

[1668 KBNL/UvA witness | logical printed p. 245 | PDF p. 296]

It is asked whether the election of this person rather than that person to
glory occurs from God's free will alone and his absolute good pleasure, or
whether it occurs according to foreknown faith as a cause, condition, or
quality required in the object.

To bring out the state of the question, it must be noted:

1. Predestination is from eternity, although the Remonstrants are now not
ashamed to say that election, contrary to what has hitherto been believed, is
a temporal and transient act.

2. God determined the final state of all human beings not rashly but by a
certain counsel.

3. The question is not whether the proximate cause of election and reprobation
is in God—namely, his will—but whether there is something outside God in a
human being which determines God's will to elect this one and reprobate that
one.

4. It is agreed that the cause of predestination in general is God's will.
Thus the Remonstrants' *Synodical Writings*, article 1, pages 5–6: “One thing is
predestination in general to life and death; another is the predestination of
this or that person dying in faith or unbelief. The first decree is entirely
free and has no other cause besides God's pure and simple will and choice.”

5. Nor is it asked why in general some are elect and some reprobate, because
the glory of justice and mercy and the adornment of the world is the cause why
some are reprobate and some elect. But the whole

[1668 KBNL/UvA witness | logical printed p. 246 | PDF p. 297]

question concerns comparative election: why this portion of the mass and clay
is destined to honor, and that one to dishonor.

6. It is agreed that God's dominion, mercy, and justice must remain secure and
unimpaired here.

7. God does not rashly send everyone to the two ends, whether permissively or
positively.

8. God's predestination is not the cause of sin.

By an absolute decree we do not mean a decree loosed from all knowledge, for
God has a reason for his decree.

2. Nor do we mean a decree loosed from the knowledge and understanding of the
final cause, which is God's glory.

Nor, 3, is it so absolute that it excludes all the means serving the decree's
execution. For Peter is so ordained to glory that by the same decree he is
ordained to all the efficacious means which lead him to glory.

4. Nor is it so absolute that it would drag the will by force, contrary to the
character of its freedom, so that the decree must stand whether the will were
unwilling or willing, as the adversaries slanderously allege. Rather, the
decree of predestination is absolute in this way: God's good pleasure in
ordaining Peter rather than Judas to glory is not moved causally to predestine
him, nor inclined occasionally, nor determined by any order of God's justice
or truthfulness, nor by

[1668 KBNL/UvA witness | logical printed p. 247 | PDF p. 298]

any meritorious cause, or by a cause moving from congruity, from fittingness,
or from any quality, disposition, or condition in Peter. Therefore, it is
asked whether faith is a condition, quality, or office prerequired in the one
whom God elects to glory, or whether faith is an effect and fruit flowing from
election.

Throughout the entire question, once it is framed, the Arminians act craftily.
For, 1, Arminius posits four decrees concerning predestination:

1. An absolute decree concerning sending a Savior into the world.

2. An absolute decree by which he determined to save the repentant and damn
the impenitent.

3. A decree concerning the sufficient and efficacious administration of the
means necessary for faith and repentance.

4. A decree to save certain singular persons whom he foreknows will finally
believe, and to damn those whom he foreknows will finally be impenitent.

They do not teach whether all these decrees, only the second, or the second and
fourth are to be denoted in the question of predestination. For elsewhere they
make the second decree the whole decree of predestination, *Hague Conference*,
page 34. Thus the question of what cause outside God moved him to decree from
eternity to save the repentant and damn the impenitent is empty—

[1668 KBNL/UvA witness | logical printed p. 248 | PDF p. 299]

that is, one could likewise ask what cause there is why those who fulfill the
law are saved and why the Rechabites who did not drink wine are rewarded by
God.

2. They deal deceitfully concerning predestined persons. For they teach that
there is no election and no reprobation of infants, the mentally incapacitated,
the insane, the deaf, and all who have heard nothing of the gospel, as though
neither heaven nor hell had been prepared for them and they were sent to those
two places by no predestination of God but by the blind impulse of fortune.

3. In the question and in their theses they do not distinguish between
complete and immutable election and incomplete and mutable election. For it is
one question why Judas is elected for a time, and another why he is
irrevocably predestined to death.

4. They do not deal sincerely concerning the prerequired condition and cause
of election, because for them Christ is the meritorious cause of election—if
indeed they acknowledge any merit in Christ. And I do not see how he is not
the meritorious cause of the non-election of others, namely, reprobation.

5. They imagine that predestination to the means of salvation and to faith is
common to each and every person: namely, God decreed that all mortals should
believe, provided that they willed. But they do not in this way usually say
that God decreed to lead each and every person to the fruit of faith—namely,
eternal glory—provided that they believed.

[1668 KBNL/UvA witness | logical printed p. 249 | PDF p. 300]

We teach against them that the cause why God from eternity elected Peter, not
Judas, to glory and to the faith of the elect is neither faith nor final
obedience, insofar as it includes conversion and the performance of good
works, as Grevinchovius says on page 37, nor Christ's merits, nor any quality
foreknown in Peter, but only God's free good pleasure, contrary to the
Arminians.

1. For human beings are elected to glory before they have performed anything
good or evil, Romans 9:11: “That God's prior appointment, which is according to
his election, might remain firm, not from works but from the one calling.”

2. For “God has mercy upon whom he wills, and hardens whom he wills”; from the
same clay the potter makes some vessels for dishonor and others for honor
because he wills, Romans 9:18, 21. Therefore, election is not from the dignity
or indignity of the elect person.

3. For he raised up Pharaoh for this ultimate end: that the glory of his power
and justice might be declared in destroying him; and others so that the glory
of mercy might be declared. Therefore, moved by his will alone, he intended
these ultimate ends, in the same place, verse 17.

4. For “God elected us in Christ before the foundation of the world was laid,
that we should be holy and blameless before him in love.” Therefore, he elected
us in order that we should believe. For faith is the greatest part of holiness.
Therefore, he elected us from his will alone, not because we were holy and
believing, Ephesians 1:4.

[1668 KBNL/UvA witness | logical printed p. 250 | PDF p. 301]

5. For “he predestined us to adoption as sons through Jesus Christ unto
himself, according to the benevolent affection of his will,” Ephesians 1:5—to
faith through which we are reckoned sons of God, John 1:12; Galatians 3:26.
Therefore, it is not on account of faith or from foreseen faith.

6. For “we did not elect Christ, but he elected us and ordained us to bear
fruit,” John 15:16. But if we are elected because we believe, we elect Christ
first.

7. For in 2 Thessalonians 2:13, “God elected us ἀπ’ ἀρχῆς,” “from the
beginning”—that is, from eternity, Proverbs 8:23; John 1:1—“to salvation
through sanctification of the Spirit and belief of the truth.” Therefore, it
is not because we believe; rather, he elected us to salvation so that we might
believe.

8. For in Acts 13:48 the Holy Spirit gives this reason why the Gentiles
believed but the Jews did not: “As many as were ordained, or elected, to life
believed.” Therefore, God's free electing will is the cause why we believe,
and consequently faith cannot be the cause why we are elect.

9. For all those given by God the Father to the Son come to the Son and
believe, John 6:37; 17:2, 6, 9, 24. Therefore, this giving—that is,
election—is the cause why they come and believe. Consequently, the reason or
cause of election cannot be faith.

10. For Romans 8:29–30 says: “Those whom God foreknew”—as his own, John 10:27;
Psalm 1:6; Matthew 7:23—“he also predestined; those whom he predestined, he
also called; those whom he called, he

[1668 KBNL/UvA witness | logical printed p. 251 | PDF p. 302]

justified; those whom he justified, he glorified.” Therefore, predestination
is the cause and first reason of calling and justification, and thus of faith.
Therefore, justification and faith are not the cause or reason of
predestination.

11. For 1 Peter 1:2 says: “The elect are according to the foreknowledge of God
the Father, through sanctification of the Spirit, unto obedience and the
sprinkling of the blood of Jesus Christ.” If we are elected to sprinkling, and
therefore to faith, then we are not elected because we have already been
sprinkled and believed.

12. For in this way election is not certain or dependent upon God's free will
as upon a certain cause, if we are elected because we believe. For it is
certain to the Arminians that, with everything required for acting posited, we
are able to believe or able not to believe. Thus election is so uncertain and
is a contingent act of our will.

13. For if God's good pleasure were not the cause and reason why this person
rather than that one is elected, but only the free act of believing, which is
entirely within our choice so that it exists or does not exist, as they teach,
then the glory of Peter's rather than Judas's election would be owed to free
choice, not to God's gracious will. For the gracious will in the general decree
to save all who believe is not the cause why Peter rather than Judas is
elected. For given God's decree, all can be elected or all can be reprobated.
Nor is election from foreknown faith the cause why Peter rather than Judas is
elected,

[1668 KBNL/UvA witness | logical printed p. 252 | PDF p. 303]

because a thing is not its own cause. Rather, the reason and cause why Peter
rather than Judas is elected is that Peter believes, and the reason why he
believes rather than does not believe is free choice. And certainly Arriaga,
Molina, and the Jesuits—who teach that only with middle knowledge preceding,
by which God knows who will finally believe and obey God when he calls and who
will not if they were placed in this or that order of things, does God
therefore efficaciously elect to glory the person thus foreknown as finally
obedient and not elect the person foreknown as not finally obedient—also teach
that it rests with Peter's free choice whether God's eternal election of Peter
exists or does not exist.

16. For Peter would distinguish himself from Judas, because by freely
believing he makes himself an object of election, which Judas does not do
because he does not will it.

ACTS 13:48

“And as many as were ordained to eternal life believed.”

It is asked whether faith is an effect of election. “Faith can be called a
fruit of election to grace,” the Synodists say in article 1, page 9, “to this
extent: by the benefit of those means which are supplied through grace, a
human being is rendered not only able to believe but also actually believes.
But faith does not arise necessarily from this election, or rather bestowal of
grace, as fruit does from a tree.” We, on the contrary, teach that faith flows
from election as an effect from a cause.

[1668 KBNL/UvA witness | logical printed p. 253 | PDF p. 304]

1. For those whom God immutably destines to an end he destines to efficacious
means, Romans 8:30: “Those whom he predestined, he also called.”

2. For we are predestined to adoption, and therefore to faith, Ephesians 1:5.
For through faith we become and are sons of God, John 1:12; Galatians 3:26. We
are elected “to salvation through sanctification of the Spirit and belief of
the truth,” 2 Thessalonians 2:13; “to the sprinkling of blood,” 1 Peter 1:2.

3. The one who gives this person, but not that one, the ability to believe
from a certain intention of giving faith to this person, not that one, gives
faith from an infallible intention of saving. For this intention is nothing
other than election to eternal salvation. But God gives this person faith
from the certain and infallible intention that this person rather than that
one should be converted, because he knows this gift is saving and fruitful for
this person, not that one.

4. For if God “makes known the mystery of his will according to his gracious
benevolence which he had purposed in himself,” Ephesians 1:9; reveals the
mysteries of the kingdom to these, not those, “because it thus pleased him,”
Matthew 11:25; and opens the heart of this person, not that one, because from
eternity he has mercy upon this person, not that one—then God gives faith
because he pursues someone with an eternal benevolence of giving glory. But the
former is true; therefore, so is the latter.

5. God cannot make the Word “the power unto salvation to the believer” and
efficacious unto salvation and conversion unless from eternity he decreed to
give

[1668 KBNL/UvA witness | logical printed p. 254 | PDF p. 305]

salvation to such persons. But God does the former. Therefore, he decreed the
latter, and the former occurs by virtue of this decree.

6. All spiritual blessings flow from election as from a cause, Ephesians
1:2–4. Therefore, faith does also.

7. Faith is not a fruit of election to grace if, given that election, human
beings can never believe.

8. For final faith is given by God rashly and with no counsel unless God
elects to eternal glory the person to whom he gives the ability to believe.
For an adequate means is given without counsel if it is not from the prior
establishment of the decreeing will. But it is absurd and impious to attribute
such rashness to God, the supremely wise governor of the world.

9. For as many as are ordained to life believe, Acts 13:48, and they therefore
believe because they are ordained to life.

It is asked whether the entire decree of election is that God decreed glory
for human beings if they should believe. The Remonstrants affirm it; we deny
it.

1. For in election God's will to save this person rather than that one—Peter
rather than Judas—is fixed and determined, Romans 9:11. But in this decree he
decreed glory no more for Peter than for Judas the traitor, but equally for
each insofar as either believes.

2. For this decree can stand with the glorification of all or with the eternal
damnation of all. But election posits that some are

[1668 KBNL/UvA witness | logical printed p. 255 | PDF p. 306]

necessarily to be saved while others are left, because it includes the
certain efficacy of the means, Ephesians 1:3–4.

3. For in this way each and every human being would be elect. But Christ says,
“Many are called, but few are elect,” Matthew 20:16; 22:14.

It is asked whether God did not decree from eternity to save in the same order
in which he saves in time. But in time he saves those who persevere in faith
and obedience. Therefore, he decreed from eternity to save in this manner.
Fulgentius, book 1, *To Monimus*, and *On the Incarnation and Grace*, last
chapter: “He accomplishes in his work no otherwise than he has disposed in his
eternal will.”

Response. Smising, tome 1, *On God*, tractate 3, disputation 6, *On
Providence*, question 8, number 579. The proposition, “In the same order in
which God saves in time he decreed from eternity to save,” is true concerning
the eternal decree with respect to execution. For the execution and the order
which God observes in execution were decreed from eternity. Just as whatever
God does in time he decreed—and indeed in the same order—that he would do from
eternity. But this is not the decree of eternal election, but the decree of the
execution of eternal election, which differ greatly. Otherwise God saves only
Lydia the sinner hearing Paul at this hour and place, believing, and praying,
and so forth. Therefore, sin, hearing the Word, weeping, prayer, such words of
prayer, in such a place, with such a preacher, and so forth, will all be
requirements in electing, which the adversaries will not say. But in this

[1668 KBNL/UvA witness | logical printed p. 256 | PDF p. 307]

sense the proposition—“In the order in which he saves and on account of the
condition for which he saves, on account of the same he predestined human
beings to eternal glory, since he found them believing and not because he
predestined them to believe; therefore, from eternity he elected them to
eternal glory”—is denied and is the matter in question. The proposition, “In
the same order in which God saves in time he decreed from eternity to save,”
is not admitted concerning the decree with respect to the eternal and gracious
intention and election to glory. By this distinction, Smising says, Augustine
and the Greek Fathers sometimes maintain that election to glory is sometimes
gracious and from grace, and sometimes from foreknowledge of merits and from
justice. But he is undoubtedly deceived, for the Greek Fathers erred. Augustine,
however, more practiced in these controversies after Pelagius arose,
consistently teaches that election to glory is purely gracious.

It is asked whether predestination is an act of God's justice or truthfulness,
or of the absolute good pleasure and dominion of God supreme. The Arminians
contend that it is an act of justice and is restricted by justice just as much
as the actual bestowal of life is restricted. We contend that it is an act of
God's supreme good pleasure.

1. Predestination is an act of God having mercy upon whom he wills because he
wills. But the gift of glory is an act of truthfulness because he promised it.

2. For predestination is absolute and unconditional. The gift of life is
conditional, on the

[1668 KBNL/UvA witness | logical printed p. 257 | PDF p. 308 | stop before EPH. 1.4]

hypothesis that someone has preserved faith and fought the good fight.

3. Predestination is an act such as that of a potter making from the same clay
a vessel for honor and a vessel for dishonor, when the clay may not reply,
“Why have you made me such?” Romans 9. But the gift of life is not such an act,
for the condition why eternal life is given to this person rather than another
is in the human being. For the one to whom it is given believes through God's
special grace, while the other does not believe, and therefore it is not given
to him.

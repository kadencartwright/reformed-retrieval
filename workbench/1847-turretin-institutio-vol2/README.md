# Francis Turretin, *Institutio theologiae elencticae*, Volume II (1847)

> **Working-edition notice:** This directory contains an AI-assisted,
> diplomatic-normalized Latin transcription and an independent English
> translation. Both are unreviewed working drafts, not a publication-ready
> or critical edition.

## Work and edition

- **Author:** Francis Turretin (1623–1687)
- **Title:** *Institutio theologiae elencticae*
- **Volume:** *Pars secunda* (Volume II)
- **Edition:** Edinburgh, John D. Lowe, 1847
- **Opening structure:** Locus XI, *De Lege Dei*, subdivided into twenty-six
  questions

## Controlling witness and rights

The controlling witness is the public-domain University of Toronto copy at
the Internet Archive, identifier
[`institutiotheolo02turr`](https://archive.org/details/institutiotheolo02turr).
The access PDF contains 696 pages. Page images govern the transcription; its
OCR and page-aware XML are used only as aids.

The English was translated afresh from the accompanying Latin transcription
and checked against the 1847 page images. No modern or copyrighted English
translation was consulted or used as a wording source.

## Editorial method

Long *s*, ligatures, line-end divisions, and typographic `u/v` are normalized.
Edition spelling, paragraph numbers, citations, Hebrew and Greek, and
substantive marginal headings are retained. Running heads, signatures, and
catchwords are omitted. Bracketed markers identify printed pages, one-based
access-PDF pages, and the Internet Archive witness; the same marker sequence
appears in the Latin and English files.

through section XXXII on printed p. 88 / PDF 116, immediately before Question
XV. The sequence comprises 311 numbered sections across eighty-six
consecutive printed pages. Each part is a complete question, and each

All paired files are in `parts/`. Question XII treats amphibological
equivocations and mental reservations in oaths, distinguishes rhetorical and
logical ambiguity from deceptive ethical equivocation, rejects the latter in
twenty-one sections, and answers proposed biblical and practical cases. Its
final sentence ends before the heading of Question XIII on the fourth
commandment, on printed p. 68 / PDF 96. The visibly printed form
`confirmamatum` in Question XI on p. 62 is retained in the Latin with `[sic]`
rather than silently corrected. Question XIII treats the origin and nature of
the Sabbath, argues that its institution preceded Sinai, and distinguishes
the moral substance of the fourth commandment from its ceremonial
circumstances in forty-four sections. It ends before the heading of Question
XIV on the Lord's Day on printed p. 81 / PDF 109. Question XIV argues that
the Lord's Day is an apostolic institution resting on divine authority,
defends its necessary and perpetual observance, and explains its privative
and positive elements in thirty-two sections. It ends before the heading of
Question XV on feast days on printed p. 88 / PDF 116. The visibly printed
`relicta` in section XXXI on p. 88 is retained in the Latin with `[sic]`; the
grammar appears to require `relicto` with `studio`.

## Dogma VIII

*Infants lack faith and are nevertheless to be baptized.*

## Dogma X

*The children of the faithful are not baptized so that they might then first become children, having formerly been strangers to the church; rather, they are received into the church with a solemn sign because, by the benefit of the promise, they already belonged to Christ’s body.*

### Reply to Dogmas VIII and X

1. *No less manifestly false and impious is this assertion too, in which it is affirmed that infants lack a faith of their own, contrary to Christ’s express word: “Whoever causes ONE OF THESE LITTLE ONES WHO believe in me to stumble.” And infants believe more easily than adults, since the use of reason hinders judgment more than it advances faith. 1 Corinthians 2.*

Although infants lack a faith of their own, especially actual faith (since this comes from hearing the preached Word, Romans 10), they are nevertheless rightly baptized according to the formula of the covenant—“I will be God to you and to your seed,” Genesis 17:7—which their parents have embraced by faith for themselves and their children. (For this reason the children are also called holy, 1 Corinthians 7:14.) They are judged with probability to be endowed with the fruit of adoption, God’s secret judgments being left to him.

2. *Baptized infants are judged not probably but certainly to be children of God, and they are endowed with the fruit of adoption, because God cannot deceive by his promise. Those who think and teach such things about baptism therefore render consolation doubtful and entirely overthrow the power of baptism; and so they profane the sacrament itself instituted by Christ.*

3. *They look back to their impious dogma about God’s secret decree against the greatest part of the human race, whom God is unwilling to regenerate through baptism. They therefore teach that thousands who have been baptized—even at adult age—are never regenerated or received by God into grace, but perish because they were created and ordained for damnation.*

### Theodore Beza’s Reply to Dr. Andreae’s Observations on the Antithesis to Dogmas VIII and X

For my part, I pardon all the impieties and falsehoods that Andreae here fastens upon us on account of his sufficiently well-known zeal; and in turn I wish for him more true knowledge, but much less confidence. Concerning the faith of infants, however, and the false interpretation of this passage which he cites, I have discussed the matter most fully above.

God’s truth, of course, does not stand firm unless—as even the supremely confident Andreae could not establish without qualification concerning adults—it is held as most certain and necessary that every infant who is baptized is endowed with actual faith and regenerated at that very moment, so that they know and believe divine things before—

—human things. But what would I accomplish here? For if I ask that the infants themselves offer some testimony about this matter, their whole faction will flee for refuge to that miraculous power of the Holy Spirit. They do not notice that among them miracles now cease to be miracles, since those things are called miracles which happen contrary to the common order of causes, while nothing could be more ordinary than what happens daily in so many myriads of baptized infants. At least let him grant me that, out of so many thousands of baptized infants, we may inquire whether even one can be found who, on reaching adulthood, remembers that he believed and was regenerated at the time when he was baptized as an infant. Is it then the case that all little children, as they grow up, are lulled to sleep by some Lethean cup, or fall away so completely from the faith bestowed on them before that they must learn all over again from hearing the Word the first alphabet of the Christian religion? Or are these not rather sick men’s dreams or mere delusions?

See and recognize, Christian reader, from what we have answered elsewhere, with what slanders we are burdened here.

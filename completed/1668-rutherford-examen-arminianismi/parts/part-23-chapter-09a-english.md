CHAPTER NINE,

On Universal Redemption.

1 JOHN 10:11.

I am that good Shepherd. The good Shepherd lays down his life for the sheep.

From these words our theologians conclude that the λύτρον, or ransom-price,
was paid only for the sheep—that is, for the predestined and elect children of
God appointed to life who are actually saved—and consequently that Christ did
not lay down his life with the intention that each and every person should
either be made capable of salvation or actually be saved. From this arises the
celebrated controversy in which:

It is asked whether Christ

[1668 KBNL/UvA witness | logical printed p. 373 | PDF p. 424]

died for each and every person. In framing the state of the question, the
Arminians vary and are not consistent with themselves.

1. Arminius, *Against Perkins*, page 673: “That—the immediate effect of
Christ's death—is not the actual removal of sins from these or those persons,
not the actual remission of sins, not justification, and not the actual
redemption of these or those persons, none of which comes to anyone apart from
faith and the Spirit of Christ. Rather, it is the reconciliation of God and
the procurement from God of remission, justification, and redemption. By
this, notwithstanding justice, which has been satisfied, God is now able to
remit sins to sinful human beings and bestow the Spirit of grace. He was
indeed already inclined from mercy to communicate these effects to sinners.”

Hence through Christ's death God's justice was satisfied for the sins of all
mortals. Therefore—unless I am mistaken—God's wrath toward sinners is laid
aside, for in justice God cannot be angry with those for whose sins God's
justice has been most fully satisfied. But the Remonstrants assert the
contrary in the *Synodical Writings*, article 2, page 343: “For although,” they
say, “God thus far shows himself propitious to all in Christ, in that he wills
to lay aside his wrath against them if they believe the gospel, nevertheless
he does not actually lay it aside except toward those who already believe.”

From this it follows: 1. God is angry with those reconciled to him in Christ.
2. God is angry with most people who are not obliged to believe the gospel,
inasmuch as it has

[1668 KBNL/UvA witness | logical printed p. 374 | PDF p. 425]

never been revealed to them. 3. God is angry with those for whose sins justice
has been satisfied. 4. A contingent and free act of believing removes God's
wrath and changes God from angry to appeased. 5. Christ's death alone is not
what renders God appeased; the act of free choice does so as well.

2. Reconciliation and remission were procured through Christ's death for all,
and that absolutely, whether they believe or do not believe. But actual
reconciliation and remission are applied to no one except under the condition
of faith. *Hague Conference*.

Yet that procured remission is only potential remission, which coexists with
guilt and the wrath of God remaining upon those for whom it was procured.

Hence through Christ's death God the Father has been made only appeasable, not
appeased; justice could have been satisfied through it, but was not actually
satisfied. And Christ suffered death, curse, and reproaches to acquire only
the possibility of making a new covenant—whether of works, of grace, or any
other kind—which, if human beings fulfill it, will save them. It cannot be
said that Christ paid the penalties owed by Judas and Pharaoh.

From this it follows that Christ: 1. Died for those for whose sins he did not
pay the penalties. 2. Christ's death does not expiate sins but merely
reconciles some kind of justice—I know not what—or removes the penalty. 3.
Nothing else is required for the actual

[1668 KBNL/UvA witness | logical printed p. 375 | PDF p. 426]

remission of each and every sin except the bare free act of believing.

3. They say in the *Synodical Writings*, article 2, page 283, “The effect of
this procurement is not merely the bare power to save, but also the full will
to save.”

But this full will is only God's appeasability, by which he can lay aside wrath
on the condition of faith. Yet he actually lays aside wrath toward no one
except one who actually believes.

4. Thus far they will that through Christ's death all have been received back
into God's favor, so that God would will to punish no one with eternal
torments merely on account of Adam's sin. *Synodical Writings*, article 2,
page 285. They will that the end, fruit, and usefulness of Christ's death would
remain intact: 1. Even if all were damned and Christ's death were applied to no
mortal. 2. Even if God intended to apply Christ's death to no mortal. Thus
Grevinchovius, *Against Ames*, pages 46–47.

It therefore seems to me that by Christ's death God's justice, by which he
could save human beings, was redeemed rather than sinful human beings, and
that Christ died for God, not for wretched sinners. The Jesuits also teach
this: Bellarmine, volume 4, *On the Grace of God and Free Choice*, book 2,
chapter 5; Gregory of Valencia, volume 2, disputation 8, question 3, *On the
Division of Grace*, point 4: “For this is precisely what it means that Christ
gave himself for all and died for all,” that is,

[1668 KBNL/UvA witness | logical printed p. 376 | PDF p. 427]

“by his merits he acquired from justice for the eternal Father all the helps
that human beings need on God's part in order to return into his favor and
obtain salvation.”

5. They will that Christ died for each and every human being, yet he did not
die “for the obstinate world, for unbelievers and the impenitent, as such.”
For they say in the *Synodical Writings*, article 2, pages 286–287: “The grace
of remission was procured for sinners insofar as they are considered in the
common state of the fall and of sin, and in respect of their other sins, with
impenitence alone excepted. It was procured under this law: that the fruit of
that grace should return only to those who would believe in the Redeemer, but
should in no way belong to those who refused to believe. ---- Grace was not
procured for unbelievers and rebels as such.”

From this it follows: 1. Christ died for all human beings, but not for the
impenitent as such. Yet the impenitent are human beings. Therefore, he did not
die for all without qualification.

2. If the grace of reconciliation was not procured for rebels as such, then
grace was not procured for all absolutely, but conditionally—if they are not
rebels. Therefore, Christ did not die for all absolutely, but conditionally—if
they are not rebels. Consequently he died only for nonrebels, that is, only
for believers.

3. Even final unbelief and impenitence were not expiated by Christ's death;
thus it is

[1668 KBNL/UvA witness | logical printed p. 377 | PDF p. 428]

an absolutely unpardonable sin for which Christ did not pay the penalties owed
to justice.

4. Christ had no intention to die for rebels as such or to procure for them by
his death the grace of reconciliation and remission. Nevertheless, through
preachers Christ offers reconciliation and remission to rebels as such.
Isaiah 6:9 and 65:2; Matthew 13, 13:14–15; Matthew 23:37–38; John 9:39. That is,
Christ, by malicious fraud—away with such blasphemies—and with a double heart,
offers reconciliation and remission to rebels provided they believe, with the
purpose, as he himself says in his Word, that they should be saved; yet by
Christ's death this remission was procured for them no more than for the
devils.

5. Because Christ's death precedes faith and unbelief in order, they wrongly
infer that Christ did not die for the faithful or for unbelievers as such. For
Christ did die for unbelievers as such, because he died “for us while we were
still sinners and enemies,” Romans 5:6—for sinners, for the unjust. Therefore,
on the tree of the cross he expiated whatever enmity and ungodliness, and thus
unbelief toward God, was in us. He also died for believers, for friends,
brothers, and sheep as such—that is, so that he might make us such by the merit
and efficacy of his death.

6. The question “Did Christ die only for elect believers and

[1668 KBNL/UvA witness | logical printed p. 378 | PDF p. 429]

brothers with respect to the application and fruit of his death?” is futile
and foolish. For this is to ask whether Christ died for the elect and believers
with the intention that believers alone, assisted by grace, would apply his
death by the powers of free choice. For the statement that Christ died for
human beings does not formally include the act of believing in the dead
Christ, but it does include it consequentially and effectually. Otherwise
human beings could be reproached because Christ did not die for them.
Likewise, there would be a promise such as this in the gospel: “Believe, so
that in this way you bring it about that Christ died for you”; and a threat
such as this: “If you do not believe, you will bring it about that Christ did
not pour out his blood for you.”

7. This question is ridiculous: “Did Christ die for each and every mortal—for
Pharaoh, Saul, and others consigned to the eternal torments of hell—with
respect to a procured reconciliation in which they will never have any more
share than the devils?”

8. The question does not concern the intrinsic sufficiency of Christ's death,
namely, whether Christ died sufficiently for each and every person. For David
Pareus retracted that formula of speech, and Beza observes that it is an
ἀχυρολογίαν, “improper expression.” For Christ's dying for human beings means
that Christ lays down his life for them with the counsel and intention that he
should give them actual reconciliation to God, righteousness, remission of
sins, and eternal life. But

[1668 KBNL/UvA witness | logical printed p. 379 | PDF p. 430]

Christ's death is sufficient in itself, antecedently to every decree and
counsel of God. It can be said that Christ's death is sufficient for the
salvation of all mortals, but not that Christ died with the end of sufficiently
saving all.

9. Nor is it asked whether the life procured by Christ's death is offered to
all in the visible church under the condition of faith, for we teach this.

Christ's offer is twofold. One is an approving offer and one of simple good
pleasure. Matthew 23:37: “How often I willed to gather,” and so forth. Proverbs
1:24: “I called and you refused,” and so forth.

It is proved by this reasoning. 1. An external call cannot be made to the
reprobate without some offer in which τὸ credere, “the act of believing,” in
the reprobate is pleasing to God who calls, by a simple good pleasure. The
invitation to the feast proves this, Matthew 22. 2. They do not sin against
promises in which no offer has been made to them. 3. Those to whom no offer
has been made do not trample Christ's blood, according to the saying, “We
would have healed Babylon.”

The other is an intentional and decretive offer, which goes by the name of
giving. This offer is joined with bestowal.

10. Finally, neither is it asked whether in God there is a velleity and general
intention to save each and every person. We deny such a velleity. But the
adversaries teach that through Christ's death God's natural velleity to save
all was changed into a full purpose to save all

[1668 KBNL/UvA witness | logical printed p. 380 | PDF p. 431]

and each through Christ's death, provided they should believe in Christ. But no
affection or will in God can be changed. Nor is this full purpose to save all,
provided they should believe, anything other than a decree to save all
believers, because a conditional decree posits nothing in reality unless the
condition is posited. And it is remarkable that there allegedly was not even
in God a conditional intention to save each and every person, provided they
should believe in the dead Christ, unless Christ had undergone death for each
and every person. For since a conditional will posits nothing unless the
condition is posited, how could there not be such a conditional will in God
unless Christ had died? For, given Christ's death, there is in God a
conditional will to save and justify all mortals, provided they fulfilled the
law most perfectly.

The question, therefore, is whether, according to the eternal counsel and
decree of the Father and of himself, Christ paid out the λύτρον, “ransom,” on
the tree of the cross in the name of all mortals, with the end that all should
believe in him and be saved; or whether he paid this λύτρον and price to God
the Father in the name and place of the elect alone. The Remonstrants say it
was paid out in the name of all mortals. We deny it.

1. Christ bore on the tree of the cross the person of those for whom he died.
But on the tree of the cross he bore the person of the elect alone, not of the
reprobate. Therefore, he

[1668 KBNL/UvA witness | logical printed p. 381 | PDF p. 432]

died for the elect alone. The major premise is Scripture's, for to die for
another is to die in his
place, person, and stead, so that the one for whom someone died may live. Thus
David wished to die for Absalom; thus Moses wished to be blotted out of the
book of life for God's people; and Paul wished to become accursed from Christ
for the Jews so that the Jews might obtain eternal life. Add that Christ's
dying for sinners is Christ's enduring in himself both the temporal and
eternal penalties owed to sinners. The minor premise is clear, because Christ
died “for his sheep,” John 10:11; “for us,” Romans 5:8; “for the unjust,” 1
Peter 3:18; “for his friends,” John 15:13; and “for his Church,” Ephesians
25:25.

2. Christ died only for those for whom he rose from the dead and intercedes at
the right hand of God the Father in heaven. But Christ rose and intercedes at
the right hand of God the Father only for the elect, as the adversaries
confess. Therefore, he died for the elect alone. The major premise is clear.
1. Christ did not simply die for sinners; rather, he died such a death as he
conquered by dying and struggled free from by rising. This death will never
rule over those for whom he died, 1 Corinthians 15:54–55. 2. Paul joins these
two things in Romans 4:25: “He died for our sins and was raised for our
justification.” Hence it follows that Christ was raised for the justification
of those for whose sins he died. 3. The special fruits of Christ's death are
his

[1668 KBNL/UvA witness | logical printed p. 382 | PDF p. 433]

resurrection and intercession, so that we might become participants in his
resurrection and newness of life and seek the things that are above at the
right hand of God the Father, Romans 6:4; Colossians 3:1–2; Ephesians 2:5. 4.
Nor would Christ have been a perfect priest for us unless he had not only
offered his body for us on the tree of the cross but also entered the Holy of
Holies by the power of his blood, Hebrews 9:23–25. 5. As Priest, Christ
intercedes and prays for those for whom, as Priest, he offered himself to God
the Father. But he prays only for the elect, not for the world, John 17:9.
Therefore, and so forth. The reason for the major is that the typological
priest performed these two things for the people, Leviticus 6. The apostle
testifies in Hebrews 5:1–3, 8:3–4, and 9:7 that our true high priest most fully
performed these two things. It was necessary for our priest not only to offer
his blood for us but also, by the power of that blood, to appear for us in
heaven as our Advocate, Hebrews 10:12, 20, and through his blood and the veil
of his flesh, as by a new and living way, to open heaven and make an approach
to the Father.

3. Christ embraced those for whom he died with the highest love, a love
greater than any mortal possesses. John 15:13; 1 John 3:16; John 3:16;
Ephesians 3:14, 20; 1 John 4:10; Romans 5:10. But Christ does not embrace each
and every mortal with such love.

[1668 KBNL/UvA witness | logical printed p. 383 | PDF p. 434]

4. Those for whom Christ died “are reconciled to God,” Romans 5:10–11. “In him
they have redemption, even the remission of sins in his blood,” Ephesians
1.1.7; Colossians 1:13. They “have been redeemed from their empty manner of
life,” 1 Peter 1:18–19. “The chastisement of their peace was upon Christ,”
Isaiah 53:5. “He appeared by the sacrifice of himself to take away their sin,”
Hebrews 9:26. They “have been delivered from future death,” Romans 5:9. They
“have been made the righteousness of God in Christ,” 2 Corinthians 5:21. They
“have been redeemed from the curse of the law,” Galatians 3:13–14. But none of
these things can be said of all mortals, most of whom are under the law, under
the curse, under God's wrath, in their sins, not delivered from death,
children of wrath, and lost, and so forth.

5. By the merit of his death Christ acquired the grace of regeneration and
effectual calling for those for whom he died. 1. He is Savior both by merit
and by efficacy. 2. In the new covenant, a new heart and a new Spirit are
promised to the redeemed, Jeremiah 31:33–34; Acts 5:31; Romans 6:4; Hebrews
2:14–15. 3. The grace of regeneration is given in Christ—that is, on account
of Christ as the meritorious cause—Ephesians 2:4–5; 2 Timothy 1:9; Titus 3:5–6.
But the adversaries confess that Christ did not merit the grace of
regeneration by his death for all mortals and reprobates.

6. According to the adversaries, the end, fruit, and usefulness of Christ's
death could remain intact even if no mortal had believed and been saved.
Therefore,

[1668 KBNL/UvA witness | logical printed p. 384 | PDF p. 435]

Christ died for none except insofar as they believe; nor can remission and
eternal life be procured for all except insofar as they believe or will
believe. Consequently, Christ procured nothing for those who will never
believe.

7. The devil, death, and sins have been conquered for those for whom Christ
died. Colossians 2:15; Hebrews 2:14; Corinthians 15:56–58. But the devil, sin,
and death triumph over the reprobate, who are slaves of sin and in whom death
and the devil reign. Romans 6:16; Ephesians 2:2; 2 Timothy 2:26; 1 John 3:10.

8. For those for whom Christ died, God intends “sanctification and deliverance
from this present evil age,” Galatians 1:14; “redemption from their empty
manner of life,” 1 Peter 1:18. He died for them with this end, that “dead to
sin, they should live to righteousness,” 1 Peter 2:24; “that he should redeem
them from all iniquity and purify for himself a people zealous for good
works,” Titus 2:14; and “that he should present the Church to himself glorious,
not having spot or wrinkle or any such thing, but that it should be holy before
him and blameless,” Ephesians 5:25–27. But God intends no such thing in most
mortals. 1. Many have been reprobated from God's mere good pleasure, Romans 9.
2. God does not fail to attain the end intended. 3. God denies most people the
grace of the gospel and its proclamation, without which the intended
sanctification is impossible. 4. Among those who hear the gospel,

[1668 KBNL/UvA witness | logical printed p. 385 | PDF p. 436]

he denies many the inward grace special to the elect. Deuteronomy 29:4; Isaiah
6:9; Romans 9:15–18; Romans 11:4–7.

9. If Christ had died for each and every person, the Scripture's arguments
full of consolation and drawn from Christ's death would be empty. Romans 8:34:
“Christ died; therefore, no one can condemn those for whom he died.” 2
Corinthians 5:15; 1 Timothy 1:15: “Christ came to save sinners”; therefore,
verse 17, “To the immortal, eternal, invisible King be glory.” Likewise,
Hebrews 10:19–21: “We have a high priest who opened heaven by his blood;
therefore, let us draw near with full assurance.” But such consolations would
belong equally to reprobates and believers, which is absurd.

10. If Christ died for each and every person, then the gospel of salvation
procured for each and every person would be revealed. For the intention to
save all through Christ's death cannot coexist with the denial of the gospel
to most nations.

11. Those for whom Christ died “were given to Christ by the Father”; “they are
not their own, but were bought with a price,” 1 Corinthians 6:19–20. But not
all were given to Christ by the Father, because those given to him cannot
perish, John 6:37–38. Not all are heirs, Romans 8:14; John 1:12. Not all belong
to Christ, John 8:44.

12. For the sins of those for whom Christ died, he paid the penalties owed to

[1668 KBNL/UvA witness | logical printed p. 386 | PDF p. 437]

divine justice on the tree of the cross and satisfied God. But he did not make
satisfaction for the sins of each and every person, for the final impenitence
and unbelief of those who reject Christ, or for the sins of the many who have
heard nothing whatever of Christ even by report. 1. He did not die for
unbelievers as such, as the adversaries confess. 2. The reprobate pay the
penalties of their sins eternally in hell. But God's justice does not demand
two satisfactions for the same sins.

13. “The blood of Christ, who through the eternal Spirit offered himself
blameless to God the Father, cleanses the conscience of those for whom it is
offered from dead works to worship the living God,” Hebrews 9:14.

But not everyone's conscience is cleansed by God, as experience makes known.

14. If Christ died for all with the purpose that all should be saved, then: 1.
The eternal election of each and every person to eternal life stands. 2. All
are God's covenant people. 3. All have been reconciled to God. 4. All can be
saved, if only they will; and conversion will not be from grace but from free
choice.

15. This is Pelagian, as Prosper says in his *Letter to Augustine*: “They say
that our Lord Jesus Christ died for the entire human race and that absolutely
no one is excluded from redemption by his blood, even if he passes this whole
life with a mind utterly alienated from him, because the sacrament of mercy

[1668 KBNL/UvA witness | logical printed p. 387 | PDF p. 438 | ends before EPH. 1.7.]

belongs to all people. The reason that very many are not renewed by it is that
they are foreknown not even to possess the will to be renewed.” And the
Council of Valence, *Against the Pelagians*, chapter 4: “The Pelagians declare
that Christ's blood was shed even for those ungodly people who, from the
beginning of the world until the Lord's passion, died in their ungodliness and
were punished with eternal damnation.” And Augustine, *Against Julian*, book
3, chapter 3: “They say, ‘They are redeemed but not delivered.’ These
paradoxes belong to the Pelagian heretics.”

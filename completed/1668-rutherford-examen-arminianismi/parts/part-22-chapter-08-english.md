CHAPTER EIGHT,

ON THE STATE OF GRACE,

1 TIMOTHY 2:3–4.

For this is good and acceptable in the sight of God our Savior, who wills all
kinds of people to be saved and to come to the knowledge of the truth.

This one passage is wretchedly twisted by the adversaries to support the
breadth and universality of divine grace.

[1668 KBNL/UvA witness | logical printed p. 352 | PDF p. 403]

Hence it must be investigated whether the universality of grace is validly
concluded from it by a sound inference. Therefore:

It is asked whether the Arminians justly contend and strive for the dignity of
Christ's merit and for the breadth, universality, sufficiency, and excellence
of divine grace. We deny it.

1. A grace under which every single person whom God has embraced with such
grace can nevertheless never believe and can perish eternally is not broad,
worthy, or sufficient—for grace that actually saves is far worthier. But, as
they themselves confess, their grace is of this kind. Therefore, and so forth.

2. A grace which cannot even expel hardness of heart, although it operates most
powerfully, if the human will should happen to resist in the opposite
direction, is not saving grace.

3. A grace which has no dominion over free acts, but leaves conversion or
nonconversion and eternal salvation or damnation in equilibrium and entrusts
them to the indifferent choice of changeable and inconstant human liberty, is
not broad and sufficient grace. But their grace is of this kind.

4. A grace which leaves us as liable to fall and to be damned as Adam and the
not-yet-fallen devils were before the fall is not the broad, sufficient, and
worthy grace procured for us through the merits and death of the Son of God.

[1668 KBNL/UvA witness | logical printed p. 353 | PDF p. 404]

5. No more people are saved through their grace than through the grace which
the Reformed churches defend according to the Scriptures, although they hawk
foot-and-a-half-long words about the sufficiency and breadth of God's grace and
Christ's merits.

6. They propose for debate whether the noblest action of grace is moral
persuasion. This is to debate whether the mere letter of the law and gospel is
that sole and unique saving grace of Christ. This is plainly Pelagian, as
Augustine says in *On the Grace of Christ*, chapter 24: “Let the Pelagians read
and understand, behold and confess, that God works in human hearts not by the
law and teaching sounding outwardly, but by an inward and hidden, wonderful
and ineffable power—not only new revelations, but also good wills.”

7. The grace of which Scripture speaks as saving is not only sufficient, but
is also efficacious in second act and produces the effect, 2 Corinthians 12:9;
2 Corinthians 10:5; 1 Corinthians 10:13; Ephesians 1:17–20. It brings about in
us both willing and accomplishing, Philippians 2:13. It removes the stony heart
and implants a new and fleshly one, Ezekiel 36:26. It brings about that all who
have heard and learned from the Father come to Christ, John 6:45. But their
grace leaves and entrusts to the creature's choice—a pivot even more
changeable—whether we overcome temptation or not, will and accomplish or not,
receive a new heart or not, and come to Christ or do not come.

[1668 KBNL/UvA witness | logical printed p. 354 | PDF p. 405]

8. Notwithstanding Arminian grace, human beings can show themselves finally
rebellious against God's most powerful grace, render God's gracious counsel
void, revoke eternal election and change it into the black decree of
reprobation, frustrate God's gracious intention, render Christ's intercession
and advocacy empty and ineffectual, reduce God's φιλανθρωπίαν,
“loving-kindness toward humanity,” in Christ almost to nothing with respect to
its efficacy, and finally make Christ's death idle and useless.

9. Their grace leaves all God's gracious promises—whether made by the Father
to his beloved Son or proposed to us by God—ratified or void, effectual or
ineffectual, according to our choice. According to them, it depends upon our
choice whether the promises are fulfilled or not fulfilled.

10. They contend that we are justified by our works, which free choice can
perform or not perform, and especially by the act of faith as the formal cause
of our justification. And this is their grace.

11. Their grace utterly overthrows confidence, glorying in God, fear, hope,
gratitude, the necessity of prayer, and humble submission toward God, because
free choice is established as the cause which differentiates and separates the
converted from the unconverted, the persevering from the apostate, and the
saved from the damned.

[1668 KBNL/UvA witness | logical printed p. 355 | PDF p. 406]

12. If it had seemed so to Adam's free choice, God's φιλανθρωπία,
“loving-kindness toward humanity,” in Christ, saving grace, and Christ's death
and merits could have lain hidden eternally from both human beings and angels.

It is asked whether the Arminians rightly contend for the dignity and glory of
mercy, φιλανθρωπίας, “loving-kindness toward humanity,” and Christ's merits.
They affirm it. We deny it.

1. If they are to be believed, all the actual glory of mercy, of
φιλανθρωπίας, “loving-kindness toward humanity,” and of the efficacy of
Christ's merits rests with free choice.

2. They teach that God embraces all the reprobate and eternally damned with a
greater love than any mortal possesses, John 15:13.

3. All for whom Christ died and whom, from his mercy and φιλανθρωπία,
“loving-kindness toward humanity,” he willed and decreed to be saved can be
damned eternally.

4. Whoever sins and perishes does so while God simply wills otherwise and is
unwilling—even those whom God has embraced with saving grace in Christ.

5. They teach that there is in Christ the Mediator no gracious election and no
intention to apply Christ's merits for the peoples of the Indies and the
Americas, for many other nations, or for the mentally incapacitated, deaf,
insane, and infants.

6. Myriads of infants overtaken by fate before adulthood are saved without the
merits

[1668 KBNL/UvA witness | logical printed p. 356 | PDF p. 407]

of Christ. For since original sin is not sin in them, they must die free from
sin and be co-adopted into the roll of the glorified, without any need to sing
in heaven that song of eternal redemption to the Savior's glory.

7. God has not actually reconciled the world but has merely made it
reconcilable, and by his death Christ has acquired for us only a certain
possibility of being appeased. By this possibility, without prejudice to
justice, he can prescribe to human beings any condition for obtaining eternal
life—whether good works, faith, or abstinence from eating the apple.

8. In the intention of God the Father and of the dying Christ, Judas is no less
loved unto eternal life than Peter, Pharaoh than Moses, and the reprobate than
the elect.

9. Christ is the Savior of all people in merit, power, example, and teaching,
provided that they will to be saved—as they say with the Socinians—but not by
the efficacy and certain, indefectible application of his death to those whom
he graciously loved above others.

10. Even when Christ's death attains its end, all can perish eternally. It can
happen that Christ poured out his life for his wife, subjects, and members and
yet is a widower without a wife, a king without subjects, a head without
members, and a victor and triumpher over death,

[1668 KBNL/UvA witness | logical printed p. 357 | PDF p. 408]

sin, hell, and the devils, without redeemed captives, forever.

11. The φιλανθρωπία, “loving-kindness toward humanity,” and mercy of a God who
cannot save human beings without overturning liberty and free obedience is a
buskin and an empty title.

12. That φιλανθρωπία, “loving-kindness toward humanity,” and mercy which does
not effectually deliver from the most extreme evil—one far more grievous than
the punishments of hell, namely, the hardness of an impenitent heart—is not
excellent.

13. They deny that the fathers under the Old Testament explicitly believed in
Christ who was to die. Therefore, Christ's death is not as broad as they
proclaim it to be.

14. They maintain that venial weaknesses, even in Turks and Americans, are
expiated by Christ's death without any application of Christ's merits. Nor is
it necessary for their consciences to be touched by their weaknesses, or for
them to grieve and repent over them. Yet with us they acknowledge that final
impenitence was never expiated even by Christ's death, since the reprobate pay
its punishment eternally.

15. They maintain that by his death Christ expiated the murders, parricides,
adulteries, and all sins of all the Gentiles who have heard nothing of his
death, yet that these same people must pay the eternal punishments of those
same sins.

It is asked whether Arminianism and its doctrine concerning God's grace is
Pelagianism and

[1668 KBNL/UvA witness | logical printed p. 358 | PDF p. 409]

Semi-Pelagianism. The Remonstrants deny it. We assert it.

1. Pelagius customarily confused grace with nature, as Bonaventure teaches,
distinction 2, figure 38. Faustus taught that grace extends as broadly as
nature—for here I mix the Semi-Pelagians with the Pelagians: “The whole is
grace, but God offers and presses it upon everyone unto salvation,” as
Augustine says in *On Grace and Free Choice*, chapter 4. So also Corvinus,
*Against Molina*, chapter 43, section 8, who says that grace and nature extend
equally broadly.

2. The Pelagians adorned nature itself with the title of grace. Augustine,
sermon 11, *On the Words of the Apostle*: “See, my brothers, how they proclaim
that general grace by which the human being was created, by which we are human
beings.” So the Arminians in the Synodical Writings, article 1, page 63, and in
the *Apology*, chapter 11, and Corvinus, *Against Tilenus*, chapter 6, pages
234–235, teach that the law of nature, good education, an upright disposition,
and the remnants of God's image in human beings are grace.

3. Pelagius placed grace in the law and teaching, “revealing and showing what
ought to be done,” as Augustine says in *On the Grace of Christ*, chapter 24,
and *Against Pelagius and Cælestinus*, book 1, chapter 3. The Arminians sing the
same tune in the Synodical Writings, article 4, page 173: God converts human
beings by an action which is not irresistible, but by gentle persuasion, as
Arminius says in *Against Perkins*, page 253. And in the *Apology*, chapter 17,
folio 166, they teach that the stony heart is removed and a

[1668 KBNL/UvA witness | logical printed p. 359 | PDF p. 410]

new one implanted by external instruction, in the manner that a teacher
teaches a pupil. As Corvinus says with Socinus, the teacher tells the pupil, “I
will make another man out of you.” But it is certain that a teacher neither
infuses the Holy Spirit nor adds new powers to the pupil's will.

4. Pelagius taught that all human beings can believe if the truth is revealed.
Thus Prosper: “Once the doctrine of the gospel is known, say the Pelagians, a
human being can complete the course by free choice.” So the Remonstrant
*Apology*, chapters 34–35.

5. Pelagius, I confess, added “the awakening of the stupefied will” to the
illumination of the mind, as Augustine says in *On Grace, Against Pelagius*,
chapter 10. But that awakening occurred only persuasively, which the Arminians
also teach.

6. Augustine teaches in *On the Grace of Christ*, chapter 5, that the Pelagians
taught “that God can only assist us so that we are able to will and act,” and
in chapter 47 of the same book, that “only the possibility, not the will and
action, is divinely assisted.” The Arminians teach this. 1. They teach that
resistance is always possible even alongside the action of the most powerful
grace. Therefore, according to them, God grants that we can believe, love, and
so forth, not that we do believe and love. 2. They teach that God gives a new
heart only by gentle persuasion, which a human being can always resist—that is,
he leaves it to our power whether we have a new heart.

[1668 KBNL/UvA witness | logical printed p. 360 | PDF p. 411]

7. Augustine, *Against Pelagius and Cælestinus*, chapter 18: “We have,” says
Pelagius, “a capability for either side implanted by God.” And Prosper, letter
to Rufinus: “Through the grace of the orthodox, nothing is left to free choice,
but it is completely destroyed.” The common doctrine of the Arminians is that
in every state, even that of corruption, the will essentially possesses
indifference and the power to obey or not obey; and unless this is posited,
grace will be nothing but a force of divine omnipotence which fatally
necessitates the will and utterly destroys its liberty.

8. Pelagius taught that a human being merits grace through works of nature.
Prosper, *Against the Collator*, chapter 9: “The beginnings of good wills
proceed from us through nature.” So Faustus, as Augustine says in book 2,
chapter 8: “Do you wish to be healed? You see that the gift of salvation is not
bestowed unless the desire of the will is first inquired into. And in the
centurion, because the will preceded grace, grace therefore preceded
regeneration.” So the Remonstrants, Synodical Writings, articles 2 and 3, pages
327–328, and Corvinus, *Against Molina*, chapter 39, teach that by the good use
of common gifts, including the law of nature, human beings merit the grace of
conversion.

9. The Pelagians said, as Augustine teaches throughout *On the Grace of Christ*
and in *On the Perfection of Righteousness*, and Jerome in the letter to
Ctesiphon, that God's commandments are easy and possible to observe. Thus
Arminius, response to articles 30–31, pages 164–165: “I do not oppose that
Augustinian position which holds that

[1668 KBNL/UvA witness | logical printed p. 361 | PDF p. 412]

a human being can be without sin in this life.” Likewise, in *Articles for
Consideration*: “Whoever says that through Christ's grace the regenerate can
perfectly fulfill the law in this life is not a Pelagian, does no injury to
God's grace, and does not establish justification from works.” They establish
the same doctrine in the Apologetic Writings and everywhere.

10. Pelagius taught that grace is necessary for acting more easily, not for
acting simply. The Arminians teach that, once permission—that is, Arminian
grace—is offered, a human being can believe or can abstain from the act of
unbelief. Therefore, Peter's abstinence from an act of unbelief does not
necessarily require greater grace than exists in Judas the traitor, who does
not believe.

11. The Pelagians and Semi-Pelagians taught that each person is converted and
assisted because he himself wills to be converted, not because divine grace by
its power effects conversion. They therefore subjected divine grace to our
will. Thus Cyril of Alexandria in the Acts of the Council of Ephesus, chapter
5, says: “The Celestians boast that God gives salvation οὐχ ἑκάστῳ καθῶς
βέλεται—not to each person just as God himself wills—but ἀλλὰ ὅτι ἡ
κατατεταγμένη τῷ ἀνθρώπῳ φύσις, rather, as human nature has been constituted.
Αὐτὴ κατὰ τὴν τῆς προαιρέσεως ἀξίαν, τὸ πνεῦμα τὸ ἅγιον καὶ προσκαλεῖται καὶ
ἀποθεῖται: this nature itself, by the merit of the will, both summons and
repels the Holy Spirit.” Thus the Arminians teach that,

[1668 KBNL/UvA witness | logical printed p. 362 | PDF p. 413]

when everything required for conversion has been posited, a human being can
receive or repel grace and can convert or not convert himself. Prosper, letter
to Augustine: “The Pelagians so preferred the human will to the divine that a
person is assisted because he willed, rather than wills because he is
assisted.” So the Remonstrants say that because we will to be converted, God
therefore wills to convert us, not the reverse. Cassian's familiar claim was:
“God's mercy stands ready when merely an occasion of good will is offered to it
by us.” Cassian, *Conference* 13, chapter 7: “When God's kindness has perceived
even the smallest spark of good will flashing forth in us, or one which the
human being himself has, as it were, struck from the hard flint of our heart,
it fosters and kindles it.” Peter the Deacon, *On the Incarnation and Grace*,
last chapter. Faustus, opposing the apostle, “subordinates the desire of grace
to human labor.” Thus the Arminians say that our will begins, in the order of
causality, before grace exercises its influence. Grevinchovius, page 209;
Corvinus, *Against Molina*. Hence these pronouncements of theirs: “Of two
people preveniently assisted by equal habitual grace, one believes through
grace because he wills; the other does not believe because he does not will.”
Likewise: “A person is not converted because he does not will to be converted,
although sufficient grace stands ready, if only he willed to be converted.”

It is asked whether sufficient grace is given through which all could be saved
if only they willed. First, the question does not concern the absolute
necessity of grace. For, if we consider God's justice, God could have shown not
even one person

[1668 KBNL/UvA witness | logical printed p. 363 | PDF p. 414]

grace through Christ. Arminius, *Against Perkins*, page 734. 2. The question
does not concern only grace bestowed on those who are within the visible
church, but that bestowed on all human beings within and outside the church.
3. It concerns not only those who never obey God when he calls, but also the
hardened and those severely punished with a judicial inability to obey: do
they have sufficient assistance of grace by which they can rise again and be
converted? 4. The question does not concern grace immediately sufficient for
conversion. Corvinus, *Against Tilenus*, chapter 11, page 425, denies that such
grace is given to everyone. 5. Nor is grace understood here as the light of the
gospel. Corvinus, the same work, chapter 3, page 119. 6. Nor does the question
concern whether grace is given to one who uses the light of nature well.

Rather, it is asked whether, now that the new covenant with humanity has been
inaugurated, God gives every single person, whether within or outside the
visible church, grace sufficient in its own kind, by which a person could be
advanced through degrees toward conversion, so that even the judicially
hardened could rise again; whether he does so ordinarily on account of Christ
the Mediator and because he is moved by the covenant, or by covenantal justice,
through which he intends the salvation of every single person; or whether God
denies such grace from his own good pleasure. The Jesuits and Arminians, with
their Socinians, affirm the former.

We, however, teach that God, out of mere grace and his absolute good pleasure,
gives special grace in Christ the Mediator only to certain people graciously
elected from eternity to glory,

[1668 KBNL/UvA witness | logical printed p. 364 | PDF p. 415]

while all the others are left with certain external means and more common
helps. By abusing them, they are rendered ἀναπολόγητοι, “without excuse.”

1. Isaiah 9:2, “The people who walk in darkness have seen a light; upon those
dwelling in the land of death's shadow a light has shone,” is explained in
Matthew 4:15–16 of the Gentiles before Christ's advent, who were destitute of
God's grace and the light of the gospel. Likewise, Isaiah 44:6, “I will give
you as a light to the Gentiles,” is explained in Acts 13:47 of the preaching of
the gospel—not preaching such as was given to the obstinate Jews in the same
chapter, but preaching which is accompanied by the grace of inward revelation.
Therefore, they did not have a supernatural measure and sufficient grace, for
sitting in the shadow of death and having sufficient grace are opposed as
light and darkness.

2. Those to whom God denies the gospel do not have grace to believe the gospel.
But God denies the gospel to most nations. The major premise is Romans 10:14:
“How, then, will they call upon him in whom they have not believed? And how
will they believe him of whom they have not heard? And how will they preach
unless they have been sent?” The minor premise is proved from Psalm 147:19–20:
“He shows his word to Jacob and his statutes and judgments to Israel; he has
not done so with every nation.” Matthew 10:5: “Do not go into the way of the
Gentiles, and do not enter a city of the Samaritans.” Acts 16:6–7: “They were
forbidden by the Holy Spirit to speak God's word in

[1668 KBNL/UvA witness | logical printed p. 365 | PDF p. 416]

Asia. When they came to Mysia, they attempted to go toward Bithynia, but the
Spirit did not permit them to go.”

3. Those who are themselves darkness; whose wisdom is hostility against God
and cannot be subject to God's law; who cannot receive and understand the
things of God's Spirit; who are dead in sins; who are outside Christ, without
whom they can do nothing; and who cannot even think anything good from
themselves—these do not have sufficient grace through which they can be
advanced by their own degrees toward saving conversion. But all mortals are of
this kind by nature.

Objection. This argument concludes that human beings cannot believe in Christ
by nature. But it is wrongly inferred from this that human beings cannot
believe in Christ through the universal grace which God has denied to no
mortal.

Response. If this distinction is to be admitted, it must simply be denied that
there is anyone in the nature of things who cannot believe in Christ. For if
all mortals can believe through sufficient grace which is given to all from
the womb, as they maintain, no one is simply unable to believe. A person who
is able through grace which extends as broadly as nature itself is simply
able. With this distinction in place, according to their doctrine Adam before
the fall could not receive and understand the things of God's Spirit and could
not believe the gospel; nevertheless he could do so through

[1668 KBNL/UvA witness | logical printed p. 366 | PDF p. 417]

supernatural grace. Accordingly, a human being after the fall is thus dead in
sins with respect to performing supernatural works just as Adam was dead in
sins before the fall, which is absurd.

4. If God does not send prophets to foreign nations which would hear and obey,
Ezekiel 3:6; if he denies miracles to the Tyrians who would repent, while he
sends prophets and grants miracles to many who never obey—then God does not
intend salvation for all and does not give grace to believe. But the former is
true. Therefore.

5. If God gives some people the word, signs, trials, and external means, and
yet does not give them a heart to know, eyes to see, and ears to hear, then he
denies sufficient grace to many. But the former is true, Deuteronomy 29:3–4.
Therefore.

6. If such grace were present in all human beings and God intended the
obedience and salvation of all, then he would not deny the motion which he
foreknew would be effectual and suitable for fulfilling his will, while giving
the motion which he knew would be wholly void and ineffectual. But Scripture
and experience bear witness that the latter happens to most human beings. The
major premise is certain, because God converts those whom he pursues with mercy
and a gracious intention to save, as in Ephesians 2:4: “God, rich in mercy,
embraces us in Christ with immeasurable love.” Therefore, verse 5, “he makes us
alive with Christ.” Likewise, 2 Timothy 1:9: “God ordained

[1668 KBNL/UvA witness | logical printed p. 367 | PDF p. 418]

grace for us in Christ Jesus before the world was founded.” Therefore, “he
saves us”—that is, effectually calls us. Similarly, Titus 3:4–5: “God pursues
us with kindness and great love.” Therefore, “he saves us through the washing
of regeneration and renewal of the Holy Spirit.” Likewise, Romans 8:32: “God
gave his Son for us.” Therefore, “with him he will give us all things,” and so
also effectual calling.

7. The hardened cannot believe, John 12:39. And it is impossible for those who
sin against the Holy Spirit to be renewed again, Hebrews 6:4–5, no more than a
new sacrifice can be offered for them or Christ can be crucified again for
them, Hebrews 10:26. Therefore, they do not have sufficient grace.

8. If the apostle forbids prayer for one who sins unto death, 1 John 5:16, then
such a person does not have sufficient grace.

9. If Christ says in Matthew 13:11 that it is given to the disciples to
understand the mysteries of the kingdom of heaven but is not given to the
people, then the people lacked sufficient grace to understand those mysteries.

10. If God reveals the mysteries of the gospel to infants and hides them from
the wise; and if no one knows the Son except the Father, nor does anyone know
the Father except the Son and anyone to whom the Son wills to reveal him—then
the grace of that revelation is not given to all. But the former is true,
Matthew 11:25, 27. Therefore.

11. If God permitted the nations to walk in their own ways and overlooked the
times of ignorance, but now, once the light of the gospel has arisen, commands

[1668 KBNL/UvA witness | logical printed p. 368 | PDF p. 419]

people everywhere to repent, Acts 17:30, then they formerly lacked sufficient
grace to believe.

12. If no one can come to Christ unless drawn by the Father, John 6:44, and it
is certain that not all are drawn, then not all can come.

It is asked whether that distinction of grace into sufficient and effectual is
to be admitted. The Arminians affirm it.

We say that the external means are sufficient: 1. ad ἀναπολογίαν, “for leaving
people without excuse”; 2. for external discipline; and 3. for acquitting
God's justice. But no grace is sufficient for conversion which is not also
effectual.

1. Every sufficient cause produces the effect. But the Arminians' sufficient
grace does not produce the effect—namely, conversion.

2. Every sufficient cause is able to produce the effect. But their grace is
not able to produce the effect, because it cannot conquer the defective power
of resisting God when he calls and move the will indefectibly to obedience.
For, as they teach, it would thereby overturn liberty. Therefore, it is not
sufficient.

3. A grace which supposes the devil to be stronger than God is not to be
admitted. But if there is posited such a universal grace, which cannot be
effectual unless the will possesses a power of resistance, as they maintain,
then the wicked one who is in the world will be stronger than God who dwells in
God's children.

[1668 KBNL/UvA witness | logical printed p. 369 | PDF p. 420]

But the apostle teaches the contrary to be true in 1 John 2:14 and 1 John 4:4,
because from the saints' effectual victory over sin he implies that God is
stronger than the devil.

4. If in the truly regenerate there is a sufficient grace which can always
become ineffectual, as they teach, then the faithful God would permit his own
to be tempted beyond what they are able to bear, contrary to 1 Corinthians
10:13.

It is asked whether the Remonstrants in the *Apology*, chapter 17, folio 159,
rightly teach that God always joins the power and operation of grace to the
word, or at least is always prepared to join it. We deny it.

1. If God is only prepared to give grace and does not give it at every moment
of time, then a person who hears the gospel is not furnished with sufficient
grace at every moment of time. If they say that God is prepared to give grace
and will actually give it if the human being, by failing to cooperate with God,
does not fail God when he is about to give it, then before grace is bestowed
the human being does not yet have that sufficient grace. Thus he does not sin
because he fails God when he is about to give grace, since, as they teach, no
one sins who sins from a lack of grace and power to obey.

2. It is Pelagianism to say that grace is given according to the merits and
efforts of free choice.

3. It is said of many within the visible church who hear the gospel that they
are dead in

[1668 KBNL/UvA witness | logical printed p. 370 | PDF p. 421]

sins, blind, powerless, and so forth. All these statements would be false if
the preached word were always accompanied by grace and the power to obey.

4. The word is preached to the judicially hardened, to those who sin against
the Holy Spirit, and to many who, out of incorrigible malice, treat the word of
the gospel with total indifference and despise it. But Arminius in *Against
Perkins* and Corvinus in *Against Molina* teach that grace to obey is denied to
such people who hear the word, and that this is a just punishment for the
despised gospel.

It is asked whether the capacity to believe has been bestowed on all mortals.
The Arminian Synodists, article 4, page 61, affirm it. We deny it.

1. Human beings are dead in sins and powerless to come to Christ unless drawn
by the Father, John 6:44.

2. The capacity to believe is either bestowed through the word, infused apart
from the ministry of the word, or naturally inherent in us. Not the first,
because, since the word operates resistibly, as they teach, and most people
resist the word when it is actually preached, the capacity to believe cannot
be bestowed on all through the preached word. Not the second, because this is
ἐνθεσιαστικὸν, “enthusiastic,” and ἄγραφον, “unwritten.” Not the third, because
then our fleshly wisdom would be no more opposed to the gospel than to
Aristotle's metaphysical conclusions, since, according to them, the capacity

[1668 KBNL/UvA witness | logical printed p. 371 | PDF p. 422]

to believe the gospel and the capacity to believe Aristotle when he
philosophizes are equally inherent in us by nature.

It is asked whether God's grace effectually determines the will. The Arminians
and Jesuits deny it. We affirm it.

1. We ask God for such determination in our prayers. Psalm 119:36: “Incline my
mind to your statutes.” Psalm 86:11: “Unite my mind to fear your name.” Psalm
5:8: “Lead me, Jehovah.” Psalm 119:27: “Cause me to understand the way of your
precepts.”

2. Grace which predetermines the will is promised by God. Isaiah 44:3: “I will
pour water upon the thirsty, and streams upon the dry ground; I will pour my
Spirit upon your offspring,” and so forth. Ezekiel 36:26: “I will also give
you a new heart.” Deuteronomy 30:6: “Jehovah will circumcise your heart and the
heart of your offspring to love the Lord your God.” Likewise Jeremiah 24:7 and
31:33.

3. We give thanks to God because he bends and inclines our will to the good,
Matthew 11:25.

4. Grace alone separates the believer from the unbeliever, 1 Corinthians 4:7.

5. God will not retain the glory of supernatural acts if the created will so
determines itself to those acts that it could fail to determine itself to
them.

6. No one could hope and trust

[1668 KBNL/UvA witness | logical printed p. 372 | PDF p. 423 | ends before CAPUT NONUM]

that God, in accordance with his truthfulness and faithfulness, would
indefectibly bend that person's will to final obedience.

7. God will retain neither dominion over free acts nor the glory of
truthfulness in his promises to give a new heart and final perseverance,
Jeremiah 32:34; Ezekiel 36:26; 1 Corinthians 1:8; Isaiah 54:10 and 59:20–21.
For he cannot actually perform these things in second act unless he brings it
about that the free will indefectibly embraces the good.

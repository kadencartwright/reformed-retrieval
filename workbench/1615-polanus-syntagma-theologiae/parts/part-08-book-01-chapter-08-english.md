CHAPTER EIGHT

On the Theology of the Blessed

THE THEOLOGY of Christ’s members is the wisdom of divine things communicated
by Christ to his members for the glory of God and the everlasting salvation
of Christ’s members. John 1:16: “From his fullness we have all received, and
grace for grace.”

It belongs either to the blessed or to wayfarers.

The theology of wayfarers is subordinate to the theology of the blessed.

The theology of the blessed is the wisdom of divine things communicated by
Christ through the Holy Spirit to the blessed in heaven, through the clear
vision or intuitive knowledge of God, for his glory. It is also called the
theology of clear vision and, by synecdoche, the theology of vision, because
the vision of God is also attributed to Christ’s human nature, as it is to the
faithful who are still living in this life.

Knowledge in a rational creature is twofold: intuitive and abstractive.

Intuitive knowledge is knowledge of a present thing as it is present. That
is, it is the sort of cognition by which we reach a thing in such a way that
the presence of the object is discerned in itself and by the force of the
cognition itself, not because it is proved from some other source. Such is the
knowledge by which someone sees whiteness or another color on a wall, by which
he hears a sound, and by which the blessed behold God. It must be immediate,
so that the thing is not perceived in another thing already known, as Caesar
is known in his statue. For through intuitive cognition a thing is beheld in
itself, which cannot occur when the thing is seen in something else that was
seen first. Intuitive knowledge is also called the knowledge of vision.

Abstractive knowledge is knowledge of a thing through a representation
abstracted from it, and so is cognition of a thing not as it is present. For
example, it is the knowledge by which I think about the absent Emperor Rudolf,
that by which an astronomer at home considers an eclipse he does not behold,
and that by which a philosopher knows from created things that God exists.
Although these cognitions tend toward a thing under the aspect of its actual
existence, they do not tend toward it in such a way that the object’s presence
is discerned through them. This is also called knowledge of simple
understanding.

The knowledge by which the children of God are made blessed and see God they
receive from Christ’s humanity, and they must draw it from the fullness of his
grace which resides in that humanity. For Christ is the author of their
salvation through the communication of the grace and gifts that are in him.
Hebrews 2:

[1615 printed locator A]

10–11: “For it was fitting that he, for whom all these things exist and
through whom all these things exist, in bringing many sons to glory, should
perfect the prince of their salvation through sufferings. For both he who
sanctifies and those who are sanctified are all from one. For this reason he
is not ashamed to call them brothers.”

The theology of the blessed belongs either to angels or to men.

The theology of the blessed angels is the wisdom of divine things
communicated by Christ to the holy angels, by which they are blessed as they
know God and his mysteries perfectly. Matthew 18:10: “See

[1615 printed locator B]

that you do not despise one of these little ones, for I tell you that their
angels in heaven behold the face of my Father who is in heaven at all times.”
First Peter 1:12: “It was revealed to them that they were administering not
for themselves but for us the things now announced to you through those who
preached the gospel to you by the Holy Spirit sent from heaven, things into
which the angels desire to look.” Luke 1:31 and the verses that follow: “And
behold, you will conceive in your womb and bear a son, and you will call his
name Jesus. He will be great and will be called the Son of the Most High, and
the Lord God will give him the throne of his father David. He will reign over
the house of Jacob forever, and there will be no end of his kingdom,” and so
forth.

[1615 printed locator C]

The theology of blessed men is the wisdom of divine things communicated in
heaven, by the power of God’s Spirit and from Christ’s allotted measure, to
elect men who have now been raised to perfection. By this theology they are
blessed, now seeing God clearly as he is, face to face. Of this Matthew 5:8
says: “Blessed are the pure in heart, for they will see God.” First
Corinthians 13:12: “Then we will see face to face.” First John 3:2: “We will
see him as he is.” Second Corinthians 5:7: “We walk by faith, not by sight”—
that is, clear sight. Revelation 22:4: “They will see his face.” For otherwise
we also see God by faith in this life, that is, we know him, just as David
says in Psalm

[1615 printed locator D]

63:2–3 that his soul thirsts for God and his flesh longs for him, intent upon
seeing God’s power and glory, as he saw him in the holy place—that is, knew
him. Likewise John 6:40: “This is the will of the Father who sent me: that
everyone who sees the Son and believes in him may have eternal life.” John
8:56: “Abraham, that father of yours, rejoiced that he would see this day of
mine; and he saw it and was glad.” John 12:45: “He who sees me sees him who
sent me.” John 14:7: “If you had known me, you would also have known my Father,
and from now on you know him and have seen him.” And verse 9: “He who sees me
sees my Father,” namely, with the eyes of faith. First Corinthians 13:12:

[1615 printed locator E]

“Now we see through a mirror and through a riddle, but then we will see face
to face.” Second Corinthians 3:18: “But we all, with unveiled face beholding
the Lord’s glory as in a mirror, are transformed into the same image from
glory to glory, as by the Lord’s Spirit.” Hebrews 2:9: “But we see that Jesus,
crowned with glory and honor.” First John 3:6: “Whoever sins does not see him
or know him.” It follows from this that there is a vision both of wayfarers and
of those who are in the homeland. Accordingly, the theology of vision must be
distinguished as either the theology of clear vision or of obscure vision.
The theology of blessed men, or of clear vision, is also called the theology
of comprehensors, that is, of those who

[1615 printed locator F]

have now attained the goal of blessedness toward which they strove in this
life. This name comes from Paul’s passage in First Corinthians 9:24: “Do you
not know that those who run in the stadium all indeed run, but one receives
the prize? Run in such a way that you may attain it.” And Philippians
3:11–14: “Trying whether by any means I may reach the resurrection of the
dead—not that I have already attained the goal or am already perfected, but I
press on, trying whether I too may attain that for which I was also taken hold
of by Christ Jesus. Brothers, I do not reckon that I myself have attained the
goal. But one thing I do: forgetting what lies behind and striving toward what
lies ahead, I move toward the

[1615 printed locator G]

goal, to the prize of God’s heavenly calling in CHRIST JESUS.”

VERSE 12. The sleep of the person who labors is sweet, whether he has eaten
little or much; but the rich man's fullness does not permit him to sleep.

This is an anticipation of an objection. Its objection is drawn from the
accompanying fullness, delights, and rest or leisure in which the head of the
household surpasses his servants when they use these goods. The reply is that
the condition of the head of the household is no better in this respect. For
although servants are fed neither so sumptuously nor so abundantly, nevertheless
the labor in which they are

engaged, continued by skill or nature, makes them enjoy sleep more than the
head of the household does. His fullness is brought on by a variety of foods
and delicacies. Especially when leisure and freedom from labor accompany that
fullness, his sleep becomes both shorter and less pleasant. Consequently,
servants preserve their health better through a more sparing diet and hard
labor than the head of the household himself does, although health is the
proper end to which nourishment of the body ought to be directed. The saying
is true that more people die from fullness than from hunger.

This is to say nothing here about the household head's anxiety both in
acquiring and in preserving grain and provisions, in addition to the anguish
of mind he receives when his goods are lost or spoiled. Moreover, the Savior's
testimony establishes that the rich enter the kingdom of heaven with
difficulty. Luke 18. This testimony of Solomon likewise establishes that the
rich person's way is not the most advantageous one for this life. This warns
the rich to relax their pursuit of wealth somewhat, just as it also admonishes
the poor to bear the burden of poverty calmly, since through it they are
exempted from many troubles in which the rich are entangled.

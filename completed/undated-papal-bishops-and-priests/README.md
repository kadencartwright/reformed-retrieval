# For the Bishops and Priests of the Papacy

This directory presents the complete French text and a new English translation
of the consilium printed by the *Calvini Opera* editors as *Pour les evesques et
curez de la papaulté*.

## Work identity

| Field | Value |
|---|---|
| Author | John Calvin |
| CO consilium | II.3, *Pour les evesques et curez de la papaulté* |
| Date | November 1561 in the heading of CO's copy B; no date occurs in the body |
| Language of the work and CO presentation | French |
| Principal CO witness | Geneva Library, Cod. 145, fol. 146 |
| Other witnesses reported by CO | Geneva copy B after fol. 148; a derivative Dupuy 102, fol. 68 copy |
| Addressee | No recipient is named in the body; “Pour M. Remond Chauvet” is a later note on the Dupuy copy |
| Genre | Ecclesiastical advice prompted by an inquiry |
| Subject | Reception and office of Roman bishops and priests who embrace the Reformation |
| CO locator | X/1, pp. 184–187 |

Copy B bears a longer descriptive heading ending *fait en Novembre 1561*.
That supplies defensible witness evidence for the month and year but is not an
authorial dateline. The transmitted closing *Ieh. Calvin* is not autograph and
is absent from B. The later notation naming Remond Chauvet does not securely
identify him as Calvin's actual addressee. These layers should not be collapsed
into the body text.

## Source and transcription

The transcription follows Wilhelm Baum, Eduard Cunitz, and Eduard Reuss, eds.,
*Ioannis Calvini opera quae supersunt omnia*, vol. X, part 1 (*Corpus
Reformatorum* XXXVIII; Brunswick: C. A. Schwetschke, 1871), printed pp.
184–187. The University of Geneva supplies the [complete *Calvini Opera*
collection](https://archive-ouverte.unige.ch/unige:650) and a [direct CO X/1
PDF](https://access.archive-ouverte.unige.ch/access/metadata/f526a338-c95c-4f87-b600-04df723f502b/download).
The local PDF used for collation is 33,386,580 bytes with SHA-256
`025973d6e4533769d0c2b870dd324497943bc14050eb2bcbcc4c964d4f2a1777`.

Printed p. 184 is the right half of UNIGE PDF p. 113; pp. 185–186 are the left
and right halves of PDF p. 114; and p. 187 is the left half of PDF p. 115.
All four pages were checked line by line against 350-DPI renders. `french.txt`
preserves CO's historical spelling, accents, apostrophes, and punctuation while
joining printer line-end divisions. It excludes the CO heading, running heads,
page numbers, source notes, footnote signals, variant readings, and the Greek
and Latin comparison in the apparatus. The p. 186/187 turn divides printed
*re-ceue*; under the repository's split-word policy the p. 187 marker precedes
the complete normalized word *receue*, and the English marker precedes
“received.”

## Work and witness distinction

The body is French advice in Calvin's voice, preserved in scribal copies.
CO also records an early-modern Latin translation in the collected *Epistolae
et responsa*. That Latin is a derivative witness, not Calvin's source-language
body, and is not used as the translation base here. The displayed CO title is
an editorial organizing heading; copy B's longer heading is separate witness
metadata.

## Translation status and cruxes

Mary D. Beaty and Benjamin Wirt Farley translated the *Consilia* corpus in the
copyrighted 1991 volume *Calvin's Ecclesiastical Advice*; no wording from that
translation was consulted or reused here. No complete public-domain English
version was located in the repository's audit. This English is an
**AI-assisted scholarly draft**, translated afresh from the French and checked
against the complete facsimile. It has not received independent review by a
specialist in sixteenth-century French.

At the p. 184/185 turn the principal text reads *des heures* where copy B has
*deux heures*. The English “scarcely had any time” represents the sense of the
principal reading without importing B's numeral. On p. 185, *remetre en son
entier ce qui avoit esté corrumpu* is the restoration of a corrupted ministry
to an integral state, not automatic recognition of the old papal appointment.
On p. 186, *appetit de victoire* is the contentious desire to win; the
translation retains that point rather than treating “ambition” as merely
private advancement.

Suggested citation:

> John Calvin, *Pour les evesques et curez de la papaulté*, November 1561 in
> the heading of copy B, *Calvini Opera* X/1 (1871), 184–187, normalized French
> and draft English translation, *Reformed Retrieval*.

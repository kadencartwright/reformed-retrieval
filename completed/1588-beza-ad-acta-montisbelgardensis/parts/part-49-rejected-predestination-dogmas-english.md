## Part XLIX: Rejected Predestination Dogmas

[Part II | PDF page 234 | logical source scan 233 | printed page 232]

“WE REJECT,” SAY THE WÜRTTEMBERG DOCTORS, “AS IN NO WAY AGREEING WITH God’s word and as removing every consolation from godly minds, when it is taught:”

### Dogma I Condemned by the Württemberg Doctors

“Reprobation is God’s most wise purpose by which, before all eternity—as they speak—he constantly decreed without any injustice NOT TO HAVE MERCY ON THOSE whom he did not love but passed by, so that through their just condemnation he might declare his wrath against sins and his glory.”

#### Theodore Beza’s Reply to the Following Dogmas

1. The first dogma, which you reject, we defend as truly Christian and orthodox.

1. It is horrendous to hear and abominable that God by his eternal decree DID NOT LOVE some people, but left to just condemnation those ON WHOM HE DOES NOT WILL TO HAVE MERCY.

### Dogma II Condemned by the Württemberg Doctors

“The efficient cause of reprobation is God’s purpose, by which it pleased God to create some people and RAISE THEM UP FOR THIS USE, that he might display his wrath and power and be glorified in them too.”

#### Theodore Beza’s Reply to Dogma II

1. If “reprobation” means God’s decree, the statement is inept: God’s purpose, that is, his decree, is its own efficient cause. But if by this name you mean the execution of his decree, we confess what you reject is false, as we showed concerning Theses V and VI.

1. It is also horrendous and abominable to assert that God CREATED some and raised them up for this use, that he might display his wrath and power and also be glorified in them. For although the apostle testifies that in this life he raised Pharaoh up to display his power in him in this age, we nowhere read that he created Pharaoh for eternal damnation, so that by absolute will he refused to will Pharaoh to repent and be converted. What is said of hardening was not the effect of an absolute decree but a just punishment for the preceding cruelty Pharaoh most savagely practiced against Israel’s children.

2. Whether by “reprobation” the decree or execution of the decree is meant, it is nevertheless impious to assert that by absolute will God decreed NOT TO HAVE MERCY upon some human beings. This openly conflicts with his revealed will.

### Dogma III Condemned by the Württemberg Doctors

“God destined the reprobate, NOT YET BORN, for destruction without regard to unworthiness, and some were CREATED BY GOD for just judgment.”

### Dogma V Condemned by the Württemberg Doctors

“The cause of the decree of reprobation and election is both eternal kindness in HIM WHOM IT PLEASED HIM to predestine to salvation and eternal hatred of evil in HIM WHOM IT PLEASED HIM TO ORDAIN FOR JUST DAMNATION. But why HE DESTINES THESE rather than THOSE for salvation or damnation has NO OTHER initiating CAUSE THAN HIS OWN WILL.”

#### Theodore Beza’s Reply to Dogmas III and V

By this dogma you oppose Paul himself, Romans 9:11. For it is as false that unbelief is the cause of this divine decree justly to destroy some as it is false that foreseen faith or good works are the cause of predestination or of the elect. This is the Pelagian dogma, as was said concerning the same Theses V and VI.

1. It is no less horrendous to hear and abominable that Beza dares deny UNBELIEF is the cause of God’s decree to destroy human beings. For Christ openly says, “The one who does not believe is judged already.” Likewise, “The Holy Spirit will convict the world of sin, BECAUSE THEY DO NOT BELIEVE in me.” Likewise, “The one who does not believe will be condemned.” We therefore by no means oppose Paul, who nowhere says some were CREATED for eternal damnation, as Beza teaches.

2. Faith in Christ belongs not to nature or our human powers but is the Holy Spirit’s work. Therefore, when faith is called the cause of election, it by no means savors of the Pelagian dogma, whose adherents attributed to nature’s powers what only the Holy Spirit can furnish.

### Dogma IV Condemned by the Württemberg Doctors

“Adam indeed fell voluntarily into these calamities, yet not only with God foreknowing but GOD JUSTLY ORDAINING and decreeing it.”

[Part II | PDF page 236 | logical source scan 235 | printed page 234]

#### Theodore Beza’s Reply to Dogma IV

Since the apostle’s statement—which you yourselves cite—that God shut up all things under unbelief, Romans 11, must necessarily be referred to Adam’s fall as the first origin, and since it is profane to suppose that the most excellent work of God fell into such misery while God was unwilling, idle, or acting at random, we most rightly approve what you condemn here. Nor do we therefore transfer the fault of Adam’s fall to God. It inheres wholly in Adam’s will, which was in no way coerced and was not then subject to concupiscence, since concupiscence had not yet entered the world.

1. The apostle by no means says Adam fell into these calamities WITH GOD JUSTLY SO ORDAINING AND DECREEING. Paul’s words mean that all human beings are sinners, on all whom God wills to have mercy if they receive Christ by true faith. But he does not say God ordained that all human beings sin and perish.

2. It is a human thought that humanity sinned because God was unwilling, idle, or drowsy. But we must judge, speak, and think concerning God and his will ONLY FROM GOD’S WORD, and his majesty must not be scrutinized.

3. It was said shortly before: if Beza’s and his brethren’s opinion concerning God’s absolute decree—by which God ordained and decreed that humanity sin—were true, who would transfer the fault to God unjustly? This is submitted to the reader’s judgment. Indeed, we expressed this more openly in our fifth thesis using their own words, where they say, “Why HE DESTINES THESE rather than those for salvation or damnation has NO OTHER initiating CAUSE THAN HIS OWN WILL.” How then is the fault transferred to those against whom an eternal and immutable decree of damnation was made by God’s BARE AND SOLE will, with no other initiating cause preceding?

### Dogma VI Condemned by the Württemberg Doctors

“Since Holy Scripture cries out that all mortals were enslaved to eternal death in the person of one man, THIS CANNOT BE ASCRIBED TO NATURE but PROCEEDED FROM GOD’S WONDROUS COUNSEL.”

### Dogma VII Condemned by the Württemberg Doctors

“That Adam’s fall involved so many nations together with their children and so many infants in eternal death without remedy has no other cause than THAT IT SO SEEMED GOOD TO GOD.”

#### Theodore Beza’s Reply to Dogmas VI and VII

The answer above refutes this thesis too as incorrectly written. Neither Adam’s fall proceeded from anywhere but his spontaneous will, nor his descendants’ stubbornness from anywhere but their corruption, although neither occurred without the decree. Hence Prudentius’s most skillful saying: “No one is guilty if fates govern what is lived and done; rather, whoever willingly dares what is not permitted is guilty.”

1. Since according to God’s decree it was so ordained that the first human beings must fall, as their words were recited by us in Theses VIII and X, how can it be truly said that the fall proceeded from nowhere but humanity’s own will? In this matter Beza and his associates manifestly and openly contradict themselves.

### Dogma VIII Condemned by the Württemberg Doctors

“God foreknew what outcome humanity would have before creating humanity BECAUSE HE SO ORDAINED IT BY HIS DECREE.”

#### Theodore Beza’s Reply to Dogma VIII

Since in the order of causes God knows nothing will occur prior to decreeing it will occur—unless we suppose, most absurdly, that something exists or occurs apart from God’s decree—it follows that the decree precedes knowledge. Nor is it a matter of will that knowledge is the proper cause of things.

1. It is one thing to foreknow something will have such an outcome and another to ordain it by one’s decree. The former belongs to the intellect, the latter to the will. If God’s will concurs in Adam’s sin, it cannot be excluded from the cause. For what anyone wills, and what occurs with him ordaining and decreeing, he is also rightly called its cause.

### Dogma IX Condemned by the Württemberg Doctors

“Adam could not fall except by GOD’S decree and ordination.”

#### Theodore Beza’s Reply to Dogma IX

We spoke of this concerning Dogmas VI and VII.

1. This further confirms the impiety just recited. For if Adam could not fall unless God so decreed and ordained that he had to fall, how do you not openly establish God’s will as the cause of his fall?

### Dogma X Condemned by the Württemberg Doctors

“Humanity HAD TO fall from its purity and integrity.”

[Part II | PDF page 238 | logical source scan 237 | printed page 236 | signed close]

#### Theodore Beza’s Reply to Dogma X

This necessity is from the hypothesis, not from Adam’s own nature, as the human race is today enslaved to sin. Thus in GOD’s word it is said that Christ, the second Adam, had to be crucified.

1. This hypothesis that Beza and his associates invent is not only false but impious and blasphemous against God. It is as though God made an absolute decree such as the adversaries invent, as if they had sat in God’s secret counsel.

2. This hypothesis is true because the LORD made a decree to redeem the human race through his Son’s blood. This could not be done without his death. But it is not an absolute necessity, as though God could not have ordained everything otherwise.

### Dogma XI Condemned by the Württemberg Doctors

“God does not will those destined to reprobation TO BE SAVED OR HIS SON’S DEATH TO BENEFIT THEM.”

Jacobus Andreae, Doctor

Lucas Osiander, Doctor

#### Theodore Beza’s Reply to Dogma XI

1. We explained concerning Theses V and VI the sense in which what you condemn here is rightly and orthodoxly said.

1. All the truly godly, and even those only moderately instructed in the rudiments of the Christian faith and religion, can readily understand and judge that no godly sense inheres in the adversaries’ words when they say and assert that God DOES NOT WILL those destined to reprobation TO BE SAVED OR HIS SON’S DEATH TO BENEFIT THEM.

Since this entire doctrine of our adversaries tends toward the overthrow of the whole gospel ministry, the certainty of the divine promises concerning Christ, baptism, the Lord’s Supper, and absolution, and removes every consolation from godly and afflicted consciences, it must deservedly be avoided, as the colloquy that follows clearly teaches.

Theodore Beza, Minister of the Genevan Church

Abraham Musculus, Minister of the Bernese Church

Peter Hubner, Public Professor of the Greek Language in the Bernese Academy

Antoine de la Faye, Minister of the Genevan Church

Claude Aubery, Doctor and Professor of Philosophy in the Academy of Lausanne

Montbéliard, March 29, 1586.

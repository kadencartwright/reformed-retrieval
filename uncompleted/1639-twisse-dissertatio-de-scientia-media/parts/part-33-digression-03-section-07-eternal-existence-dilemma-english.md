[Book I, printed p. 70; BSB canvas 00096]

Again, this existence of things in eternity is either something or nothing. If it is nothing, how
does it contribute anything to God’s intuitive knowledge, since your opinion requires the presence
of things in order to terminate God’s intuitive knowledge? If it is something, it is either created
or uncreated. If it is something created, it must be produced by God. Perhaps you will say that it
is produced by God insofar as it results from the production of those same things in time, a
production which is still future. But I reply that a thing resulting from the production of another
thing has never been heard to precede the production of the thing from which it results, much less
to precede it from eternity.

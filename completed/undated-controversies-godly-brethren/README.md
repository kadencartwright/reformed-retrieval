# On Certain Controversies among Godly Brothers

This directory presents the complete Latin text and a fresh English
translation of the corporate advice printed under the editorial title *Ad
quasdam piorum fratrum controversias* at CO X/1 229–230.

> **Editorial status:** This is an AI-assisted draft transcription and
> translation checked line by line against a high-resolution facsimile image,
> not a peer-reviewed critical edition. A qualified Neo-Latinist should
> independently collate it before formal publication or reliance on a
> disputed reading.

## Work identity

| Field | Value |
|---|---|
| Attribution | John Calvin in CO's *Consilia*, conveying the judgment of his Genevan ministerial body |
| Addressee | One unnamed “excellent man” and “brother to be cherished from the heart” |
| Community | “The church of N.”; the suppressed place is not recoverable from the CO main text |
| CO title | *Ad quasdam piorum fratrum controversias* (editorial, not part of the advice body) |
| Date | Undated |
| Place | Geneva |
| Original language | Latin; CO gives no notice of translation from another language |
| Genre | Corporate ecclesiastical advice (*consilium*) |
| Subjects | Persecution, clandestine worship, clandestine marriage, night burial, baptism under papal rites, Christian steadfastness |
| Printed locator | CO X/1, pp. 229–230 |

The body has no initial salutation or final signature. Its singular speaker
addresses an individual who is expected to strengthen frightened brothers,
but the answer is explicitly written *ex collegii nostri sententia*, “in
accordance with the judgment of our collegium,” and closes with greetings from
the speaker's fellow ministers. The corporate voice and Geneva closing are
part of the transmitted text; the Calvin attribution belongs to CO's
collection and earlier printed tradition rather than to a surviving signature.

## Edition and printed transmission

The transcription follows Wilhelm Baum, Eduard Cunitz, and Eduard Reuss,
eds., *Ioannis Calvini opera quae supersunt omnia*, vol. X, part 1, *Corpus
Reformatorum* XXXVIII (Brunswick: C. A. Schwetschke, 1871), pp. 229–230.

CO's title note records the earlier Geneva *Epistolae et Responsa*, p. 331;
Lausanne, p. 628; Hanover, p. 708; Chouet, p. 455; and Amsterdam, p. 208. It
does not identify an autograph, manuscript copy, date, addressee, or textual
variants. This edition follows the CO printed main text without inventing the
place hidden by `N.` or treating the editorial heading as an authorial title.

## Authorial and editorial matter

[`latin.txt`](latin.txt) begins with *Ecclesiae N. calamitas* and ends with
*Symmystae quoque mei plurimum salutant. Genevae.* It includes all three
questions and their argumentation, the corporate-source statement, blessing,
fellow-ministers' greeting, and place. It excludes the CO heading, title-note
cue and bibliography, running head, and foliation. CO prints no variant
apparatus for the item.

The preceding lines on p. 229 are the closing of *Caelibatum in ministro non
ita requirendum esse*. The next printed page begins a new section,
*Quaestiones matrimoniales*. Nothing between the main-text opening and the
Geneva closing is omitted.

## Normalization and textual cruxes

The transcription follows [`TRANSLATION_POLICY.md`](../../TRANSLATION_POLICY.md):
printer line wrapping is removed, edition punctuation and spelling are
retained, and both printed pages are marked. The page turn divides no word:
p. 229 ends *noctu et clam* and p. 230 begins *convenire viros et mulieres*.
Forms such as *persequutio*, *Hierosolymae*, *Quamquam*, *iudaicae*, and
*Symmystae* are retained as printed.

Interpretive points include:

- `N.` suppresses the afflicted church's place. The main text offers no secure
  basis for expanding it.
- “Paul's saying” about hoping in the living God and enduring reproaches
  alludes to Pauline language but has no editorial verse reference in CO; no
  reference is silently added to the body.
- *Conventus ... privati et clandestini* are private and clandestine worship
  assemblies, defended as non-tumultuous responses to coercion rather than as
  inherently preferable church order.
- *Tabulae* in the marriage paragraph are civil instruments or records. A
  marriage documented in that political order is not clandestine merely
  because the couple refuses what the writer calls a profaned papal blessing.
- *Simulatio* in the baptism paragraph is fearful outward pretense or
  conformity that would amount to tacit approval of impieties, not merely
  prudent silence.
- *Collegium* denotes the writer's ministerial body or company, not a modern
  educational institution; *symmystae* are fellow ministers.

## Translation independence

Mary D. Beaty and Benjamin Wirt Farley published a copyrighted translation in
*Calvin's Ecclesiastical Advice* (1991). It was not consulted or used for the
wording of this translation, which was made afresh from `latin.txt` and then
checked against the facsimile.

Suggested citation:

> John Calvin (attributed), *Ad quasdam piorum fratrum controversias*, undated
> corporate Genevan advice, *Calvini Opera* X/1 (1871), 229–230, normalized
> Latin and draft English translation, *Reformed Retrieval*.

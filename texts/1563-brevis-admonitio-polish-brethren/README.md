# A Brief Warning to the Polish Brethren (1563)

This directory presents the complete Latin prose and a new English translation
of John Calvin's *Brevis admonitio ad fratres Polonos, ne triplicem in Deo
essentiam pro tribus personis imaginando, tres sibi Deos fabricent* (*A Brief
Warning to the Polish Brethren, Lest by Imagining a Threefold Essence in God
in Place of Three Persons They Fashion Three Gods for Themselves*).

## Work and edition identity

The CO prolegomena report that Calvin wrote this warning near the end of 1562
after receiving reports of renewed trinitarian controversy in Poland. In a
letter to Heinrich Bullinger dated 16 January 1563, Calvin said that he had
written down his judgment at Bullinger's request and arranged to have it
published. The CO editors based their text on a rare copy of Perrin's 1563
Geneva edition then held by the Zurich civic library. They also record Latin
reprints in the collected *Tractatus* of 1576, 1597, 1611, and the Amsterdam
edition, as well as French versions in the collected *Opuscules* of 1566 and
1611.

This admonition is a complete, independent publication. It must not be merged
with Calvin's later *Epistola Ioannis Calvini qua fidem Admonitionis ab eo
nuper editae apud Polonos confirmat*, printed separately at CO IX 641–650.
That follow-up letter, addressed to nobles of Lesser Poland and citizens of
Kraków and internally dated 30 April 1563, is a distinct work.

CO IX 629–630 reproduces the 1563 publisher's title-page wording and imprint;
it is source paratext rather than Calvin's argumentative prose. CO IX 631–632
is blank. The running heads at 633–638, CO page numbers, and terminal rule are
nineteenth-century typography. The square-bracketed markers such as
`[pag. 4]` in CO's body text identify pages in the 1563 printing and are
editorial locator apparatus. Four French footnotes reproduce amplifications
from the early French state of the work. `latin.txt` therefore begins with
Calvin's prose at p. 633 and excludes the running heads, old-edition page
markers, and French comparison notes. The complete source title is preserved
as the document identifier and in the metadata above.

## Files

- [`latin.txt`](latin.txt) is a complete diplomatic-normalized transcription
  of Calvin's Latin prose. It preserves CO spelling and punctuation, joins
  printer line-end divisions, and marks every printed CO page containing the
  prose.
- [`english.md`](english.md) is a fresh English translation made from that
  transcription and checked against the facsimile. Its notes explain important
  lexical, grammatical, historical, and theological ambiguities without
  silently resolving them.

## Verification and use

The Latin was extracted for orientation and then collated manually, word by
word, against 300-DPI renderings of all three scan pages containing the prose.
Each scan page contains two numbered CO columns. The turns at 633/634,
634/635, and 635/636 divide the printed words *perpetuam*, *Ascendisti*, and
*veteres*; the normalized transcription joins each word and places the new CO
marker before the whole word. Page 638 begins with the complete word
*extendere*, in the middle of a sentence, and is marked there.

No complete public-domain English translation was located in the project's
bibliographic survey completed 11 August 2026. This translation was made
afresh from the Latin; no modern English translation supplied its wording.

The English in this directory is an **AI-assisted scholarly draft**. It has
received a sentence-by-sentence source check but not an independent review by
a human Latinist. Until that review occurs, quote the Latin and printed-page
locator for exact-wording claims and identify the English as this project's
draft translation. Calvin's descriptions of named adversaries and his
historical claim about Athanasius are translated as claims in the source, not
endorsed as neutral modern judgments.

Suggested citation:

> John Calvin, *Brevis admonitio ad fratres Polonos*, Geneva: François Perrin,
> 1563, in *Calvini Opera* IX (1870), 629–638, normalized Latin and draft
> English translation, *Reformed Retrieval*.

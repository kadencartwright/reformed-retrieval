# Influential Reformed Latin Works Lacking a Public-Domain English Translation

Bibliographical audit current to **12 August 2026**.

> **Calvin audit note:** the Calvin-only research is expanded in [*John Calvin
> Texts Without a Located Public-Domain English Translation*](calvin-no-public-domain-english.md).
> Use that document for the exhaustive Calvin audit. Henry Cole's complete
> public-domain English of *De aeterna Dei praedestinatione* is incorporated
> below, so that work is not treated as an outstanding target.

## Purpose and scope

This document records influential Reformed works for which the audit did not
locate a complete, reusable public-domain English translation. It gives
particular attention to John Calvin, Theodore Beza, and the English and Scottish
Presbyterian traditions.

The requested translation phase includes the non-Calvin candidates below.
**Every Calvin item is excluded from that phase.** Calvin is retained in this
document for bibliographical completeness only.

Translation progress, source blockers, and incomplete working files are tracked
separately in [`workbench/TRANSLATION_STATUS.md`](../workbench/TRANSLATION_STATUS.md).

This is a report of search results, not proof that no translation has ever
circulated. An unpublished manuscript, privately circulated translation, or
poorly cataloged edition may exist. Before beginning any individual translation,
the exact title should therefore receive a fresh catalog and full-text search.

### Status labels

- **Untranslated** — no complete English translation was located.
- **Partial** — only excerpts, translated tables of contents, abridgments, or
  machine-assisted renderings were located.
- **Modern only** — a complete English translation exists, but it remains under
  copyright; no public-domain or CC0 English version was located.
- **No target found** — the author's important surviving works were already in
  English, or no substantial Latin-authored treatise was located.

“Freely accessible” is not synonymous with “public domain.” In particular,
modern all-rights-reserved editions and Creative Commons editions containing
noncommercial or no-derivatives restrictions do not provide a public-domain or
CC0 text for this repository.

### Audit method and principal catalogs

The audit compared author bibliographies and digital witnesses in the
[Post-Reformation Digital Library](https://www.prdl.org/) with exact-title
catalog searches, public scans, early English book records, and records for
modern translations. Important source repositories include e-rara, Google
Books, the Internet Archive/Open Library, EEBO-TCP, the University of Utrecht,
and the Bavarian State Library. For Calvin, the University of Geneva's
[complete digitized _Calvini Opera_ collection](https://archive-ouverte.unige.ch/unige:650)
provides the principal public witness. A positive record for a modern English
edition establishes only that the modern edition exists; the claim that no
public-domain version was located rests on the wider exact-title audit.

## Highest-priority prospective projects

These were the strongest candidates at the beginning of the audit when
influence, relevance to Presbyterian history, the apparent translation gap,
and source availability were considered together. They are a priority history,
not a live progress list; completed and active working drafts are tracked in
[`workbench/TRANSLATION_STATUS.md`](../workbench/TRANSLATION_STATUS.md).

1. **Theodore Beza, _De vera excommunicatione et christiano presbyterio_**
   (1590). Beza's answer to Erastus and a major defense of ecclesiastical
   discipline. **Untranslated.** A [public-domain scan is available through
   Wikimedia Commons](https://commons.wikimedia.org/wiki/File:Tractatus_pius_et_moderatus_de_vera_excommunicatione_et_christiano_presbyterio..._Theodoro_Beza..._auctore_(IA_tractatuspiusetm00bzet).pdf).
2. **David Calderwood, _Altare Damascenum_** (1623). Probably the most important
   untranslated Latin monument of early Scottish Presbyterian polity. The 1621
   English _Altar of Damascus_ is much shorter; the Latin is a greatly expanded
   reworking, not merely the same text. See the [Latin scan](https://commons.ptsem.edu/id/altarasce00cald)
   and [short English antecedent](https://quod.lib.umich.edu/e/eebo/A17571.0001.001?view=toc).
3. **Samuel Rutherford, _Disputatio scholastica de divina providentia_**
   (1649). The major Scottish treatment of providence, concurrence,
   predetermination, sin, and freedom. **Partial:** only the table of contents
   has received a human English translation. See the [Latin scan](https://www.digitale-sammlungen.de/en/details/bsb10351095)
   and [translated contents](https://reformedbooksonline.com/wp-content/uploads/2014/07/rutherford-samuel-table-of-contents-to-treatise-on-providence.pdf).
4. **Anthony Tuckney, _Praelectiones theologicae_** (1679). Lectures and
   Cambridge determinations by an important Westminster divine and principal
   drafter. A scholarly discussion describes the work as “yet untranslated.”
   See the [bibliographical record](https://catalog.folger.edu/record/430688)
   and [translation-status discussion](https://www.galaxie.com/article/wtj81-1-01).
5. **William Twisse, _Vindiciae gratiae, potestatis ac providentiae Dei_**
   (1632). The principal Latin dogmatic work of the Westminster Assembly's
   prolocutor, answering Arminius on Perkins. **Untranslated.** See the
   [Latin editions and scans](https://www.prdl.org/author_view.php?a_id=52&sort=topic).
6. **Robert Parker, _De politeia ecclesiastica Christi et hierarchica opposita_**
   (1616). A foundational English Presbyterian/Puritan ecclesiology against
   episcopal hierarchy. **Untranslated.** See the [Latin editions and scans](https://www.prdl.org/author_view.php?a_id=2099).
7. **Samuel Rutherford, _Exercitationes apologeticae pro divina gratia_**
   (1636). Rutherford's first great Latin anti-Arminian work, treating decrees,
   grace, freedom, and concurrence. **Untranslated.** See the [Latin scan](https://openlibrary.org/books/OL25388785M/Exercitationes_apologeticae_pro_divina_gratia).
8. **John Brown of Wamphray, _De causa Dei contra antisabbatarios_**
   (1674–1676). Two volumes on divine law, worship, sacred time, the Decalogue,
   and the Lord's Day. **Partial:** only small portions appear to have been
   translated. See [Brown's Latin corpus](https://www.prdl.org/author_view.php?a_id=3335).
9. **Theodore Beza, _De haereticis a civili magistratu puniendis_** (1554).
   Beza's historically consequential response to Castellio on the civil
   punishment of heresy. A contemporary French translation exists, but no
   complete English translation was located. See the [Latin scan](https://books.google.com/books?hl=en&id=Jv07AAAAcAAJ&printsec=frontcover).
10. **John Calvin, _Defensio orthodoxae fidei de sacra Trinitate contra errores
    Michaelis Serveti_** (1554). Calvin's defense of Nicene doctrine and of the
    punishment of Servetus. A recent independently published English rendering
    appears to exist, but no public-domain or established scholarly translation
    was located. See the [1554 Latin edition](https://openlibrary.org/books/OL45031128M/Defensio_orthodoxae_fidei_de_sacra_Trinitate).
    **Excluded from the requested translation phase.**

For a practical first series, the initial audit recommended Beza's _De vera
excommunicatione_, Parker's _De politeia_, a carefully delimited section of
Tuckney's _Praelectiones_, and Rutherford's _Disputatio de providentia_. The
Beza and Rutherford projects in that recommendation now have complete
AI-assisted working drafts; Parker and Tuckney remain queued. Together these
projects give a particularly direct line from Geneva through English Puritan polity to
the Scottish and Westminster traditions.

### Beza false positives and exclusions

Several Beza titles are not translation gaps because sixteenth-century English
translations already exist. These include his _Confessio_, predestination
table, Christian questions and answers, and treatise on the visible marks of the
church. They should not be added to the present translation queue without a
separate edition-specific reason.

## Scottish and English Presbyterian traditions — included in translation scope

### Thomas Cartwright

- [_Metaphrasis et homiliae in librum Salomonis qui inscribitur Ecclesiastes_](https://catalog.folger.edu/record/371032)
  (1604). **Untranslated.** A Latin paraphrase and homilies on Ecclesiastes.
- [_Commentarii succincti et dilucidi in Proverbia Salomonis_](https://commons.wikimedia.org/wiki/File:Commentarii_succincti_%26_dilucidi_in_Proverbia_Salomonis_(IA_commentariisucci00cart).pdf)
  (1617). **Untranslated.** A Latin commentary on Proverbs.

These are commentaries or homilies rather than strict polemical treatises.
Cartwright's Gospel harmony apparently received an early English translation,
and his English anti-Rhemist and Presbyterian writings are not translation
targets.

### Samuel Rutherford

- [_Exercitationes apologeticae pro divina gratia_](https://openlibrary.org/books/OL25388785M/Exercitationes_apologeticae_pro_divina_gratia)
  (1636). **Untranslated.** Rutherford's anti-Arminian treatment of divine
  decrees, grace, human freedom, and concurrence.
- [_Disputatio scholastica de divina providentia_](https://www.digitale-sammlungen.de/en/details/bsb10351095)
  (1649). **Partial.** No complete English translation was located. A human
  translation of the [table of contents](https://reformedbooksonline.com/wp-content/uploads/2014/07/rutherford-samuel-table-of-contents-to-treatise-on-providence.pdf)
  is available.
- [_Examen Arminianismi_](https://books.google.com/books/about/Examen_Arminianismi.html?id=9IhiAAAAcAAJ)
  (1668, posthumous). **Partial.** A 2019 project supplies
  [human-translated contents and excerpts from every chapter](https://reformedbooksonline.com/wp-content/uploads/2019/10/Johnson-Fentiman-Rutherfords-Examination-of-Arminianism-the-Tables-of-Contents-with-Excerpts-from-Every-Chapter-1.pdf).
  A [full machine-based web rendering](https://www.monergism.com/examination-arminianism-ebook)
  now exists, but it is not a reviewed public-domain human translation.

### George Gillespie

**No target found.** No substantial Latin-authored treatise was located. His
major ecclesiological and Westminster works were written in English, so
Gillespie is not a promising Latin-to-English translation target. This is a
negative finding, not an accidental omission from the queue. The
[Dictionary of National Biography bibliography](https://en.wikisource.org/wiki/Dictionary_of_National_Biography,_1885-1900/Gillespie,_George)
corroborates the English-language shape of his principal corpus.

### Robert Baillie

- [_Operis historici et chronologici libri duo_](https://openlibrary.org/books/OL7235448M/Operis_historici_et_chronologici_libri_duo)
  (1668). **Untranslated.** It covers sacred and profane history to Constantine,
  chronological questions, and three theological diatribes. It is interesting
  but less directly Presbyterian than Calderwood or Rutherford. A
  [public scan is available through the Internet Archive](https://archive.org/details/operishistoricie00bail).

### David Calderwood

- [_Altare Damascenum; seu Ecclesiae Anglicanae politia_](https://commons.ptsem.edu/id/altarasce00cald)
  (1623). **The expanded Latin text is untranslated.** The shorter 1621 English
  [_Altar of Damascus_](https://quod.lib.umich.edu/e/eebo/A17571.0001.001?view=toc)
  does not contain much of the Latin material and should not be mistaken for a
  complete English translation. A modern translation of selected propositions
  is [likewise only partial](https://reformedbooksonline.com/wp-content/uploads/2021/04/Calderwood-David-Propositions-on-Indifferent-Things-and-Ceremonies-in-Worship-Final.pdf)
  and is released under CC BY-ND rather than placed in the public domain.

### John Brown of Wamphray

Brown's Latin editions are listed in the [Post-Reformation Digital Library](https://www.prdl.org/author_view.php?a_id=3335).

- _De causa Dei contra antisabbatarios tractatus_ (1674–1676), two volumes.
  **Partial.** The work treats divine laws, worship and sacred time, the nature
  and origin of the Sabbath, the Decalogue and fourth commandment, the Lord's
  Day, and its sanctification. Only small portions, including material on psalm
  singing, appear to have been translated; relevant translated material was
  published in the [_Confessional Presbyterian_](https://print.confessional.org/journal/2007-volume-3.pdf?raw=).
- _Libri duo: in priori Wolzogium … in posteriori Lamberti Velthusii sententia
  libertino-Erastiana …_ (1670). **Untranslated.** The first book concerns
  anti-Socinian hermeneutics; the second addresses a libertine-Erastian account
  of idolatry and superstition, with ecclesiological material on the visible and
  invisible church and separation.

### William Twisse

Twisse was the prolocutor of the Westminster Assembly. His [Latin editions and
scans are cataloged by PRDL](https://www.prdl.org/author_view.php?a_id=52&sort=topic).

- _Vindiciae gratiae, potestatis ac providentiae Dei_ (1632; later edition
  1648). **Untranslated.** It answers Arminius's examination of William Perkins.
  Twisse's English _The Riches of God's Love_ (1653) addresses related
  controversies but is not a complete translation: it also contains
  augmentations by other English authors and does not precisely preserve the
  Latin work's section enumeration.
- _Dissertatio de scientia media tribus libris absoluta_ (1639).
  **Untranslated.** A three-book treatment of middle knowledge.
- _Ad Jacobi Arminii Collationem … Animadversiones_ (1649).
  **Untranslated.** Animadversions on Arminius.

### Robert Parker

Parker's [Latin editions and scans are cataloged by PRDL](https://www.prdl.org/author_view.php?a_id=2099).

- _De politeia ecclesiastica Christi et hierarchica opposita libri tres_
  (1616; later edition 1638). **Untranslated.** A foundational work of English
  Presbyterian/Puritan ecclesiology opposing episcopal hierarchy.
- _De descensu Domini nostri Jesu Christi ad inferos libri quatuor_ (1611).
  **Untranslated.** Hugh Sanford began the work and Parker completed it; that
  mixed authorship should be retained in any edition metadata.

### Anthony Tuckney

- [_Praelectiones theologicae, nec non determinationes quaestionum theologicarum_](https://catalog.folger.edu/record/430688)
  (1679). **Untranslated.** A large volume of Latin academic lectures and
  disputations, approximately 750 pages, by a major Westminster divine. A
  scholarly treatment explicitly describes it as [“yet untranslated”](https://www.galaxie.com/article/wtj81-1-01).

### Robert Rollock

- [_Quaestiones et responsiones aliquot de Foedere Dei et de Sacramento_](https://www.prdl.org/author_view.php?a_id=482)
  (1596). **Modern only.** Aaron Denlinger's [_Some Questions and Answers about God's
  Covenant and the Sacrament That Is a Seal of God's Covenant, with Related
  Texts_](https://www.perlego.com/book/881084/some-questions-and-answers-about-gods-covenant-and-the-sacrament-that-is-a-seal-of-gods-covenant-with-related-texts-pdf)
  appeared in 2016. No public-domain English version was located.

Rollock's _Effectual Calling_ and several other works received early English
translations and should not be treated as gaps without further work-specific
evidence.

### John Sharp (Johannes Scharpius)

Sharp taught at Edinburgh. His [Latin editions and bibliography are cataloged
by PRDL](https://www.prdl.org/author_view.php?a_id=492).

- _Tractatus de justificatione hominis coram Deo_ (1609).
  **Untranslated.** On justification before God.
- _Tractatus de misero hominis statu sub peccato_ (1610).
  **Untranslated.** On the miserable state of humanity under sin.
- _Cursus theologicus_ (1618–1620), two volumes. **Untranslated.** A substantial
  Reformed system of theological controversies answering Rome and Bellarmine.
- [_Symphonia prophetarum et apostolorum_](https://catalog.folger.edu/record/383137)
  (1625). **Untranslated.** A harmonizing treatment of apparent contradictions
  among prophets and apostles.

### Robert Howie

- [_De reconciliatione hominis cum Deo, seu de humani generis redemptione_](https://www.prdl.org/author_view.php?a_id=1937)
  (1591). **Untranslated.** An early Scottish treatment of redemption and
  covenant theology, influenced by continental federal theology. The volume is
  a main theological treatise followed by two further disputations, _De
  communione fidelium cum Christo_ and _De iustificatione hominis coram Deo_.
  A [complete BSB witness](https://www.digitale-sammlungen.de/en/view/bsb00021301)
  supplies 169 canvases with page-level hOCR.

### Robert Boyd of Trochrig

- [_In epistolam Pauli apostoli ad Ephesios praelectiones supra CC_](https://www.prdl.org/author_view.php?a_id=156)
  (1652; another edition 1661). **Untranslated.** More than two hundred Latin
  lectures with extensive theological commonplaces. This is a very large
  commentary rather than a discrete treatise.

### William Ames

Ames was not strictly Presbyterian, but he was an enormously influential
English Puritan.

- [_Coronis ad Collationem Hagiensem_](https://onlinebooks.library.upenn.edu/webbin/book/lookupid?key=ha009706352)
  (1618; later editions). **Untranslated.** Ames's major anti-Remonstrant work.
- [_Bellarminus enervatus_](https://www.e-rara.ch/zuz/content/titleinfo/13738592)
  (1625–1629; collected editions followed). **Untranslated.** A four-volume
  anti-Roman system engaging Bellarmine.

## Additional major Reformed works — included in translation scope

These works lie outside the specifically British Presbyterian focus, but they
meet the narrower criterion that no public-domain English translation was
located.

### Franciscus Junius

- _De theologia vera_. **Modern only.** It was first translated as _A Treatise
  on True Theology_ in 2014. The [online edition is explicitly
  all-rights-reserved](https://www.juniusinstitute.org/companion/junius_de_vera/index.php).
  A practical independent Latin base is Abraham Kuyper's 1882 public-domain
  edition in [_Opuscula theologica selecta_](https://archive.org/details/opusculatheologi00juni),
  whose complete authorial unit occupies printed pp. 39–101: title and
  dedication on pp. 39–40, the thirty-nine-thesis synopsis on pp. 41–43, a
  blank p. 44, and the continuous exposition and concluding prayer on pp.
  45–101. It has downloadable OCR and retains the original folio locators from
  Junius's collected works.

### Francis Turretin

- _Institutio theologiae elencticae_. **Modern only.** The Giger/Dennison
  translation of 1992–1997 remains the [only complete English translation](https://www.heritagebooks.org/products/institutes-of-elenctic-theology-3-volume-set-turretin.html)
  located in the audit and remains copyrighted. A complete public-domain Latin
  working set is the 1847 Edinburgh edition at the Internet Archive:
  [volume I](https://archive.org/details/institutiotheolo01turr),
  [volume II](https://archive.org/details/institutiotheolo02turr), and
  [volume III](https://archive.org/details/institutiotheolo03turr), each with
  downloadable OCR.

### Amandus Polanus

- _Syntagma theologiae christianae_. **Modern open translation in progress, but
  not public domain.** The new English project uses CC BY-NC-SA, which permits
  some reuse but is neither public domain nor compatible with a CC0 corpus. See
  the [project description](https://mereorthodoxy.com/the-faith-received/polanus-syntagma/).
  The 1615 author-revised Latin is available from the [Bavarian State
  Library](https://www.digitale-sammlungen.de/en/details/bsb00014667) with 774
  canvases and page-level hOCR.

## Translation-phase scope summary

### Excluded

- **All John Calvin works**, including every Calvin candidate in this document.
- Works already available in adequate public-domain English, including the Beza
  false positives noted above.
- George Gillespie, because no substantial Latin-authored target was located.
- The five Calvin/Socinus/baptism documents already translated in this
  repository.

### Included, subject to source preparation

- Every Beza candidate listed in the Beza table that is marked untranslated,
  partial, or modern only.
- The listed works of Cartwright, Rutherford, Baillie, Calderwood, Brown,
  Twisse, Parker, Tuckney, Rollock, Sharp, Howie, Boyd, and Ames.
- The wider Reformed candidates by Junius, Turretin, and Polanus.

The “included” designation records the user's requested scope. It does not
mean that a Latin witness has already been transcribed or that a complete
translation can responsibly be produced without first checking pagination,
missing leaves, OCR quality, edition identity, and the copyright status of any
modern source used for consultation.

## Source-acquisition and editorial-readiness audit

The following work-level audit turns the bibliography above into an actionable
translation queue. Page and scan counts describe the particular digital
witness, not an estimate of how many pages a future English edition will occupy.
OCR is only a transcription aid and does not replace collation with the page
images.

### Beza source map

For the first six works the preferred base is Beza's author-revised 1582
_Tractationes theologicae_, which e-rara marks with the Public Domain Mark:
[volume I](https://www.e-rara.ch/zuz/content/titleinfo/7831557),
[volume II](https://www.e-rara.ch/zuz/content/titleinfo/7595651), and
[volume III](https://www.e-rara.ch/zuz/content/titleinfo/7595652). The PDFs are

The source audit therefore identifies two genuine blockers rather than silently
promising nonexistent inputs: Sharp's _Symphonia_ cannot be translated from an
open witness until a scan is obtained, and a number of Google-only witnesses
need reliable image/OCR acquisition before batch transcription. Rollock is not
an untranslated work in the absolute sense, but remains in scope only because
the existing complete translation is copyrighted.

## Editorial and legal cautions for subsequent translations

1. Translate directly from an identified public-domain Latin witness; do not
   silently use a copyrighted English version as the base text.
2. Record the edition, scan provider, stable link, pagination, and any missing
   or illegible leaves before transcription begins.
3. Distinguish a normalized transcription from a critical edition, in keeping
   with the repository's [translation and transcription policy](../TRANSLATION_POLICY.md).
4. Label AI-assisted translations as working editions until a named reader
   competent in early modern theological Latin has checked them against page
   images.
5. Preserve the qualifiers in this audit. “No complete English translation was
   located” is more accurate than an unqualified claim that a work has never
   been translated.

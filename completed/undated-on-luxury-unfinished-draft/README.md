# On Luxury: Unfinished Draft

This directory presents every word of the surviving Latin draft printed as
*De luxu* at CO X/1 203–206, including four notes that CO identifies as
Calvin's marginalia, together with a fresh English translation.

> **Status and limits:** This is an **unfinished youthful working draft**, not
> a completed treatise and not evidence of Calvin's final arrangement or
> formulation. CO reports an autograph with unfinished phrases and authorial
> marginal notes. The present transcription and translation are AI-assisted
> drafts checked line by line against high-resolution images of CO, not a
> peer-reviewed critical edition and not a fresh inspection of the autograph.
> A qualified Neo-Latinist should independently collate Geneva Cod. 145,
> fol. 125 before formal publication or reliance on a disputed reading.

The surviving unit begins in mid-conception with *Bellum quum suscipio* (“When
I undertake a war”) and already breaks off its first sentence with *in quorum
etc.* It gradually changes from connected prose into short prompts, source
reminders, and undeveloped topics. It ends with an Augustine citation and
quotation, not with an authorial conclusion, date, place, or signature. The
subjects include food, clothing, buildings, funeral display, long hair,
monastic habits, perfume, dinners, dancing, mirrors, and the relation of
private thrift to public magnificence. Those themes do not turn the surviving
notes into a finished systematic argument.

## Edition, witness, and title apparatus

The transcription follows Wilhelm Baum, Eduard Cunitz, and Eduard Reuss,
eds., *Ioannis Calvini opera quae supersunt omnia*, vol. X, part 1, *Corpus
Reformatorum* XXXVIII (Brunswick: C. A. Schwetschke, 1871), pp. 203–206.

CO's title note states that the text comes from Calvin's autograph in the
Geneva library, Cod. 145, fol. 125. The editors say it appears to be an
imperfect youthful composition to which no final arranging hand was applied,
and that unfinished phrases and the author's marginal notes occur throughout.
That notice is the basis for the witness, attribution, age, and draft-state
statements above. This project has inspected CO's facsimile, not Cod. 145
itself; consequently it does not claim to verify CO's autograph attribution,
its reading of Calvin's hand, or the exact spatial relation of body and margin
in the manuscript.

Both the class heading *Ad disciplinam ecclesiasticam* and the item title *De
luxu* are CO editorial matter. They are useful identifiers and are retained in
metadata, but neither is silently inserted into [`latin.txt`](latin.txt) as
Calvin's wording.

## Completeness and authorial/editorial audit

[`latin.txt`](latin.txt) begins with *Bellum quum suscipio* on p. 203 and ends
with *et necessaria non habent pauperes* on p. 206. Nothing within those
main-text boundaries is omitted. The following items have been distinguished
explicitly:

- All of CO's main text is retained, including sentence fragments, source
  prompts, `etc.`, abbreviations, and the unresolved printed token `H....um`.
- CO prints four footnotes with `(C.)`, marking their contents as Calvin's
  authorial marginal notes. All four are retained at their cues under supplied
  bracketed labels: *Hoc quam dicimus rident ...* on p. 203, and *De offic. in
  fol. 349*, *Senec. Epist 20*, and *Senec. in fine epist.* on p. 204. The
  labels are project apparatus; the words following them are authorial. CO's
  footnote numbers and `(C.)` sigla are editorial and are excluded.
- CO's section heading, item title, running heads, printed page numbers, title
  note, and type-setting footnote cues are excluded from the historical body.
- CO's p. 203 note *habent, vox dubia* and p. 206 note *vox dubia. Horum (?)*
  are editorial apparatus, not Calvin's body text. Their evidence is recorded
  below rather than interpolated into the transcription.
- Printed p. 202 belongs to the preceding French response on images and is
  excluded. Printed p. 207 begins the separate item *Si omnis disciplinae
  tolerantia negatur* and is likewise excluded.

This treatment preserves the unusual case in which material physically placed
in CO's footnote zone is nevertheless reported by CO to be authorial, while
ordinary CO apparatus remains outside the body.

## Normalization and page turns

The transcription follows
[`TRANSLATION_POLICY.md`](../../TRANSLATION_POLICY.md): printer line wrapping
is removed, words divided merely at line endings are joined, edition spelling
and punctuation are otherwise retained, and every printed page is marked.
Forms such as *quum*, *autoribus*, *iniiciet*, *coenae*, *denunciatio*,
*capitiis*, and *Nunquam* are not modernized. Authorial abbreviations and
working forms such as *Genuens.*, *Senec.*, *Genes.*, `etc.`, and *Epimelian*
are not expanded in the Latin.

Two printed page turns divide words:

- pp. 204–205: *ex-colendo* is normalized as *excolendo*, wholly under the
  p. 205 marker;
- pp. 205–206: *ana-choritae* is normalized as *anachoritae*, wholly under the
  p. 206 marker.

The pp. 203–204 turn divides no word. The English markers follow the same
203–204–205–206 sequence. The p. 205 marker precedes “cultivating,” the phrase
corresponding to *excolendo*; the p. 206 marker precedes “anchorites,” the word
corresponding to *anachoritae*.

## Textual and translation cruxes

- The opening *in quorum etc.* is an authorial break, not missing project text.
  Many later `etc.` forms likewise mark undeveloped matter.
- The printed *beneficium ignoscet* is difficult Latin. The translation
  preserves the sense of *ignoscet* (“forgive”) and does not silently emend it
  to the contextually tempting *agnoscet* (“acknowledge”).
- CO calls the second *habent* in *cuius exemplum vident aut quod exemplum
  habent* a doubtful word but offers no replacement. It is therefore retained.
- *Epimelian* appears to be a Latin-script form of Greek *epimeleian* (“care”
  or “attention”). The transcription preserves the printed form; “care” is a
  contextual translation, not a Latin emendation.
- Such strings as *Haec ciborum oblectamenta quae ...*, *ne quid sit ...*,
  *Genuens.*, *Inscripsero Deum sceleri et prosequi hunc locum ...*, *Contra
  caesariem virorum*, and the short p. 206 prompts remain syntactically or
  referentially incomplete. The English exposes rather than repairs their
  working-note character.
- The facsimile reads *Vide Sen. C. 1, ep. 5.* The meaning of `C. 1` is not
  established here. It is neither silently read as `O. 1` nor expanded into a
  modern Seneca locator. Other folio and page numbers are Calvin's working
  source reminders as transmitted through CO, not locators independently
  verified against a particular modern edition.
- CO prints `H....um`, labels it a doubtful word, and suggests *Horum (?)*. The
  main transcription preserves `H....um`; the English identifies it as
  unresolved and does not adopt *Horum*.
- *Vestiarium neutro quem apud Senec. fol. 354* has a conspicuous mismatch
  between the stated neuter and masculine *quem*, besides lacking a finite
  verb. It is presented as unfinished lexical shorthand, not silently repaired
  to *quod*.
- The final Greek working term is *μισοτεχνία*, “hatred of” or “aversion to
  art/craft.” The English transliterates it as “misotechnia” and explains the
  sense without replacing the code-switch in the Latin.

## Translation independence

Mary D. Beaty and Benjamin Wirt Farley published a copyrighted translation in
*Calvin's Ecclesiastical Advice* (1991). It was not consulted or used for the
wording of [`english.md`](english.md), which was translated afresh from the
normalized Latin and checked against the facsimile.

> *De luxu*, undated, *Calvini Opera* X/1 (1871), 203–206, normalized Latin and

# William Twisse, *Animadversiones ad Arminii collationem* (1649)

> **Translation preparation:** This directory contains an AI-assisted,
> diplomatic-normalized Latin working transcription and an independent English
> working translation. Both are unreviewed. Neither file is publication-ready
> until a qualified editor has checked it in full against the page images.

## Work and edition

- **Author:** William Twisse (1578–1646)
- **Editorial preface:** André Rivet (1572–1651)
- **Full title:** *Guilielmi Twissi Anglo-Britanni S.S. Theologiæ Doctoris, Ad
  Jacobi Arminii Collationem cum Francisco Junio; & Johan. Arnoldi Corvini
  Defensionem sententiæ Arminianæ, De Prædestinatione, Gratia, & Libero
  Arbitrio, &c. Quam adversus Danielis Tileni Considerationem edidit,
  Animadversiones*
- **Edition used:** Amsterdam, Joannes Janssonius, 1649
- **Form:** a composite volume containing two separately paginated
  animadversions, with Rivet’s editorial preface and separate subject indexes

Part 01 begins Twisse’s first body, the animadversion on the Arminius–Junius
conference concerning predestination. It does not absorb Rivet’s preface, the
second Corvinus/Tilenus body, or either index into Twisse’s first argument.

## Audited composite map

The physical and authorial sequence is:

- **Canvas 1:** composite title page.
- **Canvas 2:** blank title verso.
- **Canvases 3–8:** André Rivet’s separately attributed preface to the reader.
- **Canvases 9–12:** index of scriptural passages for both bodies. Its printed
  key explicitly identifies `(1)` as the Arminius–Junius part and `(2)` as the
  part against Corvinus’s defence.
- **Canvases 13–147:** first body, separately paginated pp. 1–135, Twisse’s *In
  Arminii cum Iunio de prædestinatione collationem animadversio*.
- **Canvas 148:** blank separator after the first body.
- **Canvases 149–622:** second body, separately paginated pp. 1–474, Twisse’s
  *In Ar[noldi] Corvini defensionem sententiæ Jac. Arminii … adversus Tileni
  considerationem animadversio*.
- **Canvases 623–628:** subject index to the first, Arminius–Junius body.
- **Canvases 629–645:** subject index to the second, Corvinus/Tilenus body.
- **Canvas 646:** blank terminal leaf.

The second pagination therefore begins only at canvas 149. A bare marker such
as “p. 1” is unsafe in this witness; every Part 01 marker says `first body` and
also records its BSB canvas.

## Editorial method

The Latin is diplomatic-normalized: long *s*, ligatures, printer line-end
divisions, and obvious hOCR character substitutions are normalized. Printed
wording, spelling, Greek, argument labels, and the embedded Arminius/Junius
exchange are retained. Running heads, signatures, and catchwords are omitted.
Page images control whenever hOCR merges the two folio columns.

The English is a full working translation of the paired Latin, not a summary.
Speaker and structural labels are retained because Twisse’s response alternates
quoted propositions and replies by Arminius and Junius with his own
`ANIMADVERSIO`. No modern English translation was used.

## Part 01: exact authorial boundary

Part 01 occupies **first-body printed pp. 1–16 / BSB canvases 13–28** and has
sixteen synchronized source markers. It contains Propositions I–V and
Proposition VI, Sections I–IV. The opening is the ruled first-body title and
`PROPOSITIO PRIMA` on canvas 13, not the composite title or Rivet’s preface.

The unit stops on the right column of printed p. 16 / canvas 28 after Twisse’s
sentence ending:

> `… ad sententiæ manifesto falsæ structuram deprehendendam minus instructus
> accederet.`

It stops immediately before the ruled heading `SECTIO QUINTA` on that same
page. This is a coherent sixteen-page authorial unit and an explicit shared-page
boundary. The next part must begin with `SECTIO QUINTA` and repeat the marker
for **first-body printed p. 16 / BSB canvas 28**; it must not advance directly
to p. 17.

## Part 02: exact authorial boundary

Part 02 occupies **first-body printed pp. 16–32 / BSB canvases 28–44** and has
seventeen synchronized source markers. It resumes on the shared right column

The unit stops in the left column of printed p. 32 / canvas 44 after Twisse's
sentence ending:

> `… Neque enim ab æterno fuit homo peccator.`

It stops immediately before the ruled heading `PROPOSITIO SEPTIMA` lower in

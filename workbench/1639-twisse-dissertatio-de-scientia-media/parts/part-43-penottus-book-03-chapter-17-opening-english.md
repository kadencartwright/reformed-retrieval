WILLIAM TWISSE

ON BOOK THREE, CHAPTER XVII,

OF GABRIEL PENOTTUS'S

*Bulwark of Human Liberty*

The coexistence of creatures from eternity is further refuted from St. Thomas himself and his
interpreters.

PENOTTUS.

The second part of the second proposition which we established in the preceding chapter was that
the opinion teaching that all creatures according to their real existences coexist with God from
eternity in eternity was never in any way the opinion of St. Thomas. This is what must be proved in
this chapter. First of all, that distinction drawn from St. Thomas between the coexistence of things
in their proper measure and in an alien and inadequate measure must be rejected. Once it is removed,
every refuge is closed to those who impose this opinion upon St. Thomas. It is rejected because,
when Thomas says that creatures according to their existences are present to God from eternity, he
speaks of the being of actual existence which a thing possesses in itself when it is placed outside
its causes. This is distinguished from future being, because one who attains only the future being
of a thing in its causes cannot have certain knowledge of it, while one who attains the present
being of a contingent thing which it possesses in time, when it is outside its causes in reality,
has certain knowledge of it.

Now from eternity God has present not only the future being which contingent things possess in
their causes, but also the being of actual existence which they possess outside their causes in
each difference of time. In this manner he can have certain knowledge of contingent futures. Thus
the existence according to which things are said to be present to God from eternity is the
existence of things in the proper difference of present time, distinguished from the difference of
future time by which they are measured while they are still in their causes. It is the actual
existence of things when they actually subsist in reality; and thus Thomas speaks. But not a single
word is found in Thomas concerning the existence of those same things, or their coexistence from
eternity outside their proper measure.

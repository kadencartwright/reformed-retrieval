VERSE 11. Although God should prolong the days of a sinner who does evil a
hundred times, nevertheless I also know that it will be well with those who
fear God, who fear before his face.

VERSE 12. But it will not be well with the ungodly, nor will he prolong his
days, which are like a shadow—the person who does not fear before the face of
God himself.

Having interrupted the argument with this concession concerning the delay of
vengeance, together with its effect, Solomon returns to that former judgment
of the Holy Spirit against wicked Princes. He confirms it from the contrary
recompense of the godly and those who fear God. Since it is beyond controversy
that it will be well and blessedly with them, it must likewise be certain that
it will be ill and unhappily with the flagitious and those who cast off the
fear of God.

But here the order seems rather disturbed. For it appears that he ought to
say: although the sinner should repeat his crimes a hundred times and God
should prolong the life during which he commits these crimes, nevertheless I
know that it will be ill with him, but, on the contrary, well with those who
fear God. Yet we should understand that this recompense of the godly and those
who fear God is suitably inserted, because nearly all the crimes Tyrants
perpetrate are directed against the godly and those who fear God. The faith of
the godly could therefore be struck down and shaken unless they were sustained
by this promise.

It is therefore just as if Solomon were saying: although these Tyrants should
exercise their fury long and greatly by laying frequent blows upon the backs
of the godly, nevertheless it is certain that at last it will be well with the
godly, Isaiah 3:11. Therefore, let no one who has committed a crime be secure
on the ground that the punishment decreed by God and designated by the Word is
not immediately put into execution. For heaven and earth will perish sooner
than one jot or one little point should fall away from those things God has
threatened in the Law. For this very reason the curses are said not only to
come, but also to pursue offenders as they flee and never grow weary until
they overtake the evildoers; and a little later, “they will pursue until they
have ruined you.”

It should be noted that he says: even if

the sinner should repeat the crime a hundred times out of the malice of his
heart that has arisen from the delay of punishment, nevertheless he will not
go unpunished. It is well known that if people escape the punishment of a
committed crime three or four times, only then do they persuade themselves
that they will be immune from punishment even if they repeat that crime more
often. And grant that you escape with impunity while doing this a hundred
times: nevertheless punishment will hold you at last.

The sinner then thinks that God prolongs his days and life so that he may
repeat these flagitious deeds so many times; and he does not readily bring
himself to believe that God would do so if God abhorred those crimes so
greatly. And, says Solomon, grant that God prolongs your days so that you may
burden yourself with crimes; nevertheless, do not suppose that God will fail
to avenge himself fully—not even when all things flow according to your desire
as you sin.

The description of those who fear God should be noted. They are those who fear
before God's face, who abstain from evil and pursue what is good because they
have God as their observer, even if no mortal were to see them, mindful that
all the ways of a man are before Jehovah, Proverbs 5:21. By contrast, the
ungodly are described as those who do not fear before his face, although they
may be terrified before the face of human beings. The statement that the
ungodly, especially Tyrants, will not prolong their days—like the other curses
that belong to the present life—is to be understood as applying generally.
See the final verse of Psalm 55. Thus far concerning the judgment of the Holy
Spirit upon the perverse administration of rulers; the judgment of the Flesh
follows.

*Translator's notes:*

The source divides `cen-` at the foot of printed p. 322 from `ties` at the top
of p. 323. The paired texts place the complete normalized `centies` after the
PA323 marker.

The Greek `ἐπὶ τὸ πολὺ` means “for the most part” or “generally”: Cartwright
qualifies the present-life curse rather than treating it as an exceptionless
rule.

The next repeated lemma is Ecclesiastes 8:13, but the witness prints `VERS.
12.` The paired texts preserve that error at the boundary and identify the
actual verse without silently renumbering it.

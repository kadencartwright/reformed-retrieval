### GLOSSES Charged by Dr. Andreae against Theodore Beza in His Treatise on Predestination

1. Ezekiel 18: “I do not will the sinner’s death,” that is, “I will the death of the dying sinner created for damnation.”

2. Matthew 23: “How often I willed to gather your children,” that is, “I refused to gather you because I created, ordained, and destined you for eternal damnation.”

#### Theodore Beza’s Reply

To see how intolerable this man’s audacity is in corrupting everything truly and rightly said both from God’s pure word and from the church’s orthodox consensus against the Pelagians, see, if you please, Christian reader, what I wrote concerning these passages: on the causes of damnation, page 158; concerning an absolute will of God such as he fastens upon me, page 161 and following; in what respect God is to be said to will the damnation of the reprobate, pages 164 and 188; on the passage of Ezekiel, pages 168 and 196; on the passage of Matthew, page 169; and concerning the will of sign and good pleasure, page 173.

3. 1 John 2: “He is the propitiation not only for our sins but for the sins of the whole world,” that is, “only of the elect out of the world.”

4. John 3: “God so loved the world,” that is, “only the elect in the world.”

#### Theodore Beza’s Reply

Again, all these things are slanderous. For, with Christ himself as my authority, I restricted the designation “world” to those alone whom the Father gives him, excluding those for whom he testifies he does not pray and therefore was not sent by the Father. But I added the reason he calls the whole company of the elect the world—and indeed the whole world—namely because every distinction of nations has been removed. Does Dr. Andreae deny it? Let the apostle answer him in almost the whole Epistle to the Galatians, Ephesians 1, Acts 2, and Romans 1: “The gospel,” he says, “is the power of God for salvation TO EVERYONE WHO BELIEVES,” that is, to the Jew first and also to the Greek.

5. 1 Timothy 2: “God wills all human beings to be saved,” that is, “some.” Luke 24: “Repentance must be preached among all nations,” that is, “some.” Romans 11: “God has shut up all things under unbelief that he might have mercy upon all,” that is, “some.” 1 Peter 3: “The Lord wills all to return to repentance,” that is, “some.”

#### Theodore Beza’s Reply

May the Lord restrain the spirit of slander and even falsehood. I answered all these things one by one, both

[Part II | PDF page 252 | logical source scan 251 | printed page 250]

from comparison of the scriptural passages, from the testimonies of Augustine especially, and from the firmest arguments. Nowhere did I interpret ALL as SOME, but as ANY whomsoever from the whole number of human beings divinely elected, who themselves also constitute their own totality. It is no wonder that Dr. Andreae preferred shamelessly to corrupt rather than refute this interpretation, since the former is exceedingly easy for a man most practiced in this art, while lovers of truth cannot even attempt the latter. But what if, from his many not only false but also crude and ridiculous GLOSSES, as he calls them, I charge him with this one on the apostle’s passage: “God has mercy on whom he wills,” that is, “everyone”? “And he hardens whom he wills,” that is, “no one”? Both necessarily follow from his dogma. After his custom, of course, instead of a refutation he would hurl back at me whatever came into his mouth—anathemas and insults. And yet, if this slanderer of ours is believed, I did not produce even one testimony of Scripture; I repeated GLOSSES and recited false reasonings; I removed not only Christ from the sacraments but even the Word itself of the divine promises concerning the free remission of sins. Finally, I abolished every use of Holy Scripture and the sacraments for raising troubled consciences, and utterly overthrew all certainty of obtaining our salvation. Since all these things, says this good man, “have been refuted by us with the strongest testimonies of Holy Scripture, IT IS MANIFEST TO EVERYONE THAT God’s WORD and the text of Holy Scripture stand most openly for our side.” He then said this in a Stentorian voice. Although I wondered that the most illustrious prince could wink at it, I remembered what became me rather than so audacious an adversary, and, content with the murmuring of the greater part of the audience, answered not what that Thrasonical boast deserved but what Solomon then suggested to me, imposing silence upon me against him in Proverbs 26:4. Yet his scribes recorded the substance of my reply and inserted it into these Acts with the same faithfulness with which they received most other things. But with what inventions and ravings he defended his dogmas from beginning to end, let the judgment belong to fair readers and lovers of truth, until such time as—what all good people have long desired with deep sighs—a lawful assembly of true theologians and moderate men can be obtained from godly princes and civic magistrates, in which, once the insolence of contentious men has been restrained, all these controversies may be settled from God’s word. Meanwhile, whether the Lord finally grants us this remedy so necessary for establishing the churches’ peace, or whether, because our sins so deserve, this unbridled license to write and defend anything whatever proceeds still further, I have certainly determined not to set even one little word hereafter against any people of this kind.

Against their scribblings, yet, God helping me, so long as I breathe I will by no means betray the truth, but I will proceed so as not hereafter to spend even the smallest portion of time in vain rubbing their scabies and irritating their itch.

### In the Fifth Colloquy concerning Predestination, Agreement Was Reached on These Things

Both sides agree in all matters pertaining to the eternal decree of election of those to be saved and to their salvation in Christ.

But on this there is no agreement:

The reverend Württemberg colloquy participants acknowledge no eternal decree concerning those to be reprobated. For they say God loved each and every person from eternity in Christ and wills them to be saved, but that the fact so many perish must be attributed to those people themselves, because they neglected God when he called.

The participants of the other side, however, teach that the destruction of those who perish proceeds not from God but from themselves, that is, from their corruption and its fruits. But this does not prevent God—who could without any injustice have destined not one person to salvation in Christ, and who is debtor to none—from having decreed from eternity to leave in their native corruption those whom he pleased, finally to be condemned because of their sins.

The fair reader will perceive that we faithfully gathered these summaries of each preceding disputation by comparing the theses presented by both sides with one another. When, after the disputation ended, we sent them together with the writing appended here into the room of the Württemberg doctors who participated in the colloquy, we hoped they would not refuse to subscribe to them, and so we would carry home a great benefit from this colloquy. They, on the contrary, added these annotations in their own hand in the margin and sent them back a little afterward. The writing was composed in these words:

Since these things are so established, and since by God’s grace both sides agree upon the very foundations of the Christian religion despite those matters on which there is not yet agreement, and since there is hope that God most good and great will finally in his infinite mercy grant that they agree in everything, the participants of both sides pledged mutual friendship and brotherhood by giving their right hands. Both sides promised faithfully to take care, among whomever they could, that henceforth there should be abstention from those hateful terms “Zwinglians,” “Calvinists,” “Sacramentarians,” as also “Lutherans,” “Brentians,” “Ubiquitarians,” “Consubstantiators,” and the other terms of this sort, both in preaching and in speaking and writing; and finally that there should be abstention from all bitterness, and that with sincere minds they should devote themselves to procuring and advancing by whatever means possible the concord of the Christian churches against the common enemy, the Roman pontiff.

### Reply to the Preceding Annotations

Although, having suffered this rejection, we sufficiently understood the disposition of our adversaries, and with our greatest sorrow foresaw what soon happened—namely, that occasion would be seized from this very colloquy not only to renew old disputes but also to stir up new ones—nevertheless, so that we might leave nothing unattempted, in the presence of the most illustrious prince himself we requested the same things from them as kindly and almost as humbly as possible, as contained on page 566. When Dr. Andreae answered these things with an utterly inflamed mind, as appears from his words published by himself, testifying that he wondered how we could seek brotherhood with those against whom Beza in public writings had charged so many errors and so many condemned heresies—which they nevertheless did not teach but detested—I thought something must be said concerning this too, as concerning the preceding annotations. I therefore confess that, while Dr. Brenz was still alive and surviving, when I opposed what he calls his *Recognition*, I wrote and, as I still judge, showed at length in a written book by the clearest testimonies of Scripture and the orthodox ancient church that the dogma concerning the ubiquity, or what they call the omnipresence, of Christ’s flesh was compounded from Nestorianism, in which the natures are separated, and Eutychianism, in which the properties of the natures are confused. Yet I did so with no insults whatever; indeed, with many entreaties from me, the most illustrious and humane Prince Duke Christoph of blessed memory was asked for a calm and Christian colloquy concerning this controversy. For I had previously learned from three embassies undertaken before his highness for the French churches that the most illustrious prince himself was by no means opposed to this plan. Who thereafter stoked this fire, compelled the theologians of our side to write so many Apologies, and compelled me too among others—though by no means difficult [δύσχολον], as I call all the observers of my life to testify—to deal more vehemently with some, I call as witnesses the readers of what was fully written on both sides, and especially that letter of mine prefixed to certain exceedingly learned and orthodox little books of Dr. Hesshus, very well suited to settling these controversies, which I inscribed to the most illustrious Prince of the Chatti and Lord, William, Landgrave of Hesse. But how much I would prefer both sides to reflect thus on the past, both as regards the matters themselves and the style, so that provision might be made for the future with the spirit that befits pastors and teachers of churches, and so that we might no longer furnish our common adversaries an occasion for profane exultation against God’s truth! I am not at all surprised that Dr. Andreae said he had wondered. Indeed, I would wonder if he had not done so. I said that although we did not agree in those most serious disputations, we nevertheless

[Part II | PDF page 255 | logical source scan 254 | printed page 253]

agreed on the very foundations of the Christian religion. He wishes that in his observation this had been truly said by me; I maintain that it was truly said by me. For whatever subject of our colloquy is considered, I affirm that what they build upon these foundations is destroyed in no way better and more certainly than by the foundations laid beneath and conceded both by them and by us. I do not refuse to have all fair readers judge whether I say this truly or falsely, once they have read the summary of the matters on which we agreed and disagreed. But who wonders that those lawless people [ἀθεμίτους] conduct themselves in this way in the church? They would not even permit this to be sought from themselves if they would at least read the ancient church’s history and set before themselves for imitation the example of the ancient, most learned and most religious bishops. Those bishops were continually mindful not only of common brotherhood but also of mutual reverence among bishops, even when writing to those who were already guilty of the most condemned errors but had not yet been deprived of authority by synodical authority. Indeed, the apostle himself does not disdain to address the Achaian and Galatian churches by the designation “brothers,” although among the former there was a serious dispute about the very article of the resurrection of the flesh, while the latter had for the most part defected to a false gospel. But by God’s grace we do not at all fear what our adversaries instead must fear; otherwise we would by no means have persevered for so many years in demanding a just and lawful synod of the holy churches. Let those men consider with what shame, conscience, and audacity they have abused godly princes’ accommodation and thrust upon the church—what was never permitted there—the definitions of a few self-appointed men [αὐθαιρέτων], and indeed of the very men who instead should have rendered an account of their writings and deeds. Let them consider how they disturbed so many consciences, deprived so many churches of faithful pastors and teachers, and finally, so far as they were able, condemned the memory of so many men illustrious in all learning, piety, integrity, and every other virtue, whose very sandals those men do not deserve to lick, and how, as disciples ungrateful to their teachers, they brought such devastation upon Germany’s most celebrated academies. To return to the matter: it is true that we insistently demanded the right hand of brotherhood, not as if we agreed in everything, but so that, once every offense of mind was removed, an entrance might afterward open to calm examination. But what Dr. Andreae recounts in his Acts, that we refused to extend the right hand of friendship, he tells with the same faithfulness with which, after returning home in each direction, they wrote very many other things in the letters I produced in their place. For the matter does not stand so. The truth is that when they expressly refused to promise us brotherhood, I replied that they were offering the right hand—the sign of that reality—which they denied in reality. From this they certainly should also have inferred that it is not our practice to strip the sacraments of the things signified, as they charge us, since even in outward dealings we detest all hypocrisy in words

[Part II | PDF page 256 | logical source scan 255 | printed page 254]

and reality.

As for that narrative concerning the French who were then exiles for religion and were received most humanely at Montbéliard by the most illustrious prince, which Dr. Andreae thought should be appended to his Acts with his own scholia also added, I have only this to answer. It is true that both replies of the most illustrious prince were given to the French, and especially that the latter was handed to me as I was already mounting my horse. Being absent, I can testify nothing concerning the rest except from hearing. But I heard these things from not a few of those most honorable and trustworthy men: everything contained in the instrument about which the most illustrious prince complains is entirely true and was heard and recorded by the notaries and witnesses themselves. Although it was not drawn up and written in the presence and with the knowledge of the Montbéliard ministers, the instrument cannot therefore be accused either as false or as drawn up and prepared secretly. As those of whom I have spoken testify with a good conscience, it was requested from that notary for no other reason than to prevent, when they returned to France, anything being charged against them as admitted by them that conflicted with the sworn French confession. So far were they from seeking to detract from the most illustrious prince’s authority, whose greatest benevolence and humanity they had experienced, from disturbing in any way the church in which they were received most kindly, or, finally, from harming anyone. But it is not my purpose to treat these matters at greater length or either accuse or excuse that deed. I say only that it is established that, just as the Montbéliard church, when the most illustrious Prince Count George of blessed memory ruled, was first planted through the ministry of that faithful servant of God and father of almost all the churches from the jurisdiction of Montbéliard as far as Geneva, William Farel, and was afterward piously and prudently cultivated by Lord Toussain, also of blessed memory, so no other doctrine was proclaimed and received there than what was common in every respect to all the Swiss and Savoyard churches. But when the monster of ubiquity finally raised its head, new confessions and liturgies were not only brought in but thrust upon them, certainly with no fruit but with the greatest disturbance of very many churches and the offense of the neighboring churches, which had previously been most closely united. While the most illustrious Prince Count Frederick of Württemberg was still a boy, the whole condition of those churches was greatly disturbed, although citizens often testified that they stood and would continue to stand in their old confession. Hence came that twofold Montbéliard confession: one more recent, which caused the French exiles there to withdraw from communion in the Supper; the other old and consistent with the French confession, about which, as I hear is contained in that instrument, the French thought they had at last reached agreement with the Montbéliard ministers. Otherwise I myself never even saw it.

From this it appears what injury Osiander and others did to the French when they spread among their Tübingen associates, and dared write to Strasbourg and as far as Wittenberg, that very many French people had been converted from that allegedly most foul heresy of the Sacramentarians to the Brentian truth and testified to it in the sacred communion at Montbéliard. This is certainly as fabricated as ubiquity itself, and whatever is supported by ubiquity’s foundation will, when the Lord wills and human sins permit, fall in its own time like all other things of this kind.

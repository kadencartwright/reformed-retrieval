VERSE 14. I saw all the deeds that are done under the Sun, that behold, all
those things are vanity and affliction of spirit.

The result of the entire investigation is vanity, namely as regards the
establishment of happiness and the attainment of that thing in which the
human mind may rest even in this very life, much less that by the benefit of
this knowledge and wisdom we should arrive at eternal life. Hence the Apostle
teaches that God destroys the wisdom of the wise and renders void the
understanding of the intelligent; that the world, in its own wisdom—that
is, by the power of its own reason and intellect—did not arrive through the
wisdom that God revealed in the fabric of the world at the saving knowledge
of God. Indeed, by their own wisdom they are rendered more unfit to imbibe
that saving knowledge. Hence the Apostle teaches that God made their wisdom
foolish. See the Athenian philosophers in Acts 17.

He adds, moreover, another result of this study, namely vexation of spirit. So
far was he from putting into the harbor of tranquillity by means of this
wisdom that more disquiet accrued to him from it than if he had been devoid
and empty of it. Thus, at first appearance, the saying that to know nothing is
the most pleasant life was not alien from the truth—especially if it is
understood only of human branches of knowledge. First, he employed a very
great deal of grief and anxiety in searching out and pursuing this knowledge.
There is added a certain anxiety of mind, lest things acquired by great labors
and vigils fall away from us through the frailty of memory. Finally,

it greatly torments and tortures a man's spirit that, the more earnestly he
has devoted himself to this wisdom, the farther he perceives himself to be
from the perfection of this wisdom, since there is nothing in the universe so
lowly that even the most ingenious man can thoroughly penetrate its secrets
and dissect them to the quick. For the world, and the creatures contained in
it, is a Book of such magnitude and breadth that no one can read it through.
